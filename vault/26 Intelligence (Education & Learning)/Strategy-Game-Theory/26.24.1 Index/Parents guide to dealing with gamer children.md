Parents guide to dealing with gamer children

Game addiction is a worrying aspect today. Many children spend hours a
day on computers or their video games that it has become a primary
source of entertainment. They also become a convenient baby sitter for
parent. So what could the parents do to help deal with their gaming
addicted children?

Set boundaries. Limit your child to how long they can use the computer
or videogame. The maximum should be 1-2 hours a day.

Don't use video game or computer as a reward for good behavior. You
don't want to reinforce the notion that homework or chores are something
to get through. The rewards should be varied and include activities
which include personal interaction with family and friends.

Enroll them in sports classes. These physical exercises will not only be
good for your child but it also will get them out of the house and
inculcate sense of sportsmanship.

If your child is shy or uncomfortable in social situations, and that is
why they prefer video games, find ways to get him involved in social
activities. Send him for summer camp or enroll him in tennis classes or
classes that get him to learn a new skill and at the same time make new
friends.

Pay attention to their progress in school. Pay attention to their school
reports and help them with their homework. Perhaps your child is
struggling at school and their only sense of achievement is
accomplishment. Arrange for extra help if needed.

Lastly, it may take professional help to get to the root of the problem.
If you feel that your child has a serious gaming addiction, it is time
to consult the family therapist. Go together as a family. A counselor
can help identify any underlying problems which may be causing the
addiction and provide ways for the child and family to resolve the
problem.
