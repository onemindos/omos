# CT Pistol Permit — Greenwich Process & Travel Rules

> **Status:** Research phase — permit not yet applied for
> **Location:** Greenwich, CT

---

## Why the CT Pistol Permit Is Worth It

The CT Pistol Permit is the **most powerful firearms credential in CT**. One permit covers:
- ✅ Concealed carry (and open carry where legal)
- ✅ Purchasing handguns
- ✅ Purchasing long guns (rifles/shotguns)
- ✅ Purchasing ammunition

Separately, CT offers Eligibility Certificates for pistols, long guns, and ammo — but the **pistol permit replaces all of them**. No need to get the others.

---

## Application Process (Greenwich PD)

### Step-by-Step

1. Complete a CT-approved firearms safety course (e.g., NRA Basic Pistol Course)
2. Pre-enroll in the CT background check system (CCHRS) before fingerprinting
3. Submit pistol permit application to **Greenwich Police Department**
4. Complete fingerprinting + background check (appointment-based)
5. Receive **Temporary Permit** from Greenwich PD
6. Bring Temporary Permit to **CT DESPP** to receive the **5-year State Pistol Permit**

### Cost Breakdown (Estimated — Without Training)

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Greenwich local application fee | ~$70 |
| Fingerprinting/background check | ~$88.25 |
| Fingerprinting service fee | ~$10 |
| State permit issuance fee | ~$70 |
| **Total (excl. training)** | **~$240+** |

> Training costs vary — NRA Basic Pistol courses typically run $100–$200 in Fairfield County.

## Constitutional Carry — What It Means

**Connecticut is NOT constitutional carry.** A permit is required to carry.

Constitutional carry = state allows concealed carry without a permit (as long as you can legally own a firearm).

---

## Florida — Carrying as a CT Resident

Florida is **permitless (constitutional) carry** as of 2023.

Requirements to carry in FL:
- 21+ years old
- Legally allowed to possess a firearm
- Carry **concealed** (open carry is generally not permitted in FL)

A CT pistol permit is not required to carry in FL — but having one provides reciprocity benefits in other states.

## Traveling with a Firearm

### Driving to Florida

Federal protection under **18 USC §926A** applies during interstate travel:
- Firearm must be **unloaded**
- Stored in a **locked hard container**
- **Inaccessible** to driver (trunk or rear cargo area)
- Ammunition stored **separately**

⚠️ **Avoid unnecessary stops in NY or NJ** — strict states, no reciprocity with CT permit, and local laws can expose you to liability even in transit.

### Driving Checklist

- [ ] CT pistol permit + government ID on person
- [ ] Firearm unloaded and in locked case
- [ ] Ammo in separate locked container or bag
- [ ] Firearm in trunk/inaccessible to driver
- [ ] Know destination state's laws before arrival
- [ ] No stops longer than necessary in NY/NJ

### Flying with a Firearm

- Declare at airline check-in counter (before bag drop)
- Firearm: unloaded, in a **locked hard-sided case**
- Ammo: properly packaged (original box or purpose-built container)
- Only you can have the key or combination to the case
- TSA will inspect and tag the bag — bag is checked only, never carry-on

### Flying Checklist

- [ ] Declare at counter before screening
- [ ] Locked hard case with firearm unloaded
- [ ] Ammo in compliant packaging (max 11 lbs smokeless powder)
- [ ] Only owner holds the key/combination
- [ ] Confirm airline-specific rules (some have case size/weight limits)

## New York State — Key Restrictions

- **CT permit does NOT transfer into NY or NYC**
- NY State and NYC have their own separate, significantly harder permitting process
- NYC is particularly restrictive with its own licensing authority
- A handgun requires a **NY pistol permit** even on your own private property (farm, land, etc.)

### Shooting on a Private Farm in Upstate NY

Generally legal on private land **if**:
- No local ordinance prohibits discharge
- Safe backstop is in place
- Not within restricted distances (typically 500 ft from dwellings/roads — check local regs)

⚠️ **Critical:** Even on your own land in NY, a **handgun requires a NY pistol permit**. CT permit does not authorize handgun possession or use in NY. Long guns may be different — verify with a NY firearms attorney.
