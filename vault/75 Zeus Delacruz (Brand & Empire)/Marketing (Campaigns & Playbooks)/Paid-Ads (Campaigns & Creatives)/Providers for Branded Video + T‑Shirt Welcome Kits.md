Providers for Branded Video + T‑Shirt Welcome
Kits
Delivering a welcome package that includes a video message (via an LCD screen) and a custom T-shirt can
create a memorable experience for premium clients. Below are several provider options, along with their
capabilities and considerations, to help you find a solution around a ~$50 per-kit budget (with some
flexibility). We cover full-service solutions that handle everything for you, as well as more hands-on
approaches.


Full-Service Swag Kit Providers
These companies offer end-to-end service – they source or produce the items (video brochure, T-shirt, etc.),
handle branding, assemble the kits, and ship directly to each recipient on demand. This streamlines
fulfillment and branding but can come with minimum order requirements or service fees.


     • SwagUp: A popular swag fulfillment platform specializing in custom swag boxes and global drop
       shipping. SwagUp will design and assemble your kits (e.g. printing the T-shirt with your design
       and sourcing a video brochure pre-loaded with your video). They handle warehousing and per-
       request shipping – you can trigger individual shipments via their dashboard or integrations 1 2 .
       SwagUp historically required a 50-kit minimum, but as of 2023 they lowered it to 25 units for new
       orders 1 . Pricing depends on the items in the kit: for example, one of their standard 3-item swag
       kits costs about $48.99 per pack (before shipping) 3 , so a video brochure + shirt kit will likely be in
       the $50–$70 range per unit. They offer full branding services (you supply your artwork and video,
       they handle production) and you can include additional branded goodies (flyers, mugs, etc.) as
       needed. Automation: SwagUp supports API integration and “swag portals,” making it easy to auto-
       send kits to new clients. This is a full-service solution – minimal user coordination once the project is
       set up.


     • Printfection: A swag management platform that lets you create custom kits and drop-ship them
       worldwide on-demand. Printfection will source and print your merchandise (e.g. shirts, brochures)
       through their network. They can kit items together and handle individual fulfillment – you simply
       click to send swag to a client’s address 2 . Their system supports “Drop Ship” campaigns for one-
       off sends and even automated triggers via integrations (e.g. send a kit when a client signs up) 4 .
       Printfection doesn’t list a strict minimum, but you typically purchase inventory upfront (for 1,000
       clients over 8 months, you might order a few hundred at a time to stock). They charge a platform fee
       plus item costs and shipping. Expect the per-kit cost to include the video brochure (e.g. $30–$50
       each in bulk) plus the shirt ($8–$15 each), packaging, and handling. Printfection’s value is in
       convenience and scalability – full-service fulfillment with the ability to include additional inserts or
       gifts easily. (You provide the video file and art, and they handle the rest.)


     • Gemnote (or similar concierge services): Gemnote and others (e.g. Merchloop) offer high-touch
       swag services. They can curate a premium welcome kit with your video brochure, apparel, and even

       up a branded store for you 5 . This is ideal if you want each kit highly personalized or to let clients

Pros: Turnkey solution, integrates branding across all items, handles drop-shipping to each client

Cons: Higher upfront cost; may have minimum order quantities or platform fees. Make sure to get a

volume 1,000 should get bulk pricing).


Video Brochure & Box Specialists
If you’re focused on the video screen element of the package, there are specialty providers for video
brochures and video boxes. Many of these allow a fair amount of customization (print your branding on
the brochure/box and preload your video). Some can also assist with kitting a T-shirt or other items, though
often you’ll be coordinating the apparel separately or paying extra for assembly.


     • FL Solutions (Video Boxes): A provider specializing in video brochures and video boxes. They offer
       LCD video screens built into sturdy boxes, with custom printing all around. FL Solutions explicitly
       advertises that they can “build both the video box and the products inside”, meaning they can include
       your merch (shirts, gifts, etc.) in the kit 6 . The interior of the box is custom-fit (foam or insert) to
       hold whatever items you want to include 7 . This is ideal for a high-end presentation: for example, a
       box that plays your welcome video on the lid and has the custom T-shirt folded inside. Pricing: FL
       Solutions’ pricing varies by screen size and order volume. As a ballpark, a 7–10″ video box might cost
       on the order of $50–$70 each at 500+ units, dropping closer to ~$50 at the 1,000 unit mark 8 .
       (Smaller screen sizes, like 4.3″, can be cheaper – often under $40 each in bulk 9 .) They often have a
       25–50 unit minimum for custom orders. Fulfillment: You can supply your video file and artwork; they
       will produce the video boxes with the content preloaded 10 . Ask about drop-shipping services – FL
       Solutions can likely ship kits to individual addresses if you provide them, but clarify if there’s an extra
       fee. This is mostly a full-service solution for the kit production (printing and assembly of box +
       contents), though you’ll coordinate directly with them on design and shipping details rather than
       through an automated software platform. It’s a great option if the presentation value justifies a
       higher cost per unit and you want a truly impressive unboxing.


     • PrintAVizion / Red Paper Plane: These are companies known for video brochures and mailers.
       They offer products like hardcover video brochures and even “video screen well boxes” (boxes with
       a built-in video player and a compartment for a gift). For example, Red Paper Plane lists a 7″
       hardcover video brochure at $44.98 each for 1,000 units 11 , and they have a 7″ video well box at
       around $77 each at that quantity 12 . PrintAVizion similarly has bundles and blank video players with
       no minimum order (useful if you want a small test run) 13 . These vendors focus on the printed
       video piece: you provide the video and artwork for printing, and they deliver the finished video
       brochures/boxes. Some offer mailing fulfillment services for the video brochure alone 14 .
       However, including a T-shirt would be outside their standard product – you’d either:

     • Use their video box product to house the shirt. In the latter case, you’d likely need to handle the

       source apparel. Order minimums can range from 10–100 units depending on the product; pricing
       per unit gets much lower at the 500–1,000 quantity scale (as shown by the ~$45–$60 examples). This

       the shirts made and inserted.


     • VideoBrochuresDirect / Video Plus Print: These are other specialists that produce video brochures
       with custom printing. They often have packages starting around $39–$50 each for popular models
       in bulk 15 11 . Many set minimum order around 50–100 units for custom jobs 16 . They do not
       typically handle other swag items, so you’d use them for the video piece and then assemble it with
       a shirt yourself or via a fulfillment partner. If you go this route, check if they can ship units to your
       fulfillment center or office. The advantage here is you get the core video element at a good price
       (some quote as low as ~$17 each for very large orders 17 ), but you’ll need a plan for the rest of the
       kit.


Pros: Expertise in the video product ensures a quality screen, reliable playback, and good printing. You have
flexibility to tailor the kit contents. Many options to fit budget (choose screen size, memory, etc.).
Cons: May require coordinating separate vendors (one for video brochure, one for apparel, plus a shipper).
Full-service options like FL Solutions are easier but costlier.


Promotional Product Suppliers (Kits with User Coordination)
Traditional promotional product companies can source both the video brochure and the custom T-shirt,
and even other goodies, but you might need to manage assembly or pay extra for kitting. This is a semi-DIY
approach: you leverage their products and printing, then combine them for your clients.


     • EverythingPromo (and similar distributors): Many promo product vendors carry video brochure
       products and will print custom apparel. For instance, EverythingPromo offers a “Custom Designed
       Video Box with 7″ LCD Screen” for about $50 each at quantity 1,000 18 (roughly $54 at 100 units,
       down to $48 at 1,000 18 ). This video box plays your pre-loaded video when opened and has space
       inside for a gift or product 19 – perfect for inserting a folded T-shirt or other materials. In this
       model, you would order the video boxes with your branding (and send them your video file to load
       onto each unit), and also order custom T-shirts from the same or another vendor. Many promo
       suppliers can print the shirts for you too, but you’ll need to coordinate the kitting:
     • Some companies offer assembly services (for example, you could ask them to put the shirt inside
       the video box and shrink-wrap or label it). This might incur an additional kitting fee per unit.
     • Otherwise, they ship you (or a fulfillment center) the components, and you assemble and ship to
       clients. For 1,000 units over time, you might keep inventory and send them out as needed.
     • There are also fulfillment companies that you can hire to store the items and handle per-request
       shipping (e.g. a 3PL or a service like Amazon FBA or ShipBob, if you provide the inventory).

Order minimums: Promo product companies often have minimums (e.g. 50 or 100 units for the video
boxes, dozens for shirts), but your target of 1,000 units total is well above most minimums. Pricing: The

combined cost can be kept near $50 if you negotiate bulk rates – e.g. $48 for the video box in bulk 18 and
perhaps $7–$10 for a quality custom T-shirt. Adding printed inserts (welcome letter, brochures) is usually
inexpensive. Automation: This approach doesn’t inherently include an automation platform – you’d be
manually sending out kits or using a third-party logistics provider. It’s more labor intensive on the user’s
side unless you invest in a fulfillment solution.


     • Office Supply/Print Shops: Some large firms like Staples Promotional, Vistaprint Corporate, or
       local print shops can also produce custom boxes, video brochures, and apparel. For example,
       Vistaprint (in some regions) offers employee welcome kits with shirts and bottles 20 , though adding
       a video screen would be a special request. If you prefer a one-stop vendor, it’s worth asking if they
       can source a video brochure – many have partnerships to get electronic promo items. Fulfillment:
       Again, you’d need to discuss if they do on-demand drop shipping; some do offer “direct mail”
       services for an extra charge. Personalization: If you want each package slightly different (for
       different tiers of clients), these vendors will rely on your instructions per order (no dynamic logic
       unless you manage it).

Pros: Can be cost-effective – leveraging bulk pricing across vendors. You maintain control over each
element (choose any T-shirt brand/quality and a specific video box style). Good for tiered packages – e.g.
you could have two versions of the kit (one with extra items for VIPs) by ordering a mix of products.
Cons: Less streamlined – user coordination is required to bring it all together. Without an integrated
system, keeping track of who got a kit and when to send the next could be manual (though you can
automate notifications from your CRM to yourself). If using a fulfillment center, that’s another relationship
to manage (and additional cost per pick/pack).


Print & Fulfillment Services
There are also general print/fulfillment companies that can assemble kits combining printed materials
and merchandise. They don’t specialize in swag per se, but can be adaptable for your needs:


     • Mimeo: Known for on-demand printing, Mimeo also offers kitting and drop-shipping services for
       customer or employee packages. You can have them print brochures or welcome letters and include
       branded merchandise in the kit 21 . In practice, you could have your video brochure units and T-
       shirts sent to Mimeo’s warehouse, and they would pack them with any printed collateral and ship to
       each client as orders come in. Mimeo supports white-label packaging (unbranded or your branded
       boxes) 21 . This is a more à la carte approach: you’re using Mimeo’s logistics to streamline per-
       request fulfillment, but you must source the actual video devices and shirts (they might help procure
       these, but it’s not their core service). Pricing: You’d pay for printing (any paper materials), a handling
       fee per kit, and shipping. The benefit is scalability without building your own mail operation – they
       can handle 1,000 individual shipments over 8 months easily. Value: Consider this if you already have
       the kit components lined up and just need an efficient way to get them in clients’ hands one by one.
       It’s not full-service in terms of sourcing products, but it reduces your fulfillment burden
       significantly 22 .


     • PFL (Printing For Less): PFL provides a solution that integrates with marketing automation – you
       can trigger sends of direct mail or packages as part of your CRM workflows. They specialize in
       printing but also mention sending onboarding kits with branded items 23 . You could inquire about
       using their service to send a video brochure + shirt package triggered by an event (like a client

       This is a full-service fulfillment but requires coordination to set up the campaign in their system.


Pros: These services excel at on-demand distribution. They can act as your drop-ship partner if your
primary challenge is fulfilling 1,000 separate shipments on schedule. They also ensure consistent packaging
and can include any printed inserts easily.
Cons: You still need to procure the main items (video screens, shirts) which adds steps. There will be fees

fulfillment adds say $5–$10 per kit, that’s within your $50–per-unit target but important to note.


Summary of Considerations
When choosing a provider for your branded welcome kits, keep these key points in mind:


     • Unit Cost vs. Value: Hitting the ~$50/unit goal is feasible, especially at scale. For instance, a 7″ video
       box can be ~$48 each at 1,000 qty 18 , and a basic custom T-shirt ~$7–$10, totaling near $55 before
       shipping. Full-service providers might quote higher (they bake in convenience and all handling), so
       weigh the added value (time saved, automation) against the extra cost. In industries like coaching
       or consulting, the personal touch might justify spending a bit more per client for a turnkey solution.


     • Minimum Orders: Ensure the provider can handle 1,000 units over time. Services like SwagUp and
       Printfection are comfortable with that volume (and allow smaller batch releases). Video brochure
       manufacturers often require at least 50–100 units per production run. Since you need ongoing
       fulfillment over 8 months, look for providers that allow storing inventory and releasing in waves,
       or those that will produce on-demand. SwagUp’s minimum is now 25 kits 1 , so you could start with
       a small batch test. Traditional promo suppliers might require you to buy, say, all 1,000 video
       brochures upfront for the best price – verify if you can split into multiple orders.


     • Drop-Shipping & Automation: If you want hands-off, per-request shipping, favor a provider with a
       fulfillment platform (SwagUp, Printfection, etc.) or a fulfillment partner (Mimeo, PFL). This ensures
       each new client can get a kit without you manually packaging it. Ask about integration capabilities
       – some platforms can integrate with your CRM or email system to auto-send kits when triggered. If
       using a manual vendor, you might end up with bulk inventory and need to manually send kits (or
       upload spreadsheets of addresses periodically). There’s also a middle ground: use a promo supplier
       for production and a 3PL for distribution, which can be efficient if set up correctly.


     • Content and Branding Flexibility: All these options allow you to provide your own video and
       artwork. Typically, you’ll deliver:


     • A video file (often in MP4 format) for the video screen. Ensure you can update this if needed – many
       video brochures are reusable/reloadable via USB 10 , meaning you could refresh the video content
       for future batches.
     • Design files for printing: the outside of the video brochure/box and any printed materials, plus your
       T-shirt graphics. Full-service kit companies often help with design if needed, while product suppliers
       will give you templates to place your art.




                                                       5
         • Personalization: If you want each kit personalized (e.g. client’s name on a note or on the shirt),
           check if the provider supports that. Some fulfillment platforms can insert variable data prints (like a
           personalized letter for each client). Personalizing the video message per client would require loading
           unique videos on each unit, which is more complex – most likely you’ll use one welcome video for all,
           but this is something to clarify if needed.


         • Optional/Tiered Items: A good provider will let you customize the package contents. You might
           start with the core of video player + T-shirt, and have the option to include extras for certain client
           tiers. For example, for higher-value clients you could add branded merchandise (a mug, a pen, a
           brochure about premium services, etc.). Full-service companies and fulfillment partners can manage
           multiple package versions (you’d have, say, a “Standard Kit” vs. “VIP Kit” in their system). If you’re
           doing it yourself, you can still create tiers – just maintain separate inventory and send the
           appropriate items to each group. When evaluating providers, mention the possibility of add-ons: do
           they have other gift items you can source through them easily? Many promo companies have
           extensive catalogs if you want to include, say, a printed booklet, a gift card, or a small tech gadget to
           further delight your clients.


In conclusion, for maximum ease you might choose a full-service kit fulfillment provider that handles
everything from printing to shipping (with that convenience reflected in a higher per-unit cost). If budget
control is paramount, you can source the video brochures and shirts through promotional product
suppliers (getting close to the $50 mark) and use a fulfillment service to automate the mailing – this
requires more setup work on your end but can save money. All the mentioned approaches are scalable to
1,000+ units and will allow you to deliver a highly professional, branded welcome package to your premium
clients.
