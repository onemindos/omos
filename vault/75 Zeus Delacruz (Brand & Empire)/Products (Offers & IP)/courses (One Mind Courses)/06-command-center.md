# Course 6: COMMAND CENTER

> **This is how you actually run One Mind OS every single day.**

**Tier:** Sovereign ($49/mo)
**Circle Space:** 🏛️ War Room
**Prerequisite:** Course 1 — vault live, at least one agent running
**Target Student:** Someone who has the system built but doesn't have a consistent operating rhythm — the vault exists but they're not using it daily, the agent exists but they don't have a habit of working with it
**Outcome:** A locked-in daily operating system — morning ritual, work sessions, evening review, weekly cadence — running automatically with agents supporting every phase

---

## What You'll Build

By the end of this course:
- A morning briefing ritual that takes 10 minutes and sets the entire day
- A work session protocol that keeps you in flow with agent support
- An evening review habit that closes loops and feeds the next day
- A weekly review that compounds your system over time
- A monthly review that keeps your pillars aligned
- A personal dashboard in Obsidian that shows your system at a glance
- Runbooks for every domain so you never forget how anything works
- SOPs for your most important recurring operations

## Course Structure — 6 Modules, 35 Lessons

---

### MODULE 1: THE COMMANDER MINDSET

**Lesson 1.1 — You Are the Commander, Not the Worker**
The system exists to serve you — not the other way around. This module is about the mindset shift.
- The operator vs the operator's system: two different roles
- Why most productivity systems turn you into a servant of the system
- The Commander principle: you direct, agents execute, the system reports
- What changes when you have agents: you stop doing low-leverage tasks
- The daily question: "What am I directing today?" not "What am I doing today?"
- The One Mind OS operating posture: strategic decisions in, tactical execution out

**Lesson 1.2 — The Four Operating Modes**
Not every hour of the day is the same. Different modes require different protocols.
- **Command mode**: planning, directing agents, making decisions (high leverage)
- **Deep work mode**: focused execution on your most important work (no interruptions)
- **Review mode**: processing, learning, updating the system (compounding)
- **Recovery mode**: rest, physical, relationships (non-negotiable for longevity)
- Scheduling your modes: blocking time for each daily
- Agent behavior per mode: what your agents do differently in each mode
- Protecting deep work: how to configure agents to hold messages during focus blocks

**Lesson 1.3 — The ONE MIND OS Operating Cadence**
The nested review system that compounds your intelligence over time.
- Daily review: close loops, process inbox, set tomorrow
- Weekly review: pillar check, goal progress, priorities for next week
- Monthly review: domain health, metrics, adjustments
- Quarterly review: strategy, goals reset, system upgrades
- Annual review: identity, direction, major moves
- Why the cadence is not optional: skipping reviews is how your system rots
- The × Multiply step lives here: every review cycle compounds the system

**Lesson 1.4 — The Sovereignty Principle**
Running your own life with clarity and autonomy — what this system was built for.
- Sovereignty defined: full awareness and control of your life domains
- How most people live: reactive, fragmented, captured by other people's agendas
- The Sovereign Operating Standard: nothing falls through, nothing is forgotten, nothing is urgent
- Your 4 pillars in balance: you can't win in GE while HP is in crisis
- The daily health check: 60 seconds on each pillar every morning
- The goal: running your life like a CEO runs a company — with intelligence, structure, and calm

### MODULE 2: THE MORNING RITUAL

**Lesson 2.1 — The Morning Briefing**
Your agents have been watching your life overnight. This lesson teaches you how to get the brief.
- What Legacy prepares each morning: goals check, tasks due today, anything flagged
- The briefing prompt: the exact message you send Legacy at 7am (or it sends you automatically)
- Briefing format: today's top 3 priorities, one thing per pillar, open loops from yesterday
- Reading the brief: how to consume it in 5 minutes and know exactly what to do
- Automated briefing via cron: Legacy sends it to Discord before you wake up
- Adjusting the brief: how to tell Legacy what you always want to see

**Lesson 2.2 — The Daily Intention**
Before you execute, you direct. This is the 2-minute planning step.
- The 3 MITs (Most Important Tasks): the 3 things that if done, make today a win
- Selecting MITs with Oracle: "Given my goals and tasks, what are my 3 MITs today?"
- Writing your intention in the vault: one line, dated, in your daily note
- The intention vs the task list: why this distinction matters
- Protecting MITs: blocking your calendar around them
- The anti-pattern: starting the day reactive (email, Discord, messages) before intention

**Lesson 2.3 — The Pillar Check**
A 60-second scan across all 4 pillars before the day starts.
- SO: Is my system running? Any agent alerts overnight?
- HP: How am I feeling? What does my body need today?
- LE: Anyone I need to connect with? Any family context to hold?
- GE: What moves the empire forward today?
- The pillar check prompt: ask Legacy to surface one thing per pillar
- What it reveals: imbalances you wouldn't catch until they become problems
- Hands-on: build your personal pillar check prompt

**Lesson 2.4 — Morning Ritual Stack**
Assembling the full morning into one smooth protocol.
- The ideal morning sequence: physical → brief → intention → pillar check → work
- Total time: 10–15 minutes from wake to focused work
- The ritual trigger: the first action that starts the chain
- Customizing for your life: early vs late morning, with kids vs without, travel days
- The non-negotiables: what stays in the ritual no matter what
- Hands-on: design your personal morning ritual stack and test it for 7 days

### MODULE 3: DEEP WORK AND WORK SESSION PROTOCOLS

**Lesson 3.1 — Structuring a Work Session**
Every focused work session has a start, middle, and end. This lesson defines them.
- Session start: orient (what am I doing?), clear (remove distractions), commit (start the timer)
- Session middle: do the work, use agents for lookup/research, capture tangents in Inbox
- Session end: log what was done, capture next steps, close open tabs
- The 90-minute session: why this unit matches human ultradian rhythms
- Context switching tax: why 3 uninterrupted hours beats 6 interrupted ones
- The capture habit during sessions: never lose a thought, never break flow — capture to Inbox

**Lesson 3.2 — Working With Agents During Deep Work**
How to use your agents without breaking focus.
- The async principle: send a request, keep working, get the result when you surface
- Batch agent requests: collect 3 questions, send them all at once, process the answers together
- The agent as research assistant: "Oracle, while I work on X, pull context on Y and leave it in my Inbox"
- Voice capture integration: speak a thought to Telegram → Legacy captures it → you review later
- The interruption test: does this agent interaction require immediate attention? If not, queue it.
- Deep work mode config: how to tell OpenClaw to hold non-urgent messages during focus blocks

**Lesson 3.3 — The Capture Protocol**
Nothing gets lost. Everything lands in the Inbox. This lesson makes that automatic.
- The One Inbox principle: one place for everything that needs processing
- Capture channels: Obsidian quick-add, Telegram voice, Discord DM to Legacy, manual vault write
- The capture standard: enough context to understand it tomorrow, nothing more
- Capture templates: meeting notes, ideas, tasks, references — each with a template
- The processing backlog: what to do when Inbox grows to 50+ items
- Hands-on: set up 3 capture channels that work for how you actually operate

**Lesson 3.4 — Flow State Protection**
The configuration and habits that protect your best work.
- Notification blackout: iPhone focus modes, Discord mute schedules, Telegram quiet hours
- Agent hold mode: OpenClaw batches non-urgent messages during focus blocks
- The single-tab rule: one window during deep work, everything else closed
- Physical environment: the signals to your brain that work is happening
- The flow state recovery: what to do when you get pulled out (it will happen)
- The 5-minute re-entry: getting back into flow after an interruption

### MODULE 4: THE REVIEW SYSTEM

**Lesson 4.1 — The Daily Review (15 minutes)**
Close the day completely. Nothing carries into tomorrow undecided.
- Step 1: Process the Inbox — route every item to its domain or discard
- Step 2: Update active tasks — mark complete, update progress, move forward
- Step 3: Review tomorrow — confirm the 3 MITs for tomorrow, clear the calendar
- Step 4: Gratitude and learning capture — one win, one lesson (2 sentences each)
- Step 5: Send the EOD summary to Legacy — "Here's what I completed today"
- The 15-minute hard limit: if it takes longer, your Inbox is too big
- Automating with cron: Legacy's EOD review prompt at 9pm

**Lesson 4.2 — The Weekly Review (45 minutes)**
The most important habit in One Mind OS. This is where the system compounds.
- When to do it: Sunday evening or Monday morning — pick one and protect it
- Step 1: Collect — gather everything from the week (vault changes, completed tasks, notes)
- Step 2: Pillar check — one question per pillar: "How did this pillar do this week?"
- Step 3: Goal progress — are you on track across all active goals?
- Step 4: Priorities for next week — top 3 per pillar, top 1 overall
- Step 5: System update — anything to add to the vault, update agent memory, fix anything broken
- The Legacy weekly report: what to ask Legacy to prepare before you start
- Hands-on: run your first complete weekly review using this protocol

**Lesson 4.3 — The Monthly Review (2 hours)**
Zoom out. Look at the patterns, not just the tasks.
- The monthly review is strategic — you're assessing direction, not just checking off boxes
- Step 1: Metrics review — pull your key metrics from each active domain
- Step 2: Domain health — a score (1–5) for each domain: how healthy is it right now?
- Step 3: Goal audit — still working on the right goals? Anything to add, change, or drop?
- Step 4: System upgrade — one improvement to the vault, agents, or workflow
- Step 5: Next month intentions — 3 major moves across your pillars
- The Oracle monthly brief: having Oracle pull all your domain metrics before you start
- The asset review: what did you build this month that compounds?

**Lesson 4.4 — The Quarterly Review (Half Day)**
The biggest operating lever in the system.
- Quarterly is where strategy gets updated — annual plans are too far away to act on, weekly is too close
- Step 1: Quarter retrospective — what actually happened vs what was planned?
- Step 2: Pillar deep dive — 30 minutes per pillar (HP, LE, GE), honest assessment
- Step 3: Goals reset — set or update 1–3 goals per pillar for next quarter
- Step 4: System evolution — what needs to change in the architecture of how you operate?
- Step 5: Commitments — 3 non-negotiable commitments for next quarter
- The agent-assisted quarterly: Legacy prepares a full quarter report before you sit down
- The calendar block: 4 hours, no meetings, protected like surgery

### MODULE 5: DASHBOARDS AND VISIBILITY

**Lesson 5.1 — The Obsidian Dashboard**
A single note that shows the state of your entire life at a glance.
- What belongs on the dashboard: active goals, domain health indicators, today's priorities, open loops
- Dataview queries: pulling live data from your vault into the dashboard automatically
- The dashboard structure: SO status, HP/LE/GE active goals, today's focus, Inbox count
- Updating the dashboard: it should update automatically via Dataview, not require manual work
- The 30-second dashboard scan: what you check every time you open Obsidian
- Hands-on: build your personal Obsidian dashboard with 5 live Dataview queries

**Lesson 5.2 — The OpenClaw Dashboard**
Agent health, active sessions, cron status — all in one view.
- The gateway dashboard URL: what it shows, how to read it
- What to check daily: all agents responding, cron jobs running, no error flags
- The 60-second system check: gateway up → agents responding → last cron ran → disk OK
- Agent status per agent: when did it last receive a message? Any errors?
- The health check habit: making this automatic (cron pings, not manual checks)
- Anomaly recognition: what "normal" looks like so you spot problems fast

**Lesson 5.3 — The Life Dashboard Concept**
One view that spans all 4 pillars with key indicators.
- The life metrics you should be tracking: one per domain minimum
- HP indicators: sleep score, workouts this week, energy level (1–5)
- LE indicators: quality time hours, family connections made, home environment score
- GE indicators: revenue, pipeline, brand growth metric
- SO indicators: system health, agent uptime, vault freshness
- Where to track: vault frontmatter + Dataview queries
- The 5-minute weekly dashboard update: the habit that keeps it accurate

**Lesson 5.4 — Runbooks: Never Forget How Your System Works**
A runbook is a document that tells you (or anyone) exactly how to operate one part of your system.
- Why runbooks matter: you will forget — and future you will thank current you
- The runbook format: purpose, trigger, steps (numbered), expected outcome, troubleshooting
- Essential runbooks to start with: VPS restart, agent restart, vault sync check, new domain setup
- Where runbooks live: `00-24 SO/03 Command/runbooks/`
- Keeping runbooks current: every time you do the thing, update the runbook
- Agent-assisted runbooks: asking Grid to document a procedure after you execute it together
- Hands-on: write 3 runbooks for the operations you do most often

**Lesson 5.5 — SOPs: Standard Operating Procedures for Your Life**
SOPs are runbooks for recurring life operations — not just technical ones.
- Life SOPs worth documenting: weekly grocery order, monthly bills, travel packing, new project kickoff
- The SOP standard: specific enough that you could hand it to someone else
- SOP vs checklist: a checklist is a simplified SOP — both have their place
- Domain SOPs: one SOP per recurring operation per domain
- The SOP library: your personal operational playbook that grows over time
- Agent-assisted SOP execution: "Guardian, run the security SOP for new server setup"
- Hands-on: write 2 SOPs — one personal (HP), one operational (SO)

### MODULE 6: COMMAND CENTER SETUP

**Lesson 6.1 — Your Physical Command Environment**
Where you work shapes how you think. This lesson is about designing the physical layer.
- The dedicated workspace: why location matters for cognitive performance
- Display setup: what's always visible (dashboard, calendar, Discord)
- The clean desk principle: clear surface = clear mind before sessions start
- Keyboard shortcuts and launchers: reducing friction to reach any tool
- The ambient environment: music, lighting, temperature — dial them in
- The portable command center: how to maintain the system when traveling

**Lesson 6.2 — Your Digital Command Environment**
The tools open, the layout set, every session starting from the same known state.
- Obsidian as home base: the vault open, dashboard visible, daily note ready
- Discord layout: agent channels visible, notification filters set
- The always-open apps: Obsidian, Discord, Todoist, Calendar — nothing else
- Browser profiles: one profile for deep work, one for research, one for admin
- The app dock: only what you need, nothing that distracts
- Session startup script: one click to open everything in the right configuration

**Lesson 6.3 — Todoist as Execution Layer**
Todoist is where tasks live and get done. The vault is where context lives.
- The One Mind OS Todoist structure: one project per active domain
- The task format: action verb + clear outcome + due date + priority
- The inbox in Todoist: everything captured here before routing
- The daily task ritual: Legacy pulls today's tasks from Todoist into your briefing
- Todoist ↔ vault sync: how `todoist-context-bridge` plugin keeps them connected
- The task lifecycle: capture → route → schedule → execute → archive
- Hands-on: restructure your Todoist to match your One Mind OS domains

**Lesson 6.4 — Calendar as the Physical Layer**
Time is the one truly finite resource. The calendar is where decisions become commitments.
- The Calendar architecture: time blocks, not task lists
- The 5 calendar block types: deep work, command, meetings, reviews, recovery
- Time blocking your MITs: put your 3 MITs on the calendar or they won't happen
- Agent calendar integration: Legacy can read your calendar context (with the right MCP)
- The weekly calendar setup: Sunday plan → calendar blocked → week protected
- Calendar hygiene: no meeting without a clear purpose and outcome
- Hands-on: time block next week using the 5 block types

**Lesson 6.5 — Graduation: Your Operating System Is Live**
You now have a complete personal operating system. This lesson documents it.
- The full stack you've built: vault + agents + morning ritual + work protocol + reviews + dashboard
- The Operating Manual: a single document that describes how you run your life
- The 30-day check-in: what to review after one month of running the system
- The compounding effect: what looks different after 90 days of consistent reviews
- Signs the system is working: you feel less reactive, more intentional, clearer on priorities
- What to do when the system breaks: the recovery protocol (everyone falls off, the system makes re-entry easy)
- Graduation challenge: run the full operating system for 7 consecutive days
