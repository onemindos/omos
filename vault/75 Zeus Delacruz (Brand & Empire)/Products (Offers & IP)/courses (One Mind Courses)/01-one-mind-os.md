### MODULE 1: WHY EVERYTHING ELSE FAILS
*"Before you build, you need to understand what you're replacing — and why it keeps breaking."*

**Lesson 1.1 — The Fragmentation Problem**
Your life is scattered across 47 apps. None of them talk to each other. None of them know who you are. None of them compound. This lesson frames the problem every student has felt but never named.
- The average knowledge worker uses 9+ tools daily
- None of them share context
- AI assistants are powerful but stateless — they forget you every session
- The cost: mental overhead, decision fatigue, zero compounding

**Lesson 1.2 — Why GTD, PARA, and Second Brain Break in the AI Era**
Honest breakdown of every productivity framework that came before — and the exact reason each one fails when AI enters the picture.
- GTD (2001): built for humans working alone — no AI layer, no context
- PARA (2017): solves file organization, not execution or intelligence
- CODE/Second Brain (2022): stores knowledge but can't *act* on it
- The gap: none of them tell AI *who you are*, *what you want*, or *what it can do*

**Lesson 1.3 — The One Mind Thesis**
The first principles argument for why a structured, markdown-based, AI-readable Codex is the solution — and why it outlives every platform.
- Markdown lives forever. Apps die.
- Any intelligence — human, AI, or future — can read plain text
- Structure + AI = leverage. Chaos + AI = amplified chaos
- "You don't need a Second Brain. You need One Mind."

**Lesson 1.4 — The Equation: Human + AI = One Mind**
What you bring. What AI brings. Why neither alone is enough.
- You bring: strategy, judgment, relationships, creativity, values, lived experience
- AI brings: memory, speed, pattern recognition, tireless execution, 24/7 availability
- The split: you make the calls, AI does the work
- The compound effect: every cycle makes the system smarter

**Lesson 1.5 — The Framework Overview (5-Minute Tour)**
One pass through everything: the Codex, the 4 pillars, the CODE× method, the agent layer. High altitude. Just enough to see the whole picture before drilling in.

### MODULE 2: THE CODE× METHOD
*"The 5-step loop that runs inside One Mind. This is the engine."*

**Lesson 2.1 — C: CAPTURE — Get Everything Out of Your Head**
Nothing stays in your head. Everything goes into the Inbox. This lesson builds the capture habit.
- The Inbox as sacred: it is a landing pad, not a graveyard
- Capture sources: voice memo, typed note, screenshot, email forward, AI insight
- The Obsidian Inbox folder: `99 Inbox (Queue)`
- The capture rule: capture now, sort later — never both at once
- Tools: Obsidian mobile, voice-to-text, QuickAdd plugin
- Hands-on: capture 10 things from your head right now

**Lesson 2.2 — O: ORGANIZE — Route Everything to Its Domain**
Once captured, everything gets a home. This lesson teaches the domain routing decision.
- The 4-pillar filter: Personal (HP), Home (LE), Business (GE), Operations (SO)
- The 3 routing questions: Does this improve ME? Does this protect US? Does this create VALUE?
- The domain numbering system: 00-24 (SO), 25-49 (HP), 50-74 (LE), 75-99 (GE)
- When in doubt: leave it in SO 99 Inbox and flag for review
- Hands-on: route the 10 captures from Lesson 2.1

**Lesson 2.3 — D: DIRECT — Set Goals AI Can Act On**
Direction is where most systems die. A goal without structure is a wish. This lesson teaches goal-setting that AI can actually execute.
- The goal format: outcome + deadline + metric + domain
- YAML frontmatter: why it matters for AI readability
- Priority tags: `#p1` (Critical) through `#p4` (Low)
- The decision layer: what gets done this week vs this quarter vs someday
- Hands-on: write 3 goals in proper Codex format

**Lesson 2.4 — E: EXECUTE — Deploy Humans and AI**
Execution is the step every other framework skips. This lesson is about getting things done — with help.
- Tasks vs projects vs goals: the hierarchy
- Todoist as the execution layer: why it syncs with the vault
- `tid:` — the bridge between vault goals and Todoist tasks
- Delegating to AI: what kinds of tasks agents can run unsupervised
- The autonomy levels: L0 (human only) → L3 (AI acts without asking)
- Hands-on: create your first 5 tasks in Todoist from your vault goals

**Lesson 2.5 — × MULTIPLY — The Review Loop That Compounds**
The × is not "reflect and feel good." It's measurement, learning, and system evolution. This lesson builds the review habit.
- Daily review: 5 minutes, 3 questions (What came in? What moved? What's tomorrow?)
- Weekly review: 30 minutes — scorecard, wins, stucks, priorities reset
- Monthly review: domain-level health check across all 4 pillars
- Quarterly review: goal recalibration, agent evolution
- The compound effect: every review makes the system 1% smarter
- Hands-on: complete your first weekly review template

**Lesson 2.6 — Running a Full CODE× Cycle (Live Demo)**
Zeus runs a complete CODE× cycle on camera — from raw captures to organized vault to set goals to executed tasks to reviewed week. The full loop in under 30 minutes.

### MODULE 3: THE 4 PILLARS
*"Your life has 4 domains. Everything you do lives in one of them."*

**Lesson 3.1 — SO: Sovereign Operations — The Platform**
SO is not a life domain. It's the operating layer that runs the other three. This lesson explains why the invisible infrastructure matters.
- AI agents, tech, automation, protocols — all live in SO
- SO is the brain of the system. HP, LE, GE are the life
- Without SO, your agents have nowhere to live and nothing to coordinate
- The 9 SO domains: Framework, Intelligence, Infrastructure, Command, Security, Development, Research, Automations, Assets

**Lesson 3.2 — HP: Holistic Performance — You**
Your health, mind, skills, money, identity, and joy. Everything that makes you the human running the system.
- The 10 HP domains: Identity, Spirituality, Mind, Body, Relationships, Finance, Mastery, Rhythm, Sovereignty, Joy
- Why HP is the first pillar to build — you can't run anything if you're broken
- The HP goal: build the best version of yourself, systematically
- Key concepts: the PI Score, biometric tracking, skill acquisition frameworks, financial architecture

**Lesson 3.3 — LE: Legacy Evolution — Your People**
Your relationships, home, family, community. The things that outlive you.
- The 8 LE domains: Home, Covenant, Offspring, Parents, Heritage, Health, Estate, Community
- Why LE is the most neglected pillar — and what it costs when it's empty
- The generational lens: every LE decision echoes forward
- Key concepts: the 500-year family vision, family knowledge vault, estate planning basics

**Lesson 3.4 — GE: Generational Empire — Your Work**
Your business, brand, ventures, wealth, partnerships, and impact.
- The 9 GE domains: Brand, Real Estate, Acquisitions, Holdings, Investments, Innovation, Strategy, Partnerships, Philanthropy
- GE is not just "business" — it's every vehicle for creating value in the world
- The GE goal: build assets that generate wealth without your constant presence
- Key concepts: YOU Inc., content as infrastructure, the value ladder

**Lesson 3.5 — How the Pillars Connect**
Nothing in HP, LE, or GE is isolated. This lesson shows the cross-pillar dependencies — and how SO coordinates them all.
- Your fitness (HP) affects your business energy (GE)
- Your relationships (LE) affect your identity (HP)
- Your business (GE) funds your home (LE)
- SO agents route information between pillars automatically
- Hands-on: map 5 things in your life across pillars — where does each one actually belong?

### MODULE 4: BUILD YOUR VAULT
*"Theory is over. Your Codex starts here."*

**Lesson 4.1 — Install Obsidian**
Step-by-step: download, install, create your first vault.
- Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone, Android
- Why Obsidian over Notion, Roam, Logseq — the sovereignty argument
- The free tier is everything you need
- Recommended plugins to install first (from the optimized list)

**Lesson 4.2 — Download + Import the One Mind Starter Template**
The official template vault: pre-built folder structure across all 4 pillars, frontmatter templates, folder notes, README stubs.
- What's included in the template
- How to import into Obsidian
- First customization: put your name in the identity file
- What NOT to change (the folder number system)

**Lesson 4.3 — Your First 4 Files**
The 4 files every Codex must have before anything else.
- `identity.md` — who you are, your values, your principles
- `mission.md` — what you're building and why
- `goals.md` — your active goals across all pillars
- `99 Inbox (Queue)/capture-[date].md` — today's captures

**Lesson 4.4 — Frontmatter and Why AI Reads It**
YAML frontmatter is not bureaucracy — it's the interface between your vault and your agents. This lesson makes metadata make sense.
- What frontmatter looks like: `id`, `type`, `status`, `tags`, `owner`, `domain`
- Why agents use frontmatter to route, filter, and act
- The standard frontmatter spec for One Mind
- Hands-on: add proper frontmatter to your first 4 files

**Lesson 4.5 — The Plugin Stack (Configured)**
The 24 plugins in the optimized Obsidian setup — what each one does and how to configure the ones that matter most.
- Templater: auto-fill frontmatter on new notes
- QuickAdd: capture without leaving what you're doing
- Dataview: live queries across your vault
- Todoist Sync: bridge between vault and execution layer
- Obsidian Git: version control and sync to GitHub
- Smart Connections: AI-powered semantic search across your vault

**Lesson 4.6 — Git Sync: Your Vault on GitHub**
Your Codex lives in git. This lesson sets up version control so your vault is safe, synced, and accessible to agents.
- Why git for a knowledge vault — not just code
- Obsidian Git plugin: install, configure, first commit
- Connecting to GitHub: create repo, add remote, push
- The auto-commit setting: every 5 minutes, automatic backup
- How your VPS agent reads from this repo (preview of Course 2)

**Lesson 4.7 — Mobile Capture (Obsidian on iPhone)**
The capture habit only works if you can capture anywhere.
- Install Obsidian on iPhone
- iCloud sync vs git sync — tradeoffs
- The voice memo → Inbox workflow
- Widgets and shortcuts for one-tap capture

**Lesson 4.8 — Knowledge Management: Your Codex as a PKM System**
The Codex is not a note dump. It's a structured knowledge management system that compounds over time.
- PKM defined: a system for capturing, organizing, and retrieving what you know
- Why Obsidian + Git is the superior PKM architecture (portability, version history, AI-native)
- Atomic notes: one idea per file — why smaller notes retrieve better
- The evergreen vs temporal distinction: permanent knowledge vs dated captures
- Tags vs folders vs links: the three organizational systems and when to use each
- The knowledge graph: how backlinks reveal connections you didn't plan
- Spaced repetition for your vault: surfacing old knowledge before you forget it
- Hands-on: convert 5 dated notes into evergreen knowledge notes

### MODULE 5: YOUR FIRST AGENT
*"The vault is live. Now it gets a brain."*

**Lesson 5.1 — What OpenClaw Is**
OpenClaw is the agent runtime — the thing that makes AI agents persistent, always-on, and connected to your life. This lesson explains it without jargon.
- OpenClaw vs ChatGPT vs Claude.ai — what's different
- The gateway concept: one server, many agents, all your channels
- Why you host it yourself: sovereignty, persistence, customization
- The 4 things that make an agent intelligent: personality + knowledge + tools + guardrails

**Lesson 5.2 — Install OpenClaw (Mac)**
Step-by-step installation on Mac. Written guide + screen recording.
- Prerequisites: Node.js 20+, npm, git
- `npm install -g openclaw`
- Create your first workspace: `openclaw init`
- The workspace folder structure explained
- Connect to Claude via GitHub Copilot API (free tier available)
- Start the gateway: `openclaw gateway`

**Lesson 5.3 — The 4 Files That Make Legacy Smart**
Legacy (your orchestrator agent) becomes intelligent through 4 markdown files. This lesson writes them together.
- `IDENTITY.md` — name, emoji, role, vibe ("You are Legacy. You are Zeus's AI co-pilot.")
- `SOUL.md` — personality, communication style, values, what you will and won't do
- `USER.md` — who Zeus is, his goals, communication preferences, context that never changes
- `CODEX.md` — vault location, domain structure, how to use MCP tools to read/write files
- Hands-on: write all 4 files from scratch using the templates

**Lesson 5.4 — Connect Legacy to Your Vault**
Your agent needs to be able to read your Codex. This lesson connects the dots.
- `memorySearch.extraPaths` — point Legacy at your vault folder
- MCP filesystem server: `@modelcontextprotocol/server-filesystem`
- Test: ask Legacy to read your identity.md and summarize it
- Test: ask Legacy to read your goals and tell you your top 3 priorities
- What it feels like when your agent knows your life

**Lesson 5.5 — Your First Conversation with Legacy**
First real interaction. Not a demo — your actual life.
- Ask Legacy: "What are my active goals?"
- Ask Legacy: "What domain is this task in?" (give it something from your inbox)
- Ask Legacy: "Help me plan my week based on my goals"
- Ask Legacy: "What did I capture this week?"
- The feeling: this is different from ChatGPT

**Lesson 5.6 — Connect Discord (Your First Channel)**
Get Legacy talking to you through Discord — the first always-on communication channel.
- Create a Discord server (your private command center)
- OpenClaw Discord integration: bot token setup
- Channel binding: `#legacy` → Legacy agent
- Test: send a message, get a reply, your agent is live
- Optional: Telegram setup as alternative

3. 5 tasks in Todoist synced from vault goals
