# Home Organization Playbook

> Distilled from 6 source articles into one working reference. Core principle: **clarity before storage**. Buying storage bins before you declutter is the most common organizing mistake.

---

## The A-PACT Method

A full room-by-room framework. Run this in any space that feels out of control.

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **A** — Ask | What do I want from this room? Define its purpose before touching anything. |
| **P** — Pile | Group like items together. Pull everything out and put it in piles by category. |
| **A** — Analyze | Treasure or trash — **no "I'll think about it" pile.** Everything must go one way or the other. |
| **C** — Cash-in | Remove what's leaving: donate, sell, or dump it. Get it out the same day if possible. |
| **T** — Tidy Up | Now organize what remains. This is when storage solutions make sense — after, not before. |

---

## The 3-Column Exercise (For Stubborn Items)

For items you can't decide on — write it out:

| Item | Why I'm keeping it | What I gain by getting rid of it |
|------|--------------------|----------------------------------|
| (example) Old gift I never use | "Feels rude to give away" | Counter space, less visual noise, less guilt |

> Seeing "what I gain" in writing forces honest accounting. Most people realize they're keeping things for imaginary reasons.

---

## Daily Rules

### Touch It Once
Handle every item once — don't set it down to deal with later. Open mail → file or trash it. Clothes → hung or in hamper. No staging areas.

### Keep Like Items Together
Everything in its category has one home. No searching, no "I'll put it here for now."

### One Space at a Time, 15–20 Minutes Daily
Don't try to organize the whole house in a weekend — burnout kills follow-through. Pick one drawer, one shelf, one corner. Finish it. Stop.

---

## Storage Buying Rules

1. **Never buy storage before decluttering.** You will buy containers for things you don't need.
2. **Assess what remains** after A-PACT, then buy exactly what fits that specific inventory.
3. **Cheap storage is fine** — the system matters, not the price of the bins.
4. **Label everything.** An unlabeled bin becomes a junk bin within 3 months.

## Myth-Busting

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "I'm just not an organized person — it's how I'm wired" | Organization is a skill and a habit, not a personality trait. Anyone can build it. |
| "I need expensive storage to stay organized" | Cardboard boxes with labels work. You need a system, not a Container Store haul. |
| "Getting organized takes a massive time investment" | 15–20 minutes daily compounds fast. Most spaces can be tackled in a week at that pace. |

---

## Why Bother (Personal Motivators)

Write your own reasons — vague motivation doesn't hold. Common ones:

- **Less stress**: Visual clutter is cognitive load. Clear space = clearer head.
- **More space**: You may not need a bigger home — you may just need less stuff.
- **More time**: Knowing where everything is eliminates the daily search tax.
- **Saves money**: You stop buying duplicates of things you already own but can't find.

## Delacruz Household Application

- **Current focus areas**: Kitchen, home office/studio, garage/workshop
- **Cadence**: One space per week during the homestead build-out phase
- **Decision rule**: If it hasn't been used in 12 months and has no specific future use date → it goes
- **Staging rule**: Nothing sits on a counter or floor "temporarily" for more than 24 hours
