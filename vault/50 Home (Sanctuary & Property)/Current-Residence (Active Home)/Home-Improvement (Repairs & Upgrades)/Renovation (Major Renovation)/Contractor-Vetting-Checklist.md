# Contractor Vetting Checklist

> Use this every time before accepting any contractor bid. A bad contractor will take your money and deliver poor work — or not finish at all. Due diligence takes 30 minutes and can save thousands.

---

## Before You Accept Any Bid

### References
- [ ] Ask for at least 3 references from past clients
- [ ] **Actually call them** — don't just take the list
- [ ] Ask to see a recently finished job, a job from 1 year ago, and one from 2+ years ago
  - *This shows how work holds up over time, not just when it's fresh*

### Licensing
- [ ] Ask to see their contractor's license — in person, not a photo
- [ ] If they can't produce it or make excuses, **walk away immediately** — no exceptions
- [ ] Verify the license is current with your state's licensing board (searchable online in most states)

### The Workers
- [ ] Get the names of the actual workers who will enter your home (not just the contractor)
- [ ] Search their names online — look for complaints, reviews, BBB reports
- [ ] Ask if they are the contractor's employees or subcontractors (subcontractors = less accountability)

### On-Site Supervision
- [ ] Ask how much time the contractor will personally be on-site during the job
- [ ] Prefer contractors who stay on-site, not ones who drop in and leave
- [ ] Get the supervision commitment in writing if it's a major job

### Insurance
- [ ] Confirm they carry general liability insurance AND workers' comp
- [ ] Ask for a certificate of insurance — call the insurer to verify it's current
- [ ] If a worker is injured on your property with no coverage, you can be held liable

---

## On Pricing

- **Never default to the lowest bid.** Cheap bids = cheap labor = cheap materials
- If bids vary wildly, ask the low bidder exactly where they are cutting cost
- A fair bid reflects real material costs + skilled labor — not a lowball to win the job

**Rule of thumb**: In the right ballpark means within ~15–20% of the median bid. Far below = red flag.

---

## During the Job

- [ ] If you see something wrong, say it immediately — don't wait
- [ ] Do not back down until the issue is corrected to your satisfaction
- [ ] Do not pay the final installment until the job passes your personal walkthrough
- [ ] Keep a dated photo log throughout the project

---

## Payment Structure (Recommended)

| Stage | Amount |
|-------|--------|
| Contract signed | 10–25% down |
| Midpoint milestone | 25–40% |
| Near completion | 25% |
| Final walkthrough passed | Remaining balance |

> **Never pay more than 30% upfront.** A contractor demanding 50%+ before work starts is a warning sign.
