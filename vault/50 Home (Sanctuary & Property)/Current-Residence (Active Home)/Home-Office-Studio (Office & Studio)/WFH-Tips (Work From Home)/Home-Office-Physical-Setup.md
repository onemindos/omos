# Home Office Physical Setup

> The environment shapes the work. A chaotic workspace creates friction, lost time, and mental overhead. A designed workspace removes it. This is the reference for building Zeus's home office and content studio right from day one at the new property.

---

## Desk & Surface Layout

- **Corner desk**: Maximizes usable surface — space for spreading out materials, reference books, and tech without feeling cramped
- **On the desk**: Only what gets used daily — computer, mic/camera (if studio-integrated), phone, notebook
- **Nothing decorative that consumes work surface** — awards and display items go on shelves or walls, not the desk

**Arm's Reach Rule**: Everything needed to complete a task should be reachable without standing up. If you're standing constantly to get tools, the layout is wrong.

---

## Filing System

**Two-tier approach:**

| Tier | Location | What Lives Here |
|------|----------|----------------|
| Active | 2-drawer cabinet beside desk | Current projects, active files, recent receipts, everyday supplies |
| Archive | Main storage / separate cabinet | Completed projects, older records, tax history |

When an active file gets thick → move the bulk to archive, keep only recent correspondence active.

**Monthly receipts**: One folder per month. At month start, create next month's folder and close out the last. Tax time becomes a 5-minute sort instead of a 2-hour reconstruction.

---

## Binder System

For research, reference material, and content that needs to be reviewed and compared:

- 3-ring binders by category (clients, research, brand, finance, misc)
- Pre-punched paper only — print directly onto hole-punched sheets
- Binders make it easy to see everything in a category at once; file cabinets hide it

---

## The No-Pile Rule

**Nothing sits on a surface waiting to be dealt with.**

A pile is a deferred decision. Every item in a pile carries cognitive weight — you mentally re-process it every time you see it. Piles grow. They become invisible over time. You miss things.

Rule: every item either goes to its place, gets logged in Todoist/notebook, or gets discarded. No staging.

---

## Daily Bookends

**Morning**: Start the workday with a clear desk. 5 minutes.

**Evening**: End the workday by returning everything to its place. 5 minutes.

The desk you sit down to tomorrow morning is set by what you do tonight.

## Zeus Studio Build-Out Notes

See `Studio-Setup/` for the active Todoist tasks on the physical build.

- **Dedicated room, not a shared-use space** — non-negotiable for content creation
- **Cable management**: Plan before furniture is placed, not after
- **Acoustic treatment**: Basic panels on parallel walls make a large audible difference at low cost
- **Lighting**: Window light direction first, then supplemental LED panels before investing in professional lights
- **Two zones**: Desk/chair for deep work vs standing/open area for calls and recordings — separate physical zones reduce context switching
