# Solo Operator Stress Protocol

> Entrepreneur stress has a specific shape: **feast-or-famine**. Empty pipeline = anxiety. Full pipeline = overwhelm. The goal is not to eliminate stress but to have a protocol when it spikes.

---

## The Feast-or-Famine Cycle

Work comes in bursts. Some days the to-do list is nearly empty. Two days later it's unmanageable. This is the default solo-operator experience — not a sign something is broken.

What makes it worse:
- Treating every deadline as equally critical
- Working harder when stressed instead of smarter
- No defined stopping point in the day — work expands to fill anxiety

---

## When Stress Spikes — The STOP Protocol

**Step 1: Stop.** Literally stop working for 5–10 minutes. Not scrolling. Not talking. Just stop.

**Step 2: Triage.** On paper, write every open item. Mark each: *This week / This month / Someday*. Anything in "someday" leaves your brain immediately.

**Step 3: Identify the actual blocker.** 90% of the time, stress comes from one or two items, not everything on the list. Find them. Work on those first.

**Step 4: Set one achievable goal for today.** Not a wishlist — one thing that, if done, makes today a win. Do that first.

---

## Daily Maintenance (Prevents Spikes)

| Practice | Rule |
|----------|------|
| Caffeine | Cap at 2 cups, before noon. Caffeine amplifies anxiety. |
| Movement | Minimum 15 minutes. Breaks the cortisol cycle. |
| Daily cutoff | Work stops at a set time. Non-negotiable. |
| Delegation check | Weekly: what can I hand off, automate, or eliminate? |

---

## The Delegation Threshold

Solo operators hold too much. The rule: if a task costs less than your effective hourly rate and isn't strategic, it should not be done by you.

- Admin, formatting, scheduling → delegate or automate
- Research that isn't judgment-heavy → delegate
- Repetitive customer questions → create a resource, stop answering one-by-one

The goal is protecting your time for work that only you can do.

---

## Deep Recovery (When You're Actually Burned Out)

If the stress has been building for weeks:
- One full day off with no devices — not "I'll just check once"
- Physical reset: move, sunlight, real food
- Return with a written audit of what's eating your energy — find the source, not the symptom
