# Child Stress Management Guide

> *"As parents, it's important we don't criticize our children too often. This can lead to them fearing failure — and eventually not even attempting certain tasks."*

---

## Part 1: Children Who Have Nightmares

Nightmares often have an identifiable trigger. Common causes:

- **Intense media** — Films with violence or dark themes, especially when watched alone and repeatedly. When a child has nightmares after a movie, watch it with them. Laugh at the scary parts. Reassure them: *"Those people are acting — they're fine."* Limit how often they watch it.

- **Illness and missed school** — After being sick and out of school, children worry: *Did I fall behind? Did my best friend find someone new?* Before they return, re-introduce them to missed work. Call the parents of their close friends and set up a playdate for the night before they go back. Remove the unknowns.

**The general rule:** Identify what cycle is causing the nightmare and break it directly. Don't dismiss it — work it out together.

---

## Part 2: Children Under Stress

A child who feels like a failure because they're "not in the top group" is a child who needs perspective — not pressure.

What to say:
- *"We know you're giving it your all. As long as you keep trying your best, we are proud of you."*
- Remind them of the areas where they excel (sport, art, music, personality)
- Show them that trying even when they think they'll fail is the goal

**The shift:** Children should know that making an error is how they learn — and that you'll always be there to explain where things went wrong.

---

## Part 3: Home Environment Rules for Low-Stress Kids

- **Keep homework fun** — take breaks, use humor, don't make it a battlefield
- **Praise often** when they complete something well — it builds self-esteem
- **When they're naughty**: talk to them like an adult, not shout at them
- **Don't over-criticize** — children who are criticized constantly start to fear failure and stop attempting new things
- **Always treat them fairly and with relaxed authority** — not tense judgment

---

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- If **Noah or Liam** is having nightmares, ask what they watched, what they're worried about at school — there's almost always a specific trigger
- If a child misses school, **proactively set up a friend hangout** before they go back — remove the social anxiety before it festers
- **Never shame effort** — only the behavior, and only calmly
- "I'm proud of you for trying" lands differently than "you should have done better"
- **Soluna** especially: at her age, nightmares can spike around big transitions — watch for patterns
