# ADHD Signs & Who to Trust: A Parent's Guide

> *"Don't let your child go through 2 or 3 grades before coming to a diagnosis. That only leads to more stress for everyone."*

---

## When Should You Act?

If something happened yesterday that never happened before — you're probably not dealing with ADHD. If you've seen the same behavioral pattern for **6 months or more**, take it seriously. Get an evaluation.

---

## The Professionals: What Each One Does

| Professional | Role |
|---|---|
| **Pediatrician** | Can diagnose, but mainly quick visits. Will likely refer you elsewhere for full evaluation. |
| **Developmental Pediatrician** | Full physical, social, and mental development analysis. Most thorough initial evaluation for ADHD. Will form a specialist team when needed. |
| **Child Psychiatrist** | Medical doctor, diagnoses and prescribes. Monitors medication effects. Offers little behavioral counseling. |
| **Pediatric Neurologist** | Only involved if tics or syndromes (like Tourette's) are associated. Not standard for ADHD alone. |
| **Clinical Psychologist** | PhD (not MD). Diagnoses and treats the whole child — and often the whole family. Doesn't prescribe but recommends medication to family doctor when needed. |
| **Pediatric Neuropsychologist** | PhD. Evaluates specific brain function strengths and weaknesses. Prepares detailed suggestions for parents and teachers. Does not treat — informs. |
| **Educational Psychologist** | Specialty in learning disabilities. Designs specialized education plans. Most powerful when combined with neuropsychologist's recommendations. |

---

## The Bottom Line on Professionals

None of these are definitively "best" — the right one depends on your child's specific situation and your comfort level with the professional. The most comprehensive route: **Developmental Pediatrician** first → **Clinical Psychologist** if behavioral treatment is needed.

---

## What to Say to Your Child

When the diagnosis comes: tell them they are **special**. Their brain works differently — quick, intelligent, creative. Work on finding areas where those traits are assets, not liabilities. Early diagnosis + early intervention = higher self-esteem and a more productive life.

---

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- If you or a teacher suspects something consistent over 6+ months, **act now** — not later
- Start with a **developmental pediatrician** for the most thorough evaluation
- If medication is recommended, also pursue a **clinical psychologist** for behavioral support — medication alone is not enough
- Your child is **not broken** — they are wired differently, and that wiring has advantages when channeled
- The worst outcome is years of unexplained struggle that damages their self-image before anyone names what's happening
