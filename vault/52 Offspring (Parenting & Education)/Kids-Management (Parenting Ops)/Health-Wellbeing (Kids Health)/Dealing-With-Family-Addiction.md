# Dealing With Addiction in the Family

> *"Whatever is truly in your highest good, is also in the highest good of all. When you take loving care of yourself, you open the door for others to take loving care of themselves."*

---

## Overview

Dealing with addicted family members is always a challenge. Before taking any action, there are several critical questions to explore.

---

## Are You Contributing to the Problem?

People use addictions to avoid painful feelings — anxiety, stress, emptiness, loneliness. Before judging the addicted person, ask: *Is there some way I am contributing to their pain?*

Contributing behaviors include:
- Being judgmental in an attempt to control them
- Caretaking — covering up for them or doing things they should do themselves
- Dismissing their feelings when they try to share pain with you
- Telling yourself you're responsible for them instead of taking care of yourself

---

## Accepting Your Lack of Control

Regardless of anything you're doing, their choice to act out addictively is 100% their choice. You cannot control it. When you don't accept this powerlessness, you stay in situations that are detrimental to you — trying to change someone who has not chosen to change.

---

## Staying Tuned Into Your Own Feelings

Ask yourself:
- Am I focused on the addicted person instead of my own feelings and needs?
- Am I putting myself aside in attempts to help them?
- Am I feeling sad, used, angry, or anxious most of the time?

If yes — you need to start taking care of yourself.

---

## Loving Actions Available

When you accept your lack of control and stop caretaking, you can take real action:

- **Join a 12-Step group** (Al-Anon, etc.) — to move out of enabling and enmeshment
- **Get professional help** to understand your own patterns of control and judgment
- **Contact an intervention professional** — coordinate those who love the addicted person and are willing to set firm boundaries
- **Decide you will not be involved** while the addiction continues — this may mean stepping back from the relationship
- **Accept the person as they are**, completely, and learn to take care of yourself within the situation

---

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- If anyone in your extended family or circle is struggling with addiction, this is the framework — not guilt, not rescue, not judgment
- Your first responsibility is to yourself and to your four children — you cannot pour from an empty cup
- Enabling = doing things for them that they need to do themselves. Even from love.
- Knowing when to step back is not abandonment — it's the most loving thing you can do
- Drug prevention at home ([[Preventing-Drug-Abuse-Your-Role-As-Parent]]) is the best long-term protection for Noah, Liam, Soluna, and Cielo
