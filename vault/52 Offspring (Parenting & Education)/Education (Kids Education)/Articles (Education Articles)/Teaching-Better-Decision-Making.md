# Teaching Better Decision-Making

> *"The further you think into the future, the better decisions you will make in the present to assure that future becomes a reality." — Brian Tracy*

---

## Why It Matters

Ben never listened to anyone. He did things his own way. As a consequence, his life became a series of bad decisions — each one leading to worse consequences. The prison light clicked off. Ben screamed into the dark.

You see this pattern everywhere — in celebrities, government officials, business leaders, and in your own family. Rich or poor, young or old, people get trapped in cycles of bad decision-making. The cause is almost always the same: the belief that *"I know best, so I don't need to listen."*

According to Anthony Robbins, good decision-making is a key attribute to a happy life. Teaching it early changes everything.

---

## The 6-Step Framework

1. **Define the problem or issue clearly** — What exactly is the decision you're facing?
2. **Conduct research** — Gather information before acting.
3. **Discuss with respected people** who have experience in similar situations.
4. **Consider at least two alternatives** — Never act on the only option in front of you.
5. **Select the best decision based on your value system** — Not what's easy, what's right.
6. **Move on and accept the consequences** — Good or bad, own your decision.

---

## The Deeper Principle

Your values are not set by government or church leaders. Your values give you consistency in the way you approach life. By holding to your beliefs, you stay on track toward your dreams.

Making the right decision is difficult. No one will applaud the good ones — but you'll catch heat over the bad ones. The goal is to build a *system*, not rely on gut instinct alone.

---

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- Teach **Noah and Liam** the 6-step framework explicitly — walk them through it with real decisions they face
- The "Ben" story is a powerful cautionary tale for teenagers: independence without guidance leads to darkness
- When your kids say "I know what I'm doing" — ask them: *Did you consider two alternatives? Did you research it? Did you discuss with someone you respect?*
- Model the framework yourself — let them see you use it on real decisions out loud
- The goal isn't obedience — it's teaching them to think clearly and act by their own values
