# Seven Steps to Self-Discovery for Your Children

> *"Whenever you have a high self-concept, you perform well." — Brian Tracy*

---

## The Problem With Image

Too many people — and too many children — are obsessed with what others think of them. They buy cars and homes they can't afford, get into relationships they don't want, join groups that contradict their values. Why? Because they never developed a real self-concept.

Self-concept is a combination of beliefs about how you feel about yourself and your environment. These beliefs start in childhood and continue to develop through adulthood. Your job as a father is to shape those beliefs before the world does.

---

## The Three Layers of Self-Concept

**Self-Ideal** — The combination of attributes you most admire in others (a parent, a mentor, a hero). This sets the course for your child's life. They are always measuring themselves against this model.

**Self-Image** — The way they see and think about themselves. This determines how they perform.

**Self-Esteem** — The single most important element. Defined as: *how much you like yourself.* The more they accept themselves and see themselves as valuable, the higher their energy, motivation, and enthusiasm for life.

---

## 7 Questions to Work Through With Your Children

1. How do I really feel about myself?
2. Do I have a vision of who I want to be?
3. Who am I trying to be like in my life?
4. What image am I showing to my family and friends?
5. How positive is my self-concept right now?
6. Am I in negative surroundings? Can I change them?
7. Am I willing to move toward my vision?

---

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- Go through the 7 questions with **Noah** and **Liam** as a real conversation, not a quiz
- Help each child define their **self-ideal** — who do they most admire? What qualities do they want to grow into?
- Your role is to build their **self-image** around character and values — not grades, appearance, or social status
