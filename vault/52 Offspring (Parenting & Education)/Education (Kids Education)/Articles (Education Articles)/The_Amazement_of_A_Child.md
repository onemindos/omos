# The Amazement of a Child

> *"Never do anything as a matter of routine — do it as if it were the first time. Learn from the child. Never stop being amazed."*

---

## What Children Know That We've Forgotten

Everyone who has met a little child has seen it — the amazement. A child sees a bus and gets all excited. They see a dog and can't sit still. They express their enthusiasm with their whole body and are deeply disappointed when adults can't share it.

We grown-ups have long gotten used to dogs and buses. We filter out all the "irrelevant" details we pass by every day. We have forgotten how thrilled and amazed we once were about these things.

But think about it: a dog is something exceptional. A bus is astonishing. The sparkling eyes of a little child are something totally divine.

---

## Finding Your Way Back

Open your window on a starry night. Most of those stars are so far away you can't be sure they still exist. You're looking at light from millions of years ago. You're watching the past.

Look at your own body. Your blood circulation works a thousand times more cleverly than the most advanced computer — without you ever thinking about it.

Think of the small things in everyday life. The pleasure of kneading dough. Walking in the rain. Making an angel in the snow. These things are not small — they are incredibly great.

---

## The Philosopher's Reminder

*"All true philosophers should keep their eyes open. Even if we have never seen a white crow, we should never stop looking for it."* — Jostein Gaarder

Reality is bigger than any formula we put it in. It won't be boxed. We grown-ups like to calculate, arrange, and categorize things — it gives us a sense of security. But the actual truth won't be found that easily.

Maybe not until we learn to view the world through the amazed eyes of a little child.

---

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- **Cielo and Soluna** are living this amazement right now — watch them; they are showing you the way back
- When you're exhausted or routine-numbed, drop to their level physically and see what they're seeing
- The greatest gift you can give your children is not advice — it's **genuinely sharing their amazement**
- Don't dismiss the excitement over "small things" — a dog, a bus, a puddle — those things ARE extraordinary
- Presence is not just showing up. It's showing up amazed.
