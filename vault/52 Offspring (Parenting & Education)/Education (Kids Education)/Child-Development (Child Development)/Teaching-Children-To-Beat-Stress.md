# Teaching Your Children to Beat Stress: 8 Principles

> *"Resilient children grow up to be adults who have a sense of control, a positive view of change, and an ability to find meaning and value in life."*

---

## Overview

We are sending our children into a future filled with stress and conflict. Parents have the responsibility to model behaviors and teach skills that will enable children to be productive, accepting, healthy — and above all, **resilient**.

---

## The 8 Principles

**1. Self-Understanding and Acceptance**
Self-esteem is a realistic estimate of your own capabilities and worth. People with high self-esteem are productive, responsive, imaginative, and attentive to others. Encourage your children to develop their natural aptitudes and interests. Set them up for success. Empower them to be more responsible.

**2. Adults' Understanding and Acceptance**
Give your children regular, focused, **undivided attention**. This, more than anything else, communicates unconditional love.

**3. Constructive Thinking**
We are what we think. Fill your language with statements that help your children:
- See change as positive
- View adversity as manageable
- Persist until successful
- Become oriented to the needs of others

Teach them to identify positive and negative feelings in themselves and others. Replacing destructive thinking with constructive thinking increases self-esteem and improves coping skills.

**4. Good Decision-Making Strategies**
Making a good decision requires: generating alternatives, predicting consequences, viewing the problem from others' perspectives, and considering how to implement solutions. Children as young as 4-5 can generate alternatives and predict consequences. Model good decision-making. Let children make their own decisions whenever possible.

**5. Stress-Coping Strategies**
It's not too early to teach children physical relaxation techniques — breathing exercises, basic meditation, imagery, muscle relaxation. Help them recognize their own stress triggers. And help your kids **laugh** — read funny stories, watch age-appropriate comedies, laugh at their jokes.

**6. Good Nutrition and Exercise**
Good nutrition optimizes how the mind and body work. A well-functioning mind and healthy body increase self-esteem and resilience. Make aerobic exercise and recreation a family affair. If your children see you exercise, they are more likely to build that habit for life.

**7. A Sense of Purpose and Personal Goals**
Commitment to goals gives meaning to life and a reason for existence. Children should have more than one goal, and their goals should be realistic. Teach them to be flexible about *how* they achieve goals, and help them persist when progress is slow.

**8. Social Skills and Social Supports**
Healthy relationships build self-esteem and protect against stress. Help your children build self-awareness and see situations from another's point of view. Teach them to manage conflict and disagreement positively.
