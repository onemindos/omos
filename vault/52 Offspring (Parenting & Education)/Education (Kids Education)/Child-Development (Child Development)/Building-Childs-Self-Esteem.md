# Building Your Child's Self-Esteem

> *"Self-esteem is an ongoing process. The little things add up, even when they seem unimportant."*

---

## What Self-Esteem Actually Is

Self-esteem is a compilation of how you feel about yourself — your confidence in relationships, your body image, your work life. It's not a fixed trait. It's built daily through small interactions, honest feedback, and the freedom to try and fail.

Every conversation you have with your child is either building or eroding it.

---

## 4 Core Techniques

**1. Be genuine in your praise — not inflated.**
Your child will sense dishonesty. If art isn't their top skill, don't say "that's the best drawing I've ever seen." They'll know it's not — and they won't believe your genuine compliments either.

Instead, make non-judgmental observations: *"You really used your imagination with those flower colors."* This is honest. It doesn't evaluate — it acknowledges.

**2. Discipline the behavior, not the person.**
These are two different things. When a child feels you are angry at *who they are*, it damages their self-esteem at the root.

Use "I" statements:
- ✓ *"I don't like it when you leave your toys all over the floor."*
- ✗ *"You are such a slob."*

The first addresses behavior. The second attacks identity.

**3. Let your child make decisions.**
Children are constantly told what to do, where to go, when to do it. When they get to make choices — even small ones — they develop self-reliance and independent thinking.

Simple choices: what to wear (offer two or three options), what to have for a special lunch, which activity first. These small acts of autonomy accumulate into a child who trusts themselves.

**4. Encourage trying new things.**
Natural talents build confidence in narrow areas. New experiences build resilience everywhere. If a child never tries new things, life's bigger transitions — new school, new city, new career — become overwhelming.

Start small. The willingness to try transfers.

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- When praising **Noah, Liam, Soluna, Cielo** — be specific and honest, not generically positive
- "You're so smart" → builds fragile ego. "You worked really hard on that" → builds resilience
- When correcting behavior, never attack the person. *"I don't like when you do that"* — not *"you always do this"*
- Give each child at least one daily micro-decision — it matters more than you think
- **Soluna and Cielo** especially need real choices right now as they form their identity sense
