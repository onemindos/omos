# 7 Steps to Take the Stress Out of Homework

> *"The struggle with homework can be eased by re-programming your child's attitude and approach — not by pep talks or reprimands."*

---

## The Real Problem

Homework is at best a chore for most children. Unless they are very committed, kids find it difficult — often because they aren't motivated. The battle isn't about homework itself. It's about attitude and resistance. Pep talks and reprimands build resistance. They don't solve it.

Ask yourself:
- Is homework a battle every night?
- Have you tried gentle encouragement? Harsh reprimands? Nothing works?
- Are you fighting *with* your child, or fighting *alongside* them?

The answer lies in re-programming — not enforcing.

---

## The 7-Step Approach

**Step 1 — Stop talking about *doing* homework. Talk about *how* they approach it.**
Guide your child to step back and really explore *how* they study, not just whether they do it.

**Step 2 — Find out what pressure they're under.**
Ask directly:
- Are you worried what your friends think?
- Do you believe you're not clever enough?
- Do you want this struggle to end?

**Step 3 — Discuss their answers openly.**
Never dismiss their feelings — even if you disagree. Their feelings are valid. Dismissing them only reinforces them.

**Step 4 — Acknowledge that homework is not easy.**
Show your child that resisting it makes it a bigger problem — it eats up their free time. When they approach it positively, it gets done faster and the stress disappears.

**Step 5 — Make yourself available as a tutor, not an answer machine.**
Offer help on *how* to approach problems, not the answers themselves. If you don't know something, say so and work it out together. Praise their effort, not just results.

**Step 6 — Reprogram during low-pressure moments.**
Try it on a weekend when there's no rush. Let your child stop and start, discuss problems, work through resistance. Don't announce it as special — treat it as a normal session.

**Step 7 — "Getting it done" is not the main goal of this process.**
There will be many purposeful stops and starts. Let your child express feelings. Expose their limiting beliefs. Encourage them to find their own solution.

## Key Takeaways for Zeus

- Stop the nightly battle — shift from "did you do your homework?" to "how are you approaching it?"
- Ask **Noah** and **Liam** directly what pressure they feel (friends judging? Not feeling smart enough?)
- Be a tutor alongside them — admit when you don't know something, model curiosity, praise effort
- Use the reprogramming approach on weekends first, never on a school night with time pressure
- Getting past the *resistance* IS the goal — the homework will follow once the attitude shifts
