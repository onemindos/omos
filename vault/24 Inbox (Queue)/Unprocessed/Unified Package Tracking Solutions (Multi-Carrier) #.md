Unified Package Tracking Solutions (Multi-Carrier)
Tracking packages across multiple carriers can be made much easier with dedicated tools. Below we
compare personal-use package trackers that support Amazon, USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL and more in one
place. We focus on solutions that allow nicknaming shipments, provide real-time notifications, offer
Amazon order integration, and are accessible on web (for Mac) with mobile apps for alerts. We prioritize
free options and note paid ones that offer significant advantages.


Key Features to Consider
     • Multi-Carrier Support: All top solutions support major U.S. carriers (USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL, Amazon
       Logistics) and often hundreds more worldwide. This ensures both domestic and international
       packages can be tracked from one interface.
     • Incoming & Outgoing Shipments: These tools can track any package by its tracking number,
       whether it’s incoming or a shipment you’ve sent (outgoing). There’s usually no distinction – you
       simply enter the tracking code and the system monitors it.
     • Custom Labels (Nicknames): The ability to assign a nickname or description to each tracking
       number so you can identify packages (e.g. “Mom’s Birthday Gift”). Most apps support adding a memo
       or will automatically use the item name if linked to an order.
     • Amazon Integration: This is crucial for Amazon orders, especially those delivered by Amazon’s own
       logistics. Some apps let you connect your Amazon account so that new orders are auto-imported
       once shipped 1 2 . Others achieve this by scanning your emails or letting you forward Amazon
       shipment emails to a special address.
     • Notifications: Real-time alerts (push notifications or emails) when a package status changes (out for
       delivery, delivered, delays, etc.). Ideally, the app should send immediate push notifications for
       status updates. Many also support email or in-app notifications.
     • Platform Availability: Since you want to use it on the web (Mac) and also have mobile notifications,
       we favor tools with a web app or web interface (or a Mac desktop app) plus mobile apps (iOS and/
       or Android). This way you can manage shipments on a computer and still get push alerts on your
       phone.
     • Automation & Nice-to-Haves: Some tools auto-tag or categorize shipments, and auto-archive
       delivered items (removing them from the active list or moving to a “Delivered” section). Email
       integration (auto-detecting tracking numbers from your inbox) or barcode scanning to add packages
      are other helpful features.

Below is an overview of the top package tracking solutions that meet these criteria, followed by a
comparison table for quick reference.

Top Solutions for Personal Package Tracking

AfterShip Package Tracker (Mobile App)

AfterShip is a popular free app with extensive carrier support and robust features for personal use. It
supports over 700 carriers worldwide including Amazon Logistics, USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL, etc. 3 . You can
add any tracking number and assign a custom name to it (the app lets you “label” shipments 4 ). AfterShip
shines in automation: it can auto-import orders from Amazon, Etsy, Walmart and more by linking
accounts or via its Gmail sync feature 5 . This means when you order something on Amazon, the
shipment can show up in AfterShip automatically. It provides free push notifications for key tracking
events (e.g. out for delivery, delivered) with no ads 6 . Users report that AfterShip tracks the widest range
of services, which is great if you order from niche international sellers 7 .


Platforms: AfterShip is available on iOS and Android (mobile only). There isn’t a dedicated web dashboard
for personal use, but you can track packages on their website one-by-one 8 or use their Chrome
extension 9 . Primary usage is via the mobile app (so on Mac you’d use your browser for one-off tracking
or an emulator if needed).


Price: Free. The AfterShip app does not charge for its tracking or notifications (the company makes money
via commercial tracking services, allowing the consumer app to be free and ad-free 6 ).


Pros: Completely free, enormous carrier support, Amazon auto-import, unlimited push alerts 6 , ability to
name shipments 4 .
Cons: No full web interface for managing all packages (mobile-focused). Amazon integration works via
email/account sync, which may require Gmail access or linking accounts.


ParcelsApp / “Packages” (Web & Mobile)

ParcelsApp (also known as the “Packages” app on iPhone) is a solution that offers a web-based interface
alongside mobile apps. It’s designed for online shoppers to track everything in one place 10 . ParcelsApp
supports hundreds of carriers globally (over 300+ postal and courier services, from USPS and UPS to
international posts) 11 – Amazon is explicitly supported as well 12 . A highlight is its ability to import
shipments directly from Amazon (and other marketplaces like eBay, AliExpress, Temu, etc.), so you
don’t have to manually enter tracking numbers 10 . When you connect an Amazon account or provide order
details, it can pull in the item name and tracking info automatically, effectively labeling the shipment for
you with the product name. You can also manually add tracking numbers and assign names.


ParcelsApp provides a full web application (accessible via any browser on Mac/PC) where you can log in to
manage and view all your packages. It also has mobile apps on iOS and Android that sync with the same
account. Push notifications are supported on mobile for status updates, though this requires a premium
upgrade (the free version will still let you track and refresh statuses, but background push alerts and
unlimited tracking might be limited) 13 . Delivered packages can be marked and are typically kept in
history; the interface is user-friendly for personal tracking.


Platforms: Web interface (on parcelsapp.com) for desktop use, iPhone app (called “Packages”) 14 , and
Android app (“Parcels: Track Online Orders”) 15 – all of which sync your data.

Price: Free with optional Premium. You can use ParcelsApp free with ads and a limit on the number of
active packages. Premium (about ~$0.99/month or ~$4.49/year) removes ads and lifts limits 16 , and also
enables instant push notifications 13 .


Pros: True web-based tracking plus mobile apps (great for use on Mac via browser), direct integration with
Amazon and other stores (auto-importing orders) 10 , supports a very large range of carriers 11 , and
allows custom labels (uses item names or allows editing).
Cons: Push notifications and unlimited tracking require a subscription 13 , although the fee is low. The free
tier has ads and possibly a cap on number of packages you can track at once.


Parcel by Ivan Pavlov (Apple Ecosystem)

Parcel is a highly regarded app for Apple users (macOS and iOS). It’s known for its polish and reliability, and
it supports 300+ carriers worldwide 17 . Parcel’s standout feature is its tight integration with Amazon
for personal use. You can log into your Amazon account within Parcel, and it will automatically add any new
Amazon orders once they ship 1 . This means Amazon deliveries (including those shipped by Amazon’s
own delivery service) appear in Parcel without manual entry. Parcel does not store your Amazon credentials
on servers – the login is stored locally for privacy 18 . In addition, Parcel supports all the usual suspects
(UPS, USPS, FedEx, DHL, etc.), and you can of course manually add any tracking number and give it a
nickname.


Parcel provides real-time push notifications for status changes, but note that on the free tier you can only
track a few shipments (up to 3) and push notifications are a Premium feature 19 . The premium
subscription is very affordable – historically around $3/year, recently increased to about $4.99/year 20 .
With Premium you get unlimited package tracking and notifications. Parcel also offers nice automation like
a Delivered items archive and the ability to schedule a daily summary. Many former users of the
“Deliveries” app have migrated to Parcel because it remains up-to-date with carrier changes 21 .


Platforms: macOS app and iOS app (with iCloud sync or their own sync service). There is no dedicated web
browser interface; however, Mac users can install the native Mac app (available on the Mac App Store 22 ).
There’s also an Apple Watch companion for notifications.


Price: Freemium. The app is free to download, with a limit of 3 active deliveries and no push alerts on free
tier. Premium costs ~$5/year for unlimited tracking and push notifications 19 20 .


Pros: Excellent Amazon integration (auto-adds shipped orders) 1 , very user-friendly on Apple devices,
reliable and fast updates, supports a wide array of carriers 17 , allows naming shipments. The developer is
active in updating for new carriers and changes 23 .
Cons: Not available on Windows/Android/Web (Apple only). The free version is very limited – to get real
utility you’ll need the subscription (though it’s low cost).


OneTracker

OneTracker is a newer multi-carrier tracking app that emphasizes privacy and automation. It supports
major carriers including USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL Express, China Post/EMS, Canada Post, and more 24 .
While its carrier list is a bit smaller than giants like AfterShip, it covers all common ones for U.S. users and is
continuously adding more. OneTracker allows you to add packages manually (with a barcode scanner and

automatic clipboard detection to grab tracking numbers) 25 and lets you assign each a memo/nickname. It
also provides each user a unique email address you can forward shipment emails to – when you forward
your order confirmation or shipping email, it will auto-add the package to your list 25 . This can handle
Amazon by forwarding Amazon’s shipping notification email, for example.


A key point about OneTracker is it offers free, unlimited push notifications for tracking updates 26 . Users
have praised that it delivers very fast updates compared to other apps 27 . All features of the app are free;
the only paid aspect is an optional one-time premium upgrade (~$4.99) to unlock its web interface 28 29 .
Yes, OneTracker is one of the few that recently introduced a web browser interface (so you can manage
packages on desktop) – but they’ve made this a premium feature to support development. If you purchase
the upgrade on mobile and link it to your account, you can then use OneTracker in any web browser to view
and manage your shipments 27 . Syncing across devices is supported (it requires signing up for an account,
which is used for sync and the email-forward feature).


Platforms: iOS app (iPhone/iPad) and Android app (Android version is in public beta but available) 30 . Web
interface is available in beta for premium users 28 . No dedicated desktop app, but with web access and
mobile apps, it covers all bases if you opt for the premium web feature.


Price: Free for core functionality (unlimited tracking, no ads, all notifications free). $4.99 one-time if you
want web access (optional) 27 .


Pros: Completely free mobile app with unlimited push alerts 26 , no recurring fees. Email forwarding
automation to add shipments easily 25 . Strong focus on user privacy (doesn’t require full email access,
since you can just forward specific emails). Offers a web tracking option for a modest one-time cost 27 .
Open development with frequent improvements, and known for fast status updates.
Cons: Supports slightly fewer carriers than some competitors (covers all major U.S. carriers, but very
obscure or niche international couriers might not be in yet). The web interface is still in early development
and requires a premium purchase 28 . No native Mac app (but web fills this gap if needed).


Route - Package Tracker

Route is a free app oriented towards online shoppers that consolidates tracking and also provides package
insurance/claim features (useful if something gets lost). It connects with 600+ carriers worldwide including
all the major ones (Amazon, UPS, USPS, FedEx, DHL, etc.) 31 . Route can function by scanning your email for
order confirmations or by letting you manually add tracking numbers. Notably, it offers an Amazon
integration: you can either connect your Amazon account to automatically import all orders, or you can
add Amazon orders manually by inputting the Amazon order tracking link 2 . This makes tracking Amazon
purchases straightforward. Route sends real-time push notifications for status updates and has a visually
appealing interface (it often shows a map with the package route).


While Route is free, it is primarily a mobile app (iOS/Android) without a general web management
interface for users. (Route’s focus has been on mobile and on integration with merchants: many e-
commerce stores offer “Track with Route” links.) You can track unlimited packages and get notifications at
no cost. Some extra features: Route allows you to file claims for lost/damaged packages with one click if
they were insured through Route, and it automatically organizes your orders by merchant. Privacy-
conscious users should note that enabling email scanning means giving the app access to your inbox

(Route emphasizes privacy in their marketing, claiming “unrivaled privacy” 32 , but it’s a consideration if you
use the email sync method).


Platforms: iOS app and Android app 33 . No web app for tracking multiple items (they do have a web view
for individual tracking links, but not a dashboard for all your packages).


Price: Free. The app is completely free to use with no subscription. (Route’s business model is offering
package insurance through partner merchants, so the tracking app is offered free to users.)


Pros: Free with no limits, broad carrier support 31 , can auto-import Amazon orders by linking account 2 ,
and handles other online orders well. Good notification support and a modern interface. Useful if you
frequently buy from stores that integrate with Route (it will automatically pull those in).
Cons: No centralized web interface for desktop use (mobile-only tracking management). Some users find the
email-scanning approach less helpful if they prefer not to grant inbox access 34 . Lacks some manual
organizational features like custom tags (it tends to use the order info as labels). Geared more toward
tracking orders from retailers (shopping focus) than manually entered shipments, though it can do both.


(Honorable Mention) Others

     • Deliveries (Junecloud) – A once-popular app for iOS/Mac that could track many services (and used
       to integrate Amazon). However, due to carriers closing their APIs, this app has lost significant
       functionality. For example, Amazon no longer works reliably with Deliveries 35 . The developer has
       acknowledged these issues and the app is no longer recommended for robust tracking. Many users
       have migrated to the above options 21 .
     • 17TRACK – A free website and app known for tracking packages worldwide, especially from
       international postal carriers. It supports an extremely large number of carriers (over 2400+ carriers via
       their platform) 36 . It allows labeling shipments and provides notifications. However, it does not
       integrate with Amazon accounts (you must input tracking numbers manually, which is problematic
       for Amazon Logistics shipments that lack external tracking). 17TRACK is great for manual tracking,
       but it misses the Amazon auto-sync feature that the above tools offer.
     • Shop (Shopify) – A free app that automatically tracks orders by connecting to your email (similar to
       Route). It can detect Amazon and other orders from your inbox. It’s user-friendly but has no web
       interface and is focused on showing you shopping info and product recommendations, beyond just
       tracking. If your priority is purely package tracking, the dedicated apps above are more focused.

Conclusions and Recommendations
For a completely free solution, AfterShip and Route stand out. AfterShip offers broader carrier coverage
and a proven Amazon sync (via Gmail or direct) with an easy interface – a great choice if you are
comfortable using the mobile app primarily 7 . Route is also free and user-friendly, especially if you like a
visually rich app, though it’s more limited to mobile use.


If having a web interface on Mac is a priority, ParcelsApp is excellent: you can do everything from a
browser and still get mobile notifications by using their app. The free tier is quite usable, and the premium
is low-cost to unlock notifications and unlimited tracking 13 . It’s designed for personal shoppers and
supports Amazon and other store integrations heavily 10 .


For Apple users who don’t mind a small subscription, Parcel is extremely polished. At ~$5/year, it delivers a
seamless experience on Mac and iPhone with reliable syncing and Amazon integration that “just works” 1
 42 . Given the recent issues with other apps, Parcel has become a go-to for many in the Apple ecosystem

 21 .




                                                     8
If you prefer a no-subscription approach and like tinkering, OneTracker is a hidden gem – free unlimited
tracking and notifications, plus a one-time $5 if you want web access 27 . It covers all the main carriers and
has a clever email forwarding system for automation 25 . This app is newer but has earned praise for its
fast updates and reliability 27 .


In summary, there are several great personal package trackers available. Those listed above all meet the
core needs (multi-carrier, nicknames, notifications, Amazon support) to varying degrees. Consider which
factors matter most – e.g. web vs mobile, free vs paid, and how you want to import Amazon orders –
and choose the tool that best fits your workflow. With any of these, you’ll be able to conveniently track your
incoming and outgoing shipments in one unified platform.
