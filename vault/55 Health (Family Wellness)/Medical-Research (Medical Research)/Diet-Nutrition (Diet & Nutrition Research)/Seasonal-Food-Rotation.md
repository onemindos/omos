# Seasonal Food Rotation

> *Eat with the seasons. It's cheaper, fresher, and what the body expects.*

---

## Why This Matters

Seasonal eating:
- Reduces cost — in-season produce is significantly cheaper
- Maximizes nutrition — produce at peak season has higher nutrient density
- Aligns with the homestead vision — knowing what grows when is foundational
- Builds family food literacy — kids learn what belongs to what season

---

## Seasonal Guide (Northeast / NYC-Based)

*When the family transitions to a homestead, update this with regional-specific growing seasons.*

### Spring (March–May)

**What's in season:**
- Asparagus, artichokes, peas, spinach, arugula, radishes, spring onions, leeks
- Strawberries (peak May)
- Herbs: parsley, mint, cilantro, chives

**Histamine note**: Spinach is high-histamine — limit. Strawberries are histamine releasers — use cautiously.

**Family emphasis**: Lighter eating coming out of winter. Increase greens. Begin outdoor meals and Sunday meals outside.

---

### Summer (June–August)

**What's in season:**
- Zucchini, cucumber, corn, bell peppers, eggplant, green beans, summer squash
- Blueberries, watermelon, peaches, cherries, figs, mangoes (tropical available)
- Fresh herbs everywhere

**Histamine notes**: Eggplant is high-histamine — avoid. Cherries and some berries can be liberators — watch reactions. Cucumbers, zucchini, watermelon are safe.

**Family emphasis**: Abundance season. More fresh, less cooked. Hydration focus. Maximum outdoor family meals.

---

### Fall (September–November)

**What's in season:**
- Sweet potatoes (batata), butternut squash, acorn squash, pumpkin, apples, pears, beets, carrots, parsnips, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, broccoli
- Root vegetables peak season

**Histamine note**: Butternut squash, sweet potato, apple, pear, carrots — all safe. Pumpkin — safe.

**Family emphasis**: Root vegetable season — leans into Dominican staples naturally. Warming soups, stews, sancocho season. Prep body for winter.

---

### Winter (December–February)

**What's in season:**
- Winter squash, sweet potatoes, citrus (use cautiously), kale, collard greens, Brussels sprouts, turnips, parsnips, leeks
- Root vegetables remain primary

**Histamine note**: Limit citrus. Kale and collards are safe. Root vegetables remain the base.

**Family emphasis**: Heavier, warming foods. Bone broth season. Focus on immune support through nutrition — vitamin D, zinc, omega-3 emphasis.

---

## Budget Buying Strategy

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| Buy in-season produce at peak | Costs 40–60% less than off-season |
| Shop at local markets when possible | Fresher, less traveled, often cheaper |
| Buy in bulk when something is cheap and freezable | Plantains, root vegetables, herbs (freeze fresh herbs in oil) |
| Frozen beats out-of-season fresh | Frozen vegetables are picked at peak and flash frozen — often more nutrient-dense than "fresh" shipped from far away |
| Grow what you use most | Even in NYC — windowsill herbs (recao, cilantro, parsley) cut cost and ensure freshness |

## Homestead Vision — What This Family Will Grow

When the Delacruz family transitions to a homestead, food self-sufficiency starts here:

| Crop | Why | Grows When |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Plantains | Heritage staple, calorie-dense | Tropical climate (Florida+) |
| Yuca / cassava | Heritage staple, calorie-dense, drought-tolerant | Warm climates |
| Sweet potato / batata | Nutrient-dense, stores well | Summer–Fall |
| Beans (various) | Protein, stores dry for months | Summer |
| Zucchini / squash | Prolific producers | Summer |
| Herbs (recao, cilantro, oregano, mint) | Used daily, easy to grow | Year-round with care |
| Garlic + onion | Used in every meal | Fall planted, Spring harvest |
| Fruit trees (mango, avocado, citrus) | Long-term investment | Climate-dependent |
| Chickens (eggs) | Daily fresh protein | Year-round |
