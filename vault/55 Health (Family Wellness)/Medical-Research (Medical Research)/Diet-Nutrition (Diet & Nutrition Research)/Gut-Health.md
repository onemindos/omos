# Gut Health — Research & Framework

> *The gut is not just digestion. It's immunity, mood, hormones, and histamine regulation. Fix the gut first.*

## Why This Is the Root Document

The family's low-histamine protocol exists because of histamine sensitivity. But **histamine sensitivity is a symptom — not the root cause**. The root is almost always gut-related. Understanding this changes what you do about it.

## The Histamine–Gut Connection

### How Histamine Is Regulated

The body manages histamine through two primary mechanisms:

1. **DAO enzyme (Diamine Oxidase)** — produced in the gut lining (intestinal epithelium). Breaks down dietary histamine *before* it enters the bloodstream. If the gut lining is damaged or inflamed, DAO production drops.

2. **HMNT enzyme (Histamine N-Methyltransferase)** — works intracellularly. Secondary backup to DAO.

**Conclusion**: A damaged gut = less DAO = more histamine getting through = more reactions to "normal" foods. The low-histamine diet reduces the load, but healing the gut *restores capacity*.

## Root Causes That Reduce DAO / Gut Function

| Cause | What It Does | Relevance |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| Leaky gut (intestinal permeability) | Damaged tight junctions allow particles + histamine through | Major driver |
| Dysbiosis (imbalanced microbiome) | Histamine-producing bacteria overgrow, DAO-producing bacteria decline | Major driver |
| SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth) | Bacteria in wrong location ferment food, produce histamine | Common and often missed |
| Chronic stress | Compromises gut lining via cortisol effect on tight junctions | Relevant for Zeus |
| NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin) | Damage intestinal lining with regular use | Minimize or avoid regular use |
| Alcohol | Both a histamine releaser and gut-lining irritant | Avoided per household protocol |
| Processed / ultra-processed food | Destroys microbiome diversity | Avoided per nutrition framework |
| Antibiotic overuse | Wipes out beneficial bacteria | Use only when genuinely necessary |

## Gut Healing Protocol

### Priority Order

1. **Remove** — eliminate foods and substances that damage the gut (per low-histamine protocol + above)
2. **Replace** — digestive enzymes (including DAO), stomach acid support if needed
3. **Reinoculate** — histamine-safe probiotic strains to rebuild microbiome
4. **Repair** — gut lining nutrients (L-glutamine, collagen, bone broth, zinc carnosine)
5. **Rebalance** — stress, sleep, movement — systemic support

### Probiotic Strains — Safe vs. Avoid

Not all probiotics are equal for histamine-sensitive individuals. Some strains produce histamine; others degrade it.

**DAO-supporting / safe strains:**
- *Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG*
- *Bifidobacterium longum*
- *Bifidobacterium infantis*
- *Lactobacillus plantarum*
- *Bifidobacterium breve*

**Avoid (histamine-producing strains):**
- *Lactobacillus casei*
- *Lactobacillus bulgaricus*
- *Lactobacillus delbrueckii*
- *Streptococcus thermophilus*

> Most commercial yogurts and kefirs use the avoid strains. This is why fermented dairy is off the protocol even though "fermented food is healthy" — it's healthy for most people, not for histamine-sensitive guts.

## SIBO — What to Know

SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth) is common and frequently undiagnosed. Symptoms overlap heavily with general gut issues and histamine sensitivity.

**Signs that SIBO may be present:**
- Bloating immediately after eating (within 30–90 min)
- Excessive gas, especially after starchy/fibrous foods
- Brain fog after meals
- Histamine reactions that don't improve with diet alone
- Alternating constipation and diarrhea

**Testing**: Breath test (lactulose or glucose) — ask primary care or gastroenterologist.

**Treatment**: Typically herbal antimicrobials (oregano oil, berberine) or prescription antibiotics (rifaximin) + gut repair after. Research in progress.
