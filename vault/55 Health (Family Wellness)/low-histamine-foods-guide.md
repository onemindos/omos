# Low Histamine Foods Guide

## ✅ LOW HISTAMINE FOODS (Generally Safe)

### Proteins
- Fresh chicken, turkey (cooked same day)
- Fresh eggs (not aged)
- Fresh lamb, beef (cooked immediately)

### Grains
- Rice (white, brown)
- Oats
- Quinoa
- Pasta
- Rice crackers, rice cakes

### Vegetables
- Carrots, sweet potatoes
- Broccoli, cauliflower
- Zucchini, cucumber
- Leafy greens (lettuce, kale)
- Bell peppers
- Peas, green beans

### Fruits
- Apples, pears
- Blueberries, blackberries
- Mangoes
- Melons (watermelon, cantaloupe)
- Peaches, apricots

### Dairy
- Fresh milk
- Cream cheese
- Butter
- Fresh ricotta, cottage cheese

### Other
- Olive oil, coconut oil
- Maple syrup, honey (if over 1 year)
- Rice milk, oat milk

---

## ⛔️ HIGH HISTAMINE FOODS (Avoid / Limit)

### Proteins
- Aged/cured meats (salami, bacon, ham)
- Canned fish (tuna)
- Smoked fish
- Shellfish
- Leftover cooked meat (older than 24 hours)

### Dairy
- Aged cheeses (cheddar, parmesan)
- Yogurt
- Sour cream

### Fermented Foods
- Sauerkraut
- Pickles
- Soy sauce
- Kefir, kombucha

### Fruits
- Strawberries (high histamine liberator)
- Citrus fruits (oranges, lemons)
- Bananas (especially overripe)
- Pineapple
- Kiwi

### Vegetables
- Tomatoes (and ketchup, sauce)
- Spinach (when cooked)
- Eggplant
- Avocado

### Other
- Chocolate
- Nuts (especially walnuts, cashews)
- Vinegar (except white distilled)
- Artificial colors/preservatives

---

## 💡 Quick Tips for Parents

1. **Freshness matters** — Cook proteins fresh and eat within a few hours
2. **Avoid leftovers** — Histamine increases in stored food
3. **Watch for patterns** — Keep a food diary to identify triggers
4. **Start simple** — Rice, chicken, steamed veggies, apples are safest
5. **Read labels** — Avoid artificial colors (Red 40, Yellow 5) and preservatives
