# Water & Air Quality

> *You can optimize food all day. Unfiltered water and poor air undo it.*

---

## Water

### NYC Tap Water — What's In It

NYC tap water is technically safe by EPA standards but contains:

| Contaminant | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Chlorine / chloramine | Added as disinfectant — disrupts gut microbiome with regular unfiltered consumption |
| Fluoride | Added by city — debated; reduces DAO enzyme activity according to some research |
| Lead | From aging building pipes (NYC brownstones and older apartments are a real risk) |
| Pharmaceuticals | Trace amounts pass through municipal treatment |
| Disinfection byproducts (DBPs) | Form when chlorine reacts with organic matter in water |

> **Action**: Filter drinking and cooking water. Not panic — just a practical upgrade.

### Filter Options

| Filter | What It Removes | Cost | Notes |
|--------|---------------|------|-------|
| Berkey (gravity filter) | Chlorine, fluoride (with add-on), heavy metals, DBPs, bacteria | $300–$400 | No electricity needed, countertop, 6,000 gallon filter life |
| Reverse osmosis (under-sink) | Most thorough — removes ~99% of contaminants including fluoride | $150–$400 installed | Wastes some water, may need remineralization filter added |
| ZeroWater pitcher | Chlorine, heavy metals, some fluoride | $30–$50 | Budget option, filters need frequent replacing |
| Brita / PUR | Chlorine, some metals | $20–$40 | Does NOT remove fluoride — not recommended as primary filter |

### Water for Kids

- All water for mixing baby formula or toddler food: filtered
- Bottles, sippy cups: filtered water only
- Cooking rice, pasta, soups: filtered water when possible

## Air Quality

### What to Filter For

| Pollutant | Source | Health Impact |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| Particulate matter (PM2.5) | Traffic, cooking smoke, NYC ambient | Respiratory, cardiovascular |
| Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) | Cleaning products, new furniture, off-gassing | Headaches, cognitive, liver |
| Mold spores | Moisture, bathroom, basement | Respiratory, immune, histamine trigger |
| Allergens (dust, dander) | Carpets, bedding, pets | Histamine trigger |
| Carbon monoxide | Gas stove, faulty appliances | Critical — need CO detector |

### Air Purifier Recommendations

| Purifier | Filter Type | Best For | Cost |
|---------|------------|---------|------|
| Coway Airmega 400 | True HEPA + activated carbon | Large rooms, main living area | $250–$300 |
| Levoit Core 300 | True HEPA + activated carbon | Bedrooms, smaller rooms | $80–$100 |
| Winix 5500-2 | True HEPA + carbon + PlasmaWave | Allergens, smoke | $150–$200 |

> **Key**: True HEPA required (not "HEPA-style"). Activated carbon layer handles VOCs.

**Priority rooms**: Bedrooms first (sleep is 7–8 hrs of exposure), then main living area.

### CO + Smoke Detectors

- Carbon monoxide detector: **required** if there is a gas stove (present in most NYC apartments)
- Test both CO and smoke detectors monthly
- Replace CO detectors every 5–7 years

### Mold Prevention

- Bathroom: ventilate aggressively after showers — leave fan on 15–20 min after
- Check under sinks, around windows, and in corners seasonally
- If visible mold appears: address source (moisture) first, then clean with hydrogen peroxide — NOT bleach (bleach is a lung irritant and VOC source)
- Dehumidifier in summer if humidity regularly above 60% indoors

## Homestead Upgrade Path

| Current (NYC apartment) | Future (Homestead) |
|------------------------|-------------------|
| Filter tap water | Well water or spring water with filtration at source |
| HEPA air purifier | Open windows daily, wood stove (clean burn), outdoor air |
| Limited control over building VOCs | Full control — use clean materials in construction |
| CO detector (gas stove) | Upgrade to induction cooking — eliminates gas combustion risk |
