# Kids Remedies

> *Kids get sick. This is the plan before the panic call.*

---

## Golden Rules for Kids

1. **Honey: never under 12 months** — botulism risk in infants
2. **No aspirin under 18** — Reye's syndrome risk
3. **No OTC cold/cough meds under 4 years** — not safe, not effective
4. **Dose by weight, not age** — for any OTC med, always use weight-based dosing
5. **When in doubt, call the pediatrician** — this file is first-line support, not a replacement

## Common Childhood Ailments

### Ear Pain

- Warm compress on the ear (not hot) — relief only, not treatment
- Garlic oil drops — 2–3 drops warmed garlic-infused oil in ear canal (for older kids, not infants)
- **See doctor if**: fever present, pain severe, discharge from ear — ear infections often need treatment

### Teething

- Chilled (not frozen) teething ring
- Gentle gum massage with clean finger
- Chamomile tea (cooled) — small amount for soothing
- Avoid: teething tablets with belladonna, numbing gels with benzocaine

### Diaper Rash

- Change frequently, keep dry
- Air time — diaper off, on a clean surface
- Coconut oil — antifungal, soothing
- Cornstarch (plain, not talc powder)
- If rash is bright red with satellite spots — likely yeast, needs antifungal cream

### Croup (Barking Cough)

- **Cool night air** — take child outside for 10–15 min (bundled), cool air reduces airway swelling
- **Steam bathroom** — run hot shower, sit in steam for 10 min
- Keep child calm — crying worsens airway tightening
- **Escalate if**: stridor (high-pitched inhale sound) at rest, lips turning blue, struggling to breathe → ER

### Pinkeye (Conjunctivitis)

- Warm compress on closed eye, 5–10 min, several times daily
- Clean discharge with warm wet cotton ball, wipe outer to inner corner
- **Bacterial pinkeye** (yellow/green discharge, crusty) → doctor for antibiotic drops
- **Viral pinkeye** (clear/watery) → resolves on its own, keep clean

### Skin — Minor Cuts, Scrapes, Burns

| Situation | First Response |
|-----------|--------------|
| Minor cut/scrape | Clean with clean water, apply raw honey or coconut oil, cover |
| Minor burn (small, superficial) | Cool running water 10–20 min, aloe vera, do NOT use ice or butter |
| Splinter | Sterilize needle with flame, remove, honey or coconut oil on area |
| Bug bite/sting | Baking soda paste, cool compress, calendula cream for itch |

## When to Always Go to the Doctor (Kids)

- Fever in infant under 3 months — any fever, period
- Difficulty breathing or breathing fast
- Won't drink fluids (dehydration risk)
- Rash that spreads rapidly or looks like bruising
- Seizure
- Inconsolable crying lasting more than 3 hours
- Stiff neck with fever (meningitis concern)
- Child is unusually lethargic or unresponsive
