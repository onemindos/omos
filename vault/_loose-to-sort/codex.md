## The Thesis

Every productivity framework before this was designed for humans working alone — focused on managing tasks and organizing information:
- **GTD** (2001) — Clear your head into lists
- **PARA** (2017) — Organize files into 4 folders
- **CODE** (2022) — Capture → Organize → Distill → Express

They all break in the AI era because:
1. They don't give AI **context** about who you are
2. They don't give AI **structure** to act on your behalf
3. They don't give AI **permission** to execute within guardrails
4. They don't survive **tool changes** — when you switch apps, you start over

**The Codex solves all four.**

A Codex is not an app. It's not a database. It's a **structured source of truth in plain markdown** that any intelligence — human, AI, or future — can read, reason about, and act on. Like the Bible for a religion, the Codex is the canonical text for your life. Systems die. Markdown lives forever.

## The CODEX Method — Deep Dive

### C — CAPTURE

*"Get everything out of your head and into the system."*

**The Rule:** If it enters your awareness, it enters the Codex. Every thought, voice memo, screenshot, email, sensor reading, AI insight — everything gets captured into the Inbox.

**Sources:**
- Voice (speech-to-text, ambient AI)
- Text (notes, messages, emails)
- AI (agent observations, recommendations)
- Sensors (health data, home sensors, cameras)
- Media (screenshots, photos, documents)
- External (APIs, feeds, notifications)

**AI Role:** AI listens, transcribes, pre-classifies. You don't have to think about where it goes yet. You just capture.

**Output:** Raw items in the Inbox queue.

**What makes this different from GTD's capture:**
GTD captures tasks. CODEX captures *everything* — knowledge, tasks, ideas, metrics, observations, AI insights. The Inbox is universal.

---

### O — ORGANIZE

*"Structure inputs into the four life domains."*

**The Rule:** Every item belongs to one of three domains. If it doesn't improve you, strengthen your foundation, or fuel your empire — it goes to the brain (SO) for processing, or it gets archived.

**The Four Domains:**

| Domain | Code | Question Filter | Scope |
|--------|------|-----------------|-------|
| **Holistic Performance** | HP | *"Does this improve ME?"* | Health, mind, body, skills, finance, identity |
| **Legacy Evolution** | LE | *"Does this protect US?"* | Family, home, heritage, estate, community |
| **Generational Empire** | GE | *"Does this create VALUE?"* | Business, revenue, ventures, partnerships |
| **Sovereign Operations** | SO | *"How do we coordinate?"* | AI agents, tech, automation, infrastructure |

**The 8 Entity Types** (what you organize INTO):

| Entity | What It Is | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Goal** | A desired future state with a deadline | "Credit score 750+ by June" |
| **Project** | A time-bound effort with a clear outcome | "Credit Repair Sprint" |
| **Task** | The smallest unit of work | "Send dispute letter to Experian" |
| **Workflow** | A reusable sequence of steps | "Weekly Financial Review" |
| **Skill** | A capability an AI agent can perform | "Morning Briefing Generator" |
| **Tool** | A system-specific integration | "Todoist API", "Home Assistant" |
| **Knowledge** | An atomic unit of information | "How Credit Disputes Work" |
| **Metric** | A measurable signal of progress | "Credit Score (lag), Disputes Sent (lead)" |

**AI Role:** AI suggests classification, domain routing, entity type. You confirm or override.

**What makes this different from PARA:**
PARA has 4 buckets (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives). CODEX has 4 *life domains* × 8 *entity types* = a multidimensional structure that models your actual life, not just your file system. PARA organizes files. CODEX organizes existence.

### D — DIRECT

*"Set goals, priorities, and rules for who does what."*

**The Rule:** Every project has a goal. Every goal has metrics. Every agent has guardrails. Nothing moves forward without direction.

**Components:**
- **Goals** — What you're working toward (with deadlines and metrics)
- **Priorities** — What matters most right now (P1-P4)
- **Routing Rules** — Which agent handles which domain
- **Autonomy Levels** — How much freedom each agent has
- **Guardrails** — Hard and soft rules that constrain AI behavior

**The Autonomy Spectrum:**

| Level | Trust | AI Can... | Examples |
|-------|-------|-----------|----------|
| **L3** | Full | Act without asking | Search, read, organize, log |
| **L2** | High | Act then notify | Create tasks, update metrics, send routine messages |
| **L1** | Medium | Ask first | Financial decisions, external comms, schedule changes |
| **L0** | None | Human only | Legal, health emergencies, deletions, family decisions |

**AI Role:** AI proposes priorities based on goals and current metrics. AI routes requests to the right agent. AI flags conflicts. You set the strategy; AI handles the tactics.

**What makes this different from GTD's "Reflect":**
GTD reflection is passive — you look at lists. CODEX direction is active — you're programming your AI team's behavior, setting guardrails, and delegating authority. You're not reviewing a to-do list; you're commanding a workforce.

---

### E — EXECUTE

*"Deploy skills, agents, and workflows to do the actual work."*

**The Rule:** Execution is shared between humans and AI agents. Each has strengths. The Codex defines who does what.

**Components:**
- **Tasks** — Atomic units of work assigned to human or AI
- **Skills** — Portable AI capabilities (the universal skill format)
- **Workflows** — Multi-step automations
- **Tools** — System-specific integrations (API bindings)
- **Agents** — AI actors with roles, scopes, and permissions

**The Skills vs Tools Distinction (Critical for Portability):**

| | Skills | Tools |
|---|--------|-------|
| **Format** | Markdown (`.skill.md`) | Code (JS, Python, etc.) |
| **Portable?** | Yes — any AI can read markdown | No — system-specific |
| **Contains** | What to do, when, why, how | How to call a specific API |
| **Example** | "Morning Briefing" skill file | OpenClaw `proactive-agent` JS, Letta Python function |
| **Lifespan** | Permanent — lives in the Codex | Temporary — changes with each platform |
| **Transferable** | Move between any AI system | Must be rewritten per platform |

**This is the key insight:** Skills are the **recipes**. Tools are the **kitchen appliances**. When you switch kitchens (AI systems), you bring the recipes. The recipes tell the new kitchen what to cook.

**AI Role:** AI executes tasks within its autonomy level. AI follows skill instructions. AI uses tools to interact with external systems. AI reports progress. You handle HITL approvals and high-stakes decisions.

**What makes this different from everything else:**
No other framework has an execution layer. GTD says "do it." PARA says "find it." CODE says "express it." CODEX says "**deploy AI agents to do it**, and here's exactly what they're authorized to do, how to do it, and what success looks like."

### × — MULTIPLY

*"Measure results, learn from them, evolve the system."*

**The Rule:** What gets measured gets managed. Every goal has lead and lag metrics. Every week has a score. The system improves through feedback loops.

**Components:**
- **Metrics** — Lead (actions you control) + Lag (outcomes you measure)
- **Scorecards** — Weekly path scores (PI, LI, WI)
- **Reviews** — Daily, weekly, quarterly, annual cadences
- **Analytics** — Trend analysis, pattern detection
- **Evolution** — Update skills, workflows, and goals based on data

**The Intelligence Scores:**

| Score | Path | What It Measures |
|-------|------|------------------|
| **PI** (Performance Intelligence) | HP | Personal optimization |
| **LI** (Legacy Intelligence) | LE | Family protection & heritage |
| **WI** (Wealth Intelligence) | GE | Value creation & business |
| **Unified Score** | All | (PI + LI + WI) / 3 |

**The Review Cadence:**

| Level | Frequency | Duration | Focus |
|-------|-----------|----------|-------|
| Daily | Every morning | 15 min | Top 3, energy, calendar |
| Weekly | Sunday evening | 1 hour | Scorecard, priorities, plan |
| Quarterly | Every 12 weeks | Half day | OKRs, big rocks, roadmap |
| Annual | December | 1-2 days | Vision, thesis, non-negotiables |

**AI Role:** AI tracks all metrics automatically. AI surfaces anomalies and trends. AI proposes adjustments. AI generates review summaries. You reflect, decide, and evolve.

**What makes this different:**
No framework has a built-in measurement layer. PARA doesn't measure. GTD doesn't score. CODE doesn't track. CODEX treats your life like a system with dashboards, KPIs, and continuous improvement — powered by AI that never forgets.

## The Universal Skill Format

Skills are the **most important portable component**. They're what make your AI capable, and they transfer between any system.

### Skill File Spec (`.skill.md`)

```markdown
---
id: skill-morning-brief
name: Morning Briefing
version: 1.0
domain: SO
trigger: daily 6:00 AM | on-demand
priority: P1
autonomy: L3
requires:
  - calendar_access
  - task_list_access
  - weather_api
  - health_data (optional)
outputs:
  - formatted_briefing
  - priority_task_list
tags: [daily, routine, briefing]
---

# Skill: Morning Briefing

## Purpose
Generate a comprehensive morning briefing covering today's priorities,
calendar events, weather, and health status across all life domains.

## When to Trigger
- Automatically at 6:00 AM daily
- On-demand when user asks "What's my day look like?"
- After timezone changes or travel

## Context Required
- Today's date, day of week, location
- Calendar events for today and tomorrow
- Open tasks by priority across all domains
- Weather forecast for current location
- Sleep/recovery data (if available)
- Any pending HITL approvals

## Steps
1. Fetch today's calendar events
2. Fetch top 3 priority tasks per domain (HP, LE, GE)
3. Get weather for current location
4. Get sleep/recovery score (if health integration active)
5. Check for pending approvals or blockers
6. Compile into briefing format
7. Deliver via preferred channel (voice, chat, notification)

## Output Format
```
Good morning, [Name]. It's [Day], [Date].

🌤 Weather: [Condition], [Temp] — [Note]

📋 Today's Top 3:
1. [Task] (Domain) — [Due/Context]
2. [Task] (Domain) — [Due/Context]
3. [Task] (Domain) — [Due/Context]

📅 Calendar:
- [Time] [Event]
- [Time] [Event]

💪 Health: [Sleep Score] sleep, [Recovery] recovery
[Adjustment note if needed]

⚡ Pending: [X approvals, Y blockers]
```

## Adaptation Notes
This skill is AI-system agnostic. Implementation varies by platform:
- **OpenClaw**: Proactive agent skill, JavaScript, delivers via channels
- **Letta**: Scheduled tool function, Python, writes to memory block
- **Claude Code**: Read on session start, generate briefing
- **ChatGPT**: Custom GPT instruction, user triggers manually
- **Any LLM**: Read this file → execute steps → return output
```

### Why This Format Works Everywhere

1. **It's markdown** — every AI reads markdown natively
2. **It's declarative** — says WHAT to do, not HOW (implementation is per-platform)
3. **It's structured** — frontmatter for machine parsing, body for context
4. **It's versionable** — lives in Git, tracks changes
5. **It's shareable** — community members can exchange skills
6. **It's composable** — skills can reference other skills

### Skills vs Tools vs Workflows

| Component | Portability | Format | Lifespan | Example |
|-----------|-----------|--------|----------|---------|
| **Skill** | Universal | Markdown | Permanent | "Morning Briefing" — works with any AI |
| **Tool** | Platform-specific | Code (JS/Python) | Until platform changes | OpenClaw JS skill, Letta Python tool |
| **Workflow** | Semi-portable | Markdown + triggers | Long-lived | "Weekly Review" — sequence of skills |

**Mental model:**
- **Skill** = The recipe (portable, permanent)
- **Tool** = The kitchen appliance (platform-specific)
- **Workflow** = The meal plan (orchestrates multiple skills)

## The 9 Components of a Complete Codex

Every Codex — whether you're a solo entrepreneur, a family of 12, or an enterprise team — contains these 9 components. This is the universal schema.

### 1. GOALS
*"Where are you going?"*

```yaml
# Goal Format
id: goal-credit-750
name: "Credit Score to 750+"
domain: HP
type: outcome  # outcome | behavior | milestone
target: 750
current: 680
deadline: 2026-06-01
lead_metrics: [disputes_sent, on_time_payments]
lag_metrics: [credit_score]
projects: [proj-credit-repair]
status: active
```

Goals are the North Stars. Every project, task, and skill ultimately serves a goal. Goals live inside their domain folder.

### 2. PROJECTS
*"What time-bound efforts are you running?"*

```yaml
# Project Format
id: proj-credit-repair
name: "Credit Repair Sprint"
domain: HP
goal: goal-credit-750
status: active  # active | blocked | complete | archived
deadline: 2026-06-01
outcome: "Credit score ≥ 750 on all 3 bureaus"
owner: zeus
lead_metric: disputes_sent
tasks: [task-001, task-002, task-003]
```

Projects decompose goals into manageable efforts. Each project has a clear outcome and tasks.

### 3. TASKS
*"What's the next atomic action?"*

```yaml
# Task Format
id: task-001
title: "Send dispute letter to Experian"
project: proj-credit-repair
domain: HP
owner: zeus  # or agent-id
status: pending  # pending | in_progress | done | blocked
priority: P1
due: 2026-02-28
context: [@computer]
autonomy: L1  # requires approval
```

Tasks are the smallest unit of work. They can be assigned to humans or AI agents. The autonomy level determines whether AI can execute independently.

### 4. WORKFLOWS
*"What repeatable processes do you run?"*

```yaml
# Workflow Format
id: wf-weekly-review
name: "Weekly Review"
domain: SO
trigger: "Every Sunday 6:00 PM"
frequency: weekly
steps:
  - { action: "Review scorecard", owner: ai, skill: scorecard-review }
  - { action: "Process inbox", owner: ai, skill: inbox-processing }
  - { action: "Update priorities", owner: human }
  - { action: "Plan next week", owner: human+ai, skill: weekly-planner }
automatable: partial
```

Workflows are sequences of steps that repeat. They orchestrate skills and human actions. The Two-Run Rule: first time manual, second time documented, third time automated.

### 5. SKILLS
*"What can the AI actually do?"*

Skills are the **portable capabilities** of your AI agents. They're written in markdown so ANY AI system can read and execute them. (Full format defined above in the Universal Skill Format section.)

**Skill Categories:**
- **Daily** — Morning brief, evening review, inbox processing
- **Domain** — HP coaching, LE family sync, GE revenue tracking
- **Workflows** — Weekly review, quarterly planning, annual thesis
- **Operational** — Infrastructure monitoring, backup verification
- **Social** — Community engagement, customer support, outreach

### 6. TOOLS
*"What systems are currently wired up?"*

Tools are **platform-specific integrations**. Unlike skills, they are NOT portable — they change when you change AI systems.

```yaml
# Tool Registry (tools/README.md)
active_tools:
  - name: todoist
    type: task_management
    connected: true
    access: read_write
    api: REST

  - name: home_assistant
    type: iot
    connected: true
    access: read_write
    api: REST + WebSocket

  - name: github
    type: code_repository
    connected: true
    access: read_write
    api: REST + GraphQL

  - name: calendar
    type: scheduling
    connected: true
    access: read_write
    provider: google  # or apple, outlook
```

**When you switch AI systems**, you rebuild tool connections. The Codex's skills tell the new system WHAT to do; you just need to wire up HOW it connects.

### 7. KNOWLEDGE
*"What do you know?"*

Knowledge nodes are atomic units of information organized by domain. They serve as the AI's RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) corpus — the long-term memory that any AI can search.

```yaml
# Knowledge Node Format
id: know-credit-dispute-process
title: "How to Dispute Credit Errors"
domain: HP
type: procedure  # concept | procedure | reference | experience
source: "Consumer Financial Protection Bureau"
status: canonical  # draft | validated | canonical
tags: [finance, credit, procedure]
links: [know-credit-laws, proj-credit-repair]
```

**Knowledge progression:** Draft → Validated → Canonical (Codex-grade truth)

### 8. METRICS
*"How do you know it's working?"*

Every domain has lead metrics (actions you control) and lag metrics (outcomes you measure).

```yaml
# Metric Format
id: metric-credit-score
name: "Credit Score"
domain: HP
type: lag  # lead | lag
current: 680
target: 750
unit: points
frequency: monthly
source: credit_monitoring_api
threshold:
  red: "<650"
  amber: "650-720"
  green: ">720"
```

**Intelligence Scores** aggregate metrics per domain:
- **PI** (Performance Intelligence) → HP path health
- **LI** (Legacy Intelligence) → LE path health
- **WI** (Wealth Intelligence) → GE path health
- **Unified Score** = (PI + LI + WI) / 3

### 9. REVIEWS
*"What did you learn?"*

Reviews are the feedback mechanism. They close the CODEX loop by feeding insights back into goals, skills, and workflows.

```
Daily   → 15 min → Top 3 check, energy, captures
Weekly  → 1 hour → Scorecard, priorities, plan
Quarter → Half day → OKRs, big rocks, roadmap
Annual  → 1-2 days → Vision, thesis, non-negotiables
```

Reviews generate artifacts stored in `reviews/` — these become the historical record that AI uses for pattern analysis and recommendations.

```
OpenClaw Gateway
    ├── Reads: /home/openclaw/codex/       (the Codex vault)
    ├── Uses: memory_search                (semantic search over Codex)
    ├── Skills: workspace/skills/          (JS implementations of .skill.md)
    ├── Voice: Built-in Talk Mode          (voice-first, ambient)
    ├── Channels: WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, iMessage
    └── Memory: memory/ (daily logs) + MEMORY.md (persistent)
```

**OpenClaw's strengths for OneMind:**
- Native voice (Talk Mode, ambient listening)
- Multi-channel (WhatsApp, iMessage, Telegram, Discord)
- Skill marketplace (community skills)
- Node architecture (multiple devices, locations)
- Proactive agent (heartbeat, scheduled actions)

### Alternative: Letta

```
Letta Server
    ├── Reads: GitHub repo (the Codex) via API
    ├── Uses: archival_memory_search       (vector search)
    ├── Skills: Python tool functions       (implementations of .skill.md)
    ├── Voice: Requires external (LiveKit, Vapi)
    ├── Channels: Via webhooks (Discord, etc.)
    └── Memory: Core memory blocks + archival memory
```

### Alternative: Claude Code / Copilot

```
Claude Code Session
    ├── Reads: Local filesystem (the Codex vault)
    ├── Uses: grep_search, semantic_search  (file search)
    ├── Skills: Reads .skill.md files       (follows instructions)
    ├── Voice: No (text only)
    ├── Channels: Terminal/IDE only
    └── Memory: CLAUDE.md + conversation context
```

### Future: Any System

```
Future AI System
    ├── Reads: The Codex (via filesystem, API, or Git)

    ├── Skills: Reads .skill.md files (universal format)
```

**The pattern is always the same:** Point AI at Codex → AI reads markdown → AI follows skills → AI acts within guardrails.

## The Philosophy (Why This Will Win)

### "The Bible of Your Life"

Every major institution has a canonical text:
- Religion has scriptures
- Nations have constitutions
- Companies have handbooks
- Open source has READMEs

**Your life deserves one too.** That's the Codex.

It's not a journal (temporary). It's not a wiki (unstructured). It's not an app (locked in). It's a **structured, versioned, AI-readable canonical text** that defines who you are, what you're building, and how your AI team operates.

### Why Markdown Wins

| Property | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| **Human-readable** | You can read it in any text editor, on paper, on any device |
| **AI-readable** | Every LLM reads markdown natively — zero preprocessing |
| **Version-controlled** | Git tracks every change forever |
| **Tool-agnostic** | Works in Obsidian, VS Code, Notion (export), paper |
| **Offline-capable** | No internet required to read your life's source of truth |
| **Inheritable** | Pass the Git repo to your children |
| **Searchable** | Grep, semantic search, vector search — all work on markdown |
| **Diff-able** | See exactly what changed, when, and why |
| **Composable** | Link between files, embed, reference — it's a web |
| **Free** | No subscription, no vendor, no lock-in |

### Why Skills Beat Tools

Tools are system-specific code. When the system dies, your tools die with it.

Skills are markdown instructions. When the system changes, the skills transfer to the new system instantly. The AI reads the skill file and knows what to do, regardless of what platform it's running on.

**This is the unlock.** The entire AI industry is building tools. Nobody is building portable skills. OneMind does.

### The 500-Year Test

Ask yourself: "Will this exist in 500 years?"

- ❌ OpenClaw — probably not
- ❌ Letta — probably not
- ❌ Todoist — probably not
- ❌ Any specific AI company — probably not
- ✅ **Markdown text files in a folder structure** — absolutely yes
- ✅ **The Codex** — as long as intelligence exists, context will be needed

That's why the Codex is the center. Everything else is a runtime.
