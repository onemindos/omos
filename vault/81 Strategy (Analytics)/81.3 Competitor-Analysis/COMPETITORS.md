## Tier 1 — Direct Positioning Rivals

These are the frameworks OneMind explicitly replaces. Every piece of content, every sales page, every talk should reference these as the prior era.

---

### 1. GTD — Getting Things Done
**Creator:** David Allen
**Published:** 2001
**Estimated Reach:** 2M+ books sold, globally recognized

**What it does:**
GTD is a task and workflow capture system. Its core loop — Capture → Clarify → Organize → Reflect → Engage — was revolutionary for its time. It clears mental RAM by externalizing all commitments into a trusted system.

**What it gets right (steal this):**
- The capture habit is non-negotiable. Everything out of your head, into a system.
- Contextual next-actions (what can I do *right now* with what I have?)
- Weekly review as a ritual — OneMind's heartbeat loop is the evolved version of this.

**Where it breaks in the AI era:**
- Pure task management — no knowledge, no memory, no intelligence layer
- No domain structure for life (health, family, business are all flat)
- No AI integration — designed for paper and tickler files
- Aging metaphors (manila folders, physical inboxes)
- You have to maintain the entire system manually — cognitive overhead is high

**Threat level:** 🟡 Medium — legacy brand, but aging audience. Ripe for displacement.

**Why GTD users will migrate to OneMind:**
They've mastered capture but have no synthesis layer. OneMind adds AI, domains, and generational thinking on top of what GTD built.

**Read it?** Yes. $4 on eBay. Essential founder knowledge — you must be able to speak to its gaps from firsthand experience, not assumption. "I read GTD so you don't have to" is powerful content.

### 2. BASB / PARA — Building a Second Brain
**Creator:** Tiago Forte / Forte Labs
**Published:** 2017 (course), 2022 (book)
**Estimated Revenue:** $2–3M/year
**Audience:** 100K+ email subscribers, 6,000+ course students

**What it does:**
BASB introduces PARA (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) as a universal organizational structure and CODE (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express) as the knowledge workflow. It made PKM (Personal Knowledge Management) mainstream.

**What it gets right (steal this):**
- PARA is genuinely solid. Projects vs. Areas distinction is one of the best mental models in productivity.
- Progressive summarization — distilling notes to their essence is powerful.
- The "intermediate packets" concept (reusable knowledge chunks) maps directly to OneMind's codex structure.
- Course-first business model is the proven playbook. We adapt it, not reinvent it.

**Where it breaks in the AI era:**
- Knowledge management only — no execution, no life domains, no embodiment
- AI-defensive positioning: Tiago has been slow and reluctant to integrate AI deeply
- No family/generational domain (LE) — purely individual
- No business operating system (GE) — entrepreneurship is out of scope
- No physical world awareness — all digital, no sensors/robots/environment
- Static system — doesn't learn or adapt over time

**Forte Labs Business Model (our template):**

| Product | Price | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|
| BASB Course (Standard) | $1,500 | Cohort-based, 5 weeks |
| BASB Course (Premium) | $6,000 | Includes coaching calls |
| Membership | $125/quarter | Ongoing community |
| Book | $18–28 | 250,000+ copies |

**What We Do Differently:**

| Forte Labs (2016–2022) | One Mind OS (2025–2026) |
|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Blog posts for years before course | 5 years of framework already built |
| Knowledge management focus only | Full life OS — 4 domains |
| AI-agnostic / defensive | AI-native from day one |
| Started with individuals | Individual → Family → Enterprise by design |
| Slow cohort iteration (years) | Rapid beta → full launch (months) |
| Teachable/Kajabi | Circle.SO community-first |
| Software-agnostic | Future: Native One Mind App |

**Threat level:** 🔴 High — same customer, proven playbook, 6-year head start, large warm audience. Most dangerous near-term competitor.

**Why BASB users will migrate to OneMind:**
They've organized their knowledge but it doesn't *do* anything. OneMind turns the second brain into an operating system with agents, automation, and embodiment.

**Read it?** Yes. Library or cheap used copy. Read it *after* GTD.

## Tier 2 — Structural Cousins

Same audience, different approach. These frameworks overlap One Mind's philosophy without claiming the AI-native OS position. Watch these closely — they're the ones most likely to pivot into your territory.

---

### 3. PPV — Pillars, Pipelines, Vaults
**Creator:** August Bradley
**Format:** Free YouTube series + Notion templates + paid community
**Platform:** Notion-native

**What it does:**
PPV is the closest structural analog to OneMind. It organizes life into Pillars (life domains/values), Pipelines (active projects/processes), and Vaults (reference knowledge). Bradley is obsessively systematic and speaks the same language as OneMind's audience.

**What it gets right:**
- Hierarchical life domains — most similar to HP/LE/GE/UI structure
- Systems over goals thinking — aligns with OneMind's principles
- The audience is almost identical: high-agency, systems-obsessed, self-improvers

**Where it breaks:**
- 100% Notion-dependent — tool lock-in is a strategic weakness
- No AI thesis — Bradley has not built an AI-native version
- No family/generational domain depth (LE)
- No physical world layer
- No agent infrastructure — it's a note-taking system, not a runtime

**Threat level:** 🟡 Medium — serious structural overlap but no AI thesis. If Bradley builds an AI version of PPV, it becomes a real threat.

**Why PPV users will migrate to OneMind:**
They've built the structure but it's static, Notion-dependent, and has no intelligence layer. OneMind is PPV with a brain, agents, and memory.

---

### 4. Zettelkasten
**Creator:** Niklas Luhmann (1960s–80s), popularized by Sönke Ahrens (*How to Take Smart Notes*, 2017)
**Platform:** Obsidian, Roam Research, Logseq communities

**What it does:**
Zettelkasten is an atomic note-linking system. Every note is a single idea, linked bidirectionally to related ideas. Over time, a web of knowledge emerges that generates new insights.

**Where it overlaps OneMind:**
The UI domain (Unified Intelligence / 00-24) — specifically the knowledge vault and codex architecture.

**Where it breaks:**
- Pure knowledge system — no domains, no agents, no execution
- Extremely manual — linking and maintaining notes is high-friction
- No AI integration by design (purists resist it)
- No family, business, or physical domain

**Threat level:** 🟢 Low — different philosophy, different audience segment. More of a sub-tool OneMind can absorb than a competitor.

---

### 5. 12 Week Year
**Creator:** Brian Moran & Michael Lennington
**Published:** 2013

**What it does:**
Reframes the year as 12 weeks. Forces prioritization by compressing planning cycles. Strong execution and accountability focus.

**Where it overlaps OneMind:**
GE domain — entrepreneurship execution, goal tracking, quarterly cycles.

**Threat level:** 🟢 Low — execution-only, no knowledge or AI layer. A useful tool *within* OneMind's GE domain, not a replacement for it.

---

### 6. Vivid Vision
**Creator:** Cameron Herold
**Published:** 2018

**What it does:**
A 3-year written vision document for your business/life — vivid, present-tense, detailed. Designed to align teams and family around a shared future. Strong generational and legacy angle.

**Where it overlaps OneMind:**
LE domain — family vision, generational legacy, values transfer. GE domain — business future-casting and alignment.

**Threat level:** 🟢 Low — vision-only, no system, no AI, no execution layer. It's a document, not an OS. Could be a useful *input* into OneMind's LE/GE domains.

---

### 7. EOS — Entrepreneurial Operating System
**Creator:** Gino Wickman (*Traction*, 2011)
**Format:** Book + certified implementers + consulting business

**What it does:**
EOS is a business operating system for small to mid-size companies. Vision/Traction Organizer, rocks (quarterly goals), L10 meetings, scorecards.

**Where it overlaps OneMind:**
GE domain — business systems, team ops, entrepreneurship infrastructure.

**Threat level:** 🟢 Low — B2B business framework, not a personal life OS. Different buyer, different context. Could be an integration point rather than a competitor.

## Tier 3 — Emerging AI-Era Framework Threats

The most important watch category. These are not yet dominant but could claim your territory if OneMind doesn't establish framework authority first.

---

### 7. AI-Retrofitted Productivity (The Crowded Middle)
**Players:** Ali Abdaal, Lenny Rachitsky, Sahil Bloom, Dan Koe, et al.

These creators are rapidly adding AI content to existing productivity frameworks and calling it new. They have massive distribution (1M+ audiences) and will commoditize surface-level "AI productivity" before OneMind can reach them.

**The race:** Establish One Mind OS as *the* AI-native life framework before these creators define the category with a lesser, retrofit version.

**Threat level:** 🔴 High — not because their frameworks are better, but because of their distribution advantage. Speed to category definition matters here.

---

### 8. "Second Me" / AI Twin Frameworks (Emerging)
**Players:** Various 2025–2026 startups and creators

The concept of an AI that knows you, grows with you, and acts on your behalf is entering the mainstream. Someone will codify this as a *framework* (not just a product) soon. Legacy + OneMind Codex is already this — but it needs to be taught and documented as a learnable system before someone else names the category.

**Threat level:** 🟡 Medium-High — this is the most important emerging threat. OneMind's answer is the combination of SOUL.md, AGENTS.md, and the 4-domain structure as a *teachable framework* for building your own sovereign AI.

1. **AI-native** — agents, memory, always-on runtime

4. **Physical world embodiment** — sensors, robots, environment awareness
5. **Sovereign infrastructure** — self-hosted, off-grid resilient, no vendor lock-in

That combination is unclaimed. That is the One Mind OS moat.
