# Types of Trusts Commonly Established

While the Greenwich Probate Court oversees the administration and supervision of trusts, it does not facilitate the creation of trusts. To establish a trust, you would typically work with an estate planning attorney who can draft the necessary documents tailored to your specific needs.

### Types of Trusts Commonly Established

In Connecticut, individuals often create the following types of trusts:

- **Revocable Living Trusts**: These allow you to maintain control over your assets during your lifetime and specify how they should be distributed upon your death. They can be altered or revoked as your circumstances change.([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/pf/06/revocablelivingtrust.asp?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
- **Irrevocable Trusts**: Once established, these cannot be easily modified or revoked. They are often used for tax planning and asset protection purposes.
- **Testamentary Trusts**: Created through a will, these trusts come into effect upon your death and are subject to probate court supervision.([Connecticut Probate Court](https://www.ctprobate.gov/Documents/User%20Guide%20-%20Understanding%20Trusts.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Role of the Greenwich Probate Court

While the court doesn't assist in forming trusts, it plays a crucial role in:

- **Overseeing Testamentary Trusts**: Ensuring that trusts established through wills are administered according to the decedent's wishes.
- **Supervising Trust Administration**: Monitoring the actions of trustees to protect the interests of beneficiaries.
- **Resolving Disputes**: Addressing conflicts that may arise between trustees and beneficiaries or among beneficiaries themselves.

### Steps to Establish a Trust

1. **Consult an Estate Planning Attorney**: Engage a qualified attorney to discuss your goals and draft the trust document.
2. **Define the Trust's Terms**: Specify the purpose of the trust, identify beneficiaries, and outline how assets should be managed and distributed.
3. **Fund the Trust**: Transfer ownership of the chosen assets into the trust to ensure it operates as intended.
4. **Maintain the Trust**: Regularly review and update the trust to reflect changes in your circumstances or in the law.
