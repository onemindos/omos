Diabetes is more common with humans than with cats or other animals.
The cause of diabetes is actually quite simple. Sugar, or glucose, is
found in the blood.  The level of blood sugar in the body or the animal is
kept under control by hormone insulin, which the pancreas produces.
When the pancreas doesn't produce enough insulin, diabetes is to
blame.

The symptoms of feline diabetes will vary. The most common
symptoms include;

- An increase in urine
- An increase in thirst.
- Loss of appetite
- Weight loss
- Dull or patchy coat.

Among all of the symptoms, the increase in thirst is easiest to detect,
as you will notice that your cats water dish needs filling more often.

If you don�t get your cat treated for feline diabetes immediately, the cat
will eventually become inactive, vomit on a regular basis, and eventually
fall into a coma. On the other hand, if you get the diabetes treated in
time, the cat will more than likely lead a normal and healthy life. Keep in
mind that treatment doesn't happen overnight. It takes time and
dedication.

Just like with humans, cats that have feline diabetes will need to be
given food at the same time every day. They should be prevented from
going outside as well. If your cat has diabetes, you will more than likely
have to give them insulin shots once or twice or a day. Your veterinarian
will be able to tell you how much insulin you need to give your cat. They
will also advise you how to properly feed and care for your feline friend.

Before you give your cat his insulin shot, you should always make sure
that they have eaten.  If they haven't eaten and you give them their shot
they could end up with a hypoglycemic shock. This can occur from
injecting too much insulin as well. If your cat goes into hypoglycemic
shock they could die. that is why it is important to keep a close eye on
them after you have given them their insulin shot.

When it comes to caring for a cat with feline diabetes it is very
important to be diligent. Make sure that you monitor them closely and
keep up with regular vet checks. After your cat has been on insulin for a

substances (in this case cat's).These substances can trigger side
effects like sneezing, wheezing, coughing and itching. Did you know
that in cases of severe reactions allergies can sometimes be fatal.

As for cat allergies, one of the leading causes is cat dander. The
dander, is dust that is produced by the body of the cat. I know this may
sound gross, but it's basically shed skin that comes off of your cat,
normally in the form of small flakes. Although dander can irritate your
skin, it can also get into your immune system as well, resulting in a
variety of symptoms and an almost immediate allergic reactions.

Once the dander is identified by your immune system, it is perceived to
be a threat. Even though it isn't classified as a disease, it�s more of a

time. This is because they leave behind dander and secretions
wherever they go.

When you have a cat their dander, urine, and saliva, are found
throughout your home, even if you keep a spotless house. This is
because cats constantly groom themselves, which involves rubbing
their saliva into their fur.  When they do this, they spread their dander
and allergens around.  Although you may try, there is really nothing that
you can do about it. Cats have a natural instinct to groom or bathe
themselves, no matter how many baths you give them, you simply won't

decongestants.

Decongestants on the other hand, are normally used by those who
experience coughing and swollen nasal passages.

as they are used to identify the cat to other animals.

his urine. Disgusting I know, but spraying is simply the cat�s way of
letting others know that the territory is his. Even though it stinks up your

problem. In fact, if you raise your voice or show anger towards your cat,
it can very well result in more spraying.

with other cats, or when they are feeling stressed.

need to be scooped out daily and the old litter replaced with new litter
