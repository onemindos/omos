Bedbugs are wingless tiny nocturnal parasites resting during daytime but actively feeding on human blood at night. Therefore, mattresses and beddings are favorite hang-out of bedbugs where food is close at hand.   

Even though bedbugs may hold diseases inside their bodies, human transmission is considered to be very unlikely. That is why they are not regarded as dangerous, except when an individual is allergic or sensitive to them. The presence however of bed bugs can be upsetting plus their bites are highly irritating. 

Common bed bug hiding spots

•	Along mattresses seams. 

•	Blankets and sheets. 

•	Under loosened wallpaper edges. 

•	Between wooden floor cracks. 

•	In crevices or wall cracks. 

•	Carpet. 

•	In furniture cracks and seams.

Because bed bugs love to hide in cracks, seams and crevices, reducing home clutter is necessary. Belongings scattered about the room provide many homes such bugs, increasing their hiding places.  

Although high hygiene standards and good housekeeping alone do not control or are not guarantee of bed bug infestation, keeping your home clean will expose bed bug presence at right on its onset, making them easier to control and decreasing the possibility of uncontrollable infestation. 

How to recognize bed bug infestation

Bug bites are first signs and indications of bed bug infestation.  But a meticulous examination of your place can also reveal the following: 

•	It can reveal the parasites themselves, even if they can be difficult to spot, often you will find a group of them in one location.

•	Small bed bug bloodstains from on your mattress or sheets. 

•	Dark spots or rusty bed bug waste on walls and mattresses. 

•	A sweet and musty offensive odor from the bed bug’s scent glands that generally indicates severe infestation.

Prevention

1.	Regularly launder all your bedding regularly, preferably once a week. 

2.	Vacuum your home regularly including all your furniture, upholstery, bedding and wall paper crevices. 

3.	Never purchase secondhand bedding, upholstered furniture or beds. 

4.	Eliminate crevices and cracks in walls and flooring. Repair cracks by applying plaster, paint and or wallpaper on your ceilings and walls. 

5.	Expose bedding and upholstered furniture in the sun  during sunny days.
