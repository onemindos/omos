# how-to-regain-your-happiness

How to Regain your Happiness


By Abdulelah Fallatah


Copyright 2016 Abdulelah Fallatah
Smashwords Edition
If you read the long description of my book, it means you now know
half the solution. So, congratulations!

All right; let's get started!

You never lose your will to live unless you lose what's most dear to you:
your dignity. Without it, you won't love yourself, let alone live happily
for the rest of your life.

Here are some tips:




TIP 1 - Focus on your surroundings.

Have you noticed that you could barely carry out a normal
conversation and/or unable to perform tasks (e.g. work) as diligently as
you used to?

This is because you:

(1) have too many negative thoughts roaming your mind most of the
time. Here are some:

"Why can't people understand me? Why aren't they trying?"

"How to make them like me?"

"Had I done so and so, everything would've been different."

"I don't have what he/she has."

"I'm a failure. These people believe it."

"I can change. What if people don't accept it?"
You have to know that even people have thoughts (not necessarily too
many) roaming their minds. Let's take presentations as an example.
When the speaker says something along the line of "I wrote my first
paragraph when I was ten years old," you would remember yourself at
the age of ten and stop listening to the speaker. This is normal! Give
people the benefits of the doubt when you're talking to them. Some
words trigger events, emotions, actions, etc. from their past, so their
attention shifts to a point in their past. Not everyone is a good listener,
and even good listeners get carried away at times. No one is perfect! I
know you know of this fact. So, if a person didn't listen well to you, he
or she is very likely preoccupied with something triggered by a word or
so you said. Again, give them the benefit of the doubt.

(2) are not in the mood to say nice things to people or help them
because you're the one who needs an empathetic ear. The third and
fourth tip will help you with that, so bear with me.

TIP 2 - Read your old messages and posts from a social network(s) (e.g.
Facebook, Youtube, Twitter, Skype, Live Messenger, Instagram, etc.)
starting from your first message.

Try to remember how you behaved from these messages and how
people responded.

TIP 3 - When you're by yourself at home, lie on a bed or couch, or any
comfortable surface. Then, relax.

This is easier said than done, but I'll make it easily doable.

In public, you're uneased. This uneasiness comes from a firmly
clutched hand(s), raised shoulders, uncomfortable sensation in your
head, slouched back, tilted head, kinda-rigid arms and legs,
tiredness...and so on. Add to that the scorching heat of the summer, the
freezing coldness of winter, and sitting for a long time in a car – they
make you think more negatively.

At home, lie down (on a bed, sofa, etc.) in a comfortable position, and
release the tense feeling from your entire body by opening your hands,
letting your body press against the surface, etc.. When you feel relaxed,
think only of the things you like (or the things you'd like to have) for at
least 10 full seconds. If you suddenly think of something you dislike,
start over. You can close your eyes if you want to.

In public, try to be as relaxed as you were at home, or somewhere in
between. I leave it at your discretion. But remember that being too
relaxed is not advisable in all situations. Would you loosen your grip on
the steering wheel of your car? No!

Don't worry if you can't feel relaxed in public the first time. Just try
again and again. Practice makes perfect.

TIP 4 - You VS Insults

Question\ Why people insult others?

Answer\ To hurt their feelings.




Some people say bad things to you in order to hurt your feelings. Hurt
feelings lead to hurt mentality. People do this because they either spite
you (because of race, the way you look to them, the job you do, the car
you drive aren't to their liking, etc.) or envy you.
If you think your skills are less than theirs, improve them and don't let
their taunts stop you; if they can do something, so can you. If you think
your skills are above theirs, let that be proof that you've reached a level
they can't attain or a level they find difficult to obtain, but don't let
such superiority make you complacent; improve yourself when you see
an opportunity; be proud of your skills, but don't let pride make you
look down on the innocent who don't possess your skills.

Now you know the point of insults, continue to work or go to school as
you normally did before. Shop anytime, anywhere you want. Go to the
beach anytime, anywhere you want. Basically, do whatever you desire
as long as your actions don't hurt those around you. Don't mind
people's angry faces as well as troll faces. They want to see you suffer
throughout your life.

Don't care about their insults. Focus on what's important to you right
now. If an insult or so gets under your skin, don't give up; try again and
again till you're comfortable walking around without worrying about
any. Practice makes perfect! Let them waste their breaths and time.

What if no one steps in to help you? You don't need anyone's help.
Inner happiness and content with one' life are enough. However,
there's an exception to this rule. Request the police's help if the insults
are turned into physically harm or are likely to turn into physical harm.

Inner happiness and content with one's life will make you, believe it or
not, better than the majority of the people living in this world right
now. You'll have a better chance at focusing on your goals despite the
hurtful (and hateful) insults hurled.
NOTE: If you think your situation is hopeless, change location. Try
doing that through polishing your resume or making money through,
for example, writing ebooks, or a hobby you like. I leave it at your
discretion.




Your happiness is bound to return. You'll bear the fruit of your labor.




You'll become an inspiration to those who watches you silently.




Advice #1:

I suggest find a way to connect the four tips mentioned above in your
own way.

Use a pen and paper (or a pen and physical notebook), but not a
computer. I repeat! No computer!
If you discover something new that'll help regain your happiness, write
it down in the piece of paper (or the physical notebook if you're using a
notebook) in your own way.

Advice #2:

Although I said this book will help you restore your happiness quickly,
don't rush things from now on, OK?

Where I'm from, we have a proverb, which says, "In deliberation,
there's shelter; in hurriedness, there's regret."

Also, don't think you should work too slowly just to avoid hastiness
unless you believe you need to take smaller steps. If you fail, find what
went wrong and fix it.

Advice #3:

Don't think you can make everyone (e.g. your city) like you. Every
human in this world, famous or not, has at least one critic pointing at
nothing but flaws.

Advice #4:

Don’t fluster if you don't see changes in the way people treat you. The
goal of this book isn't to bind your happiness to the way people
perceive you. But, if there's a person you'd like to have in your life, give
him or her time to get accustomed to the better you. As a personal rule
of thumb of mine, give them two days, nothing more. If they keep
disapproving of you, ignore them; set them free. It's hard to set them
free; I already know, but remember they're humans who like certain
people over the rest. You, too, like certain people over the rest. Would
you like to see a person you don't like forcing himself or herself into
your life? No!

Think about it. If you keep following people who express to you they
don't like you (orally or with a gesture(s)), imagine the image you've
portrayed in the bystanders' mind. Imagine it one more time. Now,
imagine it once more. Did you catch my draft?

Only accompany those who don't demand much from you. Now these
are people worth your time, are they not?

Advice #5:

After you restore your happiness, protect and guard it the same way
you protect your life. Don't lose it! Heal it whenever it gets scarred.

Be like big-time companies (e.g. Apple) that maintain their success
through leaving no room for complacency and failure.

THE BIGGEST ADVICE OF THEM ALL:

I'm not trying to impose rules on you. Use the book as a supplement
for personal growth.
