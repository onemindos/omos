                     INTRODUCTION


What does success mean to you?
Your idea of success might differ from what it means to
your spouse, partner, parents, family, friends or
colleagues. It is about achieving what you want.

If you do not know what you want, then you might spend
the rest of your life achieving what others want for you
and that will be their success, not yours.

You might be doing everything right according to
everyone else’s standards, but are you doing the right
thing according to your standard?

Do you want someone else’s success?
Success starts when you start doing what you truly want.
You can only do this when you know what it is that you
want. This may seem simple enough, but it isn’t.

Realizing and accepting what you want is not the same
as doing what everyone thinks is right. These two things
are entirely different.

The world is made up of people who differ from each
other in one way or another. No two people are same.
So, if you try to be like the person next to you, or in front
of you, you are not being yourself. If you are trying to be
someone you are not, you will never be really able to
achieve success because you are trying to do something
which you know is not what you are meant to do.

Make your mark.
When you put your finger on something, you leave a
mark - your fingerprint. This is unique. There’s nobody
else in the whole world who has it except you.

If you have a unique fingerprint, then don’t you think you
should have a unique mind, unique thoughts, a unique
personality, unique feelings and beliefs?

Be true to yourself. Do what you truly want and you will
achieve success. You can win at everything that you do,
if you use your energy in the right way.

To use energy, you need to first learn to recognize the
energy. Only then can you gain or develop the energy.
Energy is within you and all around you. It is readily
available. The trick is to attract that energy to you,
convert it and channel it to achieve your goals. This is
the key to success and the key lies within you.

                        CHAPTER ONE

                          The ‘I’
                 “Know thyself.” ~ Socrates

It starts with you.
Look in the mirror and tell me what do you see?

Do you see hope, happiness and potential? Or do you
feel like looking away? Are you afraid that you will see
weakness, laziness, inadequacy or a lack of confidence?

Do you like what you see when you look deep into your
eyes? Or does it scare you to see the emptiness that lies
within you and ahead of you in your future?

Look within yourself to know who you are. You are not
merely an engineer, an entrepreneur, a player, a pilgrim,
a student, a salesperson, a traveler or a teacher.

You are not just someone who goes to work and is
defined by your profession. You are not someone who
was born with talents and is therefore defined by your
birth or your circumstances. You are not someone who is
defined by your physical body or your social status or
your material belongings.

You define who you are.
The relevance and importance of your education and
your professional qualifications will vary depending on
the needs and demands of the time and place you live in.

Tomorrow, you will discover new talents that you never
knew you had before. Your body will evolve, age and
change. So will your status. Your most precious
possessions today might not worth as much in the future.

Everything changes.
You, too, will change. You have the power to change.

When you say “I am ________ (something or someone),”
you are taking the first step towards changing yourself,
your destiny, and the world.

When you say, “I am hungry,” you are making a
statement. When you say, “I am going to make
something to eat,” you are expressing your willingness
to take a positive action to change your circumstance
which is being hungry.

The same applies, when you say, “I am lonely,” or “I am
poor,” or “I am unhappy.” You and only you have the
power to change your circumstance. You can go from
being lonely to being loved, from being poor to becoming
wealthy or from being unhappy to being joyful – that is, if
you really want to.

How can you change your circumstances?

You can start by thinking “I am going to be loved, I am
going to be rich, and I am going to be happy.” Then, you
put your thoughts into words; when you start feeling and
believing in them, you should practice methods and
actions that will bring you closer to your goals. Soon, you
will start achieving the results that you desire.


What does the ‘I’ represent?
The “I” is your consciousness of who you are and your
presence in this universe. It is also the consciousness of
the presence of the universe and the infinite potential
within you.

When you say, “I can’t do something,” you are
expressing either your belief that you will fail or your
intention not to do it. When you say, “It’s difficult, but I’ll
try,” then you are doing something half-heartedly.

“..there is nothing either good or bad but thinking makes
it so,” says Shakespeare in Hamlet.

Similarly, nothing is easy nor difficult. You only think it is
and it becomes as such.

You are neither a victim nor an instrument of fate. You
are the creator of your own destiny. You are on a
journey, but where you want to go and how you will get
there is up to you to decide and act upon.

You become who you think you are.

You make your soul come alive.
                        CHAPTER TWO

                What is visualization?
       “Life is like music. It must be composed by ear,
        feeling and instinct, not by rule.” ~ Samuel
                              Butler

Life is about making choices.
What should I do? Should I do what I think is right? Or
should I just do what I feel is right? Do I follow my head
or do I follow my heart?

You may have asked yourself these questions whenever
you have to make a choice, which is quite often. After all,
life is all about making choices.

So what do you do? Do you listen to your heart or your
mind?

There are many interpretations of this dilemma. One is to
classify your choices as rational and emotional. Another
way is to say that your heart could be wrong because it
feels what is right but your mind knows what is really
right. Yet another interpretation is to call what your heart
tells you as your instinct or intuition, and what your mind
tells you as your knowledge and experience.
Know your mind.
Let’s examine how this works in our awareness so that
we can understand visualization.

Your awareness has two components: your conscious
mind and your subconscious mind. When you visualize
something consciously, you are internally manifesting
what you think is rationally possible.

So, if you see a mountain and you are aware that 9 out
of 10 people who have tried to climb it have failed, you
realize that it is difficult and so you consciously count
yourself among the 9 who have failed. You don’t imagine
yourself climbing that mountain and reaching the
summit. Instead, you imagine yourself trying to climb the
mountain and giving up because the odds are against
you. This is actually absurd because you are letting a
rule decide your fate. You are allowing the rule of
probability decide that you will fail.


Look beyond what you know.
On the other hand, if you tap into your subconscious
mind, you will be able to visualize or internally manifest
yourself standing at the summit of the mountain.

Why?

The subconscious mind does not allow existing
experience or knowledge to cloud its intentions.

This is why the earliest explorers set sail to discover
unknown lands. They relied on their subconscious mind
and followed their instincts and not the knowledge that
the earth is flat and that you could fall off the edge if you
go too far. This is how new hypotheses and theories have
evolved to disprove previous beliefs.

Open your mind to possibilities.
Dogma and knowledge are but two sides of the same
coin. They can close your mind to new awareness
because you cling on to old beliefs or what you think you
know to be true.

When the Oracle of Delphi told Socrates that he was the
wisest man in Athens, the great philosopher is said to
have responded by saying that he could prove that the
Oracle was wrong.

So Socrates went around asking questions to all the
great scholars in the city and they all gave answers that
were full of their knowledge. They all thought that they
had all the answers. Finally, Socrates is believed to have
said, “I may be after all the wisest man because I know
one thing and that is that I know nothing.”

Admitting your own ignorance is not easy and it takes
great courage and maturity to do so. It is the first step to
opening your mind.

Intuition is in. Knowledge is out.
Visualization is seeing the world as it should be. There is
nothing more real than the world you can create. It is
similar to daydreaming but it is more than mere wishful
thinking.

Visualization means believing in the world that you see
in your mind and taking steps to make it possible. You
don’t just see yourself on the summit of a mountain, you
act upon this visual and then you really reach the
summit.

How?

Know how to visualize.
You should allow your imagination to vividly create the
possibility of what you want, believe in it, and desire this
ideal with all your heart.

The conscious mind questions your actions. It preserves
old notions and wants to predict what will happen. Yes, it
will help you survive in times of danger and threat, but
will also prevent you from taking risks or realizing your
full potential.

On the other hand, the subconscious knows no fear,
hate, anger or any other negative emotion. It values
unpredictability and makes life worth living.

Your conscious mind is formulaic. It wants you to watch
movies that have the same basic plot, or watch the same
kind of game where one team is stronger and is
therefore sure to win.

It wants you do things again and again because it
doesn’t like being surprised.
However, if you listen to your subconscious, it is more
likely that you will have no clue most of the time. It’s like
watching a new movie where you don’t know what will
happen next or watching a game where the underdog
team can defeat the reigning champions.

Winning and succeeding in life is to do the unexpected.

Don’t just follow the herd; follow your intuition. Follow
your heart. Listen to your subconscious. Allow it to lead
the way and you will be calm and relaxed even in times
of crisis.

Have you ever seen a winner lose his cool whether it is
on the track, field or court?

Champions win because they visualize winning and don’t
think about losing.

To effectively visualize, you need to learn how to control
your negative thoughts.
                        CHAPTER THREE

         Thoughts can make you fail or win
       “My body has certainly wandered a good deal, but
          I have an uneasy suspicion my mind has not
              wandered enough.” ~ Noel Coward

Keep your mind clear.
When an athlete is taking part in a race, what is going on
in his or her mind? Is she thinking about the possibility
that she may stumble and fall?

When a golfer is taking aim to putt, is he or she thinking
about the gathering clouds and wondering if it will rain?

No. Their minds are blank. They are focused on the
action they are about to perform. There is no room for
thoughts.

Why?

Thoughts can be distracting. You think and then you
rationalize so that you can come up with enough excuses
to console yourself in case you fail.

Now, this may seem like advice that is contradicting to
what you’ve always been told.

“Think before you act.”
Now, you might be asking: “How can you know if you are
doing the right thing if your mind is blank?”

The winner wins when the loser thinks.
Your conscious mind behaves like a wayward wind. It
blows hither and then thither. It is easily swayed.

All the thinking that you need to do should be done much
before you act, not just before you are about to perform
the action. This is when you need to have a clear mind
because you need to concentrate.

In a tennis match between two equally skilled players,
the winner wins because the loser thinks.

When the player thinks, she blinks or her vision blurs and
she cannot focus on the fast approaching ball. In spite of
her training and her quick response, a split second of
thought creates the chink in her game. This is the chink
through which the ball falls through and when she misses
to return it. The game is lost.

It is the same in life. So, think much before you act. Not
just before or during the act. When you are doing
something, do it with concentration and conviction or
you will end up doing a worthless job.

Why do something half-heartedly when you can do it
well?
                       CHAPTER FOUR

           How to Control your Thoughts
       “Empty your mind. Be formless, shapeless, like
                  water.” ~ Bruce Lee

What are you thinking of?
Imagine a box filled with something. Shake it and it
creates a rattling noise. As you shake it more, it creates
a bigger racket. Yet, you still don’t know what’s inside,
do you?

Your conscious mind is the box. It is closed and filled with
thoughts. Unless you open it, you will not be able to see
what’s inside.

An open mind strengthens your actions.
When it rains or fogs, the windshield of your car gets wet
or misty and you can’t see clearly. Will you continue
driving without being able to see what’s ahead? No, you
don’t. What should you do then?

You turn on the wipers and it clears the windshield. Now
you can see what lies ahead and reach your destination
without delay or fear.

The thoughts in your conscious mind are like the mist. It
overcrowds your mind and creates a fog. Opinions of
others, news and information that you’ve read, rumors
that you’ve heard and your own analysis, observations,
inferences and conclusions—it’s a maze sorting through
all this, isn’t it? No wonder you become confused and
you fail to do anything worthwhile. If you spend too much
time thinking, you will never be able to do something.

Thoughts crossed my mind.
Clarity of vision comes when you are in control of your
mind. Let’s do an exercise to see how external factors
can influence your mind and how you can resist them to
stay in control.

Take a piece of chocolate and keep it in front of you. Are
you tempted to eat it? Of course, you are. The challenge
is to not eat it even if you can’t stop thinking about it.
You are distracted because your mind is thinking about
it.

Now, take the chocolate and put it away in the fridge.
Are you still thinking about it?

Yes, but now you may have realized that the influence of
the thought is much less because you cannot visually see
it.

It is still there in your mind’s eye but you know it’s not as
strong a temptation as it was when you could see it and
touch it.

Now, call your friend and offer the chocolate to him or
her. Think about the chocolate. Are you still tempted?
No, you are not. Why? What happened?
You are free from the temptation because you acted
upon it and got rid of it. Did you really stop thinking
about the chocolate? No. You are still thinking about it,
but the thought of chocolate no longer distracts you. You
are free from your thoughts because you now have
control over your conscious mind.

To learn to control your thoughts, you have to meditate.
Now, meditation is not something you have to be afraid
of. Most people think it is difficult or it is something that
only monks or ascetics can achieve.

Well, a certain level of mind control does require
dedication and devotion. However, meditating does not
mean going away to a remote place and shutting
yourself away from the world.

That’s not practical. We are talking about meditation that
is practical and has a real world application.

It takes time and effort.
Athletes do it. Actors do it. Children do it. Adults do it.
Everyone can do it.

It just takes time and effort. When an athlete is running
or a soccer player is kicking the ball, what are they
thinking of? When an actor is acting or a singer is
singing, what’s on their mind? When a child is crossing
the road to school or an adult is driving, what’s in their
head? Well, they better be thinking of nothing. Why? If
an athlete wants to perform well, his mind needs to
remain blank and his consciousness needs to focus on
the game. If an actor or a singer is being self-conscious
of his or her appearance and voice or is thinking about
the audience, then he or she isn’t giving his or her best.
If a child or an adult has thoughts meandering through
their mind when they are crossing the road or driving,
that’s when accidents happen.

Let your thoughts flow.
Your heart is pumping blood constantly, but are you
aware of it? Yes, but it does not interfere with your
breathing, does it? Your lungs are equally busy, taking in
oxygen and letting out carbon dioxide.

Meditation is not about stopping or starting your
thoughts. Meditation is about rising above the thoughts
and going beyond them. You can start by focusing on
your breath and you will soon realize that while your
thoughts are still flowing, your mind remains calm and
relaxed. This is what is called the Alpha State when your
actions produce the desired results.
                        CHAPTER FIVE

                 Master your feelings
         “Worry is like a rocking chair. It gives you
      something to do but never gets you anywhere.” ~
                      Emma Bombeck

Be cool but don’t freeze over.
Take a glass of water and keep it on a table. Tap its side.
You will see ripples move on the surface of the water.
Allow a moment to pass for the ripples to subside and for
the water surface to fall flat.

Now, take the glass in your hand. You will see that the
surface is disturbed again and you have to hold your
hand steadily if you want the water to stay placid. It’s not
easy, but it is possible.

Your emotions are like ripples. Any external factor can
cause anger, pain, hatred or love, compassion or pity.

Do you find yourself raising your voice during an
argument? Why? Is it because you think that shouting
will make what you are saying right? No. You are
shouting because you have lost control. You are no
longer thinking straight.

When we play poker, we try to keep our face straight so
that the other players won’t know what we are thinking
of or guess what cards we are holding. Emotional
intelligence is like playing poker but without keeping a
poker face. Instead, you allow your emotions to
communicate and elicit positive responses from others.

Is your mind as cold as ice?
Remember the glass of water and the ripples. Now, take
a cube of ice and tap it. Does it produce ripples? No.
Water becomes ice and ice becomes water. They are
similar yet different. They have the same composition
but are in different forms.

Your mind is like water. It can become ice. Emotional
intelligence or mastering your emotions is not about
turning your mind into ice but keeping it fluid and warm
like water but not allowing it to boil and ripple like it does
when it gets hot.

When you learn to master your feelings, you become
better by being equipped to deal with stressful situations
and you also improve your capacity to interact positively
with others. An intelligent person may be able to think
great thoughts but an emotionally intelligent person has
better social interaction skills and therefore gets along
well with other people.

Do you want to remain cold or feel the cordial warmth of
good company?
                         CHAPTER SIX

              Thoughts are not feelings
       “We fool ourselves so much we could do it for a
                  living.” ~ Stephen King

Face your thoughts but follow your heart.
When you see a blind man, you help him cross the road.
When you see a red light, you stop your car. These are
rational thoughts processed based on information. You
know the blind man needs assistance to cross the road.
You know that it is dangerous to drive through a red
light.

When you take part in a marathon for the first time, you
don’t think that you cannot do it because you’ve never
done it. No. You actually feel that you can do it because
you can visualize yourself reaching the finishing line.

This is the same as climbing a mountain or attempting to
do something which you’ve never done before such as
bungee jumping or writing poetry. It takes courage which
comes not from thinking but from feeling.

Don’t confuse thoughts with feelings.

When you say something which begins with the words, “I
think …,” think: is it really what you think or is it actually
what you feel?
Separate illusions from reality.
It is important to distinguish between thoughts and
feelings. Meditation helps you know when you are
thinking and when you are feeling.

When you say, “I am tired,” you need to understand if it
is a feeling or a thought that you have created in order to
avoid doing something.

Illusions like monsters are easy to create and even easier
to believe. People do it all the time. They watch horror
movies even though the rational part of their mind says,
“It’s all make-believe”. Yet, the emotional part of their
mind gets an adrenaline rush from the fear they feel.

Similarly, the feeling of tiredness can be from genuine
exhaustion or it could be the result of a thought in your
mind which you made up in order to avoid going to the
gym or going to work.

Recognize when something is good and when something
is not.
                       CHAPTER SEVEN

              From negative to positive
       “I am a brain, Watson. The rest of me is a mere
        appendix.” ~ Sherlock Holmes / Sir Arthur
                       Conan Doyle

Making decisions is important.
Now that you have an understanding about the “I” in “I
am” and the difference between thoughts and feelings,
let’s examine how your feelings shift according to a
situation.

Success depends on how you separate your thoughts
and feelings and react in a manner which you will not
regret later.

You have received an e-mail from a client about to place
a big order. The client requires some clarifications which
he has requested by e-mail. It will just take you a few
minutes to send the e-mail.

At the very moment you get a telephone call from your
mother informing you that your father is in the hospital.
Your immediate reaction is to drop everything and rush
to the hospital. When you reach the hospital, you find out
that your father suffered a whiplash but he is fine.
This is when you recall the e-mail that you never sent to
the client. Is it too late to send it now? If you had
enquired what exactly had happened to your father,
would you have panicked and rushed to the hospital? Or
would you have first sent the e-mail and then visited the
hospital afterwards?

In retrospect, it is easy to think calmly and logically, but
in the midst of an emergency, it is not. Success depends
on thinking straight when it is natural to act instinctively.
Success depends on your ability to function with grace
under pressure.

Know your brain to know your behavior.
Our brains consist of three parts: the reptilian brain, the
mammalian brain and the human brain. This is an
evolutionary aspect of how our brains have grown not
just in size but in complexity.

The reptilian or lizard part of our brain is that which
controls basic body functions such as breathing and
regulating body temperatures.

Reptiles are cold-blooded animals; they receive warmth
during daylight and conserve their energy by restricting
their activities when it gets dark. Their biological clock is
located in the eye itself so their brain is associated with
instinctive activities associated with food consumption,
survival, sexual behavior and other automatic responses.

In this state of existence, territories are acquired by
force and defended. Might is right.
When the mammalian brain evolved from the reptilian
brain,   an   enormous    change    took    place.   The
hypothalamus, which is responsible for the functions of
the nervous system, was developed. This part also
controls body functions such as digestion, fluid balance,
body temperature and blood pressure.

The hippocampus is the part of the brain which stores
memories and experiences. The amygdala processes
experience-based recognition of danger and prompts
reactions or responses according to past experiences.

Therefore, the mammal brain is more consciously aware
of the environment. It is involved in motivation and
emotional responses. It is observed that feelings such as
attachment, anger and fear are associated with
responses related to care, fight or flight. This is still
primitive brain behavior.

The human brain expanded enormously to contain a
massive grey matter or neocortex which envelopes the
earlier forms and amounts to over 80% of its mass. The
human brain consists of two hemispheres which are
connected.

The instinctive reaction when you received the earlier
mentioned phone call is directed by the reptilian part of
your brain. Logical, analytical, and even creative
functions are managed by the human part of the brain
and this is what we should rely on to succeed. Otherwise,
our actions will be mere reptilian reactions which we
might regret later.
Are you ready to take action?
Let’s do an exercise. Take pen and paper and write down
how you feel at this moment. Rate yourself on a scale of
0-10, where 0 is downright sad that you have failed and
10 is happy that you have achieved success.

Now think back to the moment you were 6 or 7 years old.
It’s your birthday and your parents give you a birthday
present that you have always wanted. Write down what
it is. How does the room look like? What do you see on
your left?on your right? What do you feel? What are your
thoughts?

Describe your state of mind. Write down the color, shape
or object that you would associate with your state of
mind.

Now, look at the ceiling. Recall your phone number and
say it backwards. This is called a break state from your
memories and train of thought to bring you back to the
present moment.

Think of the words you used to describe your memories
and the color and compare them with your present state
of mind. Is it the same or do you feel different?

Close your eyes and try to recall the memories and
compare them with how you feel now.

Now, look at the ceiling again and try to recall the pin
code of your bank account. Write it down and email it to
me. Of course, I am just kidding.
I just wanted to ensure that you are back in the moment
and alert. When your mind is preoccupied by worry,
anger or fear, your reactions are naturally not the most
appropriate.

Now, write down again how you feel. What number would
you give your feelings on a scale of 0-10? Does it match
the earlier number?

The point of this exercise is to demonstrate how your
feelings shift when you focus on the past and when you
come back to the present.

You will realize that you are open to new things and you
have the willingness and the energy to achieve. The
more you do this exercise, the more you will become
aware of your emotions and learn to channel your
thoughts in the right direction.

Recall past events and then fast forward to the present.
Move between positive and negative experiences and
see its effect to your awareness and thoughts. Do this
every day for a week or so and you will see how easy it is
to go from a negative state of mind to a positive one.
                       CHAPTER EIGHT

                   What is energy?
       “Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can
       only be changed from one form to another.” ~
                      Albert Einstein

To get somewhere, you have to start somewhere.
Let’s try to define energy.

A common enough dictionary meaning or definition says:
The capacity for work or vigorous activity.

I doubt you would disagree with the above definition. It’s
simple and easily understood. However let’s dive a little
deeper and examine energy in its different forms.

Energy is all around us. Energy is in everything. What are
some of the different types of energy? Take for instance,
kinetic energy which is the energy produced when
something moves or electrical energy which is a result of
the electrons in an object vibrating to produce electrical
current or an electrical field.

Where does energy come from?
We can derive energy from different sources. Energy
from the sun is solar energy. Energy can be produced
from wind or water. Energy is produced from heat or
combustion when fuel is burned. Energy is derived from
electromagnetic waves or nuclear reactions.

Light energy involves packets of energy called photons
which produce brightness when they are charged. So a
bright light which makes you shield your eyes contains
high energy while a dim light contains low energy.

Now, we can understand different energy levels.

According to the law of thermodynamics, energy is never
destroyed but merely transferred from one object to
another or one place to another. Energy is merely
converted when it is used and never destroyed; for
instance, solar energy is converted to electrical energy
to light energy or heat energy and so on.

Why do we need energy?
You may have heard of calories or have seen them on
the packages of processed food items. When we eat, we
are said to ‘consume’ calories and when we exercise, we
are said to ‘burn’ calories. Simply put, our body requires
nutrition to be alive and energetic. Food nourishes our
body; the nutrients in the food we eat are stored as
energy within our body.

Everything that has mass has energy but this energy is
just potential energy. Energy that is merely stored but
not used is meaningless.

Every cell in your body contains energy, be it muscle or
fat. You absorb, convert and store energy, then we
expend or release it. The cycle continues. It has to go on
and on. Otherwise, you are in trouble.

Why do we grow fat and lazy?
Consider energy as something that is constantly flowing
and something that has to continuously move.

Take the air we breathe for instance. When you step into
a room that has been closed for many days, you will get
a whiff of staleness and feel that the air inside is ‘stuffy’
or heavy. Why? The air has not been circulating because
there has been no room for it to do so. There is neither
an inlet for fresh air to come in nor an outlet for stale air
to go out.

If you do not eat nutritious food, you are not letting in
enough energy so you feel lethargic or you tire easily.
When you eat, you absorb and store energy and if you
don’t use it up, the body converts it to fat and stores it.
This is what makes you fat.

You have to let in energy by eating right and use that
energy by exercising or doing work which converts the
energy and releases it.

Some people like to collect books. The books sitting on
the bookshelves are worth something, or so they think.
They gather dust and get eaten by worms. The books by
themselves are worthless. It’s the information, the
knowledge and the wisdom that is contained within the
books that have value. Even if someone reads a book but
does not understand it or even if someone does but does
not apply it to life, it is still worthless, isn’t it?

Eating nutritious food is like collecting books. It’s
pointless if you don’t convert the energy that you
collected by doing some work or exercising. In other
words, you don’t get to achieve something worthwhile.

By the way, this book is worthless to someone who
doesn’t read it or reads it but doesn’t apply what he or
she learns from it. However, this book can change
someone’s life if they use the information in it wisely. I
hope you do so.

Now, let’s try to understand how energy is relevant to
achieving success.

Archimedes, one of the world’s greatest inventors, is
believed to have said, “Give me a place to stand and I
will move the earth.”

Was he metaphorically speaking when he said those
words?

No. He literally meant what he said. He was talking about
the lever, one of the most important and oft used
mechanical devices since the Renaissance.

The principle of the lever is used even to this day in big
cranes on construction sites and in industrial warehouses
to move heavy objects. It is also used in precision
surgical instruments as well as in scissors and tweezers.

What Archimedes meant was that using the principle of
the lever, you can move even the earth – that is, if you
have the right tools.

Hypothetically, if you used a lever long enough, you can
convert the potential energy of enough mass and force
into kinetic energy to move an object as big as the earth.

Well, it’s important to do something.
Have you heard of the 10,000 hours of practice that is
required to master a skill?

In his book, Outliers, Malcolm Gladwell mentions that to
realize one’s best output or performance, you need to
repeatedly do something. In other words, practice.

When you practice, you gain experience. To gain
experience is to realize that there is a method of doing
something right and it comes only by making mistakes
and learning from them.

What about talent?
Talent is important but it is often overrated. Consider
running for instance. Everyone can run, but in a race,
only one person comes first. Who is that person? He or
she is the one who has practiced the most.

This applies to playing golf or playing the violin, to
writing computer codes or writing travelogues, or to
painting masterpieces or painting houses.
Discovering your talent is about discovering who you are
and what you want to do. No one but you can define and
decide what your purpose in life is.

Once you realize what it is, never let go until you
become a master at it. There is no such thing as
overnight success. Success, invariably, always is the
result of years of practice.

Enjoy what you do.
However, if you try to do something that you don’t really
enjoy doing, then you will not succeed, no matter how
much effort and time you put into it. This is why it’s
important to figure out who you are and what you want
to do from the very beginning: that is, to discover your
talent or purpose in life or your true calling, whatever
you want to call it.

You should be happy even before you are successful. In
fact, you should be happy doing what you do to achieve
success.
