**Fair Credit Reporting Act - Tell Me about This Organization**

The Fair Credit Reporting Act is a United States federal law that was
enacted by Congress to regulate the collection and use of credit and
other financial information. The Fair Credit Reporting Act also oversees
practices the three major credit reporting bureaus, plus several smaller
reporting agencies.

The information overseen by this law and garnered by these agencies
include (but are not limited to) credit account history, loan account
history, medical records or payments, residential or tenant history,
check writing history, and insurance claims.

The Fair Credit Reporting Act also ensures that each individual has
appropriate financial privacy and is not denied for any unreasonable
piece of credit history. This is also the same act that allows
individuals to obtain a free copy of their credit report from each of
the three major bureaus per year. In addition, after any sort of denial
of credit based on reporting history, an individual has the right to
review their report for free.

Any agency that willfully does not comply with the Fair Credit Reporting
Act can be fined severely or even sued. The United States Government
takes the terms and conditions of the FCRA very seriously, and any
allegations of misconduct are thoroughly investigated.

While these are just the basic pieces of the scope in which the FCRA
upholds, the overall reason for this law is consumer protection. Check
online or with your local credit expert for further details on the FCRA,
Now that you are empowered with new information, use the FCRA to your
advantage when the collectors or the credit bureau violate the law.
