Using a DBA for “Legacy AI” vs. Forming a New LLC
Launching “Legacy AI” as a Doing Business As (DBA) under your existing Wyoming company (Legacy
Platform Holdings LLC) is a common approach. It can allow you to operate under the Legacy AI brand
without creating a new legal entity. Below, we confirm that this DBA setup is legally sufficient for your needs
and discuss whether it’s the best structure given your plans. We also outline limitations, risks, and best
practices when using a DBA versus a separate LLC.


Operating the "Legacy AI" Brand Under a DBA
Using Legacy AI as your public brand – after filing a DBA (also called a Trade Name in Wyoming) – is
generally legally sufficient for running your business. A DBA simply lets your LLC do business under a
different name. Many companies use DBAs to create distinct brand identities while keeping one legal entity
  1 . In fact, it’s common for one LLC or corporation to have multiple DBAs for different product lines or

demographics 1 . Once you have registered “Legacy AI” as a trade name for your LLC, you can:


     • Launch and market under the “Legacy AI” name: You can operate your website, Circle.so
       community, social media, and marketing materials with the Legacy AI branding. Wyoming law
       doesn’t even require prior registration to start using a trade name – you’re free to use the name on
       websites, business cards, invoices, etc. as soon as you want 2 . (Of course, filing the trade name is
       wise for legal clarity and public record.) Customers and the public can interact with “Legacy AI” as
       your brand, even though the legal entity behind it remains Legacy Platform Holdings LLC.


     • Present Legacy AI on platforms and communications: Online platforms (like Circle.so, payment
       gateways, social media, and email) typically allow you to display a business name that isn’t your legal
       name. After the DBA is filed, Legacy AI becomes the company’s public face 3 . You can list “Legacy
       AI” as your business name in profiles, email footers, and social accounts. Just ensure anywhere it
       matters (e.g. legal disclaimers or terms of service) you somewhere note the connection to the LLC
       (for example, “Legacy AI is a brand of Legacy Platform Holdings LLC”) for complete transparency.


Accepting Payments Under the Name "Legacy AI"
One big reason to file a DBA is so you can collect payments and handle finances under the brand name.
Financial institutions and payment processors need a clear link between your public name and the legal
entity. Thankfully, once your DBA is on file, you can set up accounts to receive money as Legacy AI. In
practice:


     • Bank Accounts and Payment Processors: Banks, Stripe, PayPal, and others will typically require
       your DBA documentation to use "Legacy AI" as the name on accounts or customer charges 4 . For
       example, Stripe explicitly requires that your bank account be under the company’s legal name or its
       DBA name 5 . This means you can configure Stripe/PayPal to show “Legacy AI” to customers and on

      • Checks and Merchant Services: With a DBA, your bank can allow checks made out to Legacy AI.
        Merchant service providers and credit card processors also often ask for the DBA certificate before
        letting you use the name for transactions 4 . Keeping that documentation handy will smooth out

In short, collecting revenue under the name “Legacy AI” is completely feasible with a DBA in place. Just
be prepared to show that Legacy AI is an authorized alias of your LLC when setting up financial accounts 4 .


Contracts and Legal Documents as "Legacy AI"
You can sign contracts and offer services under the Legacy AI name via the DBA. Legally, the party to the
contract is still your LLC, but you’ll be using the DBA to represent it. It’s important to do this correctly to
maintain clarity and enforceability:


      • Signing Contracts: Best practice is to sign as “Legacy Platform Holdings LLC d/b/a (doing
        business as) Legacy AI”. Including both the legal name and the DBA ensures the contract clearly
        binds the LLC operating under the Legacy AI name 6 7 . For example, the signature line could
        read:

Legacy Platform Holdings LLC, d/b/a Legacy AI, by __[Your Name]__, Title: ____.


This makes it explicit that Legacy AI is not a separate entity, but the contract is with your LLC using that trade
name. Using the correct legal name in contracts (with the DBA) avoids any confusion or disputes about who
the parties are 8 . (If a contract were accidentally made out only to “Legacy AI” without referencing the
LLC, it’s usually still enforceable if intent is clear, but it’s safer to get the names right from the start 9 .)


      • Offering Services and Products: You can advertise and sell your educational or financial literacy
        products under the Legacy AI brand freely. The DBA gives you the legal right to represent your
        business as "Legacy AI" in offers, marketing, and materials. Just remember that any official
        paperwork (invoices, disclaimers, service agreements, etc.) should somewhere mention the legal
        name or the DBA filing where appropriate. For instance, invoices might have Legacy AI in the
        letterhead but could note in the footer “Legacy Platform Holdings LLC, doing business as Legacy AI.”
        This keeps everything transparent.


      • Public Compliance: In most cases, just using the DBA name outwardly is fine once registered. Some
        states require a fictitious name notice in a local newspaper (e.g. California, or for foreign entities in
        certain states) 10 , but Wyoming itself does not have a publication requirement for a trade name.
        You’ve already taken the step of “filing a DBA”, which typically means you’ve registered the trade
        name with the Wyoming Secretary of State. That filing itself puts the public on notice of the
        connection. So, signing contracts and doing business as Legacy AI is legally permitted and common
        practice, as long as the DBA is properly registered and disclosed 10 7 .

Avoiding a Separate LLC – Is the DBA Enough?
Using a DBA under your holding company avoids the need to form a separate LLC for "Legacy AI" right
now. This is legally acceptable and often a smart, cost-saving move for a new venture. Here’s why a DBA can
be sufficient in lieu of a new LLC:


     • Single Legal Entity, Multiple Brands: Your existing LLC can house multiple projects or brands.
       Setting up “Legacy AI” as a trade name is essentially giving your one company a second name to
       do business with. You won’t have to file new incorporation documents, obtain a separate EIN, or
       manage an additional annual report for a new LLC. As one source puts it, “you can create a
       corporation (or LLC) with many DBAs … so you don't have to create another corporation”, which saves
       time and money 11 . Many small businesses start this way – “small businesses can get by with a DBA”
       when the burden of creating multiple entities isn’t justified yet 12 .


     • Legal Compliance Across the U.S.: Simply operating under a DBA name nationwide is generally fine
       as long as your base company is properly registered in its home state and you’re not running afoul
       of other states’ rules. There is no federal requirement to register a DBA beyond state/local filings.
       Since you formed the LLC in Wyoming, that state’s DBA registration covers your use of Legacy AI in
       Wyoming. If you are doing business entirely online, you typically do not need to separately
       register in every state where customers reside. Your Wyoming LLC (Legacy Platform Holdings
       LLC) can transact across state lines. However, be mindful of a couple of points about multi-state
       operations:


     • If you establish a physical presence or office in another state, or have employees in other states,
       you may need to foreign-register your LLC in those states (i.e. register as a foreign company
       there) to be in full compliance 13 . Each state has its own threshold for what constitutes “doing
       business” requiring registration. Simply selling online to customers across the U.S. usually doesn’t
       trigger this, but running the business from your home state could. (For example, if you as the
       owner are based in New York and actively operating the business from New York, technically New
       York might expect a foreign LLC registration and a local DBA filing for the name there.)


     • If you plan events, workshops, or significant activity under the Legacy AI name in certain locales,
       check those local laws. Some states require a DBA (fictitious name) filing at the county or state level
       where the business is conducted 10 14 . (A few states don’t require DBA registration at all, but many
       do.) Since Wyoming’s trade name registration is not mandatory but you’re doing it, you’re covering
       the base in that state. For full U.S. compliance, just remember: register the DBA in any new state
       where you establish a real presence or where required by local law. As a rule of thumb, the name
       should be registered wherever you’re “doing business” under that name, to avoid any fines or
       issues enforcing contracts in that state 15 . In practice, many online-only businesses focus on the
       home state, but keep this in mind as you expand.


Bottom line: You do not need a separate LLC for “Legacy AI” at launch. Using the DBA is legally sufficient
to operate and transact under that name nationwide, given the caveats above. You’ll be in compliance so
long as you’ve filed the DBA properly and adhere to any state-specific requirements as you grow.

Suitability of the DBA Structure for Your 30–60 Day Launch
Given your plans – a quick launch, using platforms like Circle.so under the Legacy AI brand, and even
introducing sub-brands – sticking with the DBA under your holding LLC is likely the best structure for now.
Here’s why it fits your situation and goals:


     • Speed and Simplicity: You intend to launch within 30–60 days. Forming a brand-new LLC (or
       multiple LLCs for sub-brands) would add paperwork, costs, and time. In contrast, a DBA registration
       is relatively quick and simple. You can even start building your online presence as Legacy AI
       immediately (Wyoming allows use of the name prior to formal registration) 2 . There’s no delay
       waiting for a new LLC approval; you leverage the existing company.


     • Using Circle.so and Other Platforms: These platforms will let you create communities or sites
       under whatever name you choose. Since Legacy AI is a DBA of your LLC, you won’t run into issues
       connecting that platform to your business accounts. Just make sure in the platform’s settings or
       terms, the official owner is your LLC (which it will be, since the LLC is the one entering any platform
       agreements, even if the community is named Legacy AI). This ensures legal clarity – the users see
       Legacy AI, but the platform’s contract is with Legacy Platform Holdings LLC (dba Legacy AI). This is a
       common scenario and is normally seamless.


     • Adding Sub-Brands under Legacy AI: Your plan to have sub-brands like Restore, Acadacom, Capitalist,
       etc. under the Legacy AI umbrella is achievable with the current setup. There are a couple of ways to
       handle this structurally:


     • If those sub-brands are more like product or program names offered by Legacy AI, you may not
       need separate DBAs for each; you can simply market them as initiatives or product lines (for
       example, “Legacy AI Restore Program” or “Acadacom by Legacy AI”). Since Legacy AI itself is the main
       brand (and it’s a DBA), additional product names don’t require separate registration as long as you
       don’t present them as independent businesses.
     • If those sub-brands will have distinct public identities or websites without always being attached
       to the Legacy AI name, you might consider registering each as a DBA as well (either directly under
       the holding LLC, or even under Legacy AI if you later spin it off). Fortunately, one LLC can have
       multiple DBA names 16 . You would just file separate trade name registrations for each name you
       plan to use. This still keeps everything under one legal entity, but lets you legally use each name.
       Many entrepreneurs choose this route to keep branding flexible. Just remember each DBA is a one-
       time (or periodic) filing with a small fee.

Using a single LLC with multiple DBAs provides scalability for branding – you can launch new sub-projects
under new names without setting up a new company each time. It’s cost-effective while you test and grow
these ideas. As the Wyoming trade name rules note, a DBA is really just a different name for an existing
business, not a new entity 17 . This means your holding LLC remains the anchor for all these sub-brands,
which keeps management simpler at the early stage.


     • Future Trademark and Standalone Entity Plans: You mentioned eventually filing trademarks or
       even converting Legacy AI into its own entity down the road. Starting with the DBA does not hinder
       these future moves. In fact, it can be a stepping stone:

       validated the business, rather than creating multiple LLCs from day one for unproven ideas.
     • Because you already named your main LLC as a “Holdings” company, it implies it’s meant to hold or

       umbrella for Legacy AI (and potentially others). In the long run, you maintain the flexibility either to
       keep everything under the one umbrella or to have the holding company own new subsidiaries if
       needed. Nothing in the DBA approach locks you in or prevents scaling; it just avoids premature
       complexity.

In summary, for a fast launch and multi-brand approach, using the DBA under the holding LLC is likely
the optimal choice at this stage. It provides the legal sufficiency, flexibility, and speed you need, while
preserving the option to evolve your structure as the business grows.


Limitations and Risks of a DBA vs a Separate LLC
While a DBA under an existing LLC is very useful, you should be aware of its limitations and potential
risks compared to setting up a separate LLC for Legacy AI. Here are the key considerations:


     • No Additional Liability Shield: A DBA does not create a new legal entity – it’s only a name. This
       means all liabilities of the Legacy AI business are liabilities of Legacy Platform Holdings LLC. You do
       have the protection of the LLC for your personal assets (that’s good), but Legacy AI doesn’t have its
       own shield separate from the holding company. If someone were to sue “Legacy AI” for any
       reason, legally they are suing your LLC (since the DBA has no independent legal status) 18 . By
       contrast, if Legacy AI were a separate LLC, any legal troubles might be contained to that entity alone,
       protecting the holding company’s other assets or ventures.

Risk management: To mitigate this, continue to run all your brands with care for liability – use proper
contracts, disclaimers (especially since you mention financial literacy products), and insurance as needed. If
one of your sub-brands (say Capitalist if it deals with sensitive financial info) seems to carry high risk, you
might in the future isolate it in its own LLC. For now, all operations under the holding company are in “one
basket,” so maintain good liability insurance coverage across the board.


     • Shared Financial and Legal Exposure: Because it’s one LLC, the finances are commingled (not in
       the sense of bookkeeping, but in legal responsibility). Debts or obligations incurred under the
       name Legacy AI are the LLC’s obligations. If Legacy AI signs a big contract and can’t fulfill it, the LLC is
       on the hook. Likewise, any other business assets in the LLC (if you have other ventures in it) are

      technically reachable by creditors of Legacy AI. A separate LLC would silo those obligations. Ensure
      that you keep clear internal records for each brand (income, expenses) and don’t mix personal
      funds, so your LLC’s veil stays intact and it’s clear these DBAs are just operating divisions of the LLC.


     • Name Protection and Branding: Registering a DBA or trade name does not give you exclusive
       rights to that name beyond your local jurisdiction. Another company could legally form an LLC or
       DBA named “Legacy AI” in another state, for example, if no trademark exists. The first user of a name
       in commerce has some common law rights 14 , but those can be limited. If Legacy AI is core to your
       brand, pursuing a trademark registration is important for broader protection. Also consider
       securing domain names and social handles for sub-brands early, since DBAs won’t prevent others
       from grabbing those. Essentially, think of the DBA as allowing you to use the name legally, but think
       of a trademark as how you protect the name in the market.


     • Administrative Maintenance: Each DBA may require renewal every few years (Wyoming trade
       names last 10 years before renewal 19 ). Ensure you renew on time to keep using the name. Also, if
       your business expands physically into new states, you’ll have to file the DBA in those locales as
       mentioned. It’s not a huge burden, but it’s another task to monitor. In contrast, a separate LLC would
       also have its own annual reports and fees – so either way there are compliance tasks, but a DBA
       tends to be simpler (and cheaper).


     • Perception and Credibility: Generally, customers won’t know the difference and Legacy AI will
       appear as a standalone brand. However, in certain B2B contexts or with more savvy partners, they’ll
       realize Legacy AI is not an independent company when they see contracts or invoices with the
       holding LLC name. This is usually fine, but occasionally a counterparty might insist on dealing with
       the “actual owner” name. As long as you can explain “Legacy AI is just our trade name; the company
       is Legacy Platform Holdings LLC,” it should be okay. Some entrepreneurs feel that having a dedicated
       LLC per venture looks more professional, but realistically a DBA under a solid company is very
       normal. If you do encounter any hesitation, just provide the DBA documentation to show the
       legitimacy.


     • Complexity if Investors or Partners Join: If you foresee taking on a partner or investor specific to
       the Legacy AI project (and not the holding company’s other ventures), the DBA structure can
       complicate things. They would have to buy into the holding LLC or you’d have to carve out that line of
       business somehow, since it’s not a separate entity. If that scenario is likely soon, you might plan to
       form a new LLC for Legacy AI sooner rather than later. On the other hand, if this is a mostly self-
       funded initiative for now, the DBA under your 100% owned LLC is perfectly fine. As mentioned, you
       can always spin it off to a new entity when needed.


Best Practices for Operating with a DBA
To ensure legal clarity and smooth operations while using the Legacy AI DBA, keep these best practices in
mind:


     • Always Link the DBA to the LLC in Formal Contexts: On contracts, legal notices, or any official
       paperwork, use the format “Legacy Platform Holdings LLC d/b/a Legacy AI” 6 . This makes it clear
       that the LLC is the legal party behind the name. It helps maintain your liability protection (by not
       accidentally acting as an individual or nonexistent entity) and avoids confusion.



                                                      6
     • Keep DBA Documentation Handy: Have your Wyoming trade name registration certificate
       readily available 4 . Banks, payment processors, new vendors, or even some clients might ask for
       proof that Legacy AI is a registered alias of your company. Providing this quickly will establish
       confidence and allow you to, for example, open a bank account in the Legacy AI name or set up
       merchant accounts without hassle 4 .


     • Use the DBA consistently in marketing: Use “Legacy AI” for all branding to build its identity, but
       don’t hide the legal entity in places where it matters (such as the website’s terms of service or
       business registrations). Consistency in using the name will also help with establishing trademark
       rights (through continuous use in commerce).


     • Monitor Your DBA and Names: Since you plan multiple sub-brands, keep a simple log of which
       names are officially filed as DBAs and where. Each time you create a new public-facing name, ask: do
       I need to register this one? If it’s just a product under Legacy AI, maybe not. If it becomes an
       independent-facing brand, then yes. Staying organized will maintain legal clarity as you expand
       your brand portfolio.


     • Plan for Evolutions: Periodically reassess if the DBA structure is still serving you. If Legacy AI or any
       sub-brand grows significantly, consider whether formal separation is beneficial at that point. This
       could be due to liability isolation, tax reasons, or investor requirements. There’s no need to change
       anything now, just have it on your radar for the future. It’s easier to transition from one LLC with
       DBAs to multiple LLCs when you’ve kept good records and treated each brand professionally (even if
       not separate on paper).


Conclusion
Filing a DBA for "Legacy AI" under Legacy Platform Holdings LLC is legally sufficient to do everything
on your list: launch and operate the online brand, use the name publicly on platforms and marketing,
accept payments through Stripe/PayPal under the name, sign contracts and offer services as Legacy AI, all
while avoiding the hassle of forming a new LLC. Essentially, the law recognizes Legacy AI as just another
name for your Wyoming LLC, which gives you flexibility to present your business how you like.


This approach is often the best structure for a fast launch with multiple brand ideas. It keeps things
simple and scalable – you can add sub-brands under the same company and remain in compliance across
the U.S., as long as you register any assumed names in the appropriate jurisdictions and follow general
business regulations. Many entrepreneurs use one holding company with multiple DBAs to save costs and
streamline operations 20 .


That said, remember that a DBA does not create a new entity or additional liability protection 21 . All the
ventures under the LLC rise and fall together from a legal standpoint. As you grow, take steps to manage
that risk (insurance, contracts in proper name, possible future entity spinoffs). Also, look into trademarking
"Legacy AI" to safeguard your brand as you expand – the DBA lets you use the name, but a trademark will
help you protect it exclusively.
