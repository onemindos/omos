Things To Be Aware Of In Linking

1) Robots.txt file 

Did you know that in the root directory of every website there is a robot.txt file that tells the search engine spiders where to go or not go?  This file can tell search engine robots to not follow your link.  After a new link exchange wait a few days (and on a regular basis if your suspicious), to check it out. Most of the time you can view it by going to 

 HYPERLINK "http://www.theirdomain.com/robots.txt" http://www.theirdomain.com/robots.txt and substituting “theirdomain.com” for the actual domain name of their site.

You can also change your user agent for your browser to be "Googlebot", and if you don't see your link on their site, neither do the search engines.  This is a little complicated to do – so you should really do a search and get some expert advice before you try it.

2) Hyperlinks

Your hyperlink was prevented from being followed by the search engines.  This is pretty easy to check.  All you have to do is view the page source.  Look for this type of code: <a href="http://www.yoursite.com" rel=nofollow">Your site name</a>

Of course, “yoursite.com” and your site name would be the link to your site. The "nofollow" part tells the search engines NOT to follow your link to your site.  If this is included in the source code, you won't be able to get a PR credit for that link.

3) Javascript 

Usually javascript is not spidered by Google (at the time of this writing).  If your link is inside java code you are probably wasting your time as your site will not be “seen” by the search engine spiders.

4) Inline Frames  

Inline frames are other websites "nested" inside a website.  You can usually tell they are there because they have a scroll bar, although they don't always.  View the page source and look for tell tale signs of an inline frame.

5)  Link Removal
