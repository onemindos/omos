Positive thinking refers to a powerful mental attitude that uses images,
words, and thoughts that are conducive to achieving personal growth and
success. With this way of thinking, you can continue expecting good results
no matter what life throws at you. A positive mind that constantly
anticipates joy, happiness, better health, and successful outcomes can also
take you a few steps closer to achieving your life goals. A person who
practices positive thinking will believe in one thing – whatever the mind
expects, it surely finds.

While positive thinking has started growing in popularity, not everyone
accepts its basic tenets and principles. Others consider it as plain nonsense
and scoff at other people who believe and accept the revolutionary ideas it
suggests. However, the growing number of books and courses about
positive thinking imply that more and more people are becoming attracted
to its principles. As this subject continues gaining popularity, it has also
changed the lives of thousands of people who wish to become better
individuals in every possible way.

Hearing people say “Think positive!” is quite common. If you have ever felt
downhearted and miserable, your friends might have told you to do the
same thing. Most people who do not take this advice seriously are usually
unaware of what it really means. They tend to forget the fact that positive
thinking can actually be very effective and useful. Try asking yourself one

                    The Power Of Positive Thinking


Here is the perfect scenario that demonstrates how powerful positive
thinking can be. John Doe applied for a new job. However, he has a very
low self-esteem and he constantly considers himself as a failure. This makes
him feel as if he were unworthy of success. All these thoughts made him
quite sure that he was not going to qualify for the job he applied for. His
negative attitude tricked his mind into believing that other applicants were
far better and more qualified for the job than he is.

John Doe got this attitude because of the negative experiences he had
during his previous job interviews. His mind was always full of irrational
fears and negative thoughts even before the date of the job interview
arrives. Things were uncertain but there was one thing he was sure of – he
would be rejected by the company he wants to work for. On the day of the
interview, he woke up late and realized that the shirt he wanted to wear was
still dirty. He ended up wearing a shirt full of unsightly wrinkles.

When he arrived at the office, he felt very nervous and tense. He kept
worrying about the littlest things during the interview so he started
displaying a less-than-likeable attitude. He did not even have enough time
to grab a quick breakfast so he felt really hungry and distracted throughout
the interview. These series of unfortunate events made it impossible for
him to focus on the task at hand. In the end, his actions made a bad
impression and materialized his great fear of walking out of the interview
without getting the job he really wanted.

Robert applied for the same position but he approached the pressing matter
in a very different way. Even before he passed his curriculum vitae, he was
certain that he will qualify for the position. A week before the interview, he
imagined himself impressing the human resources manager and getting the
rewarding job that he wanted. The night before the interview, he managed
to prepare his clothes and sleep quite earlier than he used to. The next day,
he woke up early and had ample time to eat a hearty breakfast and arrive
right in time for the interview.

              Positive Thinking Vs Positive Psychology


While positive psychology and positive thinking are terms that are used
interchangeably, you have to understand that they are completely different
from each other. For one, positive thinking is all about looking at all things
in a positive light. On the other hand, positive psychology also places an
emphasis on optimism but it also reminds people about the many benefits
of thinking positively. Sometimes, positive psychology also believes that
thinking in a realistic manner is more beneficial than seeing all things in a
positive light.

To put simply, positive psychology believes that negative thinking may lead
to better outcomes and more accurate decisions in some situations. Some
proponents of positive psychology also found out that optimistic thinking
may force you to underestimate the actual risks that might be involved in
making particular decisions. On the other hand, positive thinking places a
priority in recognizing the silver lining in every dark cloud. Once you have
read more about this subject tried its principles, you can finally take control
of your life and be whatever you want to be.

                 Optimism And Your Physical Health

If living a longer life is on the top of your priority list, you should become
more familiar with the basic principles of optimism. This mental attitude
has long been linked to the longevity of life. During the 1960s, psychologists
examined whether certain explanatory styles may serve as risk factors to
premature death. They gathered data by studying a longitudinal sample and
categorizing subjects as pessimistic, optimistic, or a mix of both. To add
more depth to the study, they also operationalized optimism with the use of
Minnesota’s Multiphasic Personality Inventory.

The researchers were surprised to discover that every ten-point increase in
a subject’s score on the scale of optimism experienced 19% fewer risks of
dying at an early age. Smokers and non-smokers who were of average
health also had 5 to 10% fewer chances of experiencing sudden death. This
study proved how massive the protective effect of living in positivity could
be. Multiple studies have also demonstrated the role of optimism in helping
people recover faster from illnesses and diseases. This is especially true in
the case of optimists who are being treated for cancer.

Optimists have a strong fighting spirit that allows them to experience less
stress and anxiety after being diagnosed with life-threatening diseases. It
also leads to fewer disruptions in normal life due to its ability to fight
against fatigue and distress. Scientific evidence also reinforces the belief
that optimism can protect people against chronic diseases. In a research
study, middle-aged women were tested for signs of atherosclerosis. Patients
who had higher levels of pessimism were found to be more likely to develop
the disease than those who keep a positive outlook towards life.

Optimism will also have a great effect on your immune system. Young
adults who received immunization for influenza signed up as subjects for a
particular study. Two weeks after being immunized, their response to the

antibodies so their immune system was significantly stronger. Multiple
studies that investigated the relationship between disease progression and
optimism in people infected with HIV also discovered that both factors are
linearly related. Subjects who exhibited greater optimism suppressed the
viral load better than pessimistic patients did.

Sephton and Segerstrom also conducted a study to examine the link
between optimism and the function of the immune system. Students who
are in the first year of law school were asked to participate in the said study.
Those who exhibit dispositional optimism, which is the tendency to view
life in a positive light, were assed together with the measures of negative
and positive affect. The researchers found out that optimism can actually
predict better cell-mediated immunity, which is of course an important
aspect of the immune system’s ability to protect the body from infectious
agents.

All of these studies suggest that optimism is not only great for
strengthening the immune system but also for helping people recover faster
from illnesses despite their compromised immune systems. Additional
studies were also conducted to investigate how optimism affects health-
related behaviors. One study discovered that high levels of optimism can
protect people from drinking problems, especially if alcoholism runs in
their family. Since genetics is one of the biggest risk factors for developing
any form of substance dependence, the protective effect of optimism
against its influence has become very important for public health efforts.

Aside from preventing alcohol dependence, optimism was also found to
help subjects break their bad habits. A study conducted by Blaney, Carver,
and Strack shows how optimism can result in greater success when it comes
to the treatment of alcohol abuse. Optimists are more likely to abstain from
alcohol and remain in treatment than pessimists. Pregnant women who
exhibit higher degrees of optimism are also less likely to use substances
during their pregnancy. These studies prove that optimists who engage in
health-related behaviors or choose to quit vices tend to experience greater
chances of success.

              Optimism And Your Psychological Health

Recent evidence also suggests that positive thinking is very important in
helping people cope with life’s biggest challenges. It has been associated
with better responses to various difficulties that may be as simple as leaving
home for college or as complicated as coping with the aftereffects of war.
Aside from playing a protective role, optimism has also been found to
correlate positively with self-esteem and life satisfaction. Optimists who
always see the good in all things tends to feel better about themselves so
they end up happy, contented, and successful in life.

A study conducted in 2010 also tried to determine whether optimism can
really predict positive effects. Its hypothesis is that changes in levels of
optimism may predict changes in the effect of the subjects’ actions. It
proved that optimism is directly linked to increasing the likelihood of
positive results, and vice versa. However, changes in levels of optimism
were not found to be related to changes in negative affect. This proves that
optimism is related to positive affect so optimists end up happier with their
lives than pessimists do.

Optimists also recover from disappointments faster because they focus on
positive rather than negative outcomes. To provide this point, a study that
examines the level of pessimism and optimism in couples who were
undergoing in-vitro fertilization was conducted. Forty-one couples were
interviewed prior to the procedure and a few weeks after they have taken a
pregnancy test. Couples who received negative results coped better with
their failed attempts to conceive than pessimists did. They endorsed
positive thoughts about how the experience strengthened their relationship
so they easily coped with the disappointing turn of events.

                 Can Pessimists Turn Into Optimists?

Martin Seligman, the proponent of positive psychology, jumpstarted his
career by conducting studies about stress, anxiety, and depression. His
work in these subjects led him to discover the optimistic explanatory styles
that people use to make sense of bad events. After conducting a series of
longitudinal studies, he concluded that positive thoughts can protect people
from developing depression every time they encounter difficult
circumstances. This kept him motivated towards understanding whether
people can learn optimism or not. He conducted multiple studies on
varying explanatory styles and discovered that optimism could be learned.

This series of research studies gave birth to the Penn Resiliency Program,
which is a comprehensive curriculum that attempts to inculcate the
optimistic explanatory style in the minds of young children. They were
selected as the subjects for the pilot study because their personalities and
attitudes are more malleable than that of adults. This means that they
represent the perfect subjects for testing the hypothesis that certain
interventions can modify the personality of a person. The curriculum
believes that optimism can protect young people from experiencing the
devastating symptoms of depression in the future.

The Penn Resiliency Program depended on licensed counselors and
professional teachers for administering twelve different sessions of
intervention. All these courses aim to teach students how they can change
thoughts that are associated with the pessimistic explanatory style. Multiple
studies were also conducted to evaluate the efficiency of the program. A
study that observed the use of PRP in changing the personality of 700
students found out that only 20% of them experienced symptoms of
depression three years after the program. This evidence strongly suggests
