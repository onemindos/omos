# **What is bulimia nervosa?**

Bulimia nervosa, sometimes shortened just to bulimia, is an eating
disorder and a mental health related condition. Typically people with
bulimia nervosa will binge eat a significant amount of food. The amount
people binge eat can differ from person to person but it is defined as
consuming more than a normal amount of food in typically a 2 hour
period. Typically this binge will involve convenient, high calorie or
fatty food.

During this binge, the sufferer of the eating disorder will feel
completely out of control. They will then feel that they have to make up
for this binge and the number of calories they have consumed, which
commonly involves self induced vomiting after binging. This can feel
like symptoms of guilt, shame or self loathing for the large amount of
food consumed. The guilt creates a new wave of motivation to eat a
stricter diet because they feel they will be happier or more respected
by other people if they lose weight.

Other people may use laxatives, diuretics, excessive or compulsive
exercise, force themselves to starve or fast for a few days after to
feel like they are back to where they were before the binge. Almost like
resetting back to 0, where they were before the binge. Some people can
actually feel almost as if they are having an out of body experience, as
if they are completely out of control during the binge or purge process.

Additionally, it's important to know that purging can only get rid of a
maximum of 50% of the calories consumed, whilst typically it's much
less. Calorie absorption starts the minute food is consumed and entered
into the person's mouth. Laxatives only get rid of 10% of the calories,
whilst diuretics do not actually have any effect on calorie absorption.
Someone may weigh less after taking diuretics but this is primarily down
to losing weight, rather than true weight loss.

Typically someone may try to control their weight and the amount they
eat during the day by eating a minimal amount of low calorie foods. Then
later on during the day or in the evening, they feel they can longer
control their hunger and will begin to binge again.

## Cycle

The whole cycle of bulimia nervosa will repeat itself as a regular
behaviour, creating a difficult trap to escape without professional
support. It is absolutely essential that if you are dealing with an
eating disorder or know someone that is, to seek medical advice as
quickly as possible.

During this whole cycle, the person with the eating disorder will be
focused on their body shape throughout. They will feel like they are
very conscious about their body image, in the form of an unhealthy
obsession. This can typically be associated with depression, anxiety,
low self esteem, self harm and alcohol misuse.

This binge purge cycle can create an erratic fluctuation in weight. The
amount of food consumed can be related to emotional problems, as the
sufferer tries to numb their pain by eating excessive amounts of food.
Depression, stress and anxiety or even a slip in diet can lead to a
binge cycle.

# **What are the symptoms of Bulimia?**

Bulimia is very hard for others to spot and quite often remains hidden,
as sufferers are secretive about their behaviour and feel ashamed about
their problem. Their weight tends to remain the same because they binge
and purge in balance, maintaining a regular level of weight. This means
that people can not physically see if someone has a problem, or they
can't tell by looking at someone's body shape.

## Physical damage

Physical damage includes tears in the esophagus, weaken the heart
muscle, ulcers, intestinal problems irregular menstruation, hormone
imbalance and dehydration.

Stomach acids caused by purging can damage your teeth and it's enamel.
Dentists are often part of the diagnosis stage because they can identify
signs of excessive vomiting due to the negative physical effects it can
have on a person's mouth. Some people also have a calice mark on their
knuckle, sometimes referred to as a "russell sign' after a British
psychologist called Gerald Russell, which is caused by someone provoking
compulsive forced vomiting.

Purging can create a feeling of control due to this obsession of losing
weight. Warning signs include taking pills after eating to excessively
urinate or control bowel movements, fasting or exercising too much,
event if it hurts, going to the bathroom shortly after eating.

The most effective treatment for bulimia is cognitive behavioural
therapy, as this breaks the binge and purge cycle. The person almost has
to relearn how to eat whilst gaining new coping strategies for dealing
with stress or emotional anxiety. Some people may also take medication
such as anti depressants to help them cope with their depression which
accompanies bulimia.

## Psychological symptoms

People with bulimia often evaluate themselves based on their weight and
can be caused by very low self esteem, attaching a disproportionate
amount of importance on their shape and weight. They may also think
other people are constantly judging them on their weight, fueling their
motivation to control it. When they evaluate themselves, they often tend
to think they are more overweight or 'fatter' than they actually are,
irrelevant or independent to their actual real weight.

A sufferer may drink excessive water or diet drinks before after
finishing their meal, as it makes the purge cycle easier. In extreme
cases, there may be a cycle of excessive water intake, followed by
purging, until only clear water is left when the sufferer purges.

Excessive exercising may be caused by caused by guilt and a fear of
weight gain. As the sufferer aims to reduce and control their weight,
they may spend extreme amounts of time exercising or at the gym.

The person may be eating unusually large amounts without gaining weight
and then uses excuses with the people around them by saying they have a
fast metabolism or it's the first time they have eaten all day.
Sometimes sufferers will also shower after eating because this offers
the perfect opportunity to purge.

Sufferers may often eat in private. Although eating is usually a social
event, a bulimia sufferer may feel guilt, shame or even paranoia when
eating in front of others.

A bulimia sufferer may have swings in their eating habits. Emotionally,
they can often eat when they feel happy in their mood, whilst restrict
their intake of food when they feel down or self conscious about their
weight.

You may also find bite marks on a toothbrush. Some people may use a
toothbrush rather than their hands to induce vomiting. The gag reflex
actually forces the sufferer to bit down on their toothbrush, leaving
physical marks or scratches. Some people actually carry their toothbrush
and toothpaste when they go out, to freshen their breath whilst purging
when out of the house.

Bulimia sufferers may also be constantly talking about how they look,
often looking in the mirror and obsessing about their image or changing
their clothes before they are remotely satisfied with how they look.
Bulimia sufferers are often in a dark place emotionally, struggling with
anxiety, depression and emotional mood swings. A short temper with
emotional outburst and tears can indicate a problem as their emotions
are intense and painful.

This may be combined with secrecy and paranoia, due to the secrecy of
the condition. They may feel ashamed about what they are doing. They go
to great efforts to conceal their behaviours in case they are caught or
found out.

# **What causes bulimia?**

Unfortunately, the number of bulimia nervosa cases is increasing due to
society becoming very health conscious, with the common theme of
desiring an attractive body shape or to be thin.

Bulimia nervosa is actually ten times more common in women than men,
especially in adolescents. However, men are the fastest growing group of
bulimia sufferers due to becoming increasingly conscious of their body
shape. There are also many men, particularly in their middle ages, who
binge eat and feel out of control whilst binging but do not compensate
for this excessive amount of eating.

There is no single cause of bulimia and it may be caused a few different
factors. However, causes can include being scared of gaining weight or
being almost obsessed with losing weight.

## Dieting

Dieting can trigger an urge to overeat, as the restriction of food can
result in the feeling of shame and guilt. This then starts the binge
purge cycle. Dieting can trigger feelings of deprivation in your mind,
which makes you want to eat more. Some people have subconscious diet
restrictions, as in they want to restrict themselves of food in the
future, which subsequently creates binge urges.

Have you ever heard 'you want what you can't have'? This is the same
with dieting and food. As soon as you tell yourself you can't have
something, this thought of food may occur in your mind regularly.

Some hypotheses state there is a primal hunger urge built within
ourselves. When we restrict ourselves of food, our instincts crave
calories and remind us to eat. This has been stated to have been
developed over thousands of years of evolution, as when there was a
famine our instincts would remind us to be on the lookout for food all
the time. Your body does not know the difference between dieting and a
famine, so when you restrict your food you will look out for food all
the time.

## Psychological factors

If a sufferer of bulimia looked at the reasons why they are dieting,
this may be caused by negative core beliefs. These might be beliefs that
you hold so deeply they are fundamental influencers in the core
decisions you make in your life. For example, looking at what causes you
to act the way you do, specifically in what you eat, how much food you
consume, when you diet etc. This could include negative core beliefs
such as 'I need to be thin to be successful' or 'I need to be thin to be
popular'. These are major influencers in why people start dieting and
can start from a very young age.

Psychological factors such as low self esteem, a difficulty in
controlling compulsive behaviours, depression, self harm or trouble with
managing anger can influence the likelihood of suffering with bulimia.
Stressful episodes, traumatic events can cause bulimia.

It's important to look at your negative core beliefs and once you
understand any dysfunctional thought patterns, you can modify them and
look at healthier alternatives that you could develop. This could
include more sustainable and realistic beliefs such as 'despite my body
size, I will always be loved and accepted' or 'no matter how much I
weigh, I will still have friends and be successful'. Every time you feel
you are influenced by your negative core belief, you can search for
evidence to back this up and actually realise there is no true evidence
to validate the negative thought pattern. Subsequently, you will see
this is an unrealistic negative thought pattern.

Changing core negative beliefs does take a lot of persistence and
repetition, however, if you use both then you will see changes in your
thought patterns and change the way you feel about yourself, dieting and
your body image. If you can work on eliminating the negative core
beliefs in your life, you will take huge strides towards a bulimia free
life that you deserve to live.

## Genes

There is some evidence to suggest that genes may play a part in bulimia,
as relatives of bulimia sufferers are more likely to be at risk of
developing similar symptoms. It may also run in families due to an
unhealthy emphasis on weight in the household.

# **How to treat bulimia and binge eating**

Taking the first step in treatment is often the hardest part of treating
bulimia for sufferers. It takes a long time to recover from an eating
disorder due it's severe psychological and physiological side effects.

## Motivation

Bulimia is incredibly common, whilst overall successful recovery from
bulimia has been reasoned down to one main attribute, motivation. To
create change, you have to truly want to recover. It is not a decision
your friends, family or other people you care about should make for you
because when you face adversity or cravings, it's your sole willpower
that has to overcome this. Ensure you truly want to recover and
understand the reasons behind your motivation because that will be your
driving force as you face difficult or troubling situations during your
recovery.

## Cognitive behavioural therapy

Cognitive behavioural therapy is a useful treatment, as you can target
the unhealthy behaviours, restore normal eating patterns and stop the
binge purge cycle. It may be useful to monitor eating patterns, learn to
cope with stress with other ways than eating and begin to eat more
regularly so that the sufferer no longer has cravings for food that are
difficult to control.

Psychologists may work with you to help identify dysfunctional views
about your body and self worth and diet. Sufferers may explore their
attitudes about eating whilst rethinking their views about self worth
and this issue relative to their weight.

The final phase involves targeting underlying emotional issues that are
the root cause of the eating disorder. Through therapy this could mean
focusing on relationship issues, managing underlying anxiety and
depression, low self esteem and feelings of isolation or loneliness.
Also, some psychologists may prescribe common antidepressants, the most
common being selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) like
Prozac, which will help the patient overcome their depression whilst
managing its long term effect through therapy.

## Late night binge eating tips

Some people struggle with controlling their eating during the night
time, which leads to the binge eating and purging cycle. To help with
this, look at whether you are nourishing yourself during the day. In the
daytime, ensure you eating foods that you enjoy the taste of whilst also
being healthy and nourishing for your body. They should taste nice and
satisfying, whilst also being healthy. Avoid bland or boring foods that
you do not like because they will make you feel hungry and restricted.

If you are really struggling with late night binging, especially during
your recovery, consider treating yourself a couple of times a day with
some foods that you love. This could be chips, chocolate, pizza etc. -
anything that reduces your craving for delicious foods. This will stop
you feeling restricted throughout the day whilst preventing you becoming
bored with the food you're already eating. Persist with the above tips
because it's inevitable that sometimes you will feel these late night
urges.

## Be mindful of how you are thinking

Another tip is to check in with yourself and your thought patterns to
see how you are truly feeling. This could include looking at your
thought patterns and seeing if you are planning on restricting yourself
again, or if you have plans to start dieting, as these are powerful
thought patterns that can trigger an urge. For example, if you plan to
go on a diet the following day, you may feel that you want to eat
excessive amounts before you begin the diet, which creates a binge
cycle. You may not even be aware of this but if you are subconsciously
scared of beginning a diet or being hungry and restricting yourself, you
will feel you need to overeat in response to this.

During this period, be honest with yourself. See what you are thinking
and what you are telling yourself about tomorrow. If you tell yourself
tomorrow is a day of eating great tasting, nourishing food, then you are
far less likely to overeat because you know the diet or famine is over.
You know tomorrow is a day of nourishing yourself, rather than
restricting your foods.

## How to reconnect with your body

For many bulimia sufferers, it may be difficult to live without feeling
the need to restrict yourself through the foods you eat. You may find
that when you begin to stop purging, you see a small amount of weight
gain. However, this is very healthy and normal. You will find your
natural weight, a healthy amount that allows you to be energetic and
your body thrives, where your body functions at an optimal level.

## Rewarding your progress

Willpower is a muscle that can fatigue from time to time. That is why
it's important to regularly reward yourself when you are making progress
because it give you a spur of momentum whilst being something to look
forward to and a goal to work towards. Think about something you could
gift yourself with instead of giving in to bulimia. It could be buying
new clothes, going out for a meal or taking a holiday.

Please also do not be too hard on yourself. Recovery is incredibly
draining and a difficult challenge. If you relapse and feel like you
have failed, do not beat yourself up but instead make sure you jolt
yourself back into recovery as soon as possible in order to keep the
momentum up.

# **Recovering from bulimia**

Beating bulimia might seem like an impossible endeavour but once you
push past the initial stage of recovery, you will truly be set free from
your inner battles. Friends and family can help with this as well.

Relapse prevention is just as important as choosing to recover from
bulimia in order to create lasting long term change. The main reason why
some bulimia sufferers relapse within a few months is because they do
not have the processes and coping strategies in place to reduce the risk
of relapsing. Inevitably and due to the nature of bulimia, you may
experience temptations and new challenges whilst recovering which are
difficult to overcome. Choosing to recover is the most difficult step,
so once you have achieved this relapse prevention should help you stay
on the road to recovery.

## Learn to enjoy it

How you position this change in your mind will become vital for your
long term recovery from bulimia. For example, if you see it as a
challenge and a battle you are constantly fighting, you may find
yourself fatigued and emotionally drained. However, you could instead
look at this as a way to truly discover your mental and physical health
again or to find the person you were before suffering from bulimia.

It could also be a way to reconnect with people you care about in your
new mind set, without the influence of bulimia in your life. Seeing this
as a positive lifestyle change, rather than a challenge, can help to
liberate you from feeling restrained. This is about self empowerment and
self development, rather than fighting a battle with yourself.

## Structured eating plan

At first when you begin your recovery it may be difficult to find a
balance whilst you reconnect and listen to your body. You may even lack
trust in your body's ability to manage its weight optimally. However,
you can relearn this ability. You can spend time rediscovering what a
wonderful thing your body is. If you treat your body well and nourish it
with great foods, it will thrive. Whilst you find this natural balance,
it may be useful to eat a structured diet to ensure you eating the right
amounts. Then as time goes on, your body heals and you will become more
aware of what your body is asking for, in terms of craving certain
foods. Structured eating might be difficult and feel unnatural but just
remind yourself that this is a temporary structure until you reconnect
with your body, which will happen as you carry on your recovery long
term.

## Recognising early signs of relapse

Relapse is a process and it's important to recognise the early stages of
a potential relapse. You could find that you are isolating yourself from
others, or feeling anxious and depressed. If you can recognise early on
that there is a potential problem with your behaviour or your mood, you
can take action to prevent any possible relapses.

Watch out for these warnings signs and if you experience them, take
corrective action. Discuss the problems or thoughts you are having
before you act on any temptations. People that care about you will be
more than willing to listen to your challenges and can offer some very
useful advice. Sometimes this can help to realign your motivation,
discussing the reasons why relapsing could be a dangerous route to take
in terms of the long term consequences. It's important to realise
debates with yourself about relapsing are natural during recovery - it's
just how you deal with these issues that counts.

## Support networks

Many recovering bulimia sufferers find that a strong support network and
having the right people to help them will ease the challenges they face
in revery. This could include reaching out to family and friends, whilst
maintaining regular counselling or group therapy sessions. Healthy
relationships with the people you care about are incredibly useful
because they provide support when you need it, help you relax and offer
an outlet to talk about your feelings openly and honestly.

## Self care

Establishing a routine and genuinely learning to take care of yourself
may be new for you but it will certainly help on the way to recovering.
This could include regular exercise, socialising more, attending regular
group support meetings and ensuring you eat a nutritious and healthy
diet. This creates a healthier lifestyle and helps stabilise your mood,
so that you are not tempted to make rash decisions that could hinder
your recovery.

Stay occupied and create a lifestyle of positivity - this could be
spending time working towards your career goals, educational and
personal goals. Goals help you work towards a long term vision, whilst
you reward yourself along the way. You can take pride in your actions,
increasing your self esteem and confidence. A positive lifestyle also
helps you to relax, so that you think clearly and be stronger in your
decisions.

## The relapse prevention toolbox 

It might help to have a plan prepared for dealing with any signs of
relapse, creating a toolbox of coping strategies that will help you stay
focused. This could include counsellors, support networks, a sponsor or
rolemodel, goals to work towards, exercise, hobbies and even meditation.
The earlier the stage of your recovery, the newer the challenges you
face, so you can use your relapse prevention tools to support you in any
potential issues or problems that arise.

Being prepared now helps you deal with threats in the future, equipping
you for long term success. Relapse prevention creates a smoother
recovery journey, letting you spot any potential warning signs before
they influence your behaviour. If you know the triggers that make you
want to drink again, you can avoid or manage them emotionally,
environmentally and physically and stop them before a relapse
progresses.

## Food diary

Diaries are very useful for tracking your progress and increasing your
motivation whilst recovering, in a similar way to how mood diaries are
used to treat mental health problems. This could include tracking
details such as the amount of what you have eaten, who you were with,
your thoughts and feelings and your overall mood during the day. This
will be a strong starting point if you are on the first part of your
recovery journey, as it can really improve your ability to understand
your thoughts and feelings.

Food diaries will also help you to track the triggers and causes that
led you to become bulimic - whether it was the people you were with, a
social situation, a certain problem you faced, emotional stress - and
therefore you can plan for the future some alternative and proactive
ways to deal with these triggers. This way you have an emergency coping
strategy in place before a problem arises, helping you to manage any
issues before they escalate.
