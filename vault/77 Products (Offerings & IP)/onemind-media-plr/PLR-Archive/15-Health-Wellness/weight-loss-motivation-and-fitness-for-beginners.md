HEALTH CHECK
Before you embark on any fitness programme please consult
your Doctor.

Do not exercise if you are unwell.

Stop if you feel pain and if the pain does not subside then see
your Doctor.

Do not exercise if you have taken alcohol or had a large meal
in the last few hours.

If you are taking medication please check with you Doctor to
make sure it is ok for you to exercise.

If in doubt at all please check with your Doctor first. It may be
helpful to ask for a blood pressure, cholesterol and weight
check. You can then have these read again in a few months
after exercise to see the benefit.
FIND YOUR REASON
Before you embark on any change it is helpful to ask yourself
why?

If you understand at the outset what you want to achieve and
why you want to achieve it, you will have a greater chance of
success.

It will help if you write down your aims and goals and your
reasons for wanting to become fitter and healthier.

You may want to look better, lose weight, gain strength and
fitness and improve your long term health and vitality.

Your reason for starting a fitness programme will be different
from the next person but whatever your reason, hold onto it
and strive to achieve it.

By making simple changes to your diet and by taking regular
daily exercise you can improve your health and vitality
considerably.

So why not

LOOK AFTER YOUR BODY SO THAT IT CAN LOOK AFTER
YOU
Take some time NOW to write down all the reasons why you
want to start a programme of improved health, fitness and
wellbeing. Be honest with yourself. Keep this note to hand to
refer to for motivation and when your will power lets you
down – as it will from time to time.
For some people something triggers a reason for wanting to
change.

“It’s only on the brink that people find the will to change.
            Only at the precipice do we evolve.”

John Cleese said this in the film The Day the Earth Stood Still.

I could relate to this from my experience with people who
joined the gym after being told by a doctor that they were in
danger of having a heart attack or other life threatening illness
caused by bad lifestyle choices.

These people had really good reasons for improving their
health and fitness and usually had fantastic results!

It is sad but true that it can take the threat of a serious health
problem to push someone to make the decision to make those
changes and actually get the results.

But why not change bad habits before cumulative bad habits
cause you problems?

Most of us know that we should not eat pizza, greasy burgers,
fries and fried chicken at every meal, and that this type of food
will cause problems if eaten regularly as part of our regular
diet.
So let’s just imagine that every time you eat a slice of pizza
loaded with cheese and fatty toppings you ended up in
intensive care from a heart attack.

Would you visit your favourite pizza parlour on a regular
basis? I’m guessing the answer is “NO WAY!”

Or, if every time you sit down after your evening meal with a
big bar of your favourite chocolate you put on a pound of fat.
Would you put those bars of chocolate on your shopping list
ready for the next shop? I’m guessing the answer is “NO
WAY!”

I would advise anyone to find their reason before starting any
diet or lifestyle change because I believe that this is a
fundamental factor for success on any weight loss, muscle
building or general fitness programme.

If you have a good enough reason you will do well.

So, if you are planning to start a healthy new lifestyle, don’t
leave it until you are at the brink to find the will to change,
find your reason now!

Your reason could be a social occasion that is planned for next
year such as a wedding or reunion that you want to look good
for or you just want to look and feel good for yourself.
If you are in the position where you know that your health is
seriously suffering, now is the time to start to do something
about it. Just remember:

YOU CAN’T CHANGE YESTERDAY BUT YOU CAN CHANGE
TOMORROW.

You have your reason, so let’s get to it.

CHANGE
Generally people don’t like change. By change I mean, change
in routine, change of a habit, change of job. Why is this?

I have figured out that this is all down to being taken out of
this thing called a comfort zone.

The truth is that this is where most people will stay. They will
stay here because it is comfortable.

If you want to bring about a change then you must do
something different.



  “If you always do what you have always done, you will
            always be what you are right now”



You need to step out of your comfort zone. It will be hard at
first but the more you do it the easier it will become.

I really like the above quote, it is true in every sense and it
works both ways.
If you sit on a sofa night after night watching TV after work
and decide that that’s how you want to play it from now on,
you will always sit on the sofa and watch TV after work.

You will not lose weight or improve your health unless you get
up and off the sofa!

On the flip side, if you decide to get off the sofa and start to do
some exercise and stick with it until you get results; you will
have moved out of your comfort zone and will start to develop
and gain many health and vitality benefits.

If you keep this up for the rest of your life, you will always be
developing your fitness and lifestyle into something special.

Making the move out of your comfort zone can be very tough.

You will find excuses yourself and other people will try to hold
you back.

This is when you should refer back to YOUR REASONS for
change and remind yourself of your reasons for improving
your health and fitness.

“I wouldn’t mind giving that a go you know”

“Your joking aren’t you? You’d pop the tyres getting on a cycle

“Yeah” laughs Dave

And although it seems like an innocent comment or friendly

It could have been a totally different story if Jon had said;

“Yeah, you should give it a go; it would be good for you”

Or even better;

“Actually, yeah, I wouldn’t mind either, lets both do it!”

The best thing you can do here is be aware of the fact that this
kind of sabotage may happen.

It’s not the fault of the person making the comment, they just
don’t realise the impact of the throw away remark or light
hearted joke.

And in all fairness neither do most people who the joke or
comment is aimed at.
Decide that you will believe in yourself and get started on your

There will be many hurdles at first so be aware that it’s not

It will also help you if you refer back to YOUR REASONS for

Once you have your self-belief sorted out you can get started
and begin to move out of your comfort zone.

Yes this will be hard at first, but that’s why it’s called a comfort

The great thing is that if you stick at this for long enough, your

Now that would be a better position to be in hey?

GOOD AND BAD HABITS
Let’s imagine that you need to put up a new fence in your
back yard.

There are a few small trees and shrubs in the way; you need to
clear this area before you set about measuring up and digging
holes for the fence posts.

You get to work; first off you come across a few small weeds
which you are able to uproot easily with your fingers, good
job.

Next you come across a bush that is a few years old, using
both hands and a few tugs; this comes out of the ground.

As you clear more of the area, you come across a well-
established tree as tall as you. Using a bit more strength and a
few hand tools, you are able to uproot this.

As you clear the area that you are working on, you start to
realise that the huge oak that has been growing for maybe a
hundred years and is towering over you is going to be in your
way.
Do you put on your gloves, crack you neck and try and yank
that one up?

Of course not! If you are to get this out of the way, you will
need to put in an awful lot of work and you will need some
heavier tools.

That’s not to say you can’t get it out of the way though, the
tree has been developing for a long time, it will be tough, but if
you really want to put that fence up, you will find a way.

This is a great metaphoric story for the demonstration of how
habits can take hold of us.

The longer we have the habit, the more that habit becomes
established, like the roots of our trees, the harder they are to
uproot.

We all have habits and routines; these can be good or bad. But
until you stop, think and identify what these are and what
causes you to keep these habits going you will find it a lot
harder to stop them.

You may not even know that you have a bad habit until you
identify it.
Let’s take smoking for instance. Now this is clearly a bad habit
and if you smoke, you know that you have a bad habit and it is
not doing your health any favours.

The thing with this kind of bad habit is that it will fit into your
routine somewhere.

For example, every morning on your way to work, you light up
a cigarette without even thinking about it, on your coffee
break or lunch time and on the way back from work, you light
up and puff away, it’s just what you do.

Now this is all part of your routine, smoking all of those
cigarettes adds up over time and the longer you do it, the
deeper the roots and the more established the habit gets, and
like our oak tree, it will become very hard to remove.
Big Dave Has Bad Habits Too

Since Dave’s Doc gave him the bad news about his health
Dave knows that he has to sort this out, he has his reason and
decides to get to work.

The first thing that Dave does is identify his bad habits.

Luckily Big Dave never took up smoking. He does have a lot of
other bad habits that have helped to cause his health
problems though.

One of his biggest vices is eating chocolates and sweet things
after each meal. This would not be as much of a problem if
Dave cut back on this.

To address the sweet tooth Dave decides to just eat one 90
calorie treat per day after his evening meal.

By making this small change to his diet, Dave will have cut the
calories drastically from his daily intake and can still afford to
have a sweet treat every day.

This may be hard at first but if Dave does this for long enough
he will find that he will no longer need these treats at all and
be able to take or leave them.
Another bad habit of Dave’s is drinking fizzy drinks
throughout the day.

This is a big deal! The types of fizzy sugary drinks that Dave
consumes are packed with useless calories and rubbish.

Dave needs to get an alternative to these. Eventually Dave will
wean himself onto ordinary water.

As Dave has been drinking these carbonated sugary drinks for
so long and the habit has become very established, Dave will
find this easier to make the transition slowly.

First he will move on to water with cordial for a week or two,
then make it slightly weaker and eventually start to just drink
plain water.

Some people will be better to just make a straight swap and
that’s great. But with all of the other changes Dave has to
make right now, this will make it a bit more comfortable for
him.
Bad habit triggers

As I mentioned earlier, there are parts of your daily routine
that trigger your bad habits such as coffee/ lunch breaks or car
journeys.

As you identify these you will have a clearer picture of what
you can do instead.

Let’s say that every time you got into your car, you lit up a
cigarette.

You need to find a practical alternative to this. By practical I
mean something you can do in the car instead.

It’s no good saying that you want to swap the cigarettes for tap
dancing, probably not the best thing to do whilst driving your
car to work.

I would invest in a “travel mug” you know the ones with the
screw top lid that you can put into your cup holder in your car.
I would make coffee or green tea in this before getting into my
car and have that instead and once you get used to this if you
want to quit coffee, you could swap this for chewing gum.

If you identify your bad habits and start to find healthier
alternatives, your bad habits will become good habits.
Just imagine turning 10 bad habits that affect your health in a
negative way into 10 good habits that affect your health in a
positive way!

Just remember that this will not happen overnight and it is
something that needs to start small.

These small changes compounded over time will literally
change your life.

There are many things you can do to break your habits.

If you can just quit cold turkey that’s great! Do it! But there is
the danger that this may be too harsh and you could crack
under the pressure.

My argument is that if you are going to make a change that
you want to be sustainable, you should do it with the view that
it is a long term goal and a project that you can work on.

Be in it for the long term and you will see a lifetime of benefits.
THE POWER OF SUSTAINED CHANGE
There is much to be said about the power and value of
compound interest when it comes to your fitness, diet and
lifestyle choices.

If compound interest is understood and used positively, it can
help in every aspect of your life.

In this book I am going to focus on the positive and negative
effects that this phenomenon has on the results you get from
your training, diet and lifestyle choices.

By the end I hope to have convinced you that you CAN get the
results that you would like to achieve, however insane you
think they sound.

I would also like to think that you will be able to take the
points in this book and use them to change your lifestyle
forever.

If you incorporate the points mentioned in this book into your
life, there is no reason why you won’t have the healthy life that
you deserve.
If you can understand the concept of compound interest and
how it can work both positively and negatively when it comes
to your health, you are well on your way.

Isn’t compound interest to do with money? I hear you ask.

Before I start explaining how compound interest can help you
with your fitness goals, I would like to explain exactly what
compound interest is and how it works.

You may have heard this before, and this in a nutshell outlines
the positive outcome of compound interest. Let’s use the
example of The Magic Penny.

This is mentioned in many books and I have actually seen it
mentioned in Stephen King’s “The happening”.

It is a great concept and really fits into the weight loss and
fitness world very nicely.

So here is my version of the “Magic penny story”;

If you start by saving one single penny and doubling it every
day, by the end of a 30 day month you will have over £10,
000,000! Yes! Ten Million pounds, Dollars or whatever you
deal in!
If you work this out for yourself, you will see, at day 15 (1/2
way) you will still only have £1638.4p Impressive, but you are
½ way and you are nowhere near 10 million!

The magic of this compound effect is working as it should but
you are nowhere near your goal.

Ok at day 25 only 5 days left, you have a considerable amount
but still nowhere near that 10 million.

Day 26 is where it really starts to take off, you have £838860.8
and the next day you hit over 1.5 million.

Then 3 million, then 6 million, then you finish on day 30 with
a massive £13421772.8!

Over 13 million! Now that to me is very impressive and
demonstrates the power of compound interest with money.

It also demonstrates that you do not get your most impressive
rewards until later on in your time scale, but when you do
start to get those rewards, what great rewards they are, and
from that point on, it is easier to get a great result and keep
those results coming.

I have used this example as most people know the value of
money and can relate to this.
That story wasn’t to get you to throw this book down and go
off and look for pennies to start saving, I want you to keep
reading, you can look for pennies later.

This book is to help you understand the value of small
investments compounded over time that affect your training
fitness and lifestyle choices.

Let’s just go back to the magic penny story for a second. Let’s
say you get to day 15 and decide that you’re not going to
bother to double your money today for whatever reason that
might be.

Because you had day 15 off you thought another couple of
days off won’t hurt, so you take day 16 and 17 off and pick it
up again at day 18.

You carry on doubling your money for another 2 days, that
puts you at day 20 and you decide to have days 21 and 22 off
then carry on until day 28 where you have a rest day this
carries over to day 29.

Even though you had a few days off, you manage to make your
last payment on day 30, so you end up with £419430

By having these breaks in the early stages of this programme
you did not hit 1 million, when you could have ended up with
over 13 million!
Sure £419430 is not a bad result but it is seriously short of the
13 million you could have taken to the bank!

If you had a few days off after you hit the 13 million mark,
that’s no big deal because you have achieved what you set out
to achieve and you can take a break or two every now and
again and still have that massive foundation to build on.

This is exactly the same principle as a weight loss / fitness
programme, or healthy life style change.

If you put in the hard work at the beginning and create a
strong foundation, you will be able to take a break or two later,
without it being that devastating to your future results.

If you can understand this and find the will to keep going you

This will take time, so stick with it because once you see this

There are many excuses for missing training days or eating

I have probably heard all of these excuses and it is the one

The bottom line is that there are no excuses.

If you are determined to change, you will find the time,

Many people will point the finger when they have cheated or

But it will be a game changer when you realise that it is your
responsibility to own your weight loss journey 100%.

No one makes you eat junk food or miss training sessions but
yourself.

“I didn’t have time to prepare my healthy lunch today so I will
grab some fast food instead”

“I finished work later tonight so I will miss my training session
for today”

“I didn’t have time to train this morning before work”

Yeah “The Time” gets a whole lot of grief from millions of
people every day and I think that this is unfair.

You can look at time as a great reliable friend because it is very
consistent, it never changes and surprises you, it is always
there and you can set your watch by it.

If you don’t have time in the morning to prepare your healthy
meals for the day, you can get up earlier, you can do it last
thing at night.

If you finish work later, you can still get home and do your
training, the gym might be closed but you can do some cardio
outside or some bodyweight exercises.
Once a good routine has been established, this will become far
less of an issue.

A very important lesson in the midst of this lifestyle change, if
not the most important thing you need to take away with you
is that it is all down to you.

You need to own your problems, take 100% responsibility and
be 100% accountable for your actions. If you can understand
this and put it into action, you will do very well. Remember;



            “IF YOU WANT TO DO IT’S UP TO YOU”
THE IMPORTANCE OF ROUTINE
In the chapter “Good and Bad Habits” we looked at how your
routine and your habits fit together like a nice jigsaw.

They work together without you even noticing. Whether you
like it or not, your routine will influence your habits and vice
versa.

I truly believe that routine is a huge factor in your success with
health/ fitness and in fact your whole lifestyle.

If you have a solid routine and you have good habits to match,
you should have no problem in getting the results that you are
after.

Let’s go back to the magic penny again;

Our routine is to double our money every day. If we do this
without fail, we have a good routine and we end up with a
good outcome at the end of the month.

If we miss a day here and there, we get out of our routine and
we affect the overall outcome.

Let’s say you start going for a 30 minute brisk walk after work
each day, you have been very good at this for two weeks now.
On the Monday of the third week you decide that you are too
tired and go home, get a nice coffee on and watch the TV for
30 minutes instead.

Now it’s Tuesday of the third week and it’s a bit damp outside
so you give Tuesday a miss too.

Wednesday arrives, and as you are breaking yourself in gently
to fitness you are not doing any exercise on the weekends.

This means that you only have three training sessions left until
Monday again so you may as well give those a miss too and
start again on Monday, the weather should have picked up by
then too.

Can you see what happens here?

If you set out on your new routine or new “good habit”, the
first few weeks and months are the most important!

You are at risk and vulnerable to failure. Just like the weeds in
your garden that needed clearing so you can put up your new
fence, the roots of your habits and routines are not strong
enough and can be pulled up very easily at this point.

This is why you need to stick to your new routine and the new
habits that you have created.
These in turn will become your new routine. The early stages
of any change need 100% focus.

You will find that if you stick to a new venture with much
dedication you will no longer find it an effort, it will be second
nature.

When you have cemented your new routine you can have the
occasional night off and drink coffee and watch the television
instead.

However you will probably miss your exercise and feel that
you have to get out there and do it!

This means that you will now be benefitting from your new
routine and it has become a habit.
TIPS FOR THE EARLY STAGES OF YOUR NEW ROUTINE
You should be aware that the changes you are about to make
will be challenging, and this is not a book that is offering you a
“magic bullet” or “new weight loss breakthrough”.

This is a book about fact and what it really takes for people to
lose weight.

There is no “magic pill” and there are no “weight loss secretes”
that personal trainers and fitness models are told to keep on
the down low.

If you see these types of claims, you should by all means look
into them but know that it is all to do with marketing, and the
principals will probably mimic the information in this book.

I have made big lifestyle changes myself before, so I know how
hard this can be.

As I have been through the process of forming new habits and
routines a lot myself, I consider myself a master of this and I
have developed several processes and techniques that can
help you in the all-important early stages of these new
vulnerable habits and routines.
For each new routine or habit you have identified you should
use the following process:



Identify the routine or habit you aim to develop. Let’s say you
want to quit smoking or eat less sugary things

Find your reason. Write it down. Why do you want to quit or
eat less? It is bad for me/ I want to lose weight.

Make your reason visible and plan a “Start Date”. Print off
some photos or short quotes that back your reasons and pin
them up in the places where you will be weak, tempted or
vulnerable to giving in or cheating. For example, your desk at
work, the fridge or your laptop screen saver. It is also
important for mental preparation to plan a start date for your
new routine/ habit change.

Tell everyone. The more people you tell, the more back up you
are likely to get and the further along you get, the more praise
you will get. Put a statement on facebook or twitter, tell
everyone at work, tell all of your friends and family.

Don’t listen to saboteurs. You will always come across
saboteurs so all you have to do is recognise this. These guys
may be close family members or close friends. They don’t
mean any harm, but are not aware of the impact that the
doubt can have on your progress.

Stick with it. The longer you stick with it, the easier it will get,
if you feel like quitting, always go back to your reason. Why are
you doing this in the first place?

Give yourself a reward. Buy something nice from the money
saved from not smoking or a buy some new clothes because
you have lost weight.

MOMENTUM
Stick with it. It will pay off!

Have you ever watched The World’s Strongest Man
competition on TV?

If you haven’t, you are seriously missing out and you should
definitely “YouTube it”!

The organisers come up with outrageous tests of strength and
novel ideas that are different every time.

Now I’m not going to tell you it’s a good idea to go and start
flipping cars down the street or start doing squats with a
tractor tyre around your neck, but I will say there are some
things here that relate to our topic.

On most contests there is a pulling event.

This is in true strongman style outrageous!

And to the mere mortal will look impossible to even attempt. I
remember one year the strongman contestants had to pull a
truck along a 50 meter track and it was timed.

Each contestant would get themselves ready in front of the
enormous lump of metal on wheels by cracking their necks,
rolling their shoulders and having the judges strap the huge
machine that towered over them to a harness that the
contestant was wearing.

When the starting gun was fired, the strongman put all of his
energy, all his aggression and all of his power into getting
those massive wheels turning.

The truck just rocks a little at first, hardly worth all of that
energy for such a small result hey?

But the more the strong man stuck with it, the more this truck
moved, from a slow rock to a very slow crawl, then a faster
crawl, then it started to roll nicely, then an amazing turn of
pace to the point that the strongman is actually jogging the
truck across the finish line with half the effort he started out
with!

So all you need to do is get yourself a truck and a harness and
start going for runs with it strapped to you and you will be
away!

Only kidding, the point I am trying to make is that the reason
that the strongman ended up jogging over the finish line with
a truck attached to him is the same reason that you need to
stick with your new plans for diet, fitness, healthy living or
muscle building.
Momentum! Once you have your momentum going, you will
end up jogging your goal over the finish line, not dragging it
around with you like a sack of potatoes.

Obviously you will not be dragging big trucks around and your
progress will take longer for you to notice, you are not doing
this over several minutes, you are doing this over a longer
period of time, so your progress will be harder to measure at
the start.

Your progress is more like the magic penny; the results are not
apparent right away.

Imagine you did have a huge sack of potatoes on your back
that you have to take ever where you go but if you stick to your
training and diet plans, you get to take one of the potatoes out
at the end of the week.

This is not great for the first several weeks and would probably
feel like it wasn’t worth doing, but understand that if you are
consistent and you always get to take a potato out of the bag
each week, you will eventually end up with an empty bag!

An important thing to know here is that if you lose your
momentum, the results could be devastating.

Some of the strongmen who attempt the truck pull don’t
make it to the finish line, they lose momentum.
After they have used all of their energy to get the truck moving,
as soon as they allow this truck to slow down, there is no going
back, these guys are exhausted and to get this moving again
would be impossible.

Pulling trucks and emptying potato sacks are very different in
practice but as demonstrated, the principle of achieving
results in both examples is the same.

Just like the strongman and his truck, your momentum will
turn up.

The strongman got his momentum after he put all of his
energy into getting that truck moving, once it was moving, it
became easier to keep moving, but if he let up on the
momentum, the truck would slow down and stop with the
momentum also grinding to a halt.

If the momentum is lost, it would have to be kicked off again,
this would take the same amount of energy to get going once
again and for the strong man it is game over at this point.

Luckily for us, we do not need this kind of energy at the
beginning, but we do need some to get the momentum going.

Like the strongman, the amount of energy we put in at the
beginning will seem like it is not worth it.
For me the turning point for fitness development i.e. fat loss,
muscle building, endurance is the point at which other people
notice you have changed. Someone will compliment you
without you prompting them, and it is things like this that will
only make you pick up more momentum!

SPEED THINGS UP
We have looked at how a few small changes made to a routine
or lifestyle can have a big difference over time. Hopefully I
have convinced you that this will work as long as you stay
focused and consistent.

I know it is hard enough getting started and staying consistent
but “a lot of a little” all goes to add up to “a lot in the end”.

So what if you could just add that little bit more?

This tip is not for everyone but it is worth taking on board.

If you have decided to start to walk one mile per day as part of
your new weight loss plan. You could go out there and do your
one mile, stop and do it again tomorrow.

You would still get your results if you stayed consistent and
you would be doing a great job.

However if you decided on some days after you had done your
1 mile to do another ¼ of ½ mile on top of this you would be
doing more than you planned and giving that little bit extra.
Just remember that every time you put in more than you are
supposed to that this extra effort will also add up to give you
heightened results.

This concept is true in whatever you are doing. If you are
doing a resistance programme and working with aerobic
exercises using sets and reps - you may decide to do 2-4 reps
extra per set.

This way you will be adding that little bit more effort on these
sets and this in turn will be boosting your results even more
over time.
As you may know, I have been into lifting weights for a fair few
years and consider myself a bodybuilder.

Like most other bodybuilders I will try to get as many reps as I
possibly can out whilst keeping my form.

If I am training on my own I may reach 10 reps and that is
good. But if I had my training partner with me he would be
able to assist me with probably two more reps for each set.

Two more reps does not sound like a lot but I would not have
been able to get those two reps out without my training
partner and I would have not been able to put this extra effort
in.

If I achieve these extra 2 reps on every set, I will be doing a
considerable amount more and my results will be better and
they will be evident more quickly.

You may therefore want to exercise with a training partner or
“buddy” so that you can encourage one another.

Doing a little extra exercise every day will soon start to pay off.
The opposite is true if you do damaging things often – no
matter how small.
This works with your diet. I have a post on my blog outlining
the amount of calories that you would consume if you drank
one regular can of cola every day for a year.

It is frightening to think that you would be putting in 51100
calories with no helpful nutritional value, so if you cut that
single can of cola out altogether, you would have effectively
burnt off this number of calories in one year without any huge
effort.

You will also have saved yourself pounds of weight and £££s
from your wallet!

If you are on a diet and plan to have a cheat day or you plan to
have a small sugary treat every day.

To put in that extra bit of effort you could cheat less on your
cheat day or have ½ a cheat day. Or you could not have a
sugary treat at all on occasional days.

Remember that these seemingly small extras will all eventually
add up for good and bad. So let’s try and do the good ones.
