## When did I start my Fiverr business?

So many people have asked me how to get started on Fiverr and with gigs
priced at a mere \$5, how do you [actually make a living on
there](http://fiverr.com/juliangoldie)? I started my Fiverr business
last year in July after returning from Thailand and realising how much I
wanted to travel the world whilst working online. At the time I had no
idea how to make money online and between me and you, I didn't even
think it was possible.

![Fiverr guide get started selling
online](media/image8.png){width="2.904040901137358in"
height="2.994792213473316in"}

Since then, I've worked with over 300 businesses on Fiverr, have nearly
[200 five star reviews](http://fiverr.com/juliangoldie) and I earn over
\$1,000 a month via this channel alone. This excites me because I wonder
what I'll achieve in the next 10 months. I wrote this guide to inspire
more people to do the same.

[Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/s2/545f06e70d) allowed me to quit my 9
to 5 job, work anywhere in the world and gave me a starting point into
building a business online. In fact, I recently spoke at the [Chiang Mai
Nomad Coffee
Club](https://www.facebook.com/events/1761959790702134/?feed_story_type=308&action_history=null)
in Thailand about how to optimise your profits on Fiverr.

Fiverr is actually in the top 100 most popular websites in the US,
meaning there are millions of visitors everyday in the marketplace --
the traffic comes to you.

You can sell almost anything on Fiverr, from people dancing in the
Amazon rainforest whilst slapping their bellies to hiring someone to
film your next TV commercial. With gigs starting from just \$5, it's
become incredibly popular for buying fun, affordable and great value
services.

By the way, [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/s2/545f06e70d) will charge
you a 20% margin on each gig you supply. It's their commission for
helping you receive each sale. Whilst some people aren't happy about
this, I completely understand why Fiverr deserves the rate. In the world
of online marketing you would pay a fortune to receive the amount of
paid traffic Fiverr offers -- especially when most of this traffic is
consisting of people who are actively looking to spend money on your
website.

Not only this but you also receive a whole host of other benefits by
using Fiverr, which I've described below...

# [Chapter 1.]{.smallcaps} 

# [Why use Fiverr to start your business?]{.smallcaps}

Fiverr has such a poor reputation for earning online but actually it can
be incredibly profitable. Personally, I absolutely love it and I think
you will too. It takes a while to get into the swing of things but once
you really invest some time into understanding it, there's no stopping
you.

Here are some of the benefits of using Fiverr to build your online
business:

-   **Start earning money today** -- setting up on Fiverr is incredibly
    > easy. [You can create your gigs today and start earning
    > tomorrow](https://www.fiverr.com/s2/545f06e70d). Seriously, that's
    > exactly what I did. I set up a gig and within 24 hours I had made
    > my first \$5.

-   **It's not just \$5 you know! --** yes Fiverr is of course known for
    > it's incredible value, with gigs starting at just \$5 a listing.
    > However, did you know that you can sell gigs for \$1,000? This is
    > the perfect way to reel customers in and then upsell to them. With
    > packages and extras (which I'll come on to later), you can
    > increase your value per customer -- meaning that you're earning
    > way more than just \$5 a pop.

-   **Learn about internet marketing** -- want a crash course in online
    > marketing? Fiverr is the perfect starting point for learning about
    > this field. It combines so many different skills involved in
    > internet marketing including finding a product/service that sells,
    > writing sales copy, understanding analytics, the power of relevant
    > traffic, how to negotiate, how to convert traffic into sales and
    > the power of upselling. All of these skills will stay with you for
    > the rest of your internet marketing career. So if you're a
    > complete beginner, Fiverr is actually a perfect starting point.

-   **Can Fiverr be passive? --** yes, you don't just have to sell
    > services on there. You can sell actual products on Fiverr, such as
    > ebooks or guides, which means once you've written a guide you can
    > sell it over and over without having to do any more work. So
    > Fiverr is a source of passive income, despite what many say.

-   **Testing your products --** want to know if your new product or
    > service sells? With millions of website visitors everyday on
    > Fiverr, you can test if your product or service sells on there in
    > front of a live audience that is looking to spend money. If you
    > see sales come in, you know you're on the right path. If you don't
    > get any sales, you either need to do some tweaking or offer more
    > value.

-   **Try something new --** don't know how to do something? Fiverr is a
    > great way to learn new things. For example, say you don't know how
    > to create a video. Set up a gig with a 3 day deadline to create a
    > video. Once you've sold this gig and you're under pressure to
    > supply this service with a short deadline, you will be forced into
    > learning how to create a video -- whether you like it or not!

-   **Build your personal brand --** [personal
    > branding](http://juliangoldie.com/personal-branding-zombie-branding-the-egotist/)
    > online is so important these days. People need to trust you before
    > they buy from you. When you're selling on Fiverr as you
    > personally, you're receiving reviews and increasing your positive
    > exposure online. People can quickly check your Fiverr profile and
    > see that you're an expert in your field, which makes it easier to
    > sell to them. This is awesome, not just for internet marketers but
    > also if you're applying for a job and want to show people how
    > proactive you are online.

Chapter 3.

# [How to start your online business with Fiverr]{.smallcaps}

This is probably the hardest part. Most people have no idea about how to
get started selling on Fiverr and they just procrastinate putting any
listings up on there. However, you should understand that your listed
services don't need to be perfect at first. Neither does your sales
copy. This is a channel where you can easily tweak and test your
services, seeing what sells and what doesn't.

So once you've created your Fiverr account, you really need to start
thinking about what you're going to sell and why people would want to
buy it.

Here are my tips for getting started on Fiverr:

-   **Don't know what to sell? --** I'd recommend focusing on something
    > you're good at and expanding on that niche. So for example if
    > you're a video marketer just focus on the services you can offer
    > -- creating a video, editing it, creating an awesome intro
    > sequence etc. Think about everything your target buyer wants on
    > Fiverr and then offer services that create value for them.

-   **Niche down --** so you might be looking at the above and thinking,
    > wait a minute, aren't there thousands of people already doing the
    > same thing? Yes, there is a lot of competition on Fiverr so it's
    > always best to 'niche down'. That means being more specific on who
    > you sell to and becoming a specialist. Using the above example,
    > you could be focusing on selling videos to the health & fitness
    > industry, or small businesses, or even offering a premium service
    > to those who prefer quality over price. When you niche down, you
    > differentiate your business from competitors and stand out from
    > the crowd.

-   **Avoid procrastination --** if there's one tip you should take from
    > this guide, it's that you should never procrastinate. Putting
    > things off until later is easy but it won't get you any sales.
    > Maybe you're thinking of the perfect service to sell, or you're
    > intimidated by the rest of the sellers on Fiverr. However, I can
    > tell you now that your listing does not need to be perfect, it
    > just needs to be valuable. Even if you have a few spelling
    > mistakes or grammatical errors in your description, people will
    > still buy it if you offer something that's genuinely useful. So
    > there's no excuse to wait or hesitate before creating a listing!

-   **Sales copy --** sales copy is extremely powerful. It's the
    > difference between having no sales and receiving 100 orders. It's
    > understanding the problems people have and how to solve them. You
    > can read my guide on [sales
    > copy](http://juliangoldie.com/how-to-write-persuasive-sales-copy/)
    > here. I would advise learning everything you can about sales copy
    > because it gives you the power to influence people in their
    > decisions, which is useful for pretty much everything in life --
    > whether that's applying for a job, selling something online or
    > even trying to get your ex back!

-   **Deliver quickly --** time is the most valuable commodity on
    > Fiverr. You therefore should aim to sell gigs that are quick to
    > supply. If you could sell a gig at \$15 and it takes just 15
    > minutes to do, you're earning a decent hourly rate. So try and
    > sell services that are really time efficient. For example, it
    > could be selling a marketing template that's already pre-written.
    > All you would need to do is upload and send the marketing template
    > to your buyer, which takes no time at all. This also means you can
    > charge extra for delivery within 24 hours and when you deliver
    > quickly, you're more likely to receive great reviews.

-   **Building your Fiverr gig sales when first starting out --** so
    > when you're first starting out, you might find your sales are just
    > trickling in, or you might not get any sales at all! One way to
    > build your sales up quickly is by using the Fiverr [requests
    > section](https://www.fiverr.com/users/juliangoldie/requests). This
    > is basically where you can respond to people's requests, offering
    > relevant services that they'll be interested in. Fiverr requests
    > might include 'help me make a video' or 'help me create a
    > marketing plan'. You can then offer an estimate price along with a
    > proposal explaining why people should use your services instead of
    > your competitor's.

-   **Research** -- it's so easy to research your competitors and find
    > out the best way to position your gigs. Find the best selling
    > competing gigs in your category and see what works and what
    > doesn't. This can help you find out their best selling services,
    > the best extras to offer, how to organise your services into
    > packages, why your competitors are so successful and what you can
    > improve on. This is very powerful information in the right hands
    > so it's important to use it correctly. If you were running an
    > offline service, your competitor's information wouldn't be so
    > freely available.

# [Chapter 5.]{.smallcaps}

# [A guide to upselling on Fiverr]{.smallcaps}

Upselling on Fiverr is where you can make the real money. It's how you
can turn a \$5 basic gig into a \$500 gig. With a \$5 price tag being
the perfect way to reel paying customers in, you can offer extras
related to your gig that people will want to buy. This means you can
increase your average value per customer -- boosting your income without
having to fish for more clients.

There are two main methods of upselling on Fiverr -- extras and
packages. I'll explain both below...

**Extras**

So you can add a list of extras to each of your gigs, offering a price
per extra. The amount you can charge for each and the number of extras
you can list varies depending on what level of seller you are. Extras
can be bought along with your original service, as add on's essentially.

So for example, you may offer to edit an image for someone as part of a
service. However, the extra may be selling the PSD file. This comes at
no extra cost for you to create as a seller but it's an opportunity to
create more income by offering it as an extra.

**Packages**

Whilst this feature isn't available for all categories, it is available
for many. This is basically where you can have up to three different
levels of services, each with a different rate. The starting rate
doesn't even have to be \$5...

So for example, if you have a marketing plan, you could have three
levels -- a basic, premium and pro package. This could range in costs
from \$20 for a basic, \$100 for a premium and \$995 for a pro package.
It's a really exciting feature for Fiverr that's helping sellers across
the platform earn way more than they used to be able to.

Whilst extras and packages are the main methods of upselling, many high
value sales happen behind closed doors.

Here's where custom offers come in...

**Inbox messages & custom offers**

You'll also find that sometimes people would like a custom offer, based
on their specific needs. Perhaps they need a service delivered within a
few hours, or they would like a bulk discount on one of your services.
Whatever the case is, once you have the details of their request you can
reply with a custom offer for that person to accept.

This can be a really powerful feature for upselling because you can
offer a few extras or a completely customised service that's worth way
more than the amount you usually sell things for. As you're going back
and forth between you and the buyer who's inboxed you, you can also
haggle with them to negotiate a price.

# [Chapter 6.]{.smallcaps}

# [How to create credibility when first starting out as a Fiverr seller]{.smallcaps}

So if you're a first time seller on Fiverr, you're going to have to
build some sort of trust between you and your potential buyers. You
might be competing against some of the biggest sellers on Fiverr --
people who have hundreds of reviews, high seller rankings and a whole
host of impressive services. The barriers for entry are therefore a
little tricky. Luckily for you, I have a few workarounds!

Here are some of my top tips for increasing buyers if you have no
existing reviews or orders:

-   **Have a great photo --** the first day I was working from Punspace
    > in Thailand, someone actually pointed out my profile pic had a
    > resemblance to Borat! This opened my eyes a little to say the
    > least! The point I'm trying to make is that your profile pictures
    > create a big first impression. So try to use something
    > professional, that also doesn't make you look like Don Corleone --
    > damn my Italian genes! If you can avoid it, just use a nice hi-res
    > photo of you personally, rather than a generic picture. People
    > like to buy from people, so your profile photo needs to show who
    > you actually are -- rather than your cat or dog for instance!

-   **Use a personal video --** this is something I'm still intending to
    > do. On each of your gigs, you can have a video selling the
    > benefits of your gig. For the same reasons as above, use a video
    > that's a personal message from you -- so people can see who you
    > really are. This creates a personable level to your seller
    > profile, making it easier for people to trust you.

-   **Offer amazing value --** for the first few orders whilst you're
    > trying to bring in some reviews, offer incredible value for money
    > so that people are more likely to buy from you.

-   **Make your sales copy really professional --** people will judge
    > how professional you are by the way you describe your services. If
    > you're offering a blog article and the gig description is written
    > in poor English, it's unlikely that someone will buy from you. So
    > make your sales copy really strong to create a professional
    > impression.

# [Chapter 7.]{.smallcaps} 

# [Dealing with poor feedback]{.smallcaps}

OK so from time to time, whether you've under-delivered, your client is
unreasonable or there's been a miscommunication, you may receive poor
feedback. With seller feedback being such an important part of who
buyers choose to use, it's a little bit of a challenge.

However, there are some ways to get round this...

-   **Offer a refund** -- honestly, you shouldn't worry about offering
    > the occasional refund here and there. It's really not worth
    > sacrificing your business over. If someone leaves poor feedback,
    > you can offer a cancellation which gives the buyer a full refund.
    > If the buyer accepts the refund, then the feedback is removed. It
    > is important to note however that the buyer has 3 days to accept
    > and if they don't respond, they'll receive a full refund and the
    > poor feedback won't be removed. So if you haven't heard from them
    > after two days, cancel the refund offer! Otherwise you'll have
    > poor feedback and less money -- I made this mistake once!

-   **Respond to the review --** you should respond to every review
    > personally anyway but when it comes to poor feedback, stay calm
    > and don't reply like a psychotic maniac! This will only leave an
    > even worse impression with those that see your reviews. Instead,
    > apologise in your seller feedback that they feel you haven't met
    > their expectations, state that you have offered a refund and ask
    > if there's anything else you can offer. This way you're proving
    > you're a reasonable person and offering great customer service at
    > the same time.

-   **Speak to Fiverr's customer service team --** this is especially
    > useful if the client is being unreasonable. So for example, if the
    > customer demands way more than they've bought and they're
    > threatening you with bad feedback, you can report them to customer
    > service and they'll be able to review your communications with
    > them. If it's clear that the customer is being unreasonable, they
    > will resolve the situation for you.

# [Chapter 8.]{.smallcaps} 

# [Measuring your gig's performance]{.smallcaps}

If you're logged in as a seller, there's a really useful tool that will
help you see how well your Fiverr gigs are performing. Float your mouse
over the top right icon where your Fiverr balance is, hover over
'Selling' and then click 'Analytics'.

Under this section, scroll down and you'll see your average conversion
rate...

From your conversion rate, you can find out how much traffic you need to
reach a sale. So say your conversion rate is 5%. That would mean you'd
need a 100 views on your Fiverr gig page to get 5 sales. That's really
useful information to know.

However, conversion rates aren't always as clear cut. This can depend on
variables such as:

-   **The quality of traffic** -- whether the traffic is relevant to the
    > service you're trying to sell. If you're not receiving targeted
    > traffic it's going to be poor quality, reducing your conversion
    > rates.

-   **Competition --** competition can influence your conversion rates,
    > as shoppers look around and compare deals before making a choice.
    > As a crude generalism, the more saturated your market, the lower
    > your conversion rates.

-   **Conversion rate optimisation --** there are various ways to
    > improve your conversion rates. For example, Fiverr states that a
    > video will increase your conversion rates by 220%. So add a video
    > and you could boost your conversions of traffic to orders by more
    > than double. This can also be achieved with stronger sales copy,
    > better images, focusing on your benefits rather than features,
    > offering more value from your service, having great reviews etc.

-   **Pricing** -- if you have a very expensive set of packages, you
    > probably won't have a high conversion rate from clicks to sales.
    > However, one conversion might be all you need to hit your target
    > for the month. If you're selling a low priced gig, you should aim
    > for a higher than average conversion rate because you want a high
    > volume of orders.

# [Chapter 9.]{.smallcaps} 

# [Niching down on Fiverr]{.smallcaps}

I'm the first to admit that this is one of my biggest weaknesses with
the current business model I use. Having said, it will be something I
build on over the next few months. Here's my take on niching down via
Fiverr...

Being a specialist in your field is one of the best ways to
differentiate yourself from your competition. You need to find your
niche and be really specific about it.

You need to drill way, way down. For example, niching down from a
marketer to digital marketer isn't enough. Niching down from digital
marketer to social media specialist still isn't enough. Social media
specialist to Pinterest marketer is OK but you could go further. A
Pinterest expert specialising in hospitality marketing is a very good
starting point.

Niching down doesn't just mean you're unique though. It also means you
appeal to a very specialist industry, where people are willing to pay
big because you're just one of a handful of people doing the same thing.
The expertise and experience you can bring to the table is so unique and
so valuable that you can start charging big.

Another benefit to niching down is the fact that it's transferable to
the rest of your freelancing business. You can integrate this into your
UpWork profile, your offline business, your website etc. You can begin
to sell books and courses that are so niche you can charge a real
premium for them. You don't even have to worry about the competition
because you've filtered out the clients relevant to you.

There is a small drawback to this method. You have to ensure the demand
is there for your business. You might niche down so much that you're no
longer relevant to anyone who uses Fiverr. So you need to create a
balance. Having said that, I have a friend working on UpWork who simply
offers services around a certain type of Google Analytics tagging. It's
so niche that he can easily charge at least \$3,000 per project.
