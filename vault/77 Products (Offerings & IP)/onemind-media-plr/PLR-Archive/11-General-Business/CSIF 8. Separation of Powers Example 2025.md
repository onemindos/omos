**OBJECTION, Demand for Relief for Violation of the Separation of Powers
Doctrine, Due Process, and Improper Authority in Child Support Order
Pursuant to 5 U.S.C. § 706, Mistretta v. United States, 488 U.S. 361
(1989), Caperton v. A.T. Massey Coal Co., 556 U.S. 868 (2009), and Stern
v. Marshall, 564 U.S. 462 (2011).**

**LEGAL NOTICE AND DEMAND FOR RELIEF**

**Objection**. I, \[Your Full Name\], hereby issue this formal notice
and demand for relief, alleging violations of my constitutional rights
under the **Separation of Powers Doctrine**, the **Due Process Clause of
the Fourteenth Amendment**, and improper judicial authority exercised in
the issuance of a child support order.

Specifically, I assert the following:

> 1\. **Dual Role of Judicial Officer Violating Separation of Powers**
>
> • The judge who presided over my divorce proceedings---a judicial
> matter---also issued the child support order, which constitutes an
> administrative action.
>
> • This action violates the constitutional doctrine of **separation of
> powers** as outlined in **Mistretta v. United States, 488 U.S. 361
> (1989)**, which affirms that judicial, executive, and legislative
> powers must remain distinct.
>
> • As child support hearings are administrative by nature, the issuance
> of a child support order by the same judicial officer overseeing my
> divorce represents a blending of judicial and administrative
> functions, contrary to constitutional principles.
>
> 2\. **Due Process Violations**
>
> • I was deprived of proper procedural safeguards required by
> the **Fourteenth Amendment**.
>
> • According to **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S.
> 306 (1950)**, due process requires proper notice and an opportunity to
> be heard. The issuance of the child support order did not afford me
> these protections.
>
> 3\. **Impartiality and Conflict of Interest**
>
> • The involvement of the same judicial officer in both my divorce
> proceedings and the administrative child support matter creates an
> appearance of bias, violating **Caperton v. A.T. Massey Coal Co., 556
> U.S. 868 (2009)**.
>
> • Additionally, under **28 U.S.C. § 455**, any judge whose
> impartiality might reasonably be questioned must disqualify
> themselves.
>
> 4\. **Exceeding Authority in Administrative Matters**
>
> • The child support order was issued in an administrative capacity but
> by a judicial officer, exceeding the judge's constitutional authority
> under **Article III**, as established in **Stern v. Marshall, 564 U.S.
> 462 (2011)**.
>
> • The administrative procedures used to impose the child support order
> were arbitrary and capricious, violating **5 U.S.C. § 706**, which
> governs agency actions.

**DEMAND FOR RELIEF**

In light of these violations, I formally request the following relief:

> 1\. **Vacate the Child Support Order**
>
> • Declare the child support order void due to the improper exercise of
> judicial authority in an administrative matter, violating the
> separation of powers and due process.
>
> 2\. **Judicial Recusal**
>
> • Disqualify the presiding judge from further involvement in my case
> under **28 U.S.C. § 455** due to the appearance of bias and the
> improper dual role.
>
> 3\. **Full Review of the Child Support Order**
>
> • Conduct a new hearing to address the child support matter in
> accordance with proper administrative procedures and constitutional
> safeguards.
>
> 4\. **Restoration of Parental Rights (if applicable)**
>
> • If my parental rights were improperly diminished or terminated as
> part of this process, I demand their immediate restoration based on
> the precedent in **Stanley v. Illinois, 405 U.S. 645 (1972)**.
