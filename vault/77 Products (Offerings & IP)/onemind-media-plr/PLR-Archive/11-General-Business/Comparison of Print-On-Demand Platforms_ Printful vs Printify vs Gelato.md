Comparison of Print-On-Demand Platforms:
Printful vs Printify vs Gelato
Introduction
Businesses looking to sell custom merchandise like t-shirts, mugs, mouse pads, pens, and athletic wear
need a reliable print-on-demand (POD) partner. Three popular options are Printful, Printify, and Gelato.
Each offers different product ranges, fulfillment models, and integrations. Below we compare their product
variety (especially for apparel, mugs, office supplies, and sportswear), printing quality, shipping speed (with
emphasis on U.S. fulfillment), profit margins, and integration with Shopify and Etsy. We also highlight which
providers excel in performance gear and niche business merchandise.


Product Range and Variety
Printful: Offers a curated catalog of 300–400+ products, covering all the POD staples: various t-shirts,
hoodies, leggings and joggers, hats, tote bags, mugs, wall art, etc 1 2 . Its selection is slightly smaller
than some competitors, but it includes most common merch categories. Notably, Printful does carry some
athletic wear (e.g. leggings, sports bras, athletic shirts) as well as basic office items like mugs and stickers
  1 . However, it lacks certain niche items (for example, custom pens are not in Printful’s catalog).




Printify: Through a network of print partners, Printify boasts one of the largest POD catalogs – over 900+
products (1,000+ by recent counts) 2 3 . This includes everything Printful has and more: a huge variety
of t-shirts and apparel (including gym wear and even swimsuits and skirts 4 ), drinkware like mugs and
tumblers, phone cases, stickers, home décor (pillows, blankets, canvases), pet products, and unique items
like shoes and high-heel footwear 5 . If you need an unusual product, chances are Printify’s network
offers it. Like others, mouse pads and desk mats are available via certain providers. That said, branded
pens are still hard to find – even Printify users report no luck finding a POD supplier for pens 6 . Overall,
Printify leads in sheer variety of product options 7 .


Gelato: Initially focused on printed stationery and paper goods, Gelato’s catalog is smaller (roughly 250+
products 3 ) but has expanded into apparel and accessories. It offers the essentials: a range of t-shirts,
hoodies, and basic apparel for men, women, and kids, plus mugs, wall art, photo books, calendars,
greeting cards and other stationery 8 . Gelato’s niche is custom paper products (cards, notebooks, etc.),
which remain a strong suit 9 . It does support apparel printing (front, back, and sleeve prints on most
garments) 10 , but the choices for athletic-specific wear may be more limited than Printful/Printify. Office
accessories like mouse pads might not be in Gelato’s current lineup (its focus is more on paper and canvas
goods). In summary, Gelato covers the core products but not the long tail of exotic items.

Product Quality and Printing Techniques
All three platforms deliver high-quality prints, but their approaches differ:


     • Printful prints in-house at their own facilities, which allows tight quality control and consistency.
       They use industry-standard equipment (e.g. Kornit for DTG printing, Tajima for embroidery, etc.) for
       uniform results 11 . Printful supports a broad range of printing techniques – direct-to-garment
       (DTG) for apparel, sublimation for mugs and all-over prints, embroidery for hats and polos, and even
       cut-and-sew and inside label printing for certain products 12 . This means you can offer
       embroidered caps, dye-sublimated all-over leggings, or sewn product labels under one roof. Product
       quality is consistently praised by merchants 13 , and Printful reports a very low return rate due to
       quality issues (around 0.24%) 14 . In short, Printful is known for premium print quality and offers
       branding options (like tear-away labels or pack-in flyers) for a polished product presentation.


     • Printify acts as an aggregator, so quality can vary by print provider. In general, Printify’s top
       providers deliver excellent results comparable to Printful 15 , but since each product can be fulfilled
       by different partners, you may see small variations in print or fabric (one t-shirt brand vs another).
       Printify does vet providers and even shows a “Print Provider Performance Score” to help sellers
       choose reliable partners 14 . It also allows switching providers or routing orders to alternate printers
       if one is out of stock. In terms of printing methods, Printify’s network collectively offers DTG,
       sublimation, embroidery, and more – but not every provider does every method. You might use one
       supplier for DTG shirts and another for embroidered hats. The upside is flexibility (you can select
       providers based on print method or blank brands), but the downside is needing to manage
       consistency. Overall, Printify can achieve high quality output, especially if you stick with well-rated
       providers, but it requires a bit more QA on the seller’s part. (Ordering samples from new suppliers is
       wise to verify quality.)


     • Gelato uses a globally distributed network of production partners, somewhat like Printify but with
       automatic routing to an optimal facility. Gelato emphasizes local production, so an order in the US
       might be printed in the US, an order to Germany printed in Germany, etc., using partners that meet
       Gelato’s quality standards. They support DTG printing on apparel and fabric, as well as embroidery
       and standard sublimation for items like mugs 16 . While Gelato’s background is in 2D prints (posters,
       paper), their apparel printing quality has improved significantly since their early beta days 17 .
       Shopify merchants have noted that Gelato’s print and embroidery quality are top-tier, comparable
       to other leading POD services 18 . In practice, Gelato’s quality is very reliable; and because they
       auto-select the printer, you don’t have to manually choose a provider (convenient but with less
       manual control than Printify). They also allow some branding options like personalized thank-you
       cards in orders 19 . To sum up, Gelato delivers consistent quality through its network and is
       especially strong for paper prints and standard apparel, though extremely specialized printing (e.g.
       all-over print cut-and-sew) might not be offered.

Shipping and Fulfillment Speed
Speed and shipping costs are crucial for customer satisfaction. Here’s how the platforms compare,
especially for U.S. orders:


     • Printful: Because Printful operates its own fulfillment centers in the US (North Carolina and
       California) and internationally (Mexico, Europe, etc.), it offers fast and predictable fulfillment.
       Average production time is 2–5 business days, and about 50% of orders ship out in under 3 days
        20 . For U.S. customers, this means orders often arrive within a week. Printful’s U.S. shipping rates

       start around $3.99 for a t-shirt (standard shipping) 21 , and they discount additional items in the
       same order. They have a reputation for reliable, on-time delivery. International shipping is also
       available from their centers; for example, European orders can be fulfilled in Europe to reduce
       shipping time. If speed is a top priority, note that Printful reports 95% of orders are ready to ship
       within 4–7 days and many much faster. Overall, Printful provides consistent shipping times and
       robust global logistics – a strong point of their service 22 .


     • Printify: Shipping times with Printify depend on which print provider you choose for each product
       and where that provider is located. The advantage is you can select printers in the US for U.S. orders
       (or even multiple providers in different regions to cover local fulfillment). Many of Printify’s US-based
       partners also turn orders around in ~2–7 days on average 23 , similar to Printful’s timeframe 24 . In
       fact, Printify notes an average fulfillment time of 2–7 days across their platform 24 . However,
       because there’s variability, some providers might take a bit longer during peak seasons or if they are
      overseas. You have the flexibility to opt for faster providers – for instance, Printify’s network includes
      some print houses known for 1-3 day production. Shipping costs on Printify also vary by provider;
      each sets their own rates. For example, one supplier might charge $4.00 to ship a t-shirt
      domestically, another $5.00 25 . Smart sellers often choose providers strategically (e.g. a printer
      on the East Coast for East Coast orders) to optimize delivery speed. Printify lacks the unified shipping
      of Printful; you’ll need to check each provider’s rates and times. That said, if managed well, Printify
      orders to U.S. customers can also arrive within a week or so. Printify recently introduced an order
      routing feature to automatically switch to an equivalent provider if the chosen one can’t fulfill an
      order, which helps avoid delays (but it’s not exactly location-based routing). Bottom line: U.S.
      fulfillment through Printify can be fast, but it requires choosing the right print partners and
      possibly juggling multiple vendors to ensure consistent 2-5 day production times.


     • Gelato: Gelato’s strength is localized printing. They have 100+ production partners across 32
       countries 26 . When an order comes in, Gelato’s system automatically assigns it to a nearby facility
       to minimize shipping distance 27 . For U.S. orders, Gelato has partners in the U.S., so those orders
       will be made domestically. Their expected fulfillment time is <4 business days on average 28 . A
       basic product like a standard tee shipped to a U.S. address typically arrives in 7–10 days with
       standard shipping, or in 4–7 days with expedited shipping 29 . Some users report even quicker
       turnarounds for local orders – Gelato cites that certain orders can be delivered in as fast as 72 hours
       when the print location and destination are close 27 . Another plus: because Gelato prints locally,
       international orders don’t suffer long transit delays; your customers in Europe or Asia can get their
       items nearly as fast as U.S. customers. Gelato provides a shipping calculator for transparency on
       delivery estimates 26 . In summary, Gelato offers competitive shipping speeds, leveraging its
       global network for quick delivery. In the U.S., it should perform on par with the other two for
       common items (roughly a week or less to fulfill and deliver).

Note on Office Supplies: For items like pens or certain office merch, none of these platforms keep them
in inventory to print on demand, so fulfillment might involve specialty providers (which could have longer
processing or require bulk ordering). If pens are critical, be aware that a POD solution may not deliver them
with the same speed as shirts or mugs, since they’re not a standard product in these catalogs 6 .


Pricing and Profit Margins
Base Product Costs: The cost you pay for each item will impact your profit margin. In general, Printify
tends to have the lowest base prices, Printful the highest, with Gelato somewhere in between (though all
are in a similar ballpark for many items) 30 . For example, a popular Bella+Canvas 3001 unisex t-shirt
costs around $9–$12 base across these platforms. One recent comparison found Printify’s price (using one
of its cheaper print providers) at just over $8 for that shirt, versus just under $11 via Gelato 17 , and around
$11.50 from Printful 31 . Similarly, a standard 11oz ceramic mug might be, say, ~$6 on Printify versus $7–$8
on Printful. Printify’s advantage is that multiple suppliers compete, often driving prices down – plus
Printify offers a Premium plan ($29/month) that gives 20% off all products, significantly boosting your
margins if you have sales volume 32 33 . Printful’s base prices are higher (they position themselves as
premium quality), but they have no monthly fees for basic use. Gelato recently reduced some base prices
and also introduced subscription tiers (Gelato+ at $24/month, etc.) that grant discounts 34 . Without a paid
plan, Gelato’s item costs can be slightly higher than Printify’s, but Gelato does sometimes match or beat
Printful’s prices for similar items 30 .


Profit Margins: Profit per item = retail price – (base cost + shipping). To compare margins, let’s use that
Bella Canvas t-shirt example. Suppose you sell it at $25 retail:


      • Printful’s base ~$11.50 + ~$4.69 shipping = ~$16.19 cost, yielding about $8.81 profit at $25 price 35
         36 .

      • Printify (with a selected provider) might be $9–$11 base; one scenario: $11.40 base & $3.99 shipping
        = $15.39 cost, profit $9.61 at $25 31 37 . With a cheaper provider or Premium discount, the cost
        could be even lower (the cited $8.57 base for Bella+Canvas with Printify Premium 17 would make
        profit margins very high).
      • Gelato’s base ~$10.95 (for that shirt) + shipping (let’s say ~$4) = around $15 cost, so profit roughly
        $10 at $25 (though note Gelato may charge sales tax/VAT on top in some cases).

As we see, Printify can offer the best margins if you leverage its lower-cost providers or premium
discounts 32 . Printful’s margins are tighter unless you price your products at the higher end of the market
(Printful apparel tends to cost a few dollars more, which you must account for in your retail price). Gelato’s
pricing is fairly competitive; on many products the difference might be only $1 or so vs. Printful/Printify 30 .
For instance, one source noted all three now offer similar prices for most products after recent adjustments
  30 .




Shipping Costs: Printful’s shipping for a single apparel item to the U.S. is about $3.99, Printify’s varies (often
~$4–$5 from U.S. providers), and Gelato’s depends on local partner rates. Interestingly, Printful often offers
slightly cheaper shipping on additional items in the same order than many Printify print providers 38 . If
selling internationally, Gelato can save money – e.g. shipping a shirt to the UK might be ~$4.50 with Printful
but $10 via a US Printify provider 39 , whereas Gelato would print within Europe and charge a low local
shipping fee (Gelato was ~$5 to UK in one comparison, vs $14 from a US printer) 40 . Thus, Gelato can

reduce shipping costs for global orders by fulfilling near the buyer. Printify sellers can mimic this by
setting up multiple print files (one for a US provider, one for a UK/EU provider) – but that’s manual.


Volume Discounts: Printful has an automatic volume discount (e.g. 5%–10% off in a month if your sales hit
certain revenue tiers) and offers 20% off sample orders 41 . Printify’s main discount comes from its
Premium subscription (20% off every order) 42 . Gelato+ subscribers get discounted product prices and
shipping rates as well. For a new seller with low order volume, all three can be used for free, so you’ll just
compare base + shipping costs. As you scale, Printify Premium can pay for itself by significantly lowering
cost per item. Gelato+ might be worth it if you do high volume globally. Printful doesn’t require a
subscription for discounts, but you only see savings after you’ve already made substantial sales (volume
pricing kicks in after $1,000 monthly, etc.).


In summary, Printify tends to be the most cost-effective option (especially with subscription) giving
higher profit margins, while Printful charges a premium for its all-in-one service and quality, and Gelato
sits in the middle but shines in saving cross-border shipping costs. Be sure to factor in both product cost
and shipping when comparing – sometimes Printful’s higher product price is offset by slightly lower
shipping, or vice versa.


Integrations with Shopify and Etsy
Seamless integration with selling platforms is a must. All three POD services integrate well with Shopify (the
user’s primary platform) and Etsy (secondary):


     • Printful: Offers extensive integration options – not just Shopify and Etsy, but also WooCommerce,
       BigCommerce, Wix, Squarespace, Amazon, eBay, TikTok, and more 43 . Printful is often praised for
       its easy setup on Shopify and very robust API as well. It also has a warehouse & fulfillment service
       if you want to sell non-POD items. In the Shopify App Store, Printful is one of the highest rated POD
       apps and many merchants mention its smooth store syncing 13 . If you plan to expand sales
       channels (sell on multiple websites or marketplaces), Printful likely covers it.


     • Printify: Also has broad integrations: native apps for Shopify and Etsy (it’s a verified Etsy integration
       partner), as well as connections to WooCommerce, eBay, Wix, and even a custom API 44 . Printify
       slightly lags Printful in number of channels supported (for example, Printify doesn’t directly integrate
       with Amazon Merch, while Printful does). But for Shopify/Etsy, Printify works great – you can
       create products in Printify and publish them to your Shopify store with one click 45 . One unique
       feature: Printify offers a “Pop-Up Store” (a simple hosted storefront) for free, which is handy for
       testing if you don’t have Shopify yet 46 . In general, Printify’s integration capabilities are nearly on
       par with Printful for most small-business needs.


     • Gelato: Provides direct integration for Shopify, Etsy, WooCommerce and a few others 47 . Setting
       up Gelato with Shopify or Etsy is straightforward (install the Gelato app and connect). It might have
       fewer e-commerce integrations than Printful/Printify (for instance, Gelato might not have an out-of-
       the-box connection to Amazon or eBay yet). However, Gelato does support orders via its API and
       through Order Desk, so with a bit of technical work it can connect to almost anything. The main
       point is that Shopify and Etsy are fully supported by Gelato 48 49 – you won’t have an issue
       there. Gelato does not have a “built-in store” feature; it assumes you have an existing storefront to
       plug into.

All three platforms offer automatic order import and fulfillment once integrated: when a customer places
an order on your Shopify or Etsy store, the POD service receives it, prints, and ships without manual

providing tracking numbers).


Summary: If Shopify is your main store, you can choose any of these – Printful and Printify have the longest
track record and very feature-rich Shopify apps, while Gelato’s Shopify app is newer but well-reviewed. For
Etsy, all three will let you list products and fulfill orders automatically (Printful and Printify have been
popular among Etsy sellers; Gelato is a newer entrant but works similarly). Printful edges out with the
widest integration list (useful if you expand to multiple sales channels) 43 . Printify and Gelato cover the
basics and have solid integration performance as well.


Standout Providers for Performance Wear (Athletic Apparel)
If performance clothing (e.g. moisture-wicking workout shirts, leggings, sports jerseys) is a focus, it’s
important to pick a POD that offers those products in quality fabrics.


     • Printful: Printful carries a number of athletic apparel options. For example, they offer leggings
       (both all-over print and solid sports leggings), sports bras, athletic shorts, and shirts made from
       performance blends (some brands like Sport-Tek or Gildan Performance line) 1 . The quality of
       Printful’s activewear is high – they use premium blanks (the leggings are thick and squat-proof, shirts
       have breathable fabric). However, the selection is somewhat limited to the basics; if you need
       specialized items like compression leggings with specific features, you might not find every variant.
       Printful’s printing on athletic gear (like sublimation for all-over designs) is excellent. So Printful is a
       safe choice for general fitness apparel, offering reliable quality and sizes up to 2XL or 3XL in many
       items.


     • Printify: Given its marketplace of suppliers, Printify can provide even more options for sportswear.
       Different print providers offer performance tees (often 100% poly or tri-blend shirts for gym use),
       mesh basketball jerseys, yoga pants, and more. For example, you may find all-over print yoga sets,
       specialized athletic leggings with cut-and-sew, and possibly niche items like sports uniforms,
       depending on the provider. The key is to search Printify’s catalog for “Sportswear” or use their filters
       – you’ll likely see categories for “Activewear” or “Sports & Fitness” 50 51 . One can design entire
       workout outfits via Printify, as they actively promote creating custom gym wear 50 . The caveat is
       that quality and material specs depend on the provider. You should sample from any new supplier to
       ensure the fabric quality (e.g. that leggings are suitably stretchy and opaque). Some Printify
       providers specialize in cut-and-sew leggings made in the USA 52 , which can be a plus. Overall,
       Printify likely offers the widest range of performance apparel (including items others may not
       have, like swimwear or yoga mats), but you’ll need to vet the specific product brand and printer for
       quality.


     • Gelato: Gelato’s apparel range is more focused on everyday clothing; it does have some sportswear
       but fewer choices. You’ll find standard t-shirts (some may be moisture-wicking), perhaps a unisex
       athletic tee or tank top, but Gelato does not yet list a large array of activewear like leggings or sports
       bras in its catalog. Gelato’s strength is not in specialized performance fabrics but rather in fulfilling
       basic apparel and prints. If your store’s emphasis is on a fitness clothing line, Gelato might feel
       limiting unless their catalog expands. They can certainly handle printing logos or designs on, say, a

       polyester dri-fit t-shirt if offered, but if you need a comprehensive athletic line (compression wear,
       track pants, etc.), you might lean toward Printful or Printify.


Other Options: If athletic apparel is your main niche, you might also consider:


     • Apliiq – a POD tailor-made for fashion brands. Apliiq lets you choose high-quality blank apparel
       (including premium fleece, joggers, and some performance fabrics) and add custom labels or
       patches 53 54 . They focus on cut-and-sew and even offer screen printing for bulk orders 55 . This
       means you can create pro-grade gym clothing with your own branding. Apliiq integrates with Shopify
       and could be a great choice for a serious athletic apparel line, albeit with slightly longer
       production times (up to 7 days) 56 and primarily U.S. fulfillment.


     • CustomCat – a POD with 300+ products that include a wide selection of sports apparel (athletic
       tees, performance polos, even team jerseys). CustomCat is U.S.-based and known for competitive
       pricing and quick turnaround. It might offer certain brand-name performance garments (like
       Badger Sport or similar) that you can’t get on Printful/Printify. CustomCat works with Shopify (app
       available) and can be an alternative if you need specific athletic items.


In summary, Printful and Printify are both strong for performance wear, with Printify offering more
variety (through multiple suppliers) and Printful offering reliably high quality and branding options on its
sports gear. Gelato is adequate for basic items but not specialized in this category. For elite fitness brands
looking for more customization, Apliiq or CustomCat are worth a look as well.


Standout Providers for Business Merchandise and Niche Items
The user is also interested in unique business merchandise – things like branded office supplies (pens,
mouse pads, etc.) or other niche accessories. Here’s how the contenders stack up and some alternatives to
consider:


     • Printful: Printful’s catalog includes some office and business accessories, but it’s mostly the usual
       suspects: mugs, notebooks, stickers, laptop cases, phone cases, etc. They do have mouse pads and
       even desk mats (e.g. gaming mouse pad) 57 , which covers one of the mentioned items. However,
       custom pens or similar small promo giveaways are not offered by Printful. If your “business merch”
       needs are things like a company t-shirt, mug, and mouse pad bundle, Printful can handle that
       seamlessly. For more unusual corporate swag (branded pens, USB drives, etc.), Printful won’t have
       those – you’d need to source them elsewhere. One unique item Printful offers is print-on-demand
       name badges and embroidered aprons, which might appeal to businesses, but again not pens.


     • Printify: Because of its vast network, Printify sometimes has quirky items through certain providers.
       For instance, some Printify suppliers offer things like stickers, magnets, keychains, maybe even
       faux leather journals or pencil cases 58 59 . There is a “Stationery” category on Printify which
       includes notebooks and possibly planners 59 . On Printify, you might find mouse pads (yes, several
       providers offer them, including large gaming mats) and even things like pet accessories or jewelry
       that can double as business gifts. But notably, pens are still absent – a search and anecdotal
       evidence confirm that none of Printify’s 900+ products are custom logo pens 6 . Printify does
       support things like travel mugs, water bottles, backpacks, and other corporate gift items which

      could be considered business merchandise. Its strength is in breadth: if you think of a product (a
      branded blanket for your office, a custom coffee tumbler, etc.), Printify likely has it. Just remember
      that for highly specific promo items (pens, stress balls, lanyards), a specialized promotional products
      supplier might be needed.


     • Gelato: Gelato’s focus on paper and prints means it’s ideal for business stationery – you can easily
       create branded notebooks, greeting cards, flyers, even wall calendars with your company’s logo.
       They also produce mugs and apparel that companies might use as swag. But Gelato does not cater
       to the small tchotchkes like pens or keychains. A cool feature of Gelato is the ability to print custom
       greeting cards or thank-you notes and include them in orders, which can be great for a business
       sending out customer gifts 19 . If a business wants to do, say, branded notebooks, calendars, and
       shirts for an event, Gelato is a solid choice. For a full array of corporate promo items, Gelato alone
       won’t cover everything.


Alternative POD providers for niche merchandise: If you’re open to alternatives for unique items, here
are a few worth noting:


     • Gooten – Gooten is a POD network similar to Printify, known for a very wide product range (500+
       product lines) including many unique items 60 . Gooten’s catalog goes beyond apparel: they offer
       things like throw pillows, puzzles, yoga mats, pet bowls, ornaments, and more. For office/
       business needs, Gooten has printed notebooks, coffee mugs, coasters, etc. They even list some
       promotional products like bottle openers and button pins which could be used as business
       giveaways 61 . If pens are a must, Gooten doesn’t have them either, but it does have a reputation for
       “exclusive” products not found elsewhere 62 . Integration: Gooten has a Shopify app and can be
       connected to Etsy via a third-party like Order Desk. It’s a good alternative to source niche merch
       such as custom home goods or event swag that Printful/Printify/Gelato might not carry.


     • Teelaunch – Teelaunch (Shopify app) specializes in unique merchandise. Their product list (~120+
       items) includes things like wireless chargers, Bluetooth speakers, engraved tumblers, cutting
       boards, pet products, and even novelty items like color-changing mugs 63 . They do offer mouse
       pads and desk mats, and while they don’t have pens, they fill other niche gaps – for instance, a
       custom engraved water bottle or a canvas toiletry bag with a company logo. Teelaunch is a great
       add-on if you want to sell creative merch that stands out. They integrate primarily with Shopify. For
       performance apparel, Teelaunch is not specialized (they mostly use Gildan and similar basics), but for
       unique accessories, they’re a strong contender.


     • SPOD (Spreadshirt) – SPOD is a POD service by Spreadshirt known for incredibly fast fulfillment
       (95% of orders in 48 hours) 64 . Their catalog includes standard merch (shirts, hoodies, mugs,
       phone cases) and some fun extras like aprons and teddy bears. They do have some office-friendly
       items like notebooks and stickers, but not an extensive promo line. SPOD’s key advantage is speed –
       if your business merchandise needs often come last-minute, SPOD can deliver quickly. They
       integrate with Shopify. The product range is more limited (no pens), but the quick turnaround is a
       standout feature.


     • VistaPrint (or similar corporate printers) – While not a traditional POD drop-ship integrator,
       companies like VistaPrint offer custom pens, USB drives, stationery sets, etc. in low quantities.
       They require manual ordering (no Shopify auto-sync), but for certain branded items (especially

       pens), you might use them alongside your POD service. For instance, you could print t-shirts on
       Printful and separately order a batch of branded pens from a promo supplier to complement your
       offerings.


In conclusion, Printful, Printify, and Gelato cover most common product needs for a business, from
shirts and mugs to basic office and tech accessories. For truly unique merch (like branded electronics,
luxury journals, or the elusive custom pen), you may incorporate a secondary POD provider like Teelaunch
or Gooten, or use a specialty printer. It often comes down to your priority: if you value one-stop
convenience and quality, Printful is strong; if you need maximum variety and competitive pricing,
Printify is excellent; if you want to print globally and include paper goods, Gelato is very appealing. Many
businesses even use a combination – for example, Printful for apparel and Printify for an additional product
that Printful doesn’t have.

Printful: Pros and Cons
Pros:
- High Quality & Consistency: Printful is renowned for its premium print quality and consistent results
across products 13 . All printing is done in-house with professional equipment, ensuring your designs come
out crisp and long-lasting. Quality control is strict – misprints or defects are rare (Printful’s reported print
defect rate is very low) 14 . This gives peace of mind that customers will get a top-notch product every time.
- Wide Range of Print Methods: With Printful you can offer more than just basic prints – embroidery,
direct-to-garment, sublimation, cut & sew, even engraving on some items are available 12 . This allows



                                                             13
for diverse product types (e.g. embroidered hats with your logo, all-over-printed yoga leggings, etc.) under
one roof. Few POD companies match the breadth of techniques.
- Branding Options: Printful lets you add inside neck labels, tear-away tags, pack-in flyers, and custom
packaging slips. This is great for building a brand – your apparel can have a printed logo inside the collar,
and you can include thank-you cards or stickers in the shipment. Such white-label services help make the
order feel like it came directly from your company.
- Fast Fulfillment (Especially in US): Printful generally fulfills orders within 2–5 days and often faster 20 .
With multiple fulfillment centers in the US (and in Europe), shipping to U.S. customers is quick – many
report receiving items within a week. They also optimize routing (east coast orders might ship from
Charlotte, west coast from LA) to minimize transit time.
- Excellent Integrations & Tools: Printful has one of the best Shopify integrations, plus it connects to Etsy,
Amazon, WooCommerce, and more 43 . It also has a nice mockup generator and even design tools (you can
create simple text designs or use their clipart without external software). The dashboard is user-friendly
with robust features (like product templates, variant syncing, and detailed reports).
- Reliable Support and Resources: Printful offers 24/7 chat support and extensive help docs. They also
have a large community of users, active blogs, and tutorial videos. If something goes wrong with an order,
Printful often fixes it quickly (e.g. sending a replacement at no cost). This level of support is valuable for
business continuity.


Cons:
- Higher Base Costs: Printful’s premium service comes at a cost – their base prices for products are on the
high side 31 . For instance, a basic shirt might cost you $12 from Printful that you could source for $8-$10
elsewhere. This means lower profit margins unless you price your products higher. If your niche is price-
sensitive, this can be a disadvantage. There are no product cost discounts unless you hit high volumes or
subscribe to their Pro plan (which doesn’t reduce base prices, just offers other perks).
- Limited Product Catalog (vs. Printify): While Printful covers all core products, it doesn’t have the huge
eclectic catalog that a network like Printify or Gooten provides 2 . You might find gaps if you want to sell
very specific items – e.g. no print-on-demand pens, jewelry, or certain home goods that some other
platforms offer. If you anticipate needing a really diverse product selection or unusual merch, Printful alone
might feel restrictive.
- No Bulk Discounts on Standard Plan: Other than sample order discounts and tiered monthly sales
discounts, Printful doesn’t give a way to lower costs via subscription (unlike Printify’s Premium). Small sellers
pay the same per-item as everyone else, which can put you at a pricing disadvantage. The profit per item is
thinner – e.g. making <$9 on a $25 shirt sale as shown earlier 37 – so you rely on volume or premium
pricing.
- International Shipping Costs: If you plan to ship a lot to customers outside of North America/Europe,
Printful’s model can incur high shipping fees or customs times because not every item has local fulfillment
worldwide. For example, certain products are only made in the US, so sending them to Asia will take longer
and cost more. Printful is continually expanding facilities, but currently Gelato or local providers might beat
it on localized global fulfillment.
- One Store Per Account Limitation: Printful’s free account typically is intended for a single store
integration (for multiple stores, they’ve historically required separate accounts or their Pro plan). By
contrast, Printify allows up to 5 stores on the free plan 69 . If you, say, operate multiple Shopify stores or
an Etsy and a Shopify, you might need to juggle multiple Printful accounts or upgrade. This is a minor
inconvenience but worth noting for multi-store operations.
- Occasional Stock Limitations: Printful sources blank products (e.g. specific shirt models). Sometimes
popular sizes or colors can stock out during peak seasons, leading to backorders or temporary




                                                       14
unavailability. While they communicate these well and often suggest alternatives, you have slightly less
flexibility than Printify where you could just switch to a different supplier of a similar item.


Bottom Line: Printful is an excellent all-around POD partner if quality and ease-of-use are top priorities and
you are comfortable with slightly higher base costs. It’s particularly strong for fashion/apparel brands who
want branding control and consistent output. For a store centered on fewer, high-quality products (like a
clothing line or professional merch), Printful’s pros often outweigh the cons. Just price your products
smartly to maintain healthy margins, or use Printful in combination with another POD for items where cost
is critical.


Printify: Pros and Cons
Pros:
- Extensive Product Variety: Printify’s biggest selling point is its massive catalog of products 2 . You can
find almost any print-on-demand item imaginable through one of their suppliers – from standard apparel to
niche lifestyle products. This makes it ideal if your business plans to expand into various categories or offer
unique merchandise. You’re not limited to what one company can produce; instead, you tap into dozens of
print providers’ inventories. For example, you could sell t-shirts, mugs, yoga mats, high-heel shoes, pet
beds, and more all via Printify 4 5 . This broad range lets you test different product types and find new
revenue streams easily.
- Competitive Pricing & Higher Margins: With multiple providers for popular items, there is often price
competition, meaning you can find very good base prices on many products. Printify often beats Printful
on price by 10–20% or more for similar items 17 . And if you subscribe to Printify Premium, you get up to
20% off every order 33 – which directly pads your profit margin. This is a boon once your sales justify the
$29 monthly fee (it pays for itself if you sell even as few as ~10–15 shirts a month, for example). Overall,
Printify enables better profit margins; sellers frequently cite pricing as the reason they choose Printify
over competitors 32 .
- Flexibility in Fulfillment: Printify’s model lets you choose or change print providers at will. This
flexibility has many advantages: You can select a printer close to your customer base for faster shipping or
select one based on specific product quality (some might use higher-quality blanks or have better reviews).
If one provider has a stock issue or delay, you can switch to another for new orders. Printify even allows
linking multiple providers to one listing (variant routing) – e.g. your canvas prints could be fulfilled by
Prodigi in the UK for EU orders and by another provider in the US for American orders. This adaptability
can lead to faster delivery times and localized fulfillment without being locked into a single production
source. It’s also a form of risk mitigation – you’re not reliant on one facility.
- Multi-Store Management: Printify allows up to 5 stores on the free plan (and more on Premium) under
one account 69 . This is great if you run several Shopify stores or an Etsy plus a Shopify, etc. All your orders
funnel into the same Printify dashboard, simplifying management. You don’t need separate logins or to pay
extra for multiple storefronts.
- User-Friendly Interface & Tools: Despite juggling many suppliers, Printify’s platform is quite
straightforward. The product creation flow guides you to pick a product, then a provider, upload your
design, and publish. It has a solid mockup generator and even a built-in AI design generator and image
background remover. They also offer a profit calculator so you can see your margin when setting prices
  77  78 . The interface for comparing providers on a product (seeing their prices, locations, print sizes,

customer ratings) is extremely useful when making decisions. Printify’s system automates order routing to
the chosen provider once orders come in, so the day-to-day fulfillment is hands-off like any POD service.
Overall, Printify’s app is beginner-friendly yet powerful for advanced users.

- Global Network & Reach: Similar to Gelato, Printify has printers around the world (US, Canada, UK, EU,
China, Australia, etc.) 79 . You can leverage this to offer global shipping at reasonable speeds by selecting
regional partners. For example, for mugs you might use a European partner to service EU customers
(avoiding customs delays). This network means you can scale internationally more easily. Printify also
continuously adds new print partners and products, staying on top of trends – giving you early access to
new POD products as they emerge.


Cons:
- Inconsistent Quality Control: Because production is decentralized through third parties, quality can
vary between providers 74 . One Printify partner might use slightly different printing equipment or blanks
than another. This means a black hoodie from provider A could feel a bit different from a black hoodie from
provider B, even with the same design. There’s also variability in print color accuracy or durability if one
partner’s calibration isn’t perfect. While Printify’s top providers are generally reliable (and you can see their
star ratings and reprint rates), the burden is on you to vet and choose good ones. There is a possibility of a
dud experience if you pick a low-rated supplier to save cost. In contrast, Printful prints are more uniform.
So, you may need to order samples from each provider you plan to use, and be prepared to intervene
(switch providers or request reprints) if issues arise. Printify does help by collecting reviews and
performance data, but it’s not a single standardized production line.
- Management Complexity: The same flexibility that is a pro can also be a complexity. Using Printify
means you’re effectively managing relationships with multiple print manufacturers through one interface. If
you sell 10 different products, you might end up selecting 5–6 different providers. Each has its own
production times, shipping rates, and possible quirks. This can get complicated: you’ll need to keep track of
which product is with which supplier, and what their specific costs and shipping speed are. There’s also the
maintenance aspect – if a provider changes their product or goes offline, you have to react (though Printify
alerts users and suggests alternatives). In short, Printify requires a bit more oversight. It’s not as “set and
forget” as Printful, especially at the beginning when you’re configuring your product offerings. For some
sellers, this overhead is worthwhile for the benefits; for others who prefer simplicity, it’s a drawback.
- Variable Shipping Costs and Times: Unlike Printful which has a straightforward shipping fee table, each
Printify provider sets their own shipping fees. If a customer orders multiple items from different Printify
suppliers, they’ll pay shipping fees for each (this can make combined orders costly, unless you eat some
cost or set up custom shipping rules). Additionally, delivery times might not be consistent if orders have to
ship from different print vendors. For example, a mug from Provider X might ship in 3 days via USPS, but a
shirt from Provider Y ships in 7 days via UPS – a customer ordering both could receive two packages at
different times. This can lead to customer confusion or higher shipping expenses. You can mitigate this by
grouping products by provider (e.g. perhaps use the same provider for both shirts and mugs if possible),
but it’s a puzzle to solve. Printify doesn’t have a unified shipping rate system for multi-item orders across
providers. So, logistics can be a bit fragmented compared to an all-in-one service.
- Limited Branding Consistency: While you can add your logo to packing slips with Printify and some
providers might support inside labels or inserts, it’s not as cohesive as Printful’s branding options. Since
each provider handles fulfillment separately, you can’t, for example, include the same branded thank-you
card in every order unless each provider supports it. A few providers do allow custom neck labels (for
apparel) or special packaging if you contact them, but it’s not a platform-wide feature. Essentially, brand
packaging is not standardized on Printify. If building a fully branded unboxing experience is important,
Printify will require more workarounds (maybe ordering your own stickers and asking a provider if they can
include them, etc., which is not guaranteed).
- Support as Middleman: Printify’s customer support will assist you via chat/email if there are issues, but
they often have to coordinate with the print provider to resolve problems. This can occasionally slow down




                                                       16
issue resolution. For example, if a customer gets a misprinted item, Printify has to verify with the printer
and then issue a reprint or refund. While they are generally responsive, it’s a two-step process (you to
Printify, Printify to provider) rather than one. Some users have reported that when things go smoothly, it’s
fine, but if there’s a hiccup, you’re one step removed from the actual producer, which can be a little
frustrating. Printify has improved in this area and tends to side with the seller if the provider clearly messed
up, but just be aware that you don’t directly control the production folks.


Bottom Line: Printify is a powerhouse for entrepreneurs who want choice, variety, and better margins.
It’s especially useful if you have a diverse product line or want to experiment with products beyond the
basics. You’ll likely save on costs, which can either increase your profit or allow you to offer more
competitive prices. The trade-off is that you become the coordinator of a network of fulfillers. If you’re
detail-oriented and don’t mind managing those moving parts, Printify can be extremely rewarding. It’s
common for sellers to use Printify for certain products (especially unique or cost-sensitive ones) while
maybe using Printful or another service concurrently for others. If you do use Printify exclusively, just invest
time in selecting great providers and establishing clear shipping strategies. Many successful POD
businesses run on Printify – just go in with the mindset that you’re the operator of a supply chain (with
Printify’s interface making it easier), rather than outsourcing everything to one company.


Gelato: Pros and Cons
Pros:
- Global Localized Production: Gelato’s standout feature is its global network of 100+ print partners in
30+ countries 26 . This means orders are automatically routed to be produced as close to the end-
customer as possible 27 . The benefit is faster delivery and lower shipping costs for international orders. If
you have a worldwide customer base (or plan to), Gelato allows you to scale internationally with ease. A
customer in New York, one in London, and one in Sydney will each get their item printed nearby, drastically
cutting delivery time compared to shipping everything from one country. This distributed fulfillment is
also more eco-friendly (less shipping distance) and can reduce customs issues for your customers.
- Strength in Stationery and Prints: Gelato began with printed paper goods, and they excel at them. If
your business needs to sell custom notebooks, planners, greeting cards, art prints, or business
stationery, Gelato is arguably the best choice. They offer high-quality paper stock options and professional
printing (several paper types, finishes, etc.) 10 . For example, you can easily create and sell your own line of
notebooks or holiday cards with Gelato’s platform. The print quality for wall art and cards is excellent,
often using local print shops that specialize in these items. While Printful/Printify also offer such products,
Gelato’s focus here means you might find more options or better pricing on paper products, plus the
advantage of local production (important for heavy items like framed prints).
- Fast and Reliable Fulfillment: Gelato’s average fulfillment time is less than 4 days, and many orders go
out in 1-3 days 28 . The fact that they can sometimes deliver an order in ~72 hours from purchase (through
local printing) is a huge plus 27 . Even on standard shipping, customers often receive their items quicker
than expected because of that local approach. Merchants have noted how Gelato’s network reduces
shipping times and costs across regions, improving customer satisfaction 18 . Gelato also has a
transparent delivery time calculator so you can get estimates for various destinations upfront 26 . For U.S.
orders, Gelato’s speed is comparable to domestic POD fulfillment (since it is domestic – a U.S. partner prints
and ships). For cross-border orders, Gelato often outperforms others. In summary, if speedy delivery
worldwide is your priority, Gelato is a strong pro.
- Ease of Use (Simplicity): Gelato’s interface is very straightforward, arguably even simpler than Printify’s
because you don’t have to choose among providers for each product. Gelato essentially acts more like




                                                      17
Printful in that you select a product, upload your design, and Gelato takes care of which print partner will
produce it. This can save time during product setup – fewer decisions to make and less research needed.
The platform itself is modern and clean, with a good design tool and decent mockup generator. It’s great
for beginners or those who might feel overwhelmed by Printify’s many choices. You still have control over
product variants and such, but you avoid the analysis paralysis of selecting suppliers.
- High Customer Satisfaction & Support: Gelato has garnered high user ratings (4.8/5 on Shopify app
store) 73 . Sellers often praise the customer service and how problems are resolved. Gelato offers 24/7
support via chat/email, and because they coordinate everything with their print partners internally, you as
the seller typically don’t have to get involved with multiple third parties. Merchants also like Gelato’s
emphasis on sustainability – they consciously optimize for shorter shipping routes and allow options like
carbon-neutral deliveries 18 , which can be a selling point for eco-conscious brands. Additionally, Gelato
provides useful features like the ability to add personalized message cards in orders (nice for thank-you
notes or discount codes for your customers) 19 . They also have an API for advanced users and integrations
with major platforms. Overall, Gelato’s service quality – both in terms of print output and merchant support
– is a major pro. Many who try Gelato comment on how smoothly it runs (“set it and forget it” for global
fulfillment).


Cons:
- Smaller Product Catalog: Gelato’s product offering, while growing, is still only about a quarter the size
of Printify’s 3 . They cover the basics (shirts, hoodies, mugs, posters, cards, photo books, phone cases,
tote bags) 65 , but if you want to sell something outside that core range, you likely won’t find it on Gelato
yet. For example, Gelato doesn’t have things like all-over-print clothing, shoes, bean bag chairs, or many
novelty items. Even within apparel, choices can be limited (maybe a few t-shirt brands whereas Printful/
Printify have dozens). This means Gelato might not be a one-stop solution if you plan on offering a very
diverse catalog or niche products. It’s best suited for a store focusing on a moderate selection of
popular product types (apparel + prints/accessories). If Gelato is missing something critical (say, you really
want to sell custom caps or leggings and Gelato doesn’t have a comparable product), you might need to use
another POD in tandem.
- Slightly Higher Base Costs (Without Subscription): On certain items, Gelato’s base prices can be a bit
higher than the lowest available elsewhere. The earlier example of a Bella+Canvas 3001 tee at ~$10.95 on
Gelato vs ~$8-9 on Printify illustrates this 17 . Gelato has improved pricing recently, but you may still find
that Printify (or even Printful for some products) is cheaper by 5-15%. Gelato offers a Gelato+
subscription (~$24/month) that gives you discounted prices and shipping, which can mitigate this 34 . But
on the free plan, if you’re penny-pinching on cost of goods, Gelato might feel tight. Basically, profit
margins might be slightly lower with Gelato unless you either charge a bit more or subscribe for discounts.
For many, the difference isn’t deal-breaking (and is offset by savings in shipping for international orders),
but pure cost-focused sellers might see it as a con.
- Limited Premium Apparel/Features: Gelato’s apparel lineup tends to be standard mass-market items.
They don’t specialize in premium fashion or unique print placements. For instance, Gelato supports front,
back, sleeve prints (which is great) 10 , but they might not support inside neck label printing or embroidery
on as many products yet. If your brand requires specific finishing touches (like custom labels, sewn-in tags,
etc.), Gelato isn’t oriented toward that in the way something like Apliiq is. Also, while Gelato does offer
embroidery, the selection of embroiderable items could be limited (hats, a couple of polos/hoodies
perhaps). All-over printing (edge-to-edge designs on entire garments) is not really part of Gelato’s catalog,
whereas Printful has some and Printify has many via suppliers. So, for creative freedom in product styles,
Gelato has constraints. It’s tailored more to straightforward printing needs.
- Subscription for Branding and Discounts: Some features on Gelato (like adding your own branding




                                                     18
elements to packing slips, or getting the lowest prices) require a paid Gelato+ subscription 76 . On the
free plan, the packing slip will show basic info and Gelato branding (though it’s fairly white-label). To have a
custom logo on the slip or to include custom inserts, you may need Gelato+. This is a minor con – many
POD services either don’t offer this at all or also charge (Printful includes basic branding on packing slips for
free, Printify allows a logo on the slip for free as well). With Gelato, you might have to invest in their
subscription to get that fully branded customer experience and also to unlock up to 30% product
discounts and cheaper shipping. If you’re doing enough volume, Gelato+ pays off, but for a small store it’s
an added cost to consider.
- Fewer Integrations & Extras: Gelato covers the main e-commerce platforms, but it doesn’t have the
breadth of integrations that Printful does 47 . For example, if you wanted to sell on Amazon or eBay, there’s
no direct Gelato integration (you’d have to use a workaround like exporting orders to Gelato manually or via
API). Likewise, Gelato’s platform, while easy, is not as feature-rich in terms of built-in design tools or push
marketing tools. It’s a bit more bare-bones: you won’t find an image library or advanced analytics in Gelato
that some others might have. They focus on the core of printing and shipping. So, if you enjoy extra
features (like Printful’s mockup maker with models, or Printify’s profit calculator, etc.), you might find
Gelato’s software side more basic. It’s worth noting that Gelato is continually developing, so this gap may
close. But currently, for multi-channel sellers or those who utilize a lot of platform features, Gelato
might feel slightly limiting in integration scope.


Bottom Line: Gelato is a fantastic choice for going global effortlessly and for sellers whose product needs
align with Gelato’s catalog (apparel, prints, mugs, etc.). It especially shines if you’re selling prints or paper
products in addition to clothing – few others can deliver a printed poster to an EU customer as cheaply and
quickly. Gelato’s emphasis on quality, sustainability, and customer support make it a very seller-friendly
platform. If your business model has enough margin to absorb or counter the slightly higher base costs (or
if you join Gelato+), the trade-off is you get a very robust fulfillment network with minimal fuss. It may not
fulfill every niche idea, but for 90% of common merch and gifts, Gelato delivers reliably. Many users pair
Gelato with another POD – for instance, using Gelato for international orders or specific products and
Printful/Printify for others. But if your focus is relatively straightforward product lines and you want to
simplify global fulfillment, Gelato could well be your primary POD partner. It’s a newer player relative to
Printful, but it’s quickly building a strong reputation as evidenced by merchant feedback and its growing
feature set.

        turnaround (often 2-3 days). CustomCat integrates with Shopify (via app) and supports
        WooCommerce and API. If your priority is low cost and a broad apparel selection including athletic
        wear, CustomCat is a solid alternative. Downsides: their interface is a bit dated, and they don’t
        natively integrate with Etsy (you can fulfill Etsy orders via manual import or Order Desk). They also
        charge a $30/month fee for their “Pro” plan to get the lowest prices, but it’s optional. In summary:

• Gooten: Mentioned earlier, Gooten is similar to Printify with a large network of print partners. It has

  candles), kitchenware (cutting boards, coasters), pet accessories, jewelry, puzzles, etc. Gooten can

• Teelaunch: A favorite for creative and techy merchandise, Teelaunch, via its Shopify app, provides
  products like engraved tumblers, color-changing lamps, Bluetooth speakers, wireless charging
  pads, and other “wow” items not commonly found elsewhere 63 . They also carry standard apparel

  certain items). Pros: Very unique catalog, decent base prices, and specialization in non-traditional
  merch. Cons: Only directly integrates with Shopify (though you could manually use it for other
  platforms), and support is smaller-scale than Printful. It’s a great supplementary POD to offer high-
  margin accessories that fans of your brand might love.


• Apliiq: For those building a fashion or fitness apparel brand, Apliiq is worth mention again. It’s
  focused on cut-and-sew apparel and branding. Through Apliiq you can get private label clothing –
  they will sew in woven labels, print inside neck info, add patches or embroidery, etc 53 55 . They
  have a catalog of quality blanks including some streetwear styles (windbreakers, joggers, etc.) and
  you can even create custom products like all-over print shorts with your own patterns. Apliiq’s
  differentiator is the ability to create a truly branded clothing line via POD. They integrate with
  Shopify. Downsides: it’s US-only fulfillment and typically slower (up to a week to make an order) 56 .
  Also, their product range is limited to apparel – no mugs or misc. items. And Apliiq’s base costs are
  higher given the custom touches. Ideal for: a seller whose main business is high-end or
  performance apparel with custom branding – like a premium gym wear line that wants its own labels
  and unique stylings.


• SPOD (Spreadshirt): If speed and reliability are paramount (say you promise very quick dispatch to
  corporate clients or event merchandise), SPOD is an option. With 95% of orders shipping in 48
  hours 64 , they are arguably the fastest POD for apparel and standard items. SPOD’s product range
  is decent (~200 items, including shirts, hoodies, mugs, bags, aprons, etc.). They also offer some

  straightforward shipping costs based on order value. SPOD integrates nicely with Shopify (they have
