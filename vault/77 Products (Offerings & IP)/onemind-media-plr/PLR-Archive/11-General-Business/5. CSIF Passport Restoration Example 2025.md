**1. Violation of the Right to Travel**

The **right to travel** is a **fundamental constitutional
right** protected by the **Due Process Clause** of the **14th
Amendment** to the United States Constitution. The **Supreme Court** has
consistently held that **freedom of movement** is protected under the
Constitution, as established in **Kent v. Dulles**, 357 U.S. 116 (1958).

The suspension of my passport and the restriction on my ability to
travel is a direct infringement on this **fundamental right**, which can
only be restricted by the government if it has a **legitimate and
compelling governmental interest**---none of which has been provided in
my case.

**2. Violation of Due Process Under the 14th Amendment**

The **14th Amendment** guarantees that no state shall deprive any person
of **life, liberty, or property** without **due process of law**. The
suspension of my passport for failure to meet child support obligations
without proper notice, hearings, or a fair opportunity to present
evidence of my ability to comply with such obligations violates
the **due process protections** afforded to all citizens under
the **U.S. Constitution**.

**3. Federal Law on Passport Restrictions for Child Support
Delinquents**

Pursuant to **42 U.S. Code § 652(k)**, the **Secretary of State** is
authorized to deny or revoke a passport for individuals who
are **delinquent in child support**; however, such actions must be
carried out **with due process** and in compliance with **federal
guidelines**. The law does not support the suspension of an
individual's **right to travel** without clear evidence of a **willful
refusal to pay** or an **inability to comply** due to financial
hardship.

Additionally, in **Tate v. Short, 401 U.S. 395 (1971)**, the **Supreme
Court** held that the government cannot penalize individuals for
non-payment of debts without considering their **ability to pay**.
Therefore, the suspension of my passport is unlawful in the absence of
consideration of my financial situation or my ability to comply with the
child support order.

**4. Failure to Follow Procedural Safeguards Under 45 C.F.R. § 303.35**

The failure to provide me with adequate procedural safeguards before
suspending my passport and restricting my travel violates **45 C.F.R. §
303.35**. This regulation mandates that **state child support
enforcement agencies** comply with specific procedural safeguards before
denying or restricting an individual's **rights**---including
their **right to travel**---due to child support delinquency.
Specifically, **45 C.F.R. § 303.35** requires the following:

-   **45 C.F.R. § 303.35(b)(1):**\
    The state child support enforcement agency must ensure that there
    is **written notice** provided to the individual subject to
    enforcement actions, such as passport restrictions, **at least 15
    days prior to taking action**. This notice must include information
    on the **nature of the action**, the **individual's right to contest
    the action**, and the **procedural safeguards** available to them.
    The notice must also inform the individual of the **penalties for
    non-payment** and the **opportunity to request a hearing** to
    contest the enforcement action.

-   **45 C.F.R. § 303.35(b)(2):**\
    The individual must be provided with the **opportunity to request a
    hearing** to contest the action, and a hearing must be provided
    within **30 days** of the request. The hearing must be conducted by
    an **impartial tribunal** that can evaluate the **individual's
    ability to comply** with the child support order and take into
    account any **extenuating circumstances** that might prevent
    compliance. This is critical in my case, as I have not been provided
    such an opportunity, nor have I been made aware of the procedures in
    place to request a hearing.

-   **45 C.F.R. § 303.35(b)(3):**\
    The state child support agency must provide an opportunity for the
    individual to **present evidence** regarding their inability to
    comply with the child support order. This includes the opportunity
    to submit evidence of **financial hardship** or other valid reasons
    for non-payment, as well as the opportunity to challenge the
    suspension of their passport based on **procedural defects** or
    the **incorrect application of the law**. In my case, this
    opportunity was never afforded to me.

-   **45 C.F.R. § 303.35(b)(4):**\
    Any enforcement action, including **passport restrictions**, must be
    based on a **final decision**, made after the hearing, that provides
    the individual with the right to **appeal** the decision. No such
    hearing or final decision has been presented in my case, and I have
    not been informed of my right to appeal.

Failure to follow these procedures and safeguards constitutes a
violation of **45 C.F.R. § 303.35** and leaves the passport restriction
in my case **legally invalid** and **void**.
