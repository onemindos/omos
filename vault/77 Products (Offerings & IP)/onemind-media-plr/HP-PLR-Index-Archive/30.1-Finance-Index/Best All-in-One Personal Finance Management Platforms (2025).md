Best All-in-One Personal Finance Management
Platforms (2025)
Managing personal finances – especially for individuals who are also entrepreneurs – is easier with modern
all-in-one platforms that support both mobile and web access. Below we highlight top personal finance
tools that meet key criteria: Plaid integration for linking bank and brokerage accounts, visual dashboards
for budgets and forecasts, investment tracking, and flexibility to grow with complex personal and business
needs. Each platform includes subscription options (free or paid) and prioritizes security and long-term
financial planning. We also note any founder-friendly extras (like merchant integrations, goal planning, or
multi-currency support).


Top Platforms for Comprehensive Personal Finance

Monarch Money

Monarch Money is a premium all-in-one finance app that consolidates budgets, investments, and goals
into one dashboard 1 2 . It was co-founded by a former Mint product manager and is often seen as a
modern Mint replacement 3 . Monarch securely connects to thousands of financial institutions via Plaid,
Finicity, and MX aggregators 4 , pulling in your bank, credit, brokerage, and even Coinbase crypto
accounts 5 for automatic tracking. The interface is highly visual and customizable – you can view your net
worth, budgets, cash flow, investment performance, and savings goals all in one place 6 . It also
supports collaboration, allowing you to share your finances with a partner or advisor at no extra cost 2 .


Pros:
- Comprehensive dashboards for budgeting, investments, net worth, and goals – provides a high-level
overview of all finances, not just expenses 2 6 .
- Flexible budgeting & goal-setting: Supports zero-based budgets and unlimited personalized savings
goals (you can prioritize goals and link them to specific accounts) 7 . Customizable reports and
categories make it easy to tailor to personal or business expense tracking needs.
- Investment tracking: Automatically updates your portfolio balances; offers insights into asset allocation
and performance. Also syncs with Coinbase for real-time crypto tracking 5 (other crypto or non-traditional
assets can be added manually).
- Modern and secure: No ads or third-party offers, and uses bank-level security for data. Built by a fintech
startup focused on long-term financial planning (Monarch’s team emphasizes continuous improvements
and security best practices).
- Mobile + Web: Full-featured web app plus polished iOS and Android apps for on-the-go access. Data syncs
in real time across devices.


Cons:
- Paid subscription only: No free tier (after a 7-day trial) 8 . Monarch costs $14.99/month or ~$99
annually 9 , which is higher than some competitors (though discounts are sometimes available).
- No built-in credit monitoring or bill negotiation: Focuses on tracking and planning; lacks extras like

- Still new: As a newer platform (launched 2020), a few niche features are limited – for example, crypto

features are robust and the app is actively updated.
- Cost – The price may be steep for those used to free apps, but many users feel the ad-free,

Empower Personal Dashboard (formerly Personal Capital)

Empower (formerly Personal Capital) is a free personal finance dashboard renowned for investment
tracking and long-term planning. It’s best for individuals with multiple accounts and investments,
offering a holistic view of your finances 11 12 . Empower connects all your bank, credit, brokerage,
retirement, and loan accounts in one place – you can track net worth, analyze your portfolio, and even
project retirement outcomes using its built-in tools 13 . The platform uses its own secure aggregation
(and now supports direct crypto account linking in beta) to pull in data.


Pros:
- Robust investment and wealth tools: Empower shines at portfolio tracking – you get detailed
breakdowns of asset allocation, investment performance, and even a fee analyzer for your funds 12 . Its
Retirement Planner lets you model future scenarios, which is great for founders planning long-term
wealth or an exit strategy.
- Net worth and cash flow at a glance: The dashboard shows your net worth trend, income vs. spending,
and portfolio value in real time 11 . It’s excellent for monitoring growth as you add assets (business
proceeds, stock options, etc.).
- Free to use: All personal finance tools in Empower’s Personal Dashboard are free 14 . There are no
subscription fees; Empower makes money by offering optional advisory services, so you can use the app at
no cost indefinitely 15 .
- Secure and established: Over a decade in the market, now under Empower (a large retirement company),
which means strong security practices. Data encryption and multi-factor authentication are standard.
- Mobile + Web: Available on web and has highly rated iOS/Android apps. You can check your investments
or budget from anywhere.


Cons:
- Budgeting features are basic: Empower tracks spending by category and lets you set a monthly spending
target, but it lacks detailed budgeting tools that other apps offer 16 . For example, you can’t create
envelope budgets or very granular spending plans – it’s more about big-picture cash flow.
- Learning curve: The interface can feel complex for newcomers. It’s geared toward seasoned investors or
those comfortable with financial data 17 . New users seeking simple budgeting might find it less intuitive
  18 17 .

- No envelope budgeting or bill management: It won’t track bill due dates or help negotiate bills. It’s
focused on tracking accounts and investments, so you might need a separate tool for day-to-day bill
reminders.
- Advisory upsell: Because it’s free, you may get occasional prompts about Empower’s paid wealth
management. However, these are relatively low-pressure, and the free features are fully usable without
opting in.
- Founder-focused features limited: Empower is excellent for personal wealth growth and retirement
forecasting (helpful to entrepreneurs planning an exit or financial independence), but it doesn’t directly

yet it won’t generate invoices or P&L statements (it’s not an accounting app). Think of it as your personal
CFO dashboard – comprehensive for personal investing and net worth, lighter for budgeting.


Quicken Simplifi

Quicken Simplifi is a powerful yet easy-to-use budgeting platform that’s entirely cloud-based (unlike old
desktop Quicken). It offers a detailed spending plan, bill tracking, and even some investment
monitoring, making it a solid all-in-one choice for individuals who want a handle on both personal and
business finances. Simplifi connects to thousands of financial institutions (using multiple aggregators like
Plaid, Finicity, Yodlee, etc. 19 ) to sync your accounts. The app’s standout feature is its customizable
monthly spending plan: Simplifi analyzes your income and expenses and suggests a budget which you can
fine-tune to your needs 20 . This flexibility means you can manage irregular income or multiple budgets
(personal vs. business) easily.


Pros:
- Robust budgeting & alerts: You can implement any budgeting style (zero-sum, 50/30/20, custom
categories) 21 22 . Set spending limits per category and Simplifi will auto-categorize transactions and
alert you when you’re nearing your limits 23 24 . This is great for keeping both personal and business
expenses in check.
- Income & expense forecasting: While not as extensive as some, Simplifi’s “Spending Plan” projects your
monthly cash flow based on upcoming bills and income. You can also input future transactions (e.g., an
upcoming large purchase or invoice) to see the impact on your budget. This helps visualize short-term
forecasts and adjust before problems arise.
- Investment and net worth tracking: Simplifi pulls in investment account balances and even
categorizes brokerage cash vs. invested assets 25 . It tracks debts and loans too – you get a full picture of
liabilities, with updates on balances (e.g., auto loans, credit cards) and the ability to add manual assets like
property 26 27 . While it won’t analyze your portfolio’s performance deeply, it will include investments in
your net worth and show their value over time.
- Subscription and refund monitoring: A unique perk is Simplifi’s attention to merchants and
subscriptions. It automatically identifies recurring charges (subscriptions) and alerts you to upcoming bills
  28 . It even lets you track expected refunds from merchants – for instance, if you’re waiting on a refund

from an Amazon return, you can link it to the transaction and Simplifi notifies you when it arrives 29 . This
level of detail is founder-friendly, as entrepreneurs can ensure business expense refunds or credits aren’t
missed.
- Custom categories & tags: You can create unlimited categories (and sub-categories) – useful if you want
to separate personal vs. business expenses or track specific projects. Auto-categorization rules can be set
(e.g., tag all Uber rides as “Travel – Business”) 30 , saving time if you have many transactions.
- Mobile + Web with real-time sync: Simplifi has a clean web app and mobile apps. Both support the full
feature set, so you can, for example, add a receipt or check your budget from your phone and see it on the
web.
- Affordable subscription: At about $5.99/month (billed annually at $71.88/year) 31 , it’s cheaper than
most competitors. Often there are promotions for new users (some have snagged ~$2.99/month deals) 32
  33 . There’s also a 30-day money-back guarantee if you’re not satisfied 34 .




Cons:
- No free tier: Simplifi requires a paid subscription (no ongoing free version) 35 . If you prefer completely

- Lacks advanced investing tools: Simplifi will list your investment accounts and balances, but no
advanced portfolio analysis or retirement planning is included (contrast with Empower). It also doesn’t
track stock performance or offer advice – you may need another app or spreadsheets for detailed
investment analysis.
- Missing some niche features: There’s no built-in debt payoff planner or tax planner 36 . You can track
debts and see them in net worth, but there aren’t specific snowball/avalanche payoff calculators. Similarly,
no goal forecasting beyond simple savings goals (though you can manually set savings targets and track
progress).
- Annual billing only: Simplifi charges annually, which can be less flexible (you pay upfront). There’s no true
month-to-month option 32 . This could be a downside if you prefer to pay monthly or just use it for part of
the year.
- Interface could overwhelm total beginners: While relatively user-friendly, the array of features
(spending plan, reports, watchlists, etc.) might be a lot for someone completely new to budgeting. However,
it’s praised for being more visually appealing and modern than some competitors 37 38 .


You Need A Budget (YNAB)

YNAB is a long-popular budgeting app known for its strict zero-based budgeting method and education-
focused approach. It’s ideal if you want to gain total control over personal spending and saving, though
it’s less of an investment tracker. YNAB’s philosophy (“give every dollar a job”) can be very founder-friendly
for instilling financial discipline – ensuring every bit of income (whether a paycheck or business revenue you
take as salary) is allocated intentionally. It supports both web and mobile and can sync accounts via bank
integration (YNAB uses reliable data aggregators to link accounts, including Plaid in many cases).


Pros:
- Excellent budgeting methodology: YNAB is built around four key rules that teach you to allocate
income, handle irregular expenses, and live on last month’s income 39 40 . This system is proven to help
users save money and pay off debt 41 . If you’re an entrepreneur with variable income, YNAB’s method
can smooth out the ups and downs by having you always budget what you have on hand.
- Real-time expense tracking: Transactions from linked accounts import automatically, and you categorize
them into your budget. Mobile apps allow quick transaction entry (useful for cash expenses or snapping
photos of receipts). The result is up-to-date insight into how much is left in each budget category.
- Goal tracking and reports: You can set savings goals or debt payoff goals within categories and YNAB
will show your progress. Monthly reports break down spending by category, helping identify where to cut
back. Over time, you can see trends like “last year I spent $X on travel” easily.
- Multi-platform with cloud sync: YNAB works on web, iOS, Android, and even has an Apple Watch app. All
data syncs via the cloud, so if you update your budget on your phone, it’s reflected on the web app
immediately.
- Education and support: YNAB offers a wealth of tutorials, live workshops, and a active community. For
users not financially savvy, this is a big plus – the app teaches you as you use it, which can be invaluable for
long-term financial growth. Many credit YNAB’s system for building strong financial habits (“the lifetime
lessons…are certainly worth the investment” 42 ).
- Secure bank syncing: YNAB uses partners like Finicity/Plaid to import transactions securely, and if you
prefer, you can opt to manually import or update accounts (some privacy-conscious users like having that
choice).




                                                       4
Cons:
- Primarily budgeting-only: YNAB lacks built-in investment tracking or portfolio visuals. You can input
investment accounts (either not linking them or just entering balances), but the app won’t show stock
performance, asset allocation, or do forecasting. If seeing your brokerage performance or retirement
projections is a priority, you’d need a separate tool for that.
- No automatic forecasting: YNAB doesn’t project your future finances beyond the current money you’ve
budgeted. It focuses on allocating today’s dollars. There’s no feature to simulate a multi-month cash flow or
long-term forecast (aside from setting targets and seeing if you’re on pace). Some users export YNAB data
to spreadsheets for forecasting scenarios.
- Subscription cost: YNAB is one of the pricier budgeting apps at about $14.99/month or ~$109/year (as
of 2025) 43 . There is no free tier after the trial. While many feel it “pays for itself” through the money they
save, the cost is a consideration, especially when alternatives exist at lower prices.
- Steeper learning curve upfront: New users sometimes find YNAB’s strict methods challenging at first –
e.g. handling overspending by moving budget funds, or the concept of “aging money.” It requires a mindset
shift from traditional budgeting. However, once learned, many stick with it for years.
- Limited founder-specific features: Apart from superb budgeting, YNAB doesn’t have extras like invoice
management or business accounting. Entrepreneurs can use it to manage personal spending (and even
incorporate business transactions by creating separate budgets or categories), but YNAB won’t produce
financial statements or integrate with merchant accounts. It’s best paired with a small business accounting
tool if detailed business finance management is needed.


Rocket Money (formerly Truebill)

Rocket Money is an all-in-one personal finance app that excels at automating savings and managing
subscriptions in addition to standard budgeting. It’s a great choice for those who want a hands-off
approach to cutting wasteful spending – helpful for busy founders who don’t have time to comb through
every bill. Rocket Money offers both a free version and a premium subscription. It connects to your bank,
credit, and investment accounts to aggregate transactions (the free version allows basic linking and
expense tracking 44 ). The app’s design is very mobile-centric, but it also has web access for desktop users
 45 .




Pros:
- Subscription management & bill negotiation: Rocket Money’s standout feature is identifying recurring
subscriptions and unwanted bills. It will show all your subscriptions in one place and, with premium, can
cancel subscriptions on your behalf or negotiate bills (like cable, phone, internet) to lower rates 46 47 .
This concierge service is a boon for a busy entrepreneur looking to trim expenses without lengthy phone
calls.
- Automated savings: Premium users can use Smart Savings features – you can set rules for the app to
automatically transfer small amounts to a savings fund, helping build an emergency fund or tax reserve in
the background 48 49 . This is optional but useful for “paying yourself first” effortlessly.
- Budgeting and alerts: Rocket Money lets you create budgets for different categories and will send alerts
when you approach or exceed them. It’s not as granular as some (free version limits the number of custom
categories 50 ), but it covers basic budgeting well. You also get notifications of large transactions or bank
fee charges, adding a layer of security and oversight.
- Net worth tracking: By linking all accounts (including investment accounts), Rocket Money provides a net
worth snapshot similar to other apps 51 . This can be motivating to see as you grow your wealth. It may not
have advanced investment analytics, but simply seeing assets vs. liabilities in one view is valuable.




                                                       5
- Free option available: The free version of Rocket Money allows account linking, expense tracking, basic
budgets, and credit score monitoring. This means you can get started at no cost and upgrade only if you
need the premium perks 35 52 . For cost-conscious users or those testing the waters after Mint’s demise,
this is attractive.
- Cross-platform: Initially a mobile app, Rocket Money now offers web access. The mobile app (iOS/Android)
is highly rated for its clean UI. Founders often appreciate being able to get notifications on their phone
about a pricey charge or a subscription due, acting like a personal finance assistant.


Cons:
- Premium needed for full functionality: Many of Rocket Money’s best features (subscription cancellation,
automatic savings, unlimited categories) require the premium subscription ($6–$12/month) 53 .
Additionally, if they successfully negotiate a bill for you, they charge 30–60% of the annual savings as a
one-time fee 46 53 . While you still save money net, it’s a cost to be aware of 54 .
- Budgeting is less detailed: Rocket Money’s budgeting tool is simpler compared to Monarch or YNAB. It
lacks advanced goal planning or forecast charts. For example, custom categories are limited on the free
plan and even on premium, you must manually assign past transactions to new custom categories 50 . It’s
sufficient for high-level budgeting, but power users might find it lacking some depth.
- No investment analysis: The app will track investment account balances, but you won’t get insights into
your portfolio or performance beyond that. It’s more for tracking spending and saving. Founders with
significant investments or complex portfolios would want an additional tool for analysis.
- Privacy trade-off: To negotiate bills or cancel services, Rocket Money may require additional info (like
account credentials for your telecom provider). While many trust it and it uses encryption, some users
might be uncomfortable outsourcing bill negotiations due to sharing those details.
- Service focus vs. customization: Rocket Money is great at doing tasks for you (canceling, negotiating),
which is a different approach than apps that help you do it yourself. This means if you’re the type who likes
to micromanage every dollar, Rocket may feel too automated. Conversely, if you want automation, this
could be a pro. Just note that for highly customized budgeting or reporting, Rocket Money falls short of
tools like Simplifi 48 .


PocketSmith

PocketSmith is a feature-rich personal finance app known for its powerful forecasting and multi-
currency support. It’s especially useful if you want to project your future finances or deal with multiple
currencies/accounts – scenarios common for entrepreneurs with global dealings or long-term financial
plans. PocketSmith connects to over 12,000 financial institutions worldwide (via bank feed providers) and
can handle accounts in 49 different currencies 55 56 . Its standout feature is a calendar-based financial
forecast: you can literally see your daily bank balance projected years into the future based on budgets,
scheduled transactions, and growth assumptions. This sets it apart as a planning tool for long-range
scenarios (up to 30 years or more into the future) 57 .


Pros:
- Advanced financial forecasting: You can model “what if” scenarios by inputting expected income,
expenses, or one-off events and PocketSmith will project your balances days, months, or decades ahead
  57 . This helps in planning for tax bills, business expansion, or even retirement. The app provides data

visualizations like forecast charts, helping you visualize cash flow over time.
- Multiple budgeting options: PocketSmith lets you budget by week, fortnight, month, or any period 58 –
useful if your finances don’t fit a strict monthly cycle (e.g., rental income or freelance projects). You can




                                                     6
maintain multiple budgets (say one for personal expenses, one for a side business) within your account
and switch views.
- Comprehensive reporting: It offers robust reports and dashboards to analyze spending, income, and
net worth. You can slice and dice transactions by category, tag, or account. The level of customization is
high, which is helpful for a founder reviewing personal vs. business spending patterns.
- Global and multi-currency ready: If you have overseas accounts or deal in different currencies,
PocketSmith will handle currency conversion and let you report in your base currency 55 . This is relatively
unique among personal finance apps. For example, you could link a USD business account and a EUR
savings account, and see a unified net worth in one currency.
- Unlimited accounts (Premium plans): Higher-tier plans support a large number of account connections
(great if you have many bank accounts or brokerage accounts, both personal and business). This scalability
means it can grow with your finances – you won’t hit a cap if your financial accounts expand.
- Flexible data import: Besides automatic feeds, you can import data via CSV, which is useful for uploading
historical data or integrating with other systems. Entrepreneurs might export data from accounting
software and import to PocketSmith for personal analysis, for instance.
- Mobile and Web: Offers a web app and mobile apps (though the web version is where it truly shines given
the complex visuals). Mobile apps allow checking balances and adding transactions on the go.


Cons:
- Costly for full features: PocketSmith’s free tier is very limited (no automatic feeds, only two accounts).
The paid plans with bank feeds and forecasting start around $14.95/month, and the top Premium plan can
be $19.95 to $39.95 per month depending on billing and features 59 60 . This makes it one of the more
expensive options for power users. Many features that other apps include are present, but you’re paying a
premium for the forecasting capability.
- Complexity: New or casual budgeters might find PocketSmith overwhelming 60 . The abundance of
options and charts is fantastic for a financial power-user, but could confuse someone who just wants to
track basic spending. There’s a learning curve to set up your budgets, forecasts, and accounts properly.
- No built-in bill pay or negotiation: It doesn’t handle tasks like bill negotiation or canceling subscriptions.
PocketSmith is more analytical. You’ll get insights but you must take action yourself. For example, it might
forecast that you’ll overdraft in 3 months unless you cut expenses, but it won’t offer to cancel your Netflix
for you – that’s up to you.
- No direct merchant integration: Aside from financial institution feeds, there’s no special integration with
specific merchants or services (e.g., no linking your Amazon account or utility accounts). All data comes
from your bank/credit card transactions. If you need to track invoices or sales from a business, you’d have to
manually input them or ensure they flow through a bank account.
- Limited investment analytics: You can include investment accounts and even forecast contributions or
growth by setting them up as part of your projection, but there’s no sophisticated investment performance
module. It won’t replace a dedicated investment tracker for analyzing your portfolio returns or asset
allocation deeply.
- Customer support/time zone: The company is based in New Zealand. While they serve a global audience,
support response might be at odd hours for U.S. users. Not a major issue, but worth noting for any urgent
issues.

Key Takeaways: All these platforms are modern, secure, and built for long-term financial growth, but
each has its niche:
