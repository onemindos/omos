# snobby-aunt-life-lessons

                                    Snobby Aunt: Life Lessons

                                           By: Terra Lee

                          Published by Terra Lee at Smashwords 2015




                                          Introduction:
       As I grow older every day, I notice my life and my past are all stories with little lessons.
These stories are the jewels of my existence and I love sharing those sparkling gems with all
who’ll listen.

                                             Chapter 1

         My first memories of my aunt Ruby are going to her house for family holidays. Her
house is always excessively clean like she spends 10 hours a day making it just perfect. Ruby is
an elementary teacher and she really enjoys controlling people. Most adults don’t care for this so
she works with small children who don’t have a choice. I try to avoid her because of the way she
talks down to me.
         During one visit I play in her basement and notice white stuff wrapped around the pipes
in her house. I’m very young, maybe 7 years old, and I get her attention stating, “Aunt Ruby, you
have asbestos insulation around your pipes. That’s very bad, they told us about this in school.”
         Ruby tilts her head back and looks down her nose at me replying, “Oh, you’re wrong,
that’s just insulation. Don’t worry, it’s perfectly safe.”
         “But, but Ruby, I know its asbestos and it is really bad!” I declare with more seriousness.
         “Go outside and play, the turkey will be done soon,” she insists, totally ignoring my
pleas.
         Feeling defeated, I slowly make my way through her house, noticing how every room is
totally spotless and a smell of chemical cleaners is strong in the air. As I walk across the living
room the sun shines through the room and I see something that catches my attention. There is no
dust floating in the air. At home, I look into the sun shining through a room and see swirling dust
with each little particle reflecting the light, but not in Ruby’s house.
         Everyone thinks Ruby is a great person because her house is so clean but she is very
uptight about every little detail. Her son is at this dinner which disturbs me because he is so
different. He scares me because he’s not normal, my father told me in private that Kenny took a
lot of drugs and almost died. He aged 30 years in a short time and moves incredibly slow. Even
though he is only 25 he looks nearly 50. His voice is slow and choppy and he appears to be very
sick all the time.
         How could a person as perfect as Ruby have such an imperfect son. A son who nearly
killed himself with heroine and is now permanently disabled. But Ruby’s house is so clean so
she must be a perfect woman!
         I never was close with Ruby because all her conversations had controlling undertones.
She lived a perfect life, never drank, never smoked, was a school teacher, and always had an
impeccably clean and sterile house. She was very guilty of putting people down who didn’t live
life like she did. I remember my mother always wanting to clean her house if Ruby was coming
to visit. It was almost like a competition between the women in my family to appear more perfect
than the other.
         My father who was her brother lived the complete opposite lifestyle. He drank, smoked,
lived at the bar, helped the unemployed by giving them a job at his construction business. He
was in the trenches of life helping out the people who were rejected by society. These are the
people Aunt Ruby wouldn’t even look twice at because she knew she was better than them. My
dad never tried to control anyone and gave all people the freedom to make mistakes and live a
life they choose without judgments.
         You will never see two siblings so completely contradictory of each other. I adored my
dad and all the good and bad of him, while most people looked down on him thinking he was
doing it all wrong. You would think a brother and a sister that are very close in age with
opposing life styles would have different endings but ironically they didn’t.
         My dad died at age 64 from a bad reaction to an anti-depressant he was prescribed to quit
smoking. His funeral was packed with so many people that they couldn’t all fit in the church.
They described him as a public servant who did what he did out of the kindness of his heart. He
may have had a beer and a cigarette in his hand but he touched many people lives and helped
more than he knew. But in my family, he was an outcast and none of the uppity women would
associate with him or let their husbands either. My dad was a bad influence with his beer and
cigarettes, they thought. Everyone treated him like a thing beneath them, except me. I always
treated him as he treated me, with respect and admiration.
         His death was devastating to me and I was an empty person for a long time. What was
really surprising is that I heard Aunt Ruby died shortly after my dad. She died of lung cancer
very suddenly. How could a woman who lived a perfect life die of lung cancer? Was it those
insulated pipes? Was it all the toxic cleaners she exposed herself to? Maybe it was both
combined?
         In the end, no matter how perfect you try to appear to people the only thing that matters is
what you do with your life. You can try to control people and die with a clean house and a small
funeral. Or you can do what makes you happy and help other people and die with a legacy that
never ends.
         The real crazy part of my father’s death is now that he’s buried in the ground, the women
in my family revere him. The go to his grave and worship his memory leaving flowers and
respect. All those years I was the only one in my family that gave him any attention and now
after he’s dead they think he’s a great man?
         I guess that goes to show you that all the haters you cross paths with in life only hate you
because you’re better than them and they know it! Don’t let the snobby losers drag you down to
their level! They may appear to be perfect from the outside but inside they are critically flawed!
