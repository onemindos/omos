# excelling-as-an-introvert

Excelling as an Introvert - Easy steps to be successful at work, and at
                                 life.

                          By Michael Lacks
Would you like to be more successful at work while still being yourself?

       Being an introvert is a unique experience. Introverts get tired easily. With enough rest, one can
make strides in social ladder ascension, but it takes almost all of the energy out of us, and it takes a
while to gain it back. Backing down from committed social engagements can be alienating and
awkward. Writing down what we need to say might take a little effort but is totally flawless once we
have enough time to get things just right, yet speaking clearly at a moments notice is something that
seems to cause us difficulty.
       This book is about providing a way to harness the inner ability for thought, quietness and
planning so that you can let your introverted tendencies be an advantage to become a successful person
at work, rather than a hindrance to your productivity. Its also small enough to finish in one sitting, so
you can spend less time reading and more time actually improving yourself. Isn't that the idea of self
help books?
                         Chapter 1 – All you need is confidence

         Introverts typically come off as having low self confidence, however being loud or boisterous
isn't the only way to assert yourself. Consider some of the qualities you have that which extroverts
may not have figured out yet or even be aware of. Introverts have an ability to look into their own
minds and evaluate how they have performed, in a way extroverts may not understand. One of these
traits is abilities is the talent of planning. Lets go into how this helps and how you can use it to your
advantage.


           Confidence is not “I will be fine if they like me.”, Its “I will be fine if they don't”


                           Generally, how does this person solve the problem?

                                                Strategy 1
 Introverts are naturally good at planning. Studies show that feeling assured in a particular task or
endeavor is simply planning for it. Writing it down, thinking it out. Searching “How to plan” on the
internet will yield thousands of results.

                                                  Strategy 2
       But what if your environment makes it difficult to plan? We know that life doesn't always grant
us control over our surroundings. Surprise meetings appear out of nowhere, deadlines rear their ugly
head days and even weeks ahead of schedule. Planning in today's workplace can sometimes feel like
learning a game where the rules always change – its super difficult to win. So rather than formulate a
programmer-like procedure for the day to day operations of your career, what you need to do is
formulate a strategy. Its deceptively simple, and you won't need to buy anything to pull the answer out
of me. I call it the “step-by-step process” Its this:

1. Figure out what you want,
2. Take a small step toward it
3. Learn from taking that that step
4. Take another step
5. Learn from that one


        This is probably something you're doing already. If so, then this is just a friendly reminder that
you already have all the tools you need to take control of your own workday. If not, then you might
benefit from an example. Lets start with a weekly report that is due every Monday morning. Your
desire is that you want it completed thoroughly and submitted on time, every week. Perhaps the issue
you're having is that you're meeting your deadline, but your report always seems to have mistakes. The
small step you're taking could be isolating one mistake out of the many that you end up submitting, and
keeping a checklist nearby when reviewing the report to keep track of the errors you want to keep from
being included. You'll learn from taking that step that you don't really have that many mistakes to worry
about, and might focus on eliminating more than just a few each week. Take another step and
concentrate on fixing even more mistakes. Learn from your checklist and and repeat the cycle again.
                                    What will the results be?

                                                 Benefit 1
        Proper planning brings focus to other parts of our life. If routine, mundane tasks can be
completed automatically, even autonomously, we can devote more brainpower to other areas of our life.
A good plan for tasks is like a well written program. After using it for a little bit it will seem second
nature to you.

                                                Benefit 2
       Proper planning also bring control. Not the Dr. Evil kind of control, but the ability to have a say
in what happens to you in life. The amount of control you have over your own life is in direct
proportion to how well you can set achievable objectives for yourself, and working to accomplish
those goals through persistence and repetition.
                               Chapter 2 – The nuts and bolts
        The meat of potatoes of being successful at work is networking. Its not always what you know,
but who you know. We as introverts hate networking because it feels inauthentic and fake. Small talk
makes us queasy. If you're like me, and really don't occupy yourself with the daily affairs of random
people, shmoozing is a four letter word! Networking can be seen as a chore, or an opportunity – its up
to you. Here's how to make the most of it.

                                                 The steps

                                                  Step 1
       Load up some questions. Networking isn't something you do during company Christmas parties,
or when you intentionally interact with co workers over social media. Networking is something that is
happening every minute of every day whether you're there or not. Eventually you'll have the
opportunity to start a conversation. Just be yourself – you're good at that. Light topics about a favorite
book or why they chose their major are easily accessible by and not too emotionally draining. Its even
okay to ask deep, meaningful questions about life, just keep the topic shallow enough to not alienate
anyone. Asking someone about how they find value in their occupation will invariably go much better
than asking them why they left their family for a co-worker's spouse.

                                                  Step 2
        Listen. Something introverts are naturally good at. Listening is probably the most important part
of networking. It might pay forward to note that humans have two ears and one mouth. Listening
intently has a few advantages to being a Chatty McChatterson. Besides the benefits of appearing to be
more observant and thoughtful than the average person, you actually learn and store from the
conversation things about life from another person. This is important, and key. After a couple of
instances shooting pre-loaded questions and listening for their answers, you'll have a magazine of
unique experiences to share every time you network.

                                                    Step 3
Respond. Carefully. Just like in basic listening how-to's, Its not always necessary to have a solution to
someone's problem on the spot. Sometimes people just want to be heard. Its important in networking to
listen not to fulfill a goal, but simply to network. People like to be heard, and if you're willing to hear a
someone out, they're going to like you. That's all you really need to do. If the networking process gets
overwhelming, politely excuse yourself from the conversation and recharge your batteries.
                                       Chapter 3 – Facing failure

                                                Obstacle 1
       Try not to over-think what you're going to say. Many people will follow the previous steps and
get caught in the cycle of perfecting their questions. Please don't be these people. People aren't perfect,
so your questions to get yourself in their good questions will actually be better off if they're not perfect.
It shows humility on your part, which gives the other person an opening to reveal some of their flaws
too. This can make the conversation more honest, and far more memorable. This will get you
remembered on a positive note.

                                                 Obstacle 2
        Fear of being judged. The fear of being judged is a very real and common part of social
networking. Heck, its part of life. Its an often-non discussed facet of every day life that even extroverts
have to deal with. Its okay to want to be the best version of yourself. Its normal to wonder if people
will look down you. Just don't let it keep you from putting yourself out there. The fear of the response
in general may also be an example of this type of mindset.

                                          How to overcome them:

                                                 Step 1
       Someone else's decision to judge you isn't something you can control, so don't worry about it.
You don't want to bother yourself with people who are going to judge you based on a few
conversations. Networking is about connecting with people, but just like any occupation, you're going
to end up running into favorable and unfavorable people. Its fine. Just keep rinsing and repeating your
method of starting up conversations and learn to prolong those with people that make you feel good,
and cut short everything else.

                                                 Step 2
      This may come as a surprise to plenty people: No one really cares about you enough to judge
you. Unless you've directly and personally affected them, just about everyone is too occupied with
themselves to spend any amount of time making fun of your silly questions. And suppose they do judge
you. What's the absolute worst thing thing that could happen? Absolutely nothing. Rest easy.

                                  Chapter 4 – The glorious wonder

       So there we have it. A fool proof method to become successful at work. Its really about,
planning, networking, and not caring what people thing when you've got something to say.

                                      Why is the hard work worth it?
       The hard work is worth it. You'll have a much easier time at work when people know that under
your cool exterior you're actually a personable person.

                                            What to expect next
       So what's next? After you develop a system for yourself to engage in conversations, you will
have the tools you need to be successful at work. Simply tailor your process for other desires and goals
in your life. Keep trying until you get the results you want.
