          Life is a series of experiences, each one of which makes us

Most of the automobiles in the early 20th century were owned by the rich and
they consisted of complicated machines which were mostly driven by
chauffeurs who were conversant with the mechanical nuances of the given
automobiles. This is where Henry Ford came in, because he had a vision of
building a car that was simple, affordable and reliable. He wanted to provide
the average American worker with the chance of owning a car too. This is
why he made two innovations namely; the assembly line and the Model T
that caused a revolution in the American society and created a basis for the
present day world.

Henry Ford was not the person who invented the car. In the actual sense, he
came up with an automobile that was affordable to the average American.
He was different because other manufacturers targeted the wealthy in order
to make higher profit margins while he went for the average person. He is
the one that came up with the design for the car and a way of manufacturing
it that made it possible for the price of the Model T to drop considerably. The
ordinary person would have pocketed the profits from this innovation but
this was not the case with Henry Ford. He opted to reduce the price of the
car therefore increasing sales to the point of changing the automobiles from
being luxury toys for the rich to becoming the mainstay of the American
society.
1908 was the year that the Model T appeared on the market and was sold at
$825.00. The quantity of this model sold in this year alone was over ten
thousand which was a new record. Its price further dropped down to
$575.00 in the course of the next four years which greatly increased sales
because more and more people could afford it then. The sales of this
automobile continued to grow over the years to a point where Ford owned
48% of the automobile market by the year 1914.

Apart from the desire to create an affordable car, Ford went further to create
an assembly line that would continuously find ways of increasing the
efficiency of the automobile and reduce its cost. Although this concept
wasn’t originally his, he perfected it.

Before Ford actually did this, teams of skilled workmen were in charge of
individually crafted cars and this procedure turned out to be very slow and
expensive. Therefore, the creation of the assembly line served to correct this
since it reversed this process used in manufacturing automobiles. This
worked in such a way that instead of workers going to look for individual
cars to work on, the cars actually came to them and a worker would perform
the same task for different cars. Therefore, everyone had their role in
assembling the automobiles. After the introduction of this process, it was
perfected and this significantly reduced the amount of time required to
assemble the Model T. While it previously took about twelve and a half hours
to do this, this new system made it possible to assembly the automobile in
less than six hours.

The Model A automobile was invented in 1903 by the Ford Motor Company.
The company proceeded in producing the Model N in 1906 although they still
hadn’t succeeded in producing a car that was simple and affordable. This
was what Henry Ford accomplished by producing the Model T. There was a
secret room specifically set aside for the design of this automobile.

According to Charles Sorensen who had become part of Ford’s team two
years earlier, Henry Ford went to see him at the Piquette Avenue plant
during the winter period of 1906-1907. He then said he wanted to show him
something and took him to the north end part of the third floor where
assembly work was taking place but the room wasn’t fully occupied. Ford
then told him to have the room finished off and put a wall up. He proceeded
to say that he wanted the door to be big enough for a car to run in and out.
On top of that, the door was to have a good lock. Ford told Charlie to inform
him when that was done in order to bring Joe Galamb to that room since it
was the beginning of a completely new job. This was the start of the
innovation of the Model T.

Within a few days, the room had been blocked off just as Ford wanted and a
few power tools had been put in place for Joe Galamb to work with in
addition to two blackboards. The blackboards were very important in making
a king-sized drawing which could be photographed after some refinements
had been made and this was purposely for two reasons. It would help in
case of patent suits that would come forward to claim the original idea and it
was also meant to substitute blueprints. A year and so afterwards, ford
introduced Model T to the world. However, Ford realized what a huge market
they had a year after unveiling it.

Charles goes on to say that something happened the summer before he
received the instructions of sealing off the room from Henry Ford. This event
affected the entire automotive industry. The United Steel Company had a
plant in Canton, Ohio which received the initial heat of vanadium steel. The
ford company had received many visits from different people in that year
and examples included J. Kent Smith who was a renowned English
metallurgist and who was among the key players in the development of
steel.

Henry Ford, Willis and Charlie paid attention to him and studied the data he
presented. They had already heard about the English vanadium steel and
read about it too. Its tensile strength was about thrice that of the steels they
were using at the time. However, they were yet to see it. Smith showed
them how tough the steel was and demonstrated that it was easier to
machine it compared to the plain steel even though it was strong. During
this meeting, Henry Ford realized the great possibility that the steel had and
he waited for Smith to leave before telling Charlie that using that steel
meant having completely new design requirements. He went on to say that
they could use it to come up with an automobile that was better, lighter and
cheaper.

Henry Ford had great common sense and this is what he incorporated into
his fresh ideas. He also had the gift of making complex problems appear
really simple. The demonstration that Ford received of the vanadium steel is
what made him decide to get into experimenting with Model T.

Model T was actually developed in about four years. There were previous
models made but these were mostly for experimentation in order to see if
they could actually create a car that would make Henry Ford’s dream of
giving everyone the chance to own a car come true. This had to be a car
that could be driven anywhere by anyone and that was easy for anyone to
repair. Several of the mechanical discoveries in the world were usually a
result of an accident in the experimentation process. However, this was not
the case with Model T. This automobile set the pace for the developments in
the transport sector. In addition to that, it started a chain reaction of
producing machine that we now refer to as automation. All the experiments
conducted with regard to manufacturing the automobile were geared
towards a fixed and amazing goal.

Although Ford announced the automobile model, Model T in March 1908,
they were not ready to produce it at that time and they did so on October 1.
This is the time they introduced it to the public. The little room on third floor
that Joe Galamb was working from came a revolutionary vehicle. This was
just the beginning and the eighteen years that followed saw the assembly of
over 15,000,000 automobiles from the same Piquette Avenue, Highland
Park.

How the Assembly Line was developed

In July 1908, the development of the assembly line started and Sorensen
and a plant foreman were in charge of this. At Ford, they practiced moving
different tasks from one worker to the next up to a point where it turned into
a complete unit. After that, the flow of these units were arranged at the
appropriate time and place until it turned into a moving final assembly line
and this is how the finished product came about. Although some of these
principles had been previously applied in the manufacture of automobiles,
the direct line that Ford used was much better and the intensification of the
automation was as a result of ideas that the Ford Motor Company created as
early as between 1908-1913. Actually, handling the necessary materials that
were brought in for the assembling of the automobile was more difficult than
the process of assembling the car.

Charlie Lewis worked hand in hand with Henry Ford in order to bring in the
necessary material for this job. They only chose the ones they referred to as
“the fast-moving materials.” There were some parts that required a lot of
space and these were mainly the bulky ones such as engines and axles.
Therefore, they resorted to storing some of the lighter materials at a storage
building. The northwest comer section of the place housed the storage place.
They then made arrangements with the storage department to the effect
that they would deliver various divisions of the material according to how
they were marked and packaged. This was to be done at regular intervals.

Handling this storage issue took up a lot of money and Henry Ford didn’t like
it. This is the time he caught himself thinking that having an assembly line
would make things easier and faster especially if he implemented the plan
he had. He was thinking that it would work if they moved the framework
along by starting at one part of the plant that has a frame. They would then
add the axles and wheels to it as it moves along. It would go past the
stockroom which was better and would save time compared to when the
stockroom would be moved to the chassis. Ford made Lewis arrange the
materials in such a way that the materials or parts they needed for the
assembly process were at the beginning point of the assembly and the rest
of the parts were placed lined along the line up to the end of the building
where the final parts were. Henry Ford and his team actually did this every
Sunday of July until the planning was over. One Sunday morning when
everything was ready, Henry Ford and Lewis together with other helpers
managed to assemble the first car and it was one of a kind because it was
built on a moving line which is something that hadn’t happened before.

They first put the frame on skids and then hitched a towrope which
connected to the front end. They then pulled the frame along up to the point
where the axles and wheels were added on to it. They then wanted to prove
what one could do with it and they did this by rolling the chassis along which
they did in notches. There were some subassemblies that needed to be
taken care of and an example is completing a radiator and giving it all the
hose fittings it needed in order to make the process of placing it on the
chassis an easy and fast one. This is what the Ford team did in the process
of demonstrating the moving line. They also applied this with the dash. After
that, the steering gear was mounted together with the spark coil.

Implanting the Assembly Line

Although Henry Ford and his team had established the basics of what made
up the assembly line, they were yet to implement it. This concept was put in
play five years later. This is due to the fact that some things were first
required for this to happen. For example, the new Highland Park had to be
constructed since it was meant to be helpful in incorporating the assembly
line. The engine was assembled at the third floor of the Ford building and it
moved down to the lower levels until it reached the ground floor and this is
where the attachment of the body to the chassis took place.

This process continued and by August 1913 everything related to the links
required for the moving assembly lines was done. The only thing remaining
was the most spectacular one and this was the last to be completed. It was
the one they had tried out five years early on one of their Sundays. They
repeated the process of hitching row to a chassis although it was pulled by a
capstan this time round. Every part of the automobile was incorporated into
the chassis at the appropriate time. The entire process followed the accurate
plan from the axles that were included at the start to the bodies put in place
in the last parts of the assembly process. These parts required different
durations to attach and so various spaced intervals were put between the
durations that the parts were delivered along the line. In order to do this,
one needed to have patient timing and be able to rearrange the various
parts correctly until the process of how the parts were flowing were perfectly
synchronized with the intervals provided along the assembly line. This had to
be done at every stage of production. This process was completed before
that year ended and out of it came a power-driven assembly line. By New
Year, they had installed three more. This was the start of a new era in the
area of industrial history and it was preceded by mass production by Ford.

My name is Henry Ford and I was born on 30 July 1863 AD in Greenfield
Township, Michigan, U.S. I am an entrepreneur and the founder of the Ford
Motor company. I became among the wealthiest and richest people in the
world as a result of owning Ford Motor Company. This led to the birth of the
concept known as “Fordism” which involved producing cheap goods in large
amounts and was characterized by giving workers higher pay. I was able to
make several innovations with regard to business and the technical field and
all these were in line with my commitment to provide products to the
American society at reduced costs.

Childhood and Early Life

I was born on a farm located in Greenfield Township which is an area close
to Detroit. My parents were William Ford and Mary Litogot. My father had his
roots in the Western England although he was born in Ireland. My mother on
the other hand originated from Michigan.

My father gave me a pocket watch when I was in my teens. By the time I
was 15 years old, I had earned the title of a “watch repairman” due to the
fact that I used to dissolve and reunited the various timepieces of my friends
and neighbors. My mother passed away in 1876 and this really depressed
me. My father hoped I would someday take over the family farm but I told
him that the only reason I loved it was because my mother was there. I left
home in 1879 and got my first job in Detroit city where I worked as an
amateur machinist. I was initially employed by James F. Flowers & Bros
before joining the Detroit Dry Dock Co. later on. I decided to go back home
in 1882 where I worked at the family farm and this is where I learnt to
operate the Westinghouse portable steam engine. With time, the company
appointed me to be in charge of steam engines servicing. During that time, I
was also a student at a college in Detroit known as Goldsmith, Bryant &
Stratton Business College where I concentrated on bookkeeping.

Career

I worked with Edison Illuminating Company as an engineer from 1897 and in
1893, I was promoted to the chief engineer. This gave me ample time to
focus on my personal experiments that I was conducting on gasoline
engines. I continued with my experiments and was able to make my first
propelled vehicle in 1896 and I gave it the name Ford Quadricyle. I took my
vehicle for a test drive several times on June 4 and this gave me ideas on
the kinds of improvements I could make on it. In 1896, I got an introduction
to Thomas Edison during a meeting and he liked my automobile experiment
which encouraged me to continue experimenting.

By 1898, I had designed my second vehicle and resigned from Edison.
Detroit lumber baron William H. Murphy had given me capital which I used
to start my company known as Detroit Automobile Company. This company
was established on August 5, 1899. The company developed expensive and
poor quality automobiles and this is why it got dissolved in 1901.

I received C. Harold’s Will’s support in coming up with the design of a 26-
horsepower automobile and then building it. This automobile became
successful which made Murphy and the Detroit Automobile Company
stockholders to join forces and establish a new company by the name Henry
Ford Company. This new company was established on November 30, 1901
and I was appointed the chief engineer. In 1902, a consultant by the name
Henry M. Leland was introduced into the company by Murphy and this is the
time I left although the company had my name. Murphy later changed this
name to Cadillac Automobile Company. I went on to join forces with Tom
Cooper who was a former racing cyclist and together we developed an
amazing racer “999” which was an 80+ horsepower. In the late 1902,
Barney Oldfield rode on it in a race and he emerged victorious.

Establishing the Ford Motor Company
Alexander Y. Malcomson was an old acquaintance and also a coal dealer in
Detroit. Alexander offered his support and together we established “Ford &
Malcomson Ltd” which was an automobile manufacturing company. My job
was to design a cheap automobile after which we both decided to lease the
factory. We had a contract with John and Horace E. Dodge who owned a
machine shop and he agreed to provide us with $160,000 although it wasn’t
in cash but in parts. During this time, the sales greatly reduced and it was
difficult to pay the Dodge brothers for their initial shipment. Therefore,
Malcomson brought in other investors which made the Dodge brothers to
own part of the company. Ford & Malcomson was later reabsorbed on the
16th of June 1903 and it was named Ford Motor Company and its original
investors were still the Dodge brothers, Horace Rackam, I, Malcomson,
James Couzens and John S. Gray who was an uncle to Malcomson.

After designing a new car, I became busy organizing an exhibition for it on
Lake St. Clair (on the ice). It only took him 19.4 seconds in this newly
designed car to drive a mile which brought the land speed record to about
91.3 miles/ hour. Barney Oldfield who was the race driver named the model
“999” after seeing its racing capabilities and how it turned out to be a
success.

Barney Oldfield was famous for his racing skills and he drove around in the
new automobile which gave me a lot of recognition in the Unites States and
almost everyone knew about my brand. Apart from that fame I was one of
the people who initially backed the Indianapolis 500.

Establishing the Model T

The Model T came in to the market on October 1, 1908. This model had
some new innovations. For example, it had a steering wheel on the left, four
slid black cylinders and it had both an enclosed engine and transmission
among other things. This was a very easy car to operate and drive. In
addition to that, it was very cheap and could be repaired at a minimum cost.
This car was sold for only $825 dollars in 1908. The price of this car
continued to go down year after year. The unveiling of this car brought me
excessive publicity because all the newspapers in Detroit carried both stories
and advertisement of this new model. With time, everyone in North America
knew about this new vehicle. I had the vision of creating cheap and efficient
products which everyone could afford and this is how I managed to set up
moving assembly belts in 1913. This benefitted a lot of people particularly
the farmers which greatly increased production. The sales kept on increasing
day by day to a point where all the second cars in America were Model T by
1918. The Ford Motors company made a final complete production of 15,
0007, 034.

I later made my son president of the Ford Motor Company in December
1918. However, I was still in charge of making the final decision authority.
Edsel Ford was my son’s name. I then established a company by the name
“Henry Ford and Son.” I made it seem like I was out to take my best
employees at Ford Motor Company and have them work for me at “Henry
Ford and Son.” In actual sense I only wanted the stockholders of Ford Motor
Company to get scared of losing money if the company collapsed and think
of selling me their stakes. My plan worked and I purchased all of their shares
together with my son therefore making it a family business. In the mid
1920s, there was a lot of competition in the automobile company which led
to a great decline in the sales of Model T. Edsel, advised me to try and
make Model T a bit similar to the newer models in the market in order to
increase sale but I was adamant.

Later Career

The sales continued declining since people preferred the new models and I
came to the realization in 1926 that I couldn’t depend on Model T only since
it wasn’t bringing in the sales it used to. I then came up with something new
“Model A.” I worked together with Edsel and we both had our roles. I was in
charge of the technical part of the car which included designing the engine
and chassis among others. Edsel was in charge of exterior body of their cars.
Model A was unveiled on December 1927 and was well received by the
public. Its production increased to over 4 million by 1931. The Ford
Company then resorted to changing their model system annually.

Establishing the Ford Airplane Company

Apart from automobiles, I also got into the aviation business during the
period of World War I when I developed Liberty engines. After the war, the
company got back to the business of automobiles. However, it became the
owner of the Stout Metal Airplane Company in 1925. I built my aircraft, 4AT
Trimotor also referred to as Tin Goose and it became successful. This plane
resembled the Fokker’s V.VII-3m. Although word of this plane had reached
far, it hadn’t yet taken its first flight and it did this on June 11, 1926 and had
12 passengers on board.

International Business

I had the vision of taking the company international. In addition to that, I
believed that international trade and cooperation was a way of achieving
global peace. This is partly why I expanded the ford assembly plants to
places like Britain and Canada. My company managed to become the largest
automobile manufacturer globally when I took it international. In 1912, I
joined hands with Agnelli of Fiat and launched Italian automotive assembly
plants which were the first of their kind. We then expanded in to other
places and the first plant was established in Australia, India, Germany and
France. We did this and found ourselves with dealerships on six different
continents by around 1929. I tried different things such as the time I bought
a commercial rubber plantation located somewhere in the Amazon Jungle.
Fordlandia was the name of this plantation and it didn’t do well. Joseph
Stalin invited me to establish a model plant at Gorky and I accepted. The
fact that the Ford Motor Company had expanded in to various countries
meant there had to be some policies put in place to enable the company to
do this smoothly. The Ford Motor Company was allowed to carry out
transactions in all the countries with diplomatic relationships with the United
States. This enables the company to extend its branches far and world by
1932, it had grown to an extent of manufacturing 1/3 of all the automobiles
worldwide. According to the public, “Fordism” was an illustration of American
capitalist development and they used the automobile industry specifically to
learn about how the United States conducted their economic and social
relations.
