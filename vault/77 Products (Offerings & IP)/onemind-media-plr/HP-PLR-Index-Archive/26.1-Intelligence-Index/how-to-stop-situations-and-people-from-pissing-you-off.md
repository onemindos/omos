board tracking' - or pathways within our brain - when a thought is repeated across them, can be

we feel, and this attitude will gradually strengthen until every day in our life is so much brighter
and happier than it was previously, or, we can think negatively and pessimistically about things
all of the time and then we feel terrible, all of the time.
    Thinking like this over a long period of time can induce physical illnesses within our body as
the brain and the body are connected much more than we realize. Doctors have known this for
many years. We can sometimes become our own worst enemy in other words. But the latter
doesn't have to be that way. Here's the thing though...only we can do this for ourselves.
    Let this article's helpful and strengthening advice help you to be able to achieve a more
positive outlook on life and about yourself as a person, as I was where you are now -
exasperated, negative, and downbeat. I chose to get myself out of that mindset after many years
of having enough of it, more than enough of it to be honest and, on reflection, I wish that I had
carried out the helpful suggestions below - a lot sooner! But we live and learn don't we, and it's
only on reflection that we see things a lot clearer - afterwards.
   I suppose we all know when we have had enough of a situation, and it is then that we do
something about it. I would like to help you to make that transition easier by providing you with
some helpful thoughts and solutions that saved me from sliding further down a slippery slope -

yourself, before it is too late. Please try the suggestions - they do work.
   I have always been a positive, optimistic soul for all of my life but, just like everyone else,
even I too have had occasions where, I have reached the bottom of the pit - caused by problems
and obstacles and annoying individuals - that I allowed to drag me down. I emphasize the word -
allowed. We allow all sorts. We don't have to. I now realize that I had a choice and that I didn't
have to allow things to happen to me. We can change that - by thinking differently!
   Some situations - (whether self-imposed or thrust upon us by others, life's pain in the ass
situations in general, and so forth) I simmered and stewed upon this and I finally decided that
enough was enough, I had reached the end of my tether, and in doing so, I have gathered and
have built up so much strength within, that it now enables me to help and to comfort others in
this world who are distressed and are tolerating what I once did, and it is my absolute pleasure to
do help others, for if just one person is helped by me to overcome what I did, then my job is done

   Would I go through everything in my life that I went through in the past, again? Yes I would,
with a resounding yes, as nothing brings me greater happiness than having the inner resources of
strength, now, to assist other people in overcoming the same things that I have over the years.

other, chronic loneliness and depression, agoraphobia and OCD, and a lot more.

the subconscious mind's way of letting us know that we need to take action to resolve whatever
anxiety-causing problem is getting to us on a conscious level.

those things from crushing my spirit and controlling me. It's very important to avoid what are
known as 'emotional vampires''; those people that suck all of the positivity out of you and
generally make you feel low, bad about yourself, and empty. To rectify this problem, associate
with people that make you feel good about yourself.

Other people's moods can rub off on us. Have only people in your life who are positively-
minded, then you will in turn feel exactly the same. It's a simple-sounding concept, but it's true
and it works.

utmost respect.

do it too.

up!

                                      Are you courageous?

    Feel the fear - and do it regardless. Overcome your fears by facing the very thing(s) that you
are frightened of (obviously if it's a dangerous situation then please don't). If it is a person, talk
to them alone, in a calm amicable and mature way, to see if the problem can be resolved
peacefully between the two of you as nobody enjoys being around someone if there is an
'atmosphere.' If not; and you cannot physically remove yourself from that person (if it's a Boss
in the workplace for example, and I've known some to be tyrants and that's the polite
definition...) then you can mentally remove yourself from the problem by detaching yourself
(visualise a wall between you and them) so that this person no longer has the power or capability
to upset you.
    Just switch off mentally (detachment), do your best in your job until you can get a nicer one -
and you will. Remember, a person can only upset us if we allow them to. Imagine that they are
sitting on the toilet, naked, then they wont seem half as intimidating! (smiles)
    If it's a phobia that is ruining your life, try and do what I did (I had agoraphobia for many
years), expose yourself to the fear (take a friend for company) and let the anxiety wash over you,
a little at a time, until you can face the situation or thing fully and completely and your anxiety
level has subsided to zero-level.
    If it's a severe phobia, and you have heart problems or high blood-pressure then please seek
medical professional help for your phobia via a therapist and do not try the method above as it
can be too much to handle. Take one step at a time and look after yourself properly.
    I have never had heart problems or high blood-pressure but I felt ill in a different way -
trapped and confined like a terrified rat in a cage. I couldn't leave my house for many years, and
whenever I attempted to do so I had to take a friend or relative with me as the extreme fear that
gripped and controlled my mind and body was quite horrific; a feeling of being absolutely
petrified (at the time, I compared the level of panic that I felt to the equivalent of a train hurtling
towards me or a roaring snarling lion descending upon me), I experienced regular heart
palpitations, I trembled and perspired all over, I had queasy sensations in the pit of my stomach, I
was depressed, felt pathetic, life generally sucked bigtime, not to mention putting up with and
tolerating the baffled facial expressions upon family member's faces and constantly listening to
ludicrous sentences like; 'Oh pull yourself together and go for a walk!'
    People that are suffering do try to do that, but if well-meaning family or friends are not going
through what you are then how can they possibly really know how difficult it is to carry out what
they say! Whenever I meet someone nowadays who is enduring the anguish that I did years ago,
I can say hand on heart that I do know what they are going through, and I provide a lot of help
and relief for them, that is the best part of going through what I did, that I can now provide
assistance to a sufferer in overcoming what they have been struggling with, alone.
    So, decades ago after I sat in the house crying for the umpteenth time, mainly with frustration
over being stuck indoors with agoraphobia and depression, I decided one day, that I had a choice;
stay indoors for the rest of my life or have freedom. I chose to have freedom as nothing could be
worse than everything that I had put up with, even the scary thought of going outside and
fainting! I didn't faint, I'd feel dizzy many times, but it was the fear of embarrassing myself in
public if I did faint that blocked me from leaving the house. But this one day I thought 'f*** it,
I've had enough!' So, each day I would sit near crowds of people, and although it was hellish at
first, it was the scariest thing that I think I have ever done in my entire life to be honest.
    The anxiety and terror finally diminished after many weeks, to the point where I can now
walk into crowded rooms with confidence and experience no fear whatsoever, and I must say it
is very very liberating, and a simple walk nowadays is a joy to me. I did see a psychologist for
approximately six months but I felt curing myself was better - for me. I am not suggesting that
you do the same. We are all different.
    Remember, we all have the power within to move figurative mountains. Write down your
fears, rip the piece of paper up, burn it, throw it away, say out loud 'I am not afraid of anything, I
can conquer anything, the fears are all in my mind'. It works. Control the things that you are
afraid of - don't let them control you. That is how I conquered my fears, you can conquer yours
too, if you want to enough. The power of the mind is incredible - please always trust in it, fully.

                  What you can do when you feel lonely or depressed:

   Here, below, are some suggestions and solutions and are what I find helpful whenever I feel
lonely and down - which is rare these days - but was daily in the past. They really do work, and
you will be surprised how much better you feel afterwards.

                           Get out and about in the fresh air!

    When we do this, it's invigorating! Feel-good endorphins are released within our brains
which provide that natural 'high' sensation within our minds - thereby helping us to see
everything in a much more positive way. There are many animals, birds and people that you will
see when enjoying your walk, and this will minimise the feelings of being lonely or unhappy as a
result of this. If you enjoy jogging or running then do that; and I can vouch for the fact as
someone who jogs, that you feel amazing afterwards and you have toned-up your entire body
too. The mental and physical benefits of exercise are vast.
   Swimming is one of the best exercises to do if you cannot walk or jog, due to arthritis or other
physical ailments. The water soothes and cushions the joints and you may make new friends at
your local Sports Centre or Swimming Pool too! I used to know a dear old lady that had to be
lowered into the water from a wheelchair due to the loss of sensation in her lower limbs, yet her
determination to get into the water helped her to have a wonderful time when she did. I admired
her immensely as she never let her physical problems deter her from enjoying herself and
maintaining fitness. Once in the water you should have seen the big smile of contentment upon
her face, bless her.



       Help out at your local Community Centre or keep a friend or relative
                                 company:
    The feeling of satisfaction that you will enjoy, by giving of your time, your help and
assistance to another person, will soon banish any unhappiness as the other person will feel
appreciative and more grateful than you realize. Stop for a moment and imagine yourself being
in their shoes and think how grateful you would feel if someone reached out the hand of kindness
and helped you. Invite a friend or relative to visit you and to keep you company.

                                             Pet a pet:

or get your own. They provide unconditional love and are wonderful companions! I adore my
cats dearly, and they are so entertaining - better than any TV show, in my opinion.




                              Plant a flower, a tree or a shrub:

   I have successfully grown some potted plants at home in my spare time and seeing them
bloom and flower from a tiny seed or a small bulb is a heartwarming thing to observe. It's much
like us really isn't it when we transform ourselves! One step at a time, we can grow and develop
for the better. We can nurture and tend to ourselves and we can make ourselves a better person
from the inside out. It may take time but the beautiful results are well worth the wait, and the
effort that we put into it all.



                                           Fly a kite!

   Kites are not necessarily for children; many an adult has been seen enjoying watching their
kite soar across the skies, brightly-coloured fabric zooming in motion.
                                         Draw or paint:

   Art is a fantastically therapeutic activity. The benefits of creating something can distract you
and can take your mind off any of life's problems (including loneliness), and you don't have to
have the ability of Van Gogh to achieve something beautiful and aesthetically-pleasing to your
eye - or to others. You could even end up selling your work - think how wonderful that will
make you feel.
   The movement in itself of brushing paint across canvas can feel so good and, looking at the
vibrant colours can cheer you up enormously - especially yellow, blue, green and pink. My
favourite colour is violet, I also adore yellow. I am not that fussy on brown, but it has never put
me off enjoying chocolate (smile)!



                                 Write down your thoughts:

   Create a journal/diary; whether it is in paper-form in a book, or electronically on your
computer in a word-processing program. Writing your thoughts and concerns down actually
makes you feel as if you have shared them with another person, and that in itself can minimize
your feelings of depression and loneliness. Try it, it works!



            Watch one of your favourite television programmes or films:

   This can help you to focus on someone or something else, and in some programmes and
films, a storyline involving how others have overcome loneliness or a case of 'the blues', can put
your feelings into perspective. You will then notice that things are not really as bad as you
thought they were.



                                        Treat your feet!

   If you have a foot spa, the sensation of letting the warm bubbling water caress and soothe
your feet is very beneficial for brightening up any sullen mood. I have one and they are not
expensive - most larger supermarkets or department stores sell them nowadays - and be warned,
they are very addictive, as once you begin enjoying the wonderful warmth of heated water on
your skin, you will wonder what you ever did without a foot spa!



                                               Cook:
   Cooking a meal or baking cakes, biscuits and so forth, can be very therapeutic, as the aromas
that you smell can lift and take away so many worries. Food can be so comforting. Comfort eat
in moderation; nobody ever became obese by enjoying one or two squares of luscious home-
made fudge or one or two chocolate-chip cookies. If curries are your weakness, there are lots of
fabulous recipes online where you can make your own to enjoy - with half of the calories that
you'd find in most other places where you'd buy them from. Home-made bread is fantastic too,
as getting all of your stress out in the kneading process is amazing therapy in itself!



                                                Knit:

   Some of my friends create stunning items out of wool, taking their time and absorbing
themselves in the creative act of handling the baby-soft wool, and the repetitive action of knitting
needles moving in a rhythmical manner can calm the mind and can enable you to smile with the
fact that you are creating something that doesn't just look beautiful, but that will last for years - a
garment or blanket that either yourself or others will enjoy for a long time to come. I plan on
creating a multicoloured blanket for this winter! I'm left-handed so I wonder if this will make
any difference, but as a versatile and adaptable person by nature, I'm sure I can find a way
around the problem if it arises.

wonderful world of escapism and adventures, and the other readers nearby, will provide
companionship - albeit in silence. Or, of course, read a book online; there are endless titles
under just about every genre that you can imagine and the characters within the pages of the
work can help you to feel like you are not alone, they can keep you company as you read about
their lives.



                        Music to soothe the soul, not just the ears:

   I find that when I'm feeling down or lonely (usually once a month at 'that' time of the month,
for instance - you'll understand completely, if you're a woman), playing some of my favourite
songs works wonders to get me out of that frame of mind immediately. Try a little dancing to
your favourite tunes, once you're up and dancing around, you wont have time or care for any
negative feelings - as you'll be too busy feeling fantastic! Please don't feel silly dancing by
yourself, just enjoy yourself and the movements of your body, as you engross your ears in the
melodious sounds that make you feel good!



                                       Punch a pillow!

  I have been known to do some serious karate punches on a well plumped-up pillow, and boy
does it make you feel soooooooooo good! It gets all of your pent-up anger and stress out and
you get a full-body workout too in the process!



                                             Smile!

   As silly as it sounds, smile whenever a negative thought crosses your mind. The physical act
of smiling forces your brain to create a chemical change within your body so that you instantly
feel better. Smile at someone when you walk past them (even if you have to force a smile, do it),
you never know whose day you may have improved by doing this one simple thing for them (and
for yourself also). Smile at yourself in the mirror (when alone, or you'll get peculiar looks in
public lol), just by looking at yourself smiling, you cannot help but to feel good inside.



                        Praise yourself with positive affirmations:

    Repeat after me; 'I love me, I think that I am a unique and wonderful human being!' I felt so
silly when first doing this years ago, but now I do it every day, and yes it does make you feel
more confident inside - and far happier - and as long as you love yourself do you really care if
others do or don't? I don't. Repeat positive affirmations to yourself every day, they really do
work and they enable you to have a strong positive view of life in general, and toward yourself
too.



                                        Go shopping!

   Buy yourself something nice. It doesn't have to be expensive. If it's an item of clothing, buy
something in a bright colour as it will enhance your positive mood and, think how great you will
look in your new; dress, coat, skirt, blouse, jacket, trousers or shoes! Shopping is extremely
therapeutic - most women (and some men) will agree!



                                 Drink water and hydrate:
   Sometimes, we can be so dehydrated that it can make us feel down and depressed. The brain
cannot cope with dehydration and you can feel sluggish, lethargic and terrible. It can be
dangerous too as bodily processes cannot perform as well as they need to. I had to force myself
to get used to the taste of water initially, but now it's very nice and the benefits are endless -
more energy, clearer thinking processes, better skin, to name but a few.



                                         Enjoy a tipple!

   If you are on medication, then obviously do not drink alcohol. If you are not, then a glass of
wine (red is good for you) is very enjoyable in moderation. Studies have shown that a glass of
wine (or whatever your favourite tipple is - mine is vodka) can provide a feel-good sensation in
the body. Moderation is the key - too much alcohol can cause lots of damaging problems -
mentally, physically and psychologically too. We are all built differently; everyone tolerates
alcohol to differing degrees, so use your own judgement and what is best for you.



                                            Chocolate:

   Research over the years has shown that a few squares of chocolate - particularly dark
chocolate with a 70% cocoa content, has health benefits. It tastes more bitter than milk
chocolate, so if your preference is for milk chocolate then treat yourself to a few squares when
enjoying your favourite film or TV show. Savour each mouthful very slowly, and relish the
sublime feeling of it melting in your mouth. Close your eyes for best effect!



                                           Make love:

   Either with yourself...or with a partner. The act of touching yourself, or another person, isn't;
seedy, bad, wrong, dirty, it is a normal and natural activity to perform, and an orgasm can work
wonders to relax and de-stress you and to help you to feel fantastic (the other person will too)!



                                               Sing!

   Even if you don't have an incredible voice like a pop star or an opera-singer, it doesn't matter.
Sing your heart out to your favourite song (or make your own up), you will feel marvellous
afterwards! If you live with someone, and they don't want to hear you (or you're too shy), sing in
your bathroom, alone. The acoustics in a bathroom are fantastic and you will sound far more
amazing!



                                       Shout and scream!

   Have a damn good rant and a whinge about whatever is bothering you. Make sure of course,
that you are alone when doing this - and that it is a reasonable hour of the day, otherwise a
neighbour's baby or dog will be woken up and will start crying or barking, as this will only add
to your problems. The last thing you need is an irate neighbour banging on your door! Shout
and scream into your pillow. Never bottle up your feelings as that can lead to physical illness, I
have found in the past.



                                       Seek Medical help:

   If you feel chronically lonely and depressed, please don't hesitate to have a confidential chat
with your Doctor or ask them to refer you to a psychologist. They can help you if it's a chemical
imbalance in your brain; with the lack of serotonin causing clinical depression, Doctors can
prescribe anti-depressants that will replace this loss of this neurotransmitter and they can, in lots
of cases, really help.
   Your Doctor can also refer you to a counsellor, to whom you can pour out your problems and
they will provide a caring listening ear and will give you lots of different solutions to whatever is
bothering you.          There is no shame in discussing your worries with a
counsellor/psychologist/psychiatrist; I did once, when suffering OCD, agoraphobia and
depression, and I found their help of great value.



                                              Laugh!

   There's an old wise saying that 'laughter is the best medicine.' There have been documented
cases of terminally-ill patients who have read/viewed/thought of funny things and, in rare cases,
their illness has disappeared. Laughter is one of the best things that you can ever do in life to
help banish for good: any feelings of loneliness or unhappiness.
   I personally watch one of my favourite TV shows or films, or I laugh and chuckle at the
amusing things in life. With three entertaining cats, that is all that I seem to do lately, and what
with my incessant ability to fart seemingly in tune after a change in my diet (more fruits and
vegetables). It's impossible to feel down or unhappy when you create some musical trumps all
by yourself! Do try not to do this in public however, as you wouldn't want to get banned from
your favourite Department Store... I haven't yet, but there's always the next time. *blushes*
How embarrassing that would be! Can you imagine?
                                                Cry:

   There is no shame whatsoever in having a damn good cry. It releases: frustration, pent-up
anguish, toxins from the body and you feel a hell of a lot better afterwards. Never feel guilty or
ashamed for crying and letting it all out. Some men may feel embarrassed about crying - due to
being brought up and taught that it is wrong - it isn't. Enjoy a good cry, it truly helps! Then pick
yourself up, dust yourself down and smile.



                                         Hug yourself!

   Try it. Then you will see what I mean. It feels great. You will feel so good. It isn't
narcissistic by the way, hugging yourself is a healthy activity to carry out.



                                            Get a fish:

    I used to have a tank full of tropical multicoloured fish. They provided endless entertainment
(for my cats too). Observing fish, produces a calmness within and looking after another creature
is a giving, caring thing to do. Besides...my three cats viewed them as fish TV I think, so it kept
them quiet and happy for many an hour!



                                      Look to the future:

   Never focus on, or remember the past, but look instead to the future; with a sense of hope,
happiness and joy. The best in your life is just around the corner... Leave the past behind - it is
comprised of just memories. It cannot hurt you. We cannot get the moments or the time back,
so why waste your energy on focusing on it. Let it go. The future is looking good for you.
Believe in it. Stay as positive as you possibly can. Optimism is everything!
