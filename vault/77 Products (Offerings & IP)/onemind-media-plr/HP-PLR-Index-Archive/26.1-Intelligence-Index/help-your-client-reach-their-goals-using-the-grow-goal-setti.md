                                      Introduction



One of the most revered techniques for helping a client to realise their goals is to use
a system known as the GROW model. This technique can then be used alongside
the SMARTER goal setting process to drill down and lock in the tasks, resources,
processes and timescales required for each goal. (You can find out more about
SMARTER goal setting at insight­academy.com.)

In this short ebook you'll find a list of 60 of the most powerful questions you could ask
during a GROW based Coaching Session, to help your client identify their Goals,
relate their Reality, identify their Options and make a decision about   What Actions
they will take to achieve those goals.

This list of 60 powerful questions, seem to offer the best response (certainly in my
experience), when utilising the GROW system of coaching, although there are
several hundred alternatives available if you have the time and inclination to search
them out for yourself.

Before each section I've given a short explanation to guide you.

They've been designed to help your client think beyond the obvious and look
deeper into themselves to discover the answers they always knew, but never
thought to look for.

As Rhonda Hess says...

    "A question is most powerful when neither the asker nor the responder
               knows the answer, until the question is answered."

I've used these questions, as have many, many other coaches, to chip away into
the deeper structure of the client's thinking processes.

                                  Goal Questions

The Goal is the 'end point' your client wishes to achieve. This point could be a
new skill, a new or better relationship, the ability to be a better friend, or parent,
a new or improved career, etc. The possibilities are innumerable, and it's your job
to help elicit their true goals, not only for the coaching relationship as­a­whole,
but their target for this session of coaching as well.

1. What do you want to achieve?

2. What would you like to get from the next 30 minutes?

3. Describe your perfect world.

4. What will make you feel this time has been well spent?

5. What do you want to achieve in this session?

6. What could we work on today that would make the biggest change to your
   life?

7. What are you aiming for in the long/medium/short term?

8. Have you unfinished tasks you dwell upon?

9. What have you been meaning to take care of?

10. What, specifically are you looking to achieve?

11. "What is the experience you are looking to create?"

12. "What might be less important than you think?"

13. "What might be more important than you think?"

14. "What outcome would be ideal in this situation?"

15. "What will make you feel like you have really achieved something today?"
                              Reality Questions
The client's reality is where they are now, in relation to where they ultimately want
to be, and in their prior attempts to reach their goals. These questions help to
bring out not only the obvious reasons for their under­achievement so far, but
also the hidden, unconscious ones.

These questions also help to unearth some helpful inner strength that the client
has, to solve the issues they've had up until now.

1. "If you don’t change this, what will it cost you in the long run?"

2. "If you changed your belief about this, what would be possible?"

3. Tell me about the current situation?

4. "What rules do you have that are getting in the way?"

5. "Do you want this for its own sake, or are you using it to avoid something
   else?"

6. "What skill, or knowledge, or attributes do you currently have?"

7. What steps have you already taken towards this goal?

8. Where are you right now in relation to your goal?

9. "What have you tried so far?"

10. "What might be holding you back?"

11. "What are three things you are doing regularly that don’t serve or support
    you?"

12. "What’s your favourite way of sabotaging yourself, and your goals?"

13. "What story do you most often hear yourself telling?"

14. "How would your life be transformed if you changed this right now?"

15. "Can we do that in the time we have available?"
                                Options Questions
This section is where you ask your client questions that will hopefully make them
think differently about their inner skills and abilities. It will help them break down
what's really important about their goal and give them the ability to determine
some different ways of achieving their goals.

1. "If I were to give you an extra hour a day, what would you do with it?"

2. "What would you try now if you knew you could not fail?"

3. "Which step could you take that would make the biggest difference, right
   now?"

4. "What might make the difference that could change everything?"

5. "How can you improve this, so it adds value forever?"

6. "How can you solve this problem so it never comes back?"

7. "What’s the worst that can happen, and can you handle that?"

8. "Will this choice move you forward or keep you stuck?"

9. "What will really make the biggest difference here?"

10. "What would have to happen for you to be able to achieve that?"

11. "What part of what you’ve just said could be an assumption?"

12. "What is the most meaningful action you could take now?"

13. If you had one day to take a step closer to your goal, what would it be?

14. If you had a week to make some form of improvement, what would you do?

15. "... and what else?"
                             What Action Questions

This section of questioning helps to solidify their commitment to act on their various
options. Now they have a plethora of options to move toward their goal, they need
to get leverage on themselves to actually carry out the tasks they've decided to
complete.

Without this final stage, it's surprising how many people would leave the coaching
environment full of energy and positive thought, just to push them to the back of
their mind and hour later.

These questions help them to understand why they want it to succeed and what will
happen if they don't!

   1. "What are you willing to do to improve this situation?"

   2. "What are you willing to stop doing to improve this situation?"

   3. "What would be the best first step?"

   4. "How can you enjoy the process of solving this problem?"

   5. Which options will work best for you?

   6. What would need to happen for you to walk away feeling that this time was
      well spent?"

   7. When are you going to start?

   8. How much energy are you willing to put into that?"

   9. Which of the options would give you the greatest satisfaction?

   10. What would be one small step you could take today to get you started?

   11. When will you take these next steps?

   12. What actions will you take?

   13. Will you need support to get that done?

   14. What will be the result of not following through on your plans?

   15. How will you know you’ve achieved these goals?
