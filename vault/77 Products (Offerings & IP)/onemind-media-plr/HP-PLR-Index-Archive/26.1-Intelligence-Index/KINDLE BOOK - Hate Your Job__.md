# **50 Hacks On How To Get Started As An Entrepreneur Whilst Travelling The World.**

## Since leaving my corporate office job a couple of years ago, I've never looked back. 

## Entrepreneur, digital nomad, solopreneur - whatever you want to call it\...I now spend my time working when and where I want, running my own business online whilst travelling to some of the most amazing places in the world. I live in a flat that's a quarter of the price I was paying back in Manchester, eating incredible Thai street food and swimming in my own pool in the 40 degree sunshine.

## **So that's my story so far, now let's begin with my 50 top tips on how to get started as a digital nomad....**

## 

## 

## **Motivation for digital nomads:**

## 1. Commit yourself -- at the end of my holiday in Thailand last year, I set the goal of moving out there within one year. I managed to do it in just 10 months, building my own freelance marketing business on [[Fiverr]{.ul}](https://www.fiverr.com/juliangoldie) and [[UpWork]{.ul}](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~016aabb73c2440f9df) within that time. If I can do it, pretty much anyone can! In that time, I spent time almost every day planning and learning how to get started as a digital nomad. Set goals that are realistic and renew your motivation every single day, so that you have something to work towards.

## 2. Think about doing something you love -- yes I know, this is one of the most cliché self development phrases you'll hear. But it is completely true. For me, my passion is online marketing and writing. I love psychology and that's the very foundation of marketing, so I decided this is the best way for me to learn how to get started as a digital nomad earning online. If you're travelling around the world, you need to source an income from something you're passionate about it because you don't have a manager motivating you or looking over your shoulder to check you're working. It's therefore important to follow your passion so that you're constantly motivated to start working early and consistently on your business.

## 3. Visualise -- before I moved out to Thailand, I dedicated some time every single day to visualising what it would be like. I knew what it would feel like, where I'd live and what I'd spend my days doing there before I even stepped foot in the country as a digital nomad. I think one of the biggest factors in success is the ability to see where you're going and have a clear direction.

## 4. Take the jump -- once you've set a target and you're on track for hitting that goal, it's important to finally take the jump. One of my mentors in my previous job some great advice -- "don't judge by the jump, judge by the fall". That makes seeing how to get started as a digital nomad much more achievable. Taking the step to quit your job, leave your family and travel the world on your own is much less daunting when you're not afraid of taking the jump.

## 5. Spend some time thinking about whether it's what you truly want -- if you're a 24 hour flight away from home, it's going to be really tricky if you decide this isn't the lifestyle you actually want. Whilst travelling the world and running and your own business sounds glamorous, it's really not for everyone. For those who get homesick easily, you might actually find this really difficult. It takes a lot of energy to socialise in a new environment, organise a completely new life and be savvy with your finances along the way. So before you jet off, just take some time to contemplate whether this is truly what you want to do.

## 6. Be mentally prepared -- this is one hell of a journey brother! From taxi drivers trying to rip you off as soon as you land to hunting online for new clients, this isn't as easy as it sounds. As much as I love it, it's not all beaches and drinking juice out of a coconut. When considering how to get started as a digital nomad, you have to be prepared for a rollercoaster lifestyle. It's fun, incredibly rewarding but it can be hard work. That's the compromise you make when you're getting started as a digital nomad.

## **Digital nomad research & prep before you go:**

## 7. Research -- I was surprised how much useful travel information there is out there when trying to learn how to get started as a digital nomad. It's incredible. We're lucky to live in a world where the amount of information freely available is vast and high quality. Wherever you're travelling to, read up on specific articles about the country you're visiting. This could include useful tips about the culture, the best food to try, where to live and how to get started as a digital nomad in that particular country. All of these things will help you prepare before moving out there.

## 8. Speak to people who are doing the same thing -- networking with people who are already living this lifestyle makes it even easier to learn how to get started as a digital nomad. Before I left, I was reading about other nomads, contacting them on [[forums]{.ul}](https://nomadforum.io/c/nomad-list) and reaching out to people on Facebook to build my networks with people. They gave me some incredibly useful advice which I think helped me hit the ground running whilst out here in Chiang Mai. Feel free to get in touch if you have any questions you'd like to ask me 🙂

## 9. Take time to plan properly -- from where you're going to live to what you should pack, a little preparation will make your life a lot easier. Take a couple of weeks off before quitting your job and moving abroad to give yourself some spare time to work out what you should plan for. Here's a useful [[manual]{.ul}](http://www.expatinfodesk.com/expat-guide/organizing-your-departure/) for moving abroad.

## 10. Vaccinations -- nobody likes vaccinations! But that being said, they can save you a lot of time and hassle. Catching yellow fever in a foreign country isn't going to end well for anyone. There are even some countries, like Vietnam for example, that require you to have had certain vaccinations before entering the country. Arrange an appointment with your GP or nurse, tell them where you're planning to visit and they'll give you all the vaccinations you need based on the locations you're going to. It's important to remember to book this visit in early because nurses can get booked up pretty quickly and some vaccinations like Hepatitis B require you to have a series of vaccinations over a few weeks. [[This]{.ul}](http://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel) link will give you an indication as to what vaccines you'll require abroad.

## 11. Travel insurance -- fortunately, I haven't had to use my travel insurance. But that's the best case scenario. Whilst many won't bother with it, I really don't like the thought of being stuck with a medical bill for thousands of pounds just because I was a cheapskate on travel insurance. When I was in Koh Phi Phi hiring a boat out for the day, two of my friends dropped their phones into seawater! One had travel insurance, the other didn't. I personally don't think it's worth the risk.

## 12. Learn the time zones -- communicating with people in different time zones is confusing at the best of times. You can just [[Google the time in your city]{.ul}](http://bit.ly/26Kb46K) to find out in seconds how far ahead or behind the person you're trying to contact is. Living in Thailand, my friends and family are 6 hours behind, so I can adjust the time I call to make sure I'm not waking them up in the middle of the night! I actually met a personal trainer from New York who has to arrange Skype calls with her clients first thing in the morning and last thing at night, between 6-8am and 9-11pm. If you're doing business online with someone you speak to regularly, keep them in the loop with your time zone so they know when to contact you. That's especially important if you offer customer service like I do, as people expect quick response times to any questions, work offers or complaints they have.

## 13. Find a destination you'll love -- this is the fun part, planning where you want to settle in before you become a digital nomad. Some people will only live at one place for a month or two before drifting on but there's something to be said for living in a city for a few months at a time. You'll find routine, regular social circles and costs are much easier to manage when you're living in one place for an extended period. Or you could just do the whole of South East Asia in one month! Up to you...

## 14. Sort out your Visa -- depending on where you go and where you're from, you'll have different Visa restrictions. You can check Visa travel information for the [[UK here]{.ul}](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice) and the [[US here]{.ul}](https://travel.state.gov/content/passports/en/country.html). It's better to arrange this before you leave the UK, although you could just wait until you enter the country for places for like Thailand -- then extend your tourist Visa whilst you're out there.

## 15. Unlock your phone -- before you leave your home country, make sure you unlock your phone. Without an unlocked phone, you can't use a foreign sim card. You'll find out why you need a sim card below...

## 16. Sim card -- once you've reached your destination abroad, it's best to get a sim card. Having 4G when I'm walking around a new city is really useful, especially if you're googling places to go, things to do and using Google Maps to navigate your way. Places like Thailand offer pretty reasonable rates for sim cards when you want to get started as a digital nomad. For example, I paid about 250 baht (£5) for a tourist sim card when I got to the country -- that included 4G with 1.5GB of data.

## 17. See what's it like to live this lifestyle -- there are some awesome vlogs on YouTube that give you a nice run through of what it's like to live and work as a digital nomad. [[Here's a link]{.ul}](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=digital+nomad+day+in+the+life) to a load of 'day in the life' videos which give you a run down of what you'll get up to day to day, with plenty of useful tips and advice from others doing the same thing.

## 18. Local events -- local events can make travelling a little tougher whilst on the road as a digital nomad. For example, if you're travelling during [[Songkran]{.ul}](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songkran_(Thailand)) with a laptop and all your other gadgets, the last thing you need is a bucket of water to be thrown over you and your bag! Check for local events, national holidays and festivals that may be happening whilst travelling because some of them can get in the way.

## 19. Weather conditions -- this is easy to forget about but exotic countries vastly range in their temperatures and some are a little extreme to say the least. Try not to move somewhere whilst they're expecting a monsoon or hurricane! It's also useful to check out their predicted temperatures for the month. [[This is a useful website]{.ul}](http://www.holiday-weather.com/) for keeping an eye on weather forecasts for the places you'll be travelling to.

## 20. Dealing with language issues -- if you're a Brit abroad, speaking loudly and slowly probably won't get you very far, especially when you land in more rural areas. [[Google Translate is a life saver]{.ul}](https://translate.google.com/), you can simply type in your native language and translate it into the local language. This can save a lot of confusion for you and the person you're speaking to!

## 21. Checking internet speeds -- [[this website]{.ul}](http://www.speedtest.net/) helps you check internet speeds in any area of the world you're interested in, so you can see whether it's realistic to run an internet business over there.

## 22. Compare all of your location options -- the world is your oyster when it comes to living abroad as a digital nomad. Whilst there's a wealth of information online for comparing cities, countries and expat communities, I love [[Nomad List]{.ul}](https://nomadlist.com/). With their tools you can filter by the cost of living, flight costs, temperature, internet quality, crime rates and a whole host of other useful factors to find the dream destination for your digital nomad lifestyle.

## **Gear & technology for digital nomads:**

## 23. Sort out a decent laptop -- before you get started as a digital nomad, you'll want a reliable laptop that's easy to carry around. Personally, I use the [[Toshiba Chromebook 2]{.ul}![](media/image24.gif){width="1.3888888888888888e-2in" height="1.3888888888888888e-2in"}](http://www.amazon.co.uk/gp/product/B00RK7LFEG/ref=as_li_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1634&creative=19450&creativeASIN=B00RK7LFEG&linkCode=as2&tag=wwwjuliangold-21), which I think is the best travel laptop you can find for it's price. It's about £200 to buy, really fast to use and so incredibly lightweight. [[Read my full review here if you'd like to find out more]{.ul}](http://juliangoldie.com/best-travel-laptop-chromebook/).

## 24. Source a power bar -- life as a digital nomad is a constant strain on all your battery powered gadgets. For when you're on the go taking pictures, using Google Maps and making your friends jealous via social media, make sure you take a power bar with you or some sort of portable battery charger. ## 25. Keeping up with your favourite films and TV shows -- when you're first getting started as a digital nomad, you'll find that your IP address abroad might block you from using Amazon Prime's Instant Video and you'll have a different selection of shows on Netflix. It's a little annoying but there are some work arounds. If you're a little black hat you could try torrenting or streaming via [[Putlocker]{.ul}](http://putlocker.is/), or you can use a proxy VPN add on to register your laptop as if you're still living in the same country. That might sound a little technical but as I personally don't work in IT, I use [[Hola's Chrome extension]{.ul}](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/unlimited-free-vpn-hola/gkojfkhlekighikafcpjkiklfbnlmeio?hl=en), which simply changes your IP address and has a range of different options without you having to configure it.

## 26. Texting and instant messaging -- if you don't want to pay for an all out sim card package that includes texts and calls, there are plenty of easy work arounds. I use WhatsApp, Line and Facebook Messenger, which all use the internet instead of your mobile network to text people. That means if you've got an internet connection, they're free to use.

If you're planning to vlog on the go, there are a couple of products that will make your life easier. ## 29. Sort out long term accommodation -- accommodation abroad is usually cheaper when you search for it offline. For example, when I looked for flats in Nimman, everything decent online was at least £400 for the month. When I actually arrived in the area and spoke to residences directly, flats were just £100 -- with much better facilities such as a swimming pool, bigger rooms and a balcony.

## 30. Book a hotel for your first night -- following on from the above tip, you'll probably only need a hotel to stay in on the first night you land. Then on your second day, you can search around the area you want to live in and sort your long term accommodation out, bearing in mind that a flat can usually be found in just a few hours. So book a hotel or flat for just your first night there using a service like [[Booking.com]{.ul}](http://www.booking.com/) or [[Airbnb]{.ul}](http://www.airbnb.com/c/juliang149?s=8). I made the mistake of hiring a hostel without air con on my first night travelling as a digital nomad and I pretty much melted whilst 2 fans below 40 degree heat around the room. Air conditioning, wherever you're staying or working is a must if you're in a hot country.

## 32. Getting organised -- personally I've never tried it but [[Evernote]{.ul}](https://evernote.com/?var=c) is very highly rated. However, I use [[Google Keep]{.ul}](https://keep.google.com/) pretty much every day for organising my to do lists, checklists, useful information and any ideas I have on the go. ## 33. Organising transport -- [[Skyscanner]{.ul}](http://www.skyscanner.net/) is my first port of call for booking flights. There's a nifty option where you can set your destination to 'everywhere', helping you to compare all potential flight locations before making a decision as to where you want go. When you first land in the airport, as a general rule it's better to book your taxi with those outside of the airport because they'll offer a better rate. Also, before paying to drive somewhere, whether that's in a taxi, tuk tuk or Songthaew, make sure the driver knows where they're going. You can use an app like Google Maps to show them the location and address.

## **Socialising as a digital nomad:**

## 40. Organise a send off -- before I moved to Chiang Mai, I spent two weeks travelling the South of Thailand with 5 of my best mates -- we went snorkelling, visited monkey beaches and experienced Thailand's famous [[Songkran]{.ul}](http://www.bangkok.com/information-festivals/songkran.htm) in Bangkok. I also had a couple of family get together's to say goodbye before going. All of this gave me a chance to say goodbye before leaving, which is important because you'll want a chance to catch up with your friends and family before not seeing them for a while.

## 41. Tell your family early -- your family will worry about you -- you're incredibly important to them! Learning how to get started as a digital nomad might be relatively common to you but to most of your family, it's a strange concept and one that they might find difficult to grasp. If you're planning to live abroad, tell them as early as possible so they're not left surprised or feeling like it's a sudden move.

## 42. Think about how you'll keep in touch with people -- personally, I make it a monthly habit to drop a note to everyone close to me and let them know how I'm getting on. I use free internet chat services like [[Skype]{.ul}](https://www.skype.com/en/), [[Facebook chat]{.ul}](https://www.facebook.com/notes/facebook-engineering/facebook-chat/14218138919/) and [[WhatsApp]{.ul}](https://www.whatsapp.com/faq/en/android/28000016). They're so easy to use and reliable that you can keep in touch with everyone you need to. The way we're connected around the world today is awesome, so you have the ability to really take advantage of that. Learning how to get started as a digital nomad means you'll find out how easy it is to speak to the ones you care about, whether they're five minutes down the road or 5,000 miles away.

## 43. Think about how you'll socialise -- we're all human and that means it's in our nature to connect with other people. When learning how to get started as a digital nomad, it's essential to understand how you're going to meet new people -- that's probably one of the most useful skills you'll ever have. So when you're on the road, you'll want friends around you. It's actually really easy to strike up a conversation with most people, as they're in a similar position to you in terms of living abroad, away from family etc. Before I left the UK, I made a list of a few ways I could meet new people whilst abroad, which included playing sports, going to the gym, joining and posting on local nomad Facebook groups, attending local meetups, using [[meetup.com]{.ul}](http://www.meetup.com/), expat clubs, retreats, nightclubs and joining coworking spaces. These are all great ways to meet new people and have served me really well in making new friends.

## 44. Haggle -- one of the most useful tips I can offer to help you get started as a digital nomad is to haggle almost everything. Buying in Asia is pretty much one big negotiation. Whether you're shopping in the night markets or hiring a tuk tuk, don't accept the first price unless you want to pay more than you should be.

## **Financial tips for digital nomads:**

## 45. Budget -- obviously when you're first starting out online, your income probably is not going to be as high as you'd like. If you're earning between \$500 to \$1,000 a month, that's enough to get started. Budget places like Chiang Mai offer incredible value, where you can eat out 3 times a day for just \$5 and you can rent an air conditioned flat with a swimming pool for \$150 a month. That means you're going to get more bang for your buck! Whereas if you're considering travelling out to somewhere like Europe or Australia, you're going to need to earn more to live sustainably.

## 46. Have some savings -- to feel really comfortable, I'd recommend having at least 6 months of your salary in savings before jetting off somewhere. With business, understandably your income will be unpredictable from time to time so it's best to have a safety net, where you know you can get by if budgets get tight. It also helps you sleep at night, knowing that you could take a break or some time off if you need it. If you get ill whilst abroad (for example, you get a rare case of dengue fever) and have to switch off for a few days, you can rely on your savings to keep you afloat.

## 47. Keep track of your expenses -- whilst this isn't essential to get started as a digital nomad, it is useful. Everyday I keep a track of how much I've spent, what I've spent this on and how much income I've brought in. This is really useful because it gives you an indication of your living costs and whether the lifestyle you're living currently is affordable. It's easy to spend too much in a foreign country with a currency you're not used to.

## 48. Check the currency conversion rates -- to make sure you're fully aware of how much you're spending, it's best to have a good grasp of the currency conversion rates. Can't work it out in your head? There are plenty of apps you can use for this, whilst [[Google also helps you quickly find this info out]{.ul}](https://www.google.co.th/webhp?sourceid=chrome-instant&ion=1&espv=2&ie=UTF-8#q=pounds%20to%20baht).

## 49. Take at least two banking cards with you -- I've been on so many holidays where at least one person loses their bank card. Whether it's at the bottom of the Atlantic ocean or in the pocket of that overly friendly ladyboy, if you only pack one debit card and lose it, you're going to be really stuck for at least a week. That's a big problem, especially in a new country where you don't know many people. Take one bank card out with you, whilst leaving the other at your flat. That way if you lose one, you have a temporary back up whilst you wait for your replacement card to be posted. Whilst first getting started as a digital nomad, this is probably one of the most valuable tips I can offer you.

## 50. Source a way of withdrawing money -- make no mistake, this can be a real pain! If you're using your normal debit card abroad, you'll quickly rack up a whole load of unnecessary costs -- like converting your cash abroad, withdrawal fees and even a commission for the amount you take out. You can easily get stung, so find a more cost effective way to withdraw money abroad. Personally, I signed up for the [[Halifax Clarity Card]{.ul}](http://www.halifax.co.uk/creditcards/clarity-card/), which is free to use abroad wherever and whenever you want. If you're already with Halifax, you can use your online banking system to sign up in a few minutes.
