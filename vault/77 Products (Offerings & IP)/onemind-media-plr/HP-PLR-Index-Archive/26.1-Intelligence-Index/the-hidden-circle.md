                                                Introduction
Be it one of the vigorous tales of the ancient Greeks, or one of the enlightening sagas of the Middle or Far
East, or one of the exemplary allegories of the Holy Bible, the Koran or the Mayan “Popol Vuh,” or one of
the gallant legends of the Middle Ages, the adventure lived by the story’s Main Character always conforms
to a similar pattern.
The heroic feats included in the story usually (but not always) divide it into four stages:
(1) The Call: a request that bids the Main Character (the Hero or Heroine of the story) to leave his or her
everyday life.
(2) Procuring the Goal: a Journey to find and secure the Prime Objective of the saga.
(3) The Return: after attaining the Goal, there comes the Return of the Hero to his known world.
(4) The Reintegration: the Display and Good Use to be given to the found or conquered Trophy.
Each of these stages can also be divided into several sub-sections.




1. The Call usually includes:
- A bidding that invites, or forces, the Main Character of the story to leave his shack, house or castle in
search of the all-important Goal. This part could include, as it many times does, a refusal to follow the Call.
If it’s accepted, the coming events, more often than not, lead the Hero to some kind of Portal or Passage
Way, from where the adventure begins.
- If the Call is heeded, generally a Guide, an Assistant or a Companion soon appears, one who has
exceptional powers. This individual many times must prepare, guide and counsel the Main Character, so that
s(he) can suitably deal with the next step of the saga.
- Then comes the actual arrival at the Door or Passage, whose crossing will prove to be a difficult test for the
emerging Hero. Many times, he’ll have to confront and overcome some kind of Guardian that oversees the
entrance to a Magic World, the place where the central plot of the story is to take place.




2. The next stage, Procuring the Goal, now within the Magic Land itself, can include:
- The Trials and Dangers that the Hero must overcome while he heads toward the desired Trophy. Now’s
when the Hero moves within a Magical World, unknown to him but that, at the same time, seems vaguely
familiar.
- Then follows the clash with enemies who, once defeated, many times become the Hero’s allies. This is the
part when the Main Character of the saga often finds out about some hidden quality that he, without knowing
it, has always had.
- After treading on ground each time more hallowed and meeting figures more and more powerful, step by
step our Hero draws nearer to the Prime Goal, the Highpoint of the adventure.
- The Hero then proceeds to Conquer and Seize the Great Prize. This is the most important part of the Saga,
the crowning point of the story.
3. The Return with the won Elixir, Prize or Trophy can include:
- The refusal to Return. Why leave the grounds of Nirvana, Mount Olympus or Camelot? Why return to the
tribulations of an insipid everyday life?
- If the Hero finally decides to return to his own realm, a fight will begin against the Obstacles that struggle
to obstruct his homecoming. It’s the conflict brought forth by the Ethereal and Timeless forces as they resist
being pulled back into the limited world of Ego and Matter.
- To conclude this part of the saga, the Hero must cross the Portal that leads him back to his familiar world.
Many different Beings and Circumstances will try to block his way, but owed to the impersonal forces of
Evolution, the Hero must cross the Boundary that separates the illuminated World of the Gods from the dark
and dreary Land of Normality.




4. Next follows The Reintegration of the Hero into the ambience or society that once saw him leave. This
part could include:
- The narrative of the saga to his family, fellow countrymen, or to the leaders of his society, which can either
be believed or not by them.
- The Official Display of the Prize won with so much effort.
- The Acceptance or Rejection of the amazing Powers of the conquered Prize. Now the Hero’s family,
friends, general public and leaders of his community will decide if they accept or not the importance of the
Trophy.
- And, finally, we’ll be offered the usage and enjoyment of the Conquered Treasure. Or, if conditions are
unfavorable to the Hero, we’ll see his withdrawal to another, hopefully friendlier environment.
Naturally, each Legend can emphasize any of the above described parts, highlighting it so much that it would
seem to be the main theme of the story. Or, it could leave out one or more of those parts. These variations
make the adventures seem different from one another. It’s what gives each of them its particular “flavor.”
                                               The Initial Call
Similar to when the Hero discovers that he is the son of a King, who at the appropriate time will have to act
out the role of a Prince, each of us, also at the proper moment, will become aware of the role that we’re to
assume in the great Adventure of Life.
In stories and legends, many times the Initial Call demands that the Hero or Heroine leave behind the
lifestyle of his or her youth, to then begin the Great Adventure of finding out who s(he) really is. Strange as it
may sound, it’s at this point where many people, maybe most of them, try to ignore the Call made by their
own destiny. Usually, the decision to accept the Call is a tough one for it involves letting go of the apparent
—but very convincing—security of our known surroundings, to then move, with courage and faith, into an
unknown and unexplored world. It’s a passage that would seem to invite the Hero to jump into emptiness
without a safety net. Still, it’s the only way to begin the Journey that’ll lead him to the much desired Prize:
the way of life that by his own right, by legitimate heritage, belongs to him.
Sometimes the change is so drastic that it would seem that the individual must “die” in regard to his former
way of life, to then “revive” or “be reborn” when he assumes his true attributes. This happens when the
thoughts, ideals and emotional patterns of a former lifestyle are to be outgrown, when the circumstances of a
“normal” life must be surpassed by the potential that yearns for a proper expression through a given
individual.
Sometimes it’s a Call that comes from a bloodline that the Hero didn’t know he belonged to; sometimes it
comes from a religious experience, as what happened to Joan of Arc, or to Gautama Sakyamuni, before he
became the illuminated Buddha.
The action may begin with what would seem to be an accident, or with a sudden inspiration, or with an
unexpected change, such as the conversion of childhood into puberty. It doesn’t matter if it was a bright and
colorful event or a drab and dreary one, or if it was a quick or gradual experience, it’ll always stand out as a
mysterious transfiguration, or an awakening, that leads us to another way of life.
Many times the Herald or Announcer of the Call is a repulsive, grimy or disgusting figure. Sometimes it’s
someone of low social standing or one of great prestige, or someone very old or very young, or very ugly or
of great beauty. But, in the legend, the Harbinger always appears when the time is ripe for the future Hero (or
Heroine) to undertake the Quest, and when the conditions of the Great Cosmic Field are favorable for his
Mission. We could say that the Announcer will seem to be an Emissary of Destiny, a Voice from the
Olympus, or a Messenger from Hades.
Most of the time, the Main Character of the story will feel either a great repulsion or an irresistible
fascination toward the Herald. It’s as if the Call woke up in the Hero something deeply familiar, something
that in spite of being unknown, or very startling, makes his former life seem to lack merit or importance.
After the Call, although the Hero might go back to his usual life, little by little, his regular activities will
begin to lose the importance they had before.
Once the Call has been received, the saga’s Hero begins to feel that his center of gravity is being shifted
away from his familiar lifestyle to a new, strange and faraway place.
The new location can be a distant land (as happened to Sinbad, the Sailor), a creepy forest or a deep jungle
(as in the story of Little Red Riding Hood, or that of Rudyard Kipling’s Mowgly). It could be an underground
kingdom or an underwater realm (as with Persephone or Captain Nemo), or a place high up in the clouds (as
in Jack and the Magic Beanstalk), or on an enchanted island (as in Treasure Island), a magic mountain (as
with Simon Bolivar, on top of Mount Chimborazo), or in a profound dream (as in Sleeping Beauty or
Snowhite).
For sure, it’ll always be a site of superhuman qualities (like the ones visited by the Prophet Mohammed in his
ascent through the “Paradises” of Islam), or a place full of dangers (as those lived by Hunahpu and
Ixbalanque, during their encounter with the Lords of Xibalba, as told by the Popol Vuh, the Sacred Book of
the Maya). Or it could be a locality filled with strange entities (as what happened to Alice in Wonderland), or
of untold pleasures (as those found by the brave Ulysses, in the land of the witch Circe).
The Hero can be taken to the strange place against his will, as it happened to Odysseus, when his ship was
swept along by Poseidon’s powerful breath. Or he can go by his own free will, as Theseus did when he went
to Crete, to deal with the horrible Minotaur.
Whichever the case may be, the saga always begins at the Hero’s homestead. Then, step by step, he’ll move
away from his familiar worldly circumstances and material surroundings. In a gradual manner, he’ll have to
leave behind the known coastline, the town he grew up in, his friends, his parents, his spouse and perhaps his
children. He’ll move away from his habitual comfort, from his riches, as well as from the familiar security of
known settings. Of the future, all that he can see is a menacing terra incognita, along with a weak but firm
promise of a better life.
                                             Rejecting the Call
Many times, both in real life as well as in legends, the Main Character of the story refuses to accept the Call
of Destiny. This usually happens when the individual has other interests that are hard to set aside. Or because
he, or she, is afraid to set off on a journey that would seem to be total madness, or give the impression of
leading one to a certain death, or a reckless jump into the terrifying void.
If the confused or frightened Hero rejects the Call, his life gradually becomes filled with hard, inefficient and
boring work. His days will be full of worries and trouble, with little or no satisfaction. After his refusal, his
existence becomes sterile and pointless, in spite of all effort to make headway.
When this happens, instead of accepting life’s changes, the Hero will try to defend his current status-quo—
by using all possible excuses—thus, missing the opportunity to improve himself and attain the “Golden
Fleece” that his destiny invites him to get. Instead of advancing toward the Light, the Hero looses himself
more and more in the dark and gloomy mental labyrinth in which his personal Minotaur resides.
Sadly, more often in real life than in legends, our Hero to-be, the one who refuses to accept the role that
destiny places before him, is what mostly abounds. It are thousands upon thousands of people who,
unknowingly, turn away from the Call made by their Essence, their own Higher Self. These people hold on to
a sad and miserable existence, a life full of misfortune, dissatisfaction and illness. And all because they’re
afraid to believe in what the Mysterious Emissary, their own Essence offers them.




In this case, the world of the frustrated Hero falls into a profound “slumber,” like what happened to Sleeping
Beauty. When this occurs, all action becomes barren, all effort is fruitless until the Prince (the person’s
Essence) finds a way to reach the lethargic individual and give the “Cosmic Kiss” that is to awaken the
Hero’s Persona (i.e., his Ego, his Conscious Self). This is the profound “sleep” shared by all those who for
one reason or other refuse to assume their true Life Purpose.
                                            Magical Assistance
Those that do accepted the Call, soon find a protecting element that provides them with all they may need to
begin their Great Adventure.




Sometimes that element will take the form of a Magician (like Merlin, in the story of King Arthur), or of a
Fairy Godmother (as in Cinderella), a Genie (as with Aladdin), or a Guardian that is to counsel and look after
the Hero during his Journey. All these figures are but “disguises” used by our Essence to help us become
aware of the powers that we can count on. In most cases, our Higher Self plays the role of wise Counselor or
shrewd Mentor that helps us, while we discover and give expression to our hidden identity.
This implies that our Essence has many powers in reserve, abilities that will not be put to use unless there’s a
genuine need for them.
In spite of what may happen, the Protector is always present. Although hidden, he mounts guard—day and
night—just beyond the Hero’s sensory and mental perception. As long as the Hero is willing to procure his
Goal—the expression of his Life Purpose—he can count on his Guardian Angel’s guidance and protection.
Soon enough, he’ll see that nothing can stop his progress.
Many times the Helper takes the form of a magical being, like a Fairy or an Elf, or that of a Genie, or a
Hermit, or a Witch. It could also be a Teacher, a Wise Man, an Old Woman, a Demi-god, or a Spirit.
But, what they all have in common is that they want to defend and help the Hero, up to where it’s convenient,
for the actual work has to be done by the Hero himself.
The undertone of most legends is that the Hero’s feat is to serve as a role model for the reader, so that he or
she can reach a new level of personal development. The main idea is for the reader to grow toward the
realization of the Main Purpose of his or her life.
Our Essence can mask itself as a Guide (as antiquity’s god Hermes / Mercury did), a Boatman (like Charon,
the Ferryman of Hades), an Angel, a Saint, a Guru, a Ghost, or as some kind of Animal (like Pinocchio’s
Jiminy Cricket, or like the mythical Unicorn). But, as I mentioned above, the Magical Figure will always be
ready to act in favor of the Hero, should he be aware of it or not.
                                        Crossing the Threshold
After meeting his Guide, the next element the Hero must face is the Guardian of the Portal that leads to the
“Mysterious Beyond.” Usually, this Guard will try to obstruct the Hero’s entrance, which is the only way to
cross the Doorway that separates our normal everyday world from the hidden realm of the Gods and
Demigods that reside in the antechamber of the Supreme Creator.
The Threshold itself has been represented in many ways: the Veil of Isis, the Opposing Columns, the
Entrance to the Cosmic Cave, the Hidden Door, the Dragon’s Jaws, the Reflection in the Mirror, and many
more.




Usually, the Gate is guarded by hostile beings, who in a rude and stubborn way demand that the Hero present
the proper “Key.” They could take on the form of ill-humored gargoyles, or two rows of sharp teeth, or two
rock walls that’ll crush whoever tries to pass between them, or a fierce beast that must be tamed or killed, or
savage warriors that must be vanquished or outsmarted. Indeed, the Guardian of the Threshold takes on many
forms.
Notice that the person who has not been called to cross the Threshold, will generally be more than happy to
stay within the limits of his familiar environment. He’ll even be proud of being able to avoid the Passage that
seems to lead to “the unknown side of reality.” Only (s)he who has been summoned—the Hero or Heroine of
the saga—is able to cross the Portal. For only (s)he will have the requested Key, or the Magic Potion that is
to put to sleep the Beast that guards the Entrance that separates this dark World of Mirages (the world we live
in) from the Kingdom of Light that lies on the other side of the Door.
What’s interesting is that everyone, including you and me, will someday be called to put on the Hero’s
armor, to then confront the Guards of the Mysterious Threshold. Or is it that we’ve already been called (?).
And to do this, we won’t have to go far, for the opaque Door that separates the world of our Persona from
the realm of our Essence is always very close by, for it is within ourselves.
Actually, what we’ll have to outsmart is the Barricade formed by our fears and superstitions, the Guardians
of our Persona that won’t let us advance. It’s the imaginary Door that mistaken beliefs won’t allow us to go
through. In other words, the ferocious Sentries that keep us from moving ahead are our own unjustified
terrors and foolish conjectures. These are the Enemies that we’ll have to tame or call off.
                                           Trials and Dangers
Once the Hero manages to free himself from his doubts and crosses the Threshold, he’ll notice that he has
“died.” That is, that he has stopped looking at life through the fears and misconceptions formerly held by his
Persona. He’ll now notice that he’s travelling through an unknown world, a realm he thinks he may have
seen in his dreams, or in a former life. Now’s when the metamorphosis of his being really begins, a process
that’ll show him who he really is, along with the true Purpose of his Life.
Remember that the Hero is still under the protection of his Escort, an assistance that appears now and then to
help him solve the many and diverse obstacles and enigmas that appear during his Journey.
Now’s the time to purify himself, a period in which the body’s senses are to be silenced, so that the Hero can
concentrate on more important matters. Now begins an interval of deep introspection to evaluate all inherited
or in-fixed cultural beliefs. The idea is to keep all genuine notions, while removing all superstitious or
mistaken opinions.




This is when the Hero discovers his true attributes, the ones he was born with, plus those he developed on his
own. And as he gets to know himself better, he’ll also notice that he can count on his Guide to help him act
out his particular Life Purpose. In short, it’s a time to learn to make better use of his own aptitudes and
talents, his inborn potential.
The Hero’s mysterious Guide will now let him know about the duties and privileges that come with the
acceptance of his Specific Function in life. Again, it’s a time to “die” and stop being who he formerly
thought he was, as well as to become more familiar with who he is to be from now on.
This is the “death” and “resurrection” experienced by Tammuz, Mithra, Adonis, Attis, Osiris and Jesus. It’s
the renewal of the Sun and of the Spirit.
But first, the Hero must advance toward the very Doors of Heaven; he must reach the Wall that encloses the
Garden of Eden. It’s a time to learn to harmonize with the most subtle and powerful energy-forces of the
Universe. Now’s when the Hero gets to know the true foundation that holds up the dream he formerly
thought to be Reality.
                                                The Pinnacle
At this stage of the story, the Hero arrives at the highest point of his Adventure. This is the moment when he
must cut loose the Gordian Knot that has kept him tied to a limited, distressing and lackluster existence.
Now, he’ll have to combine all his strong points and abilities, to then enter the Sacrosanct place that lodges
the desired Treasure and make it his own.
The Hero is now granted (or takes by force) that which allows him to see a panoramic and transcendental
view of the blueprint of reality, the structure that lies behind the poor interpretation of what our bodily senses
perceive of life.
The Hero can now envision the essential nature of the Cosmos and of Life itself. Before his eyes, not only
will his destiny be revealed, but that of all Mankind. All this presented to him in a way that his mortal
intellect can understand. This is when the Hero stops being a Homo Sapiens to become a Homo Universalis.
When the Hero reaches this point of his Quest, the potential that resides in the deepest stratum of his being
will be set free once and for all. It’s his moment of glory, the fusion of his Persona with his Essence, the
union of Man with the God that created him. From this lofty summit, from the highest point of the Cosmic
Tree, from the abode of the Gods, the Hero becomes aware and takes possession of his true place in the
world.




It’s the moment when Jason takes the Golden Fleece from the (cosmic) tree, whose roots and trunk are
eternally guarded by the fiery dragon. This is a wonderful allegory that presents, in a metaphoric fashion, the
way that the Greek hero “rescues” (that is, understands) the Golden Fleece (i.e., the position of the Sun) from
the “tree” that represents the Milky Way. The “dragon” is the Serpens constellation, the “serpent” that lies at
the foot of the “starry tree.” Jason is represented by the Orion constellation, the celestial giant.
From here on, the Prince who thought to be a pauper recognizes that he’s to become a future King, and when
Cinderella meets her Fairy Godmother (her own Essence) who brings forth her true appearance, as well as
her destiny beside her handsome Prince. Now’s when the Ugly Duckling discovers that he is a beautiful
Swan, and when the Scarecrow of the Wizard of Oz finds the intelligence that he’s always had within
himself.
This is the magic moment when the adventure really begins, as the shy and colorless apprentice becomes an
“Alexander the Great” in his own right. When the Hero reaches this high point of the saga, he understands
that he must leave behind everything that could hinder the expression of his legitimate role in life, his
Specific Function. He must now cut loose his former bonds and habits, letting go of the fears, unsuitable
alliances and unfit ambitions that correspond to a former period of his life. Now, he’ll have to “die”
regarding his previous existence, so that his new identity can “live” on.
It’s the moment of truth when Snowhite, after having been kissed by the Prince (her own Essence), leaves
behind her tormented life. It’s when Harry Potter discovers that he’s the son of a great and esteemed
magician. It’s when Superman takes off his “Clark Kent” disguise, and when Arthur, as notified by Merlin,
finds out that he’s the legitimate heir to the throne of England.
But it’s also when an ordinary person discovers that his, or her, Life Purpose is to be a writer, a singer, a
plumber, a salesperson, or an architect. It’s when each of us learns, notified by an ploy of our Essence, what
our true destiny is to be, that which will provide us the same well-being, happiness and prosperity as that
received by the Hero of the saga.
To find our Role in this life is to have found Aladdin’s Magic Lamp. To accept and assume the Main Reason
of our existence, is to grasp and rub this prodigious Lamp. This is what moves the Genie (our Essence, again)
to act in our favor; it’s what causes it to provide us with anything we might need to live our own marvelous
Odyssey. This is the true magic that holds up every legend. It’s the very Elixir of Life.
                                                 The Return
The Return begins when the Hero is to bring back his Prize, in order to share it with the rest of his peers.
Without this part of the adventure, the Elixir, the Sacred Fire or the attained Powers would be useless. This is
the part of the Story when the Hero must strive to bring the ethereal and intangible Lotus Jewel back to our
cold and intractable world.
The temptation felt by Gautama Sakyamuni, the Illuminated Buddha, to remain in Nirvana was very strong.
He thought, “Why return to the world of the ignorant, obstinate and primitive human beings that know
nothing of visions or transcendental goals? Why try to convince those that are hypnotized by the mirage of
materialism and protocol?” Were it not for the pleading of the divine Brahma who convinced Gautama to
share the Light that he had found with our agitated world, he may not have come back.
Other Heroes were not able to return when they wanted to, like what happened to the Greek lass Persephone,
or to the Japanese Empress Izanami, both main figures of a strangely similar saga: both were very anxious to
leave the Hell they were trapped in.
Nevertheless, the return of the story’s Main Character and his reinstatement into his society has to happen so
that the indispensable circulation of spiritual energy into the lower levels of reality can take place. The naïve
young girl, now transformed into a beautiful Princess, and the scatterbrained boy, now a wise Scholar, or the
underrated youngster, now an important and respected adult, must all come back to assume the role that
destiny requires of them.
It is now when the young Ostara brings back with her the stream of cosmic energy that is to awaken in
Spring the dormant and expressionless Nature. Or the return of the Phoenix that brings the Elixir that is to
redeem humanity; or the return of Lono, the Hawaiian god, or that of Chac Mol, the Aztec god, who both
bring the rains that are to favor the new year’s crops.
One way or another, while the Hero moves toward the Door that will let him back into his normal world,
everything is being prepared for the forthcoming stage where “the Power of the Gods moves within the
World of Man.”
In this part of the story, the Hero can also count on the help of his impalpable Guide, for the return to the
World of Man can be complicated due to the meddling of “Evil Magicians” or “Angry Demons.”
In this case, vested interests have to be neutralized and impersonators must be removed: the Crown must
return to the hands of the legitimate King. This is clearly seen in the saga of Richard, the Lion Hearted, when
his half-brother, Prince John, refused to give back the throne of England, when the former returned from the
Crusades.
Now’s the time to deal with intrigues and renegade emotions that oppose the advance of Essence into the
world of Persona.
                                       The Homecoming Doorway




The Door that leads out of the Garden of Eden, the Passage through which Alice exits Wonderland, the
Threshold through which Unity moves toward Plurality, all refer to the same thing: the moment when the
Hero is to leave the ethereal World of Light to return to the dense World of Matter.
The occasion marks the initial emission of pure, intangible energy as it flows into the perceptible world. It’s
the moment when the seed sends forth its first shoot; it’s when an immaterial idea begins to take its form in
the physical world.
Our Hero again must confront the “Protectors of the Normal Life,” those who strive to keep things as they
are: in their favor. They are the “Custodians of the Past,” those in charge of defending the mean interests of
entrenched power groups.
In this part of the saga, the Hero must convince his elders, as well as the skeptics and the prudish, that the
Sacred or Magical Trophy he has brought back is, in effect, the marvelous Haoma, the Royal Ambrosia or the
Divine Manna, that is, the Panacea that’ll solve all of humanity’s ailments.
The Guardians of the “Temple of Dogma” will try to ridicule, belittle or simply deny the qualities of the
conquered Prize. Some will instill doubt and suspicion, trying to discredit the remarkable attributes of the
Ring, Sword or Grail brought back by the Hero, so as to keep for themselves the power they took over when
the Hero left. Cinderella had to defend herself from the attack of her half-sisters, Jesus had to face Herod and
the members of the Sanhedrin, Jason had to stand up to the Lords of his home town. All these Heroes had to
suffer the onslaught of those who by word or by deed had decided to prevent the use of the power of the
conquered Treasure.
These will be difficult times for the Hero, for he’ll have to convince the Lackeys of the Intellect, the Vicars
of Ritualistic Creed, and the Fanatics of the Material World, that the amazing adventure he lived actually
happened, and that the power of the Trophy he brought back is absolutely real. He’ll have to fight and
conquer the mirage that everyone believes to be real—a very difficult task, in which many genuine Heroes
simply got fed up and stopped trying.
In a way, the problem to be solved is how to awaken in others an interest in the powers of the Prize, in spite
of the incredulity, the cynicism and the indifference of a society that doesn’t know it has gone astray. Now’s
the time when the Hero must be strong and prove that the earned or captured Elixir, Magic Wand or
Enchanted Amulet truly works.
The time is similar to when Agros, son of Isis and Osiris, a Demigod who represented the new forces of
Nature, is born. Also Shiva and Parvathi now unite to generate new life, and when the Celtic Druids begin to
collect the nine types of wood to ignite the sacred fire of Beltane.
Now, what has always been united but seemed to be separated begins to function as an organic whole,
making full use of the powers that this miraculous union brings forth. It’s the conjugation of what is high
with what is low, the union of yang with yin, the pairing of essence and substance. In each of these pairs, the
one without the other is powerless; but together they’re unbeatable.
                                            The Twofold Hero
After having overcome the obstacles that prevented the Hero’s return, he becomes a “Master of Two
Worlds,” a seasoned veteran that can act both in the subliminal domain as well as in the material world.
The storyteller makes the Hero seem like a super-person, as one who after having outwitted dragons, demons
and soldiers, or after having found the Nail from which the Vault of Heaven hangs, or after having gotten rid
of the impostor who had overtaken his place, finally fulfilled his destiny.
These Heroes and Heroines, as I have repeatedly said, actually are the people who responded to the Call
made by their own Essence. They are those who freed themselves from the fears and superstitions that had
kept their Persona trapped in a lethargic state. It’s the story of ordinary people, like you and me, who dared
to discover, accept and act out their destiny, that is, to assume their Specific Function in life.
The Story itself is about the struggle that goes on within ourselves, while we strive to control the “serpent”
that lives in each of us: the oldest and most primitive neuralgic part of our brain, that which stores our most
basic fears, instincts and passions.
Thus, we see that our real battle is to neutralize mistaken beliefs, conceptual errors and wrong convictions
that keep us from assuming our Specific Function. These are the “dragons” and “demons” that we must
conquer. It’s the battle against everything within ourselves that in one way or another prevents our Persona
from acting out what our Essence must accomplish, in order to carry out our destiny.




The Hidden Circle represents the path that leads the Hero (you or me) toward his Essence and his Destiny,
his Mission in Life. Be it heard by an infant, as in Hercules’ case, or by an old man, as happened to Don
Quixote, the Call is made by our own Essence.
Likewise, remember that the able and mysterious Assistant who protects and helps the Hero through-out his
Adventure is none other than his own Higher Self, his Guardian Angel, his Essence. The same happens in
each of us.
All this, and more, is what we have to experience directly in our own lives, as our Persona gradually learns

It’s curious to see how the fourfold diagram that constitutes the basic structure of most Legends imitates, in a
totally natural and impersonal way, the four phases that make up every Astronical Cycle. And, if we look at
the sequential order of each of these phases, we’ll even be able to see the sub-sections described in the
“Introduction” chapter.
One of Astronica’s most important Cycles is the Helios Cycle, an astrophysical factor that can help
determine a person’s Purpose in Life.
Every Astronical Cycle has four points where the action of its basic effect changes. These points are called
Alpha (α), Beta (β), Omega (Ω) and Delta (Δ). This is the order followed by the cycle’s action, i.e., a
counter-clockwise motion (see next diagram).
The Alpha Point is where the intangible creative forces of Reality have absolute command; it’s The Pinnacle
described above, in a former chapter. Whereas, the Omega Point is where the forces of Nature apply their
maximum creative influence on the material expression of all entities, ideas and projects. The Beta and Delta
Points are the two Portals, Passages or Thresholds that either lead from the intangible to the perceptible (at
Beta) or from Concrete Expression back to Abstract Designs (at Delta).




During the time it takes the focal point of an Astronical Cycle (for example, the Helios Factor) to move from
Omega to Delta, the influence of the Cycle favors (1) the evaluation of our material belongings, (2) the
review of our emotional context and (3) the way we understand what goes on around us. This is the period in
which the Hero first receives the Call and is invited (or forced) to leave his known surroundings (i.e., his
usual “Zone of Comfort and Security”). Furthermore, at the Delta point of the Cycle is where he’ll encounter
the Guardian of the Passage into the Ethereal Realm.
During the time it takes the same focal point to move from Delta to Alpha, the Hero must examine and
evaluate his inner values, as well the way he harmonizes (or not) with the Universal Principles that govern all
there is. During this period, he must neutralize or remove from his life any element that may complicate or
block his union with the superior forces of his Essence. This part of the Cycle ends at Alpha, an area in time
and space where the amazing faculties of Essence reign supreme. It’s when the Hero can, with more ease,
attain the Trophy that he came to get.
Once the focal point of the cycle passes its Alpha point, a torrent of new energy stimulates our Hero’s
inspiration and creativity. The passage from Alpha to Beta marks a time to make new plans, conceive new
ideas and build / test prototypes. Now’s when our Hero begins to return to his home town, together with his
recently conquered Trophy: the new projects, knowledge and designs that his Essence has given him,
together with the awareness of his true Purpose in Life. At the Beta point of the cycle, the Hero again will
have to confront a Guard that will not let him pass from the incorporeal Realm of the Gods back to his
homeland, a situation that’ll make him ponder about his Quest: was it real or just a figment of his
imagination?
As soon as the Cycle’s Beta point is passed, the Hero must start to give a material form to his new ideas.
During this phase, his new projects must begin to become a tangible reality. It is now time to convince the
world that his Trophy (i.e., his new ideas and projects) have the potential he believes them to have. The
initial implementation of his new objectives will have to be completed by the time that the Cycle’s focal
point returns to Omega, the part of the Astronical Cycle that favors the most worldly and material expression
of any objective.
What I’ve tried to make evident in the Hidden Circle is that a person’s evolution, without him knowing it,
works in sync with the events and conditions activated by the Astronical Cycles that govern Reality. And, if
this person becomes aware of the sequence followed by the main Astronical Cycles, and of the way they
enmesh with Reality, he’ll then be able to adapt his day-to-day activities to their influence, in order to
achieve the best results concerning the first implementation and subsequent development of his goals.
