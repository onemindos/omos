**OBJECTION TO JURISDICTION, NOTICE TO COURT, NOT MAKING A GENERAL
APPEARANCE IN COURT VOLUNTARILY BUT IS MAKING A SPECIAL APPEARANCE,
COURT LACKS CONSENT, COURT REQUIRED TO PROVE ITS HAS PERSONAL
JURISDICTION**

To the Honorable \[Judge\'s Name\] and the Court:

I, **\[Your Name\]**, am making this **Special Appearance** solely to
object to the **jurisdiction** of this Court over the above-referenced
child support case. This special appearance is made for the **sole
purpose of challenging the Court's jurisdiction** and does not
constitute any consent, waiver, or submission to the Court's authority
over me or the subject matter. By making this special appearance, I do
not waive any of my constitutional rights, including my right to
challenge jurisdiction, nor do I acknowledge or consent to any finding
or proceeding based on a lack of jurisdiction.

**Objection to Jurisdiction**

I respectfully object to the jurisdiction of this Court over the matters
at hand and demand that the Court provide **tangible evidence** of its
jurisdiction pursuant to the following:

1.  **Personal Jurisdiction**:\
    The Court must demonstrate that it has personal jurisdiction over me
    in compliance with due process under **U.S. Constitution, 14th
    Amendment**, which guarantees that no state shall deprive any person
    of life, liberty, or property without due process of law.

    -   The Court must show that proper service of process has been
        made, and that I have sufficient **minimum contacts** with this
        jurisdiction as required by **International Shoe Co. v.
        Washington**, 326 U.S. 310 (1945).

    -   If the Court cannot provide evidence of personal jurisdiction, I
        assert that the proceedings are constitutionally invalid, and
        any judgment entered would be void ab initio.

2.  **Subject Matter Jurisdiction**:\
    The Court must provide proof that it has subject matter jurisdiction
    over this child support case. This includes compliance with relevant
    federal and state law regarding paternity and child support
    obligations.

    -   Pursuant to **28 U.S. Code § 1331**, federal courts have
        jurisdiction over cases involving federal law, including child
        support enforcement under the **Child Support Enforcement
        Act** (Title IV-D of the Social Security Act).

    -   The Court must show evidence that it has jurisdiction over
        matters of child support, including the proper establishment of
        paternity in accordance with the **Code of Federal Regulations
        (45 C.F.R. § 303.5)**. Specifically, the state must provide
        proof that all procedural requirements for paternity
        establishment have been met, including proper notice to both
        parties and the opportunity to voluntarily acknowledge
        paternity.

3.  **Notice of Rights and Due Process Compliance**:\
    In accordance with **45 C.F.R. § 303.5**, the Court must demonstrate
    that both the mother and alleged father were properly informed of
    their rights, including:

    -   The **alternatives to acknowledging paternity**.

    -   The **legal consequences** of acknowledging paternity.
        ***(Orally or video or audio, and in writing)***

    -   The opportunity to **speak with trained staff** to answer
        questions about paternity establishment.

> Failure to provide proper notice or the opportunity to make an
> informed decision would constitute a violation of my **due process
> rights** under the **14th Amendment**.

4.  **Compliance with the Requirements of 28 U.S. Code § 1691**:\
    As mandated by **28 U.S. Code § 1691**, any writs or process issued
    by the Court must be under the **seal of the Court** and signed by
    the Clerk. The Court must provide evidence that all process and
    writs issued in this case conform to these requirements.

    -   The **seal and signature** of the Clerk must be evident on any
        process or documentation related to this case, in accordance
        with **SCANBE MANUFACTURING COMPANY v. Tryon**, 400 F.2d 598
        (9th Cir. 1968).

5.  **Violation of Due Process and Void Judgments**:\
    As established by the **Supreme Court in Baldwin v. Iowa State
    Traveling Men\'s Ass\'n**, 283 U.S. 52 (1931), and **United
    States v. Indoor Cultivation Equip. from High Tech Indoor Garden
    Supply**, 55 F.3d 1311, 1317 (7th Cir. 1995), any judgment entered
    in violation of **due process** is **void** and has no legal effect.

    -   I demand that the Court vacate any judgment entered in this case
        that was based on a lack of jurisdiction or failure to comply
        with the legal and constitutional requirements outlined above.

**Demand for Tangible Evidence**

I respectfully demand that the Court provide **tangible
evidence** demonstrating the following:

1.  That it has **personal jurisdiction** over me.

2.  That it has **subject matter jurisdiction** over this child support
    matter.

3.  That the **state** has complied with all relevant provisions of
    the **Child Support Enforcement Act** (Title IV-D of the Social
    Security Act) and **45 C.F.R. § 303.5**.

4.  That all **writs and process** issued by the Court comply with the
    requirements of **28 U.S. Code § 1691**.

5.  That due process safeguards were followed and that I was provided
    with **adequate notice** of my rights, including the **alternatives
    to paternity establishment** and the **legal consequences** of
    acknowledging paternity.

**Grounds for Setting Aside and Vacating the Judgment**

1.  If the Court fails to provide such evidence, I request that
    the **child support order** be **set aside** and vacated in
    accordance with **Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b)(4)**, which
    requires that any judgment made in violation of due process be void
    and vacated.

2.  **Violation of Due Process**

> In **County of San Diego v. Gorham**, 186 Cal.App.4th 1215 (2010), the
> Court held that a judgment entered in violation of a party's due
> process rights is a **nullity** and void from its inception, (**past,
> present, and future**). In this case, the **child support order** was
> entered against me without proper notice, without an opportunity to be
> heard, and without demonstrating that the court had the
> appropriate **jurisdiction** over the subject matter. The due process
> violations in this case are substantive, and as such, the judgment
> must be set aside.

3.  The Supreme Court in ***Howlett v. Rose, 496 U.S. 356 (1990),***
    established that **federal law is enforceable in state courts**. The
    Court emphasized that laws passed under the Constitution are as
    binding in state courts as those passed by the state legislature.
    This principle of federal supremacy, found in **Article VI, Clause 2
    of the U.S. Constitution** (the Supremacy Clause), mandates that
    state actors, including state courts and child support services, are
    bound to uphold federal law.
