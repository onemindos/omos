**Re: Demand for Notice of Nature and Cause of Action, and Violation of
the Bill of Attainder Clause**

Dear \[Recipient Name\],

I am writing to formally demand that you provide a **detailed statement
of the nature and cause of action** regarding the child support case
currently being pursued against me. This request is made pursuant to
constitutional protections under the **United States Codes**, as well as
binding **Supreme Court case law**, ensuring my right to understand the
legal grounds and jurisdictional basis of actions taken against me.

**Legal Justification and Demand for Transparency**

1.  **Right to Due Process and Notice (Fifth and Fourteenth
    Amendments)**

Under the **Fifth** and **Fourteenth Amendments** to the **United States
Constitution**, I am entitled to **due process** of law, including the
right to **adequate notice** of any legal actions taken against me.
The **Supreme Court** has consistently held that an individual must be
informed of the **nature and cause** of any legal action before they can
be required to respond or defend themselves.

-   **Mulane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.,** 339 U.S. 306 (1950):
    The Court held that due process requires proper notice and an
    opportunity to be heard regarding the **nature and cause** of any
    legal actions, ensuring individuals have the ability to make
    informed decisions.

-   **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.**, 339 U.S. 306
    (1950): Notice must contain adequate information to allow an
    individual to prepare an informed response. The Court held that
    individuals cannot be deprived of their rights without being
    provided with sufficient details on the legal actions taken against
    them.

2.  **Right to be Informed of Jurisdiction**

Pursuant to **28 U.S. Code § 1331**, which governs federal jurisdiction,
I am entitled to be informed of the **jurisdictional grounds** upon
which the court and child support services are proceeding. If the court
has not established jurisdiction under lawful legal authority, any
judgment rendered may be void.

3.  **Right to Confrontation and Information Under the Sixth Amendment**

Under the **Sixth Amendment**, I am guaranteed the **right to be
informed** of the nature and cause of the accusations against me. While
the **Sixth Amendment** generally applies to criminal cases, the
principles of **informed consent** and the **right to confront
accusations** also apply to civil proceedings where my liberty or rights
are at stake.

-   **United States v. Gouveia**, 467 U.S. 180 (1984): The Court
    emphasized that an individual has the **right to know** the charges
    and **legal basis** for the actions against them, even in civil
    matters that implicate due process rights.

-   **Faretta v. California**, 422 U.S. 806 (1975): The Court held that
    a defendant must be informed of their **constitutional rights** in
    order to make an **informed decision** about their legal
    proceedings.

4.  **Demand for the Nature and Cause of the Action**

Under **42 U.S.C. § 652(a)(1)** and **42 U.S.C. § 654(3)**, state child
support enforcement programs are required to provide individuals
with **notice of the legal grounds** for any child support actions,
including **statutory authority** for such actions. Additionally,
the **Administrative Procedure Act (APA)** mandates that agencies such
as the Child Support Services Department must provide proper notice of
the nature and cause of any action taken against individuals.

In this regard, I respectfully demand that you provide the following
information:

-   **The nature of the action** being taken by the Child Support
    Services Department and/or the court. This includes whether this is
    an initial child support determination, enforcement action, or
    modification of an existing order.

-   **The legal cause** for the action, including the specific statutory
    and regulatory authority under which this action is being pursued.

-   The **procedural history** of the case, including all documents,
    orders, or findings that establish jurisdiction over this matter.

-   All **evidence** or documents filed by the Child Support Services
    Department or the court in support of the claim and the
    jurisdictional authority.

6.  **Violation of the Bill of Attainder Clause**

Furthermore, I demand that the court provide a **full and proper
explanation** for the failure to comply with these requirements. Should
the court fail to fulfill its constitutional duties, this would be
a **violation of the Bill of Attainder Clause** under **Article I,
Section 9 of the United States Constitution**. The Bill of Attainder
Clause prohibits any legislative or judicial act that targets a specific
individual or group for punishment without a trial. Any action by the
court that fails to provide proper notice, due process, and an
opportunity to challenge the proceedings may be interpreted as
imposing **punitive measures** without due process, thus violating this
important constitutional safeguard.

-   **Ex parte Garland**, 71 U.S. 333 (1866): The Court held that
    legislative or judicial actions that impose punishment on
    individuals without a trial are prohibited under the Bill of
    Attainder Clause.

-   **United States v. Brown**, 381 U.S. 437 (1965): The Court
    reaffirmed that laws or actions that directly impose punishment
    without a fair trial violate the Bill of Attainder Clause.

**Legal Precedent Supporting Demand**

-   **United States v. Smith**, 499 U.S. 160 (1991): The **Supreme
    Court** held that an individual must be given **clear and specific
    notice** of the legal grounds and charges in any action against
    them.

-   **Hertz v. Friend**, 559 U.S. 77 (2010): The **Court** emphasized
    that individuals must be informed of the **factual basis**
    and **jurisdictional grounds** for any legal proceedings involving
    them.

-   **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.**, 339 U.S. 306
    (1950): The **Court** ruled that due process requires that
    individuals be given adequate notice and an opportunity to
    understand the **nature and cause** of legal actions.

-   **42 U.S.C. § 652(a)(1)**: Requires that **state child support
    agencies** provide **proper notice** of the actions and the legal
    grounds for child support proceedings.

8.  **Sixth Amendment Rights**\
    The Sixth Amendment to the United States Constitution guarantees
    several fundamental rights to individuals accused of crimes,
    especially in the context of criminal prosecutions. These rights
    include:\
    A. **Right to be Informed of Charges**: Defendants must be informed
    of the nature and cause of the accusations against them.\
    B. **Right to an Impartial Jury**: Defendants are entitled to a
    trial by an impartial jury of their peers.

9.  **Clarification of the Nature of the Action**\
    The undersigned Respondent demands clarification as to whether the
    proceedings in this matter are:\
    A. Civil or Criminal in nature?\
    B. Governed by Common Law or Maritime (Admiralty) Law?\
    C. Administrative or Judicial in nature?\
    D. Bound by Supreme Court rulings?

> **Explanation of Legal Nature of Cases:**

1.  **Civil Law**: Typically involves disputes between individuals or
    organizations (e.g., contracts, property disputes, personal injury
    claims), seeking remedies such as compensation or specific
    performance.

2.  **Criminal Law**: Addresses offenses against the state or society
    (e.g., theft, assault), where the government prosecutes the accused.

3.  **Common Law**: A system of law developed by judges through court
    decisions, applying to both civil and criminal matters in the United
    States.

4.  **Maritime (Admiralty) Law**: Governs nautical issues and private
    maritime disputes, with jurisdiction typically held by federal
    courts.

10. **Bill of Attainder Clause**\
    The U.S. Constitution prohibits bills of attainder, which are laws
    that declare an individual or group guilty and impose punishment
    without trial. The Supreme Court in *United States v. Brown* (1965)
    held that such actions violate constitutional protections. The
    undersigned asserts that this case constitutes a violation of the
    Bill of Attainder Clause and demands immediate dismissal if due
    process protections are not adhered to.

11. **Demand for Proof of Injury in Fact**\
    The undersigned Respondent demands proof of injury in fact as
    defined in *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife* (1992), requiring the
    Petitioner to establish:\
    A. Concrete and particularized injury.\
    B. Causal connection between the injury and the challenged conduct.\
    C. Likelihood that a favorable decision will redress the injury.

12. **Supreme Court's Standing Requirements**\
    The Supreme Court\'s decision in *Lujan v. Defenders of
    Wildlife* emphasized the necessity for plaintiffs to show actual,
    concrete injuries and a causal connection to the defendant\'s
    actions. The undersigned demands that the Petitioner establish
    standing by providing the necessary evidence, or this case must be
    dismissed.

13. **Seventh Amendment Rights and Jury Trial**\
    Under the Seventh Amendment, the undersigned Respondent is entitled
    to a jury trial in civil matters where the value in controversy
    exceeds the constitutional threshold. The recent case of *SEC v.
    Jarkesy* (2024) reinforced that federal civil enforcement actions
    must be adjudicated in federal court, with a right to a jury trial.
    This reinforces the Respondent\'s constitutional right to a trial by
    jury in this matter.

14. **Improper Venue and Due Process Violations**\
    Under the Sixth, Seventh, and Fourteenth Amendments, the undersigned
    was guaranteed a trial by a jury of peers, and as such, the venue of
    these proceedings is improper. The Court lacks jurisdiction over
    this matter and has violated the Respondent\'s constitutional
    rights. Furthermore, the case should be dismissed as it fails to
    meet the procedural and venue requirements outlined in the
    Constitution.

15. **The Supremacy Clause and Federal Precedence**\
    The Supremacy Clause, found in Article VI, Clause 2 of the
    Constitution, mandates that federal law takes precedence over
    conflicting state laws. This Court must adhere to the principles of
    the Supremacy Clause, and failure to do so would violate the
    Constitution, undermining the rights of the undersigned Respondent.
    The undersigned demands that the case be dismissed in light of the
    Supremacy Clause.

16. **Due Process Under the Fourteenth Amendment**\
    The Fourteenth Amendment guarantees that no state shall deprive any
    person of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. The
    undersigned asserts that the actions in this case violate their due
    process rights, and the case must be dismissed.

17. **Violation of Separation of Powers**\
    The undersigned Respondent contends that the Title IV-D action
    constitutes an improper exercise of executive power, violating the
    separation of powers doctrine. Title IV-D mandates enforcement
    through executive agencies, which encroach upon the judicial
    branch's functions, particularly in adjudicating disputes over
    fundamental rights. The undersigned moves to dismiss the action on
    these grounds.

18. **Demand for Nature and Cause of Action**\
    Pursuant to the Sixth, Seventh, and Fourteenth Amendments, the
    undersigned Respondent formally demands detailed clarification of
    the action brought against them, including:\
    A. Whether the proceedings are civil or criminal.\
    B. Whether the proceedings are governed by common law, maritime law,
    or administrative law.\
    C. Identification of the legal basis for the action and any statutes
    or regulations cited.\
    D. A list of all evidence, documents, or pleadings the Petitioner
    intends to rely on.

19. **Failure to Respond and Dismissal of the Case**\
    Failure by the Court to respond to this demand within 30 days of

20. **Conclusion**\
    In conclusion, the undersigned Respondent demands that the Court

    Constitution. Further, the undersigned asserts that this case
    violates fundamental constitutional rights, including due process,
    the right to a jury trial, and the separation of powers, and thus
