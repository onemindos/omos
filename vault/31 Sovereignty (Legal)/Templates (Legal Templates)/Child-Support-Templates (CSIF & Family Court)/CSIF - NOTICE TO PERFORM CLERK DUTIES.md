**Relevant Legal Points:**

> 1\. **Clerk's Role:**

Your duty is to accept filings, not interpret, analyze, or question
their content or intention. Such determinations rest solely with the
judge or jury. Attempts to reject or refuse filings based on content may
constitute an overreach and unlawful exercise of authority.

> 2\. **Obligation to File:**

Under **18 U.S.C. § 2071**, it is a crime to conceal, remove, or
otherwise obstruct the filing of legal documents. Refusal to accept
filings that challenge void judgments undermines due process and the
constitutional rights of the filer.

> 3\. **Support Orders and Void Judgments:**

Support orders issued through administrative processes rather than a
court of competent jurisdiction are not valid judicial orders. As such,
these proceedings violate due process and federal law, particularly when
adjudicated by individuals with a pecuniary interest linked to Title
IV-D funding.

> 4\. **Federal Precedence:**

Federal law, including **42 U.S.C. § 654**, establishes that Title IV-D
support proceedings are administrative and separate from judicial
processes. Additionally, **Howlett v. Rose, 496 U.S. 356 (1990)**,
reaffirms that state courts are bound by federal law and cannot ignore
or override federal rights. This ruling ensures that federal rights and
remedies remain enforceable in state courts, leaving no room for local
practices to diminish these protections.

> 5\. **Pro Se Rights:**

The Supreme Court has consistently held that filings from pro se
litigants are not to be held to the same standards as those drafted by
attorneys. Any attempt to reject or delay filing based on formatting or
technicalities constitutes an unnecessary and unlawful obstruction.

**Request for Compliance:**

I respectfully demand that my filings be accepted and processed without

but to report such actions as violations of **18 U.S.C. §
2071**, **Howlett v. Rose, 496 U.S. 356 (1990)**, and obstruction of
justice.
