# HRV Interpretation Guide

> *Understanding Heart Rate Variability for optimal recovery and performance.*

---

## What is HRV?

**Heart Rate Variability (HRV)** measures the variation in time between consecutive heartbeats. Counterintuitively, MORE variability = better health and recovery.

**Example:**
- ❌ **Low HRV:** Heartbeats at exactly 60 bpm = 1.000 seconds between each beat (no variation)
- ✅ **High HRV:** Heartbeats average 60 bpm, but vary: 0.9s, 1.1s, 0.95s, 1.05s, etc.

---

## Why HRV Matters

### Autonomic Nervous System Indicator

HRV reflects the balance between two branches of your nervous system:

| System | Function | HRV Impact |
|--------|----------|------------|
| **Parasympathetic** | "Rest & digest", recovery | ✅ Increases HRV |
| **Sympathetic** | "Fight or flight", stress | ❌ Decreases HRV |

**High HRV = Parasympathetic dominant = Well-recovered**
**Low HRV = Sympathetic dominant = Stressed/Unrecovered**

### What HRV Tells You

- ✅ Recovery status (ready to train hard or need rest)
- ✅ Stress levels (psychological + physiological)
- ✅ Sleep quality (poor sleep = low HRV next day)
- ✅ Training adaptation (improving = HRV trending up)
- ✅ Illness detection (HRV drops before symptoms)
- ✅ Overtraining warning (persistent low HRV)

---

## HRV Measurement

### How to Measure

**Best practices:**
- Measure same time daily (morning upon waking = most consistent)
- Measure before standing (lying or seated)
- Calm, quiet environment
- 1-5 minute measurement (longer = more accurate)
- Consistent position (lying vs seated can differ significantly)

**Devices:**
- **Oura Ring:** Automatic overnight measurement (gold standard)
- **Whoop Strap:** Automatic overnight measurement
- **Apple Watch:** Manual measurement (Breathe app or HRV apps)
- **Garmin:** Some models have HRV tracking
- **Chest strap + app:** Most accurate (Elite HRV, HRV4Training)

### HRV Metrics

Different devices use different HRV measurements:

| Metric | What It Is | Used By |
|--------|------------|---------|
| **RMSSD** | Root Mean Square of Successive Differences | Oura, Elite HRV, most apps |
| **SDNN** | Standard Deviation of NN intervals | Medical/research, some devices |
| **LF/HF Ratio** | Low Frequency / High Frequency power | Advanced analysis |

**Note:** RMSSD is most common and best for daily tracking.

## Understanding Your HRV Numbers

### Typical Ranges (RMSSD in milliseconds)

**Age and fitness dependent:**

| Age | Sedentary | Active | Athlete |
|-----|-----------|--------|---------|
| **20-25** | 40-50 | 60-80 | 80-120 |
| **25-35** | 35-45 | 55-75 | 75-110 |
| **35-45** | 30-40 | 50-70 | 70-100 |
| **45-55** | 25-35 | 45-65 | 65-90 |
| **55+** | 20-30 | 40-60 | 60-80 |

**Note:** These are general ranges. Your personal baseline matters more than absolute numbers.

### What's "Good" vs "Bad"?

**❌ Don't compare to others:**
- HRV is highly individual
- Genetics play a large role
- Fitness level affects baseline
- Age naturally lowers HRV

**✅ Compare to YOUR baseline:**
- Establish your average over 2-4 weeks
- Track TRENDS (direction) not single days
- Green/Yellow/Red zones based on YOUR data

## HRV-Guided Training

### Traffic Light System

Most apps/devices use a color-coded system based on your personal baseline:

#### 🟢 **GREEN (High HRV)**

**Status:** Well-recovered, parasympathetic dominant
**HRV Range:** Above your baseline (typically baseline + 10ms or more)

**Training Recommendations:**
- ✅ Full intensity training
- ✅ PR attempts
- ✅ High-volume sessions
- ✅ Skill work requiring focus
- ✅ Max effort intervals

**Lifestyle:**
- Body is adapting well
- Continue current recovery protocols
- Good time to push performance envelope

#### 🟡 **YELLOW (Normal HRV)**

**Status:** Normal recovery, balanced state
**HRV Range:** Within ±10ms of baseline

**Training Recommendations:**
- ✅ Moderate intensity training
- ✅ Standard training plan
- ⚠️ Avoid PRs or max efforts
- ✅ Technical work
- ✅ Moderate volume

**Lifestyle:**
- Maintain current protocols
- Don't add extra stressors
- Ensure adequate recovery

#### 🔴 **RED (Low HRV)**

**Status:** Unrecovered, sympathetic dominant
**HRV Range:** Below baseline by 10ms or more (or bottom 20% of your range)

**Training Recommendations:**
- ❌ No high-intensity training
- ❌ No max efforts or PRs
- ✅ Active recovery only (easy walk, yoga, mobility)
- ✅ Light technique work
- 💤 Consider full rest day

**Lifestyle:**
- ⚠️ Body needs extra recovery
- Earlier bedtime (+30-60 min)
- Stress management priority
- Review training volume
- Check for illness (HRV drops before symptoms)

## Reading HRV Trends

### Single Day vs Trend

**❌ Don't overreact to one day:**
- HRV naturally fluctuates day-to-day
- Occasional red day = normal
- Single green day ≠ fully recovered

**✅ Focus on 3-7 day trends:**
- 3+ consecutive red days = significant issue
- Trend declining = accumulating stress/fatigue
- Trend improving = positive adaptation

### Weekly HRV Average

**Track weekly average to see big picture:**

| Week | Avg HRV | Trend | Action |
|------|---------|-------|--------|
| Week 1 | 65ms | Baseline | Normal training |
| Week 2 | 70ms | ↑ Improving | Body adapting well |
| Week 3 | 55ms | ↓ Declining | Reduce volume, add recovery |
| Week 4 | 68ms | ↑ Recovering | Deload worked, resume training |

## Factors Affecting HRV

### Positive Influences (Increase HRV)

✅ **Quality sleep** (biggest factor)
✅ **Good training adaptation** (fitness improving)
✅ **Meditation/breathwork**
✅ **Low stress**
✅ **Adequate recovery time**
✅ **Good nutrition** (anti-inflammatory)
✅ **Hydration**
✅ **Cold exposure** (post-acute response)
✅ **Massage/bodywork**
✅ **Social connection**
✅ **Time in nature**

### Negative Influences (Decrease HRV)

❌ **Poor sleep** (quality or quantity)
❌ **Overtraining** (insufficient recovery)
❌ **High psychological stress**
❌ **Illness** (HRV drops 24-48h before symptoms)
❌ **Alcohol** (even 2-3 drinks tanks HRV)
❌ **Late meals** (digestion during sleep)
❌ **Dehydration**
❌ **Inflammation** (injury, poor diet)
❌ **Travel** (especially across time zones)
❌ **Lack of routine** (variable sleep/wake times)
❌ **Heat stress** (overheating during sleep)

## HRV + Resting Heart Rate (RHR)

### Combined Analysis (Most Accurate)

Using HRV AND RHR together gives clearest recovery picture:

| HRV | RHR | Status | Recommendation |
|-----|-----|--------|----------------|
| 🟢 High | ⬇️ Low | ✅ Excellent | Full intensity training |
| 🟡 Normal | ➡️ Normal | ✅ Good | Normal training |
| 🔴 Low | ⬆️ High | ❌ Poor recovery | Active recovery only |
| 🟢 High | ⬆️ High | ⚠️ Acute stress | Monitor, possible illness |
| 🔴 Low | ⬇️ Low | ⚠️ Chronic stress | Deload, increase recovery |

**RHR Guidelines:**
- Know your baseline RHR (average over 2 weeks)
- RHR elevated 5+ bpm = body under stress
- RHR + low HRV = strong signal to rest

## Advanced HRV Interpretation

### Acute vs Chronic Stress

**Acute Stress (1-3 days):**
- Sudden HRV drop
- Clear cause (hard workout, poor sleep, stressful event)
- **Action:** Extra recovery, will bounce back quickly

**Chronic Stress (7+ days):**
- Persistent low HRV
- No clear single cause
- Feeling fatigued, unmotivated
- **Action:** Significant intervention needed (deload, lifestyle changes)

### Overtraining Syndrome

**Warning signs (persistent 2+ weeks):**
- 🔴 Chronically low HRV (10-20+ ms below baseline)
- ⬆️ Elevated RHR (5+ bpm above baseline)
- 📉 Declining performance despite training
- 😴 Poor sleep quality
- 😞 Low motivation
- 🤒 Frequent illness
- 🧠 Brain fog, poor focus
- 💔 Mood disturbances

**Recovery protocol:**
1. Reduce training volume by 40-60%
2. Prioritize sleep (+1-2 hours)
3. Active recovery only for 1-2 weeks
4. Stress management focus
5. Review nutrition (anti-inflammatory)
6. Consider professional help (coach, doctor)

**Full recovery time:** 2-8+ weeks (don't rush it)

### Illness Detection

**HRV typically drops 24-48 hours BEFORE symptoms:**

- Sudden 15-20% HRV drop with no clear cause
- May feel "off" but not obviously sick
- **Action:** Preemptive rest, extra sleep, immune support
- Often prevents full illness or reduces severity

**Training with illness:**
- Above the neck (stuffy nose, mild sore throat): Light activity may be okay
- Below the neck (chest congestion, body aches, fever): Absolute rest
- When in doubt: Monitor HRV, prioritize recovery

## HRV Recovery Strategies

### Immediate (When HRV is Low)

**Same day actions:**
- [ ] Extra 30-60 min sleep (earlier bedtime)
- [ ] Active recovery instead of training
- [ ] 10-20 min meditation or breathwork
- [ ] Cold plunge or contrast therapy
- [ ] Eliminate unnecessary stressors
- [ ] Nourishing, anti-inflammatory meals
- [ ] Extra hydration (electrolytes)

### Short-term (2-7 days of low HRV)

- [ ] Deload training (reduce volume 40-50%)
- [ ] Prioritize sleep (8-9 hours)
- [ ] Daily recovery modalities (sauna, cold, massage)
- [ ] Stress audit (identify and reduce stressors)
- [ ] Anti-inflammatory nutrition
- [ ] Meditation/mindfulness practice
- [ ] Reduce alcohol, caffeine

### Long-term (Build HRV Baseline)

**Consistent practices (3+ months):**
- Consistent sleep/wake schedule
- Regular moderate exercise (not excessive)
- Daily breathwork or meditation (10+ min)
- Stress management practices
- Anti-inflammatory diet
- Strong social connections
- Time in nature
- Purposeful recovery weeks (every 4-6 weeks)

## HRV Breathwork

**Slow breathing directly increases HRV:**

### Resonant Frequency Breathing

**Protocol:**
- Breathe at 5-6 breaths per minute
- Inhale 5 seconds, exhale 5 seconds
- Duration: 10-20 minutes
- Frequency: Daily (ideally)

**Benefits:**
- Immediately increases HRV during practice
- Builds HRV baseline with consistent practice
- Balances autonomic nervous system
- Reduces stress response

### Best Practices

**When to practice:**
- Morning (set tone for day)
- Before bed (enhance parasympathetic)
- During stress (acute intervention)
- Pre-measurement (increase HRV reading)

**Note:** Practicing slow breathing before HRV measurement can artificially elevate it. For accurate baseline, measure before breathwork.

## HRV Tracking Best Practices

### Consistency is Key

- [ ] Measure same time daily (morning upon waking)
- [ ] Same position (lying or seated, stay consistent)
- [ ] Before standing, coffee, or phone checking
- [ ] Calm state (deep breathing can artificially raise)
- [ ] Track in app/journal along with notes

### What to Track Alongside HRV

**Daily notes:**
- Sleep duration and quality
- Training (type, intensity, volume)
- Stress level (work, life events)
- Alcohol consumption
- Illness or injury
- Nutrition quality
- Mood and energy

**Weekly review:**
- HRV trend (improving/declining/stable)
- Average HRV
- Training load
- Recovery quality
- Stressors

### Long-term Tracking

**Establish baseline (4-8 weeks):**
- Track daily without changing anything
- Calculate average and standard deviation
- Identify your green/yellow/red zones
- Note seasonal variations

**Adjust training based on data (ongoing):**
- Not a crystal ball, but a guide
- Trust your subjective feeling too
- HRV + how you feel = best decision
- Be flexible with training plan

## HRV Myths & Misconceptions

### ❌ Myth: Higher HRV is always better

**Reality:** Too high HRV can indicate:
- Overtraining (paradoxical response)
- Illness (immune system activation)
- Extreme detraining (too much rest)

**Best:** Stable HRV slightly above your baseline

### ❌ Myth: You should train when HRV is green only

**Reality:** Training is what drives adaptation. Training on yellow days is normal and necessary. Just avoid max efforts when red.

### ❌ Myth: HRV is the only recovery metric needed

**Reality:** HRV is powerful but not perfect. Combine with:
- Resting heart rate
- Subjective feeling
- Sleep quality
- Performance in training
- Motivation levels

### ❌ Myth: You can "hack" HRV for better readings

**Reality:** Yes, slow breathing before measurement raises HRV acutely. But this defeats the purpose. You want accurate baseline data, not manipulated readings.
