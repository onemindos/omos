# Therapy Preparation Guide

> *Maximize therapy effectiveness through preparation, active participation, and integration.*

---

## Overview

Therapy is most effective when you actively participate, prepare for sessions, and integrate insights between sessions. This guide helps you get the most value from your therapeutic work.

**Investment mindset:** Therapy is expensive (time + money). Preparation ensures you don't waste sessions figuring out what to talk about.

---

## Finding the Right Therapist

### Types of Therapy

**Common evidence-based approaches:**

| Type | Best For | Approach |
|------|----------|----------|
| **CBT** (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) | Anxiety, depression, thought patterns | Change thoughts to change feelings/behaviors |
| **DBT** (Dialectical Behavior Therapy) | Emotion regulation, BPD, self-harm | Skills: distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness |
| **ACT** (Acceptance & Commitment Therapy) | Anxiety, chronic pain, values alignment | Accept thoughts/feelings, commit to values-based action |
| **Psychodynamic** | Relationship patterns, unconscious patterns | Explore past to understand present |
| **EMDR** | Trauma, PTSD | Eye movements to process traumatic memories |
| **IFS** (Internal Family Systems) | Complex trauma, inner conflict | Work with different "parts" of self |
| **Somatic / Sensorimotor** | Trauma held in body, dissociation | Body-based trauma processing |

**Note:** Many therapists use integrative approaches (combine multiple modalities)

---

### Finding a Therapist

**Directories:**
- **Psychology Today:** Filter by insurance, specialty, approach
- **TherapyDen:** LGBTQ+-affirming, diverse representation
- **Inclusive Therapists:** BIPOC, LGBTQ+, marginalized communities
- **OpenPath Collective:** Affordable therapy ($30-80/session)
- **Your insurance provider:** Often has searchable directory

**What to look for:**
- [ ] Accepts your insurance (or affordable out-of-pocket)
- [ ] Specializes in your concerns (anxiety, trauma, depression, etc.)
- [ ] Training in evidence-based modalities
- [ ] Licensed (LMFT, LCSW, psychologist, psychiatrist)
- [ ] Good initial "fit" (rapport matters)

---

### Initial Consultation Questions

**Ask potential therapists:**

**Logistics:**
- What's your fee? Do you take my insurance?
- What's your cancellation policy?
- Do you offer telehealth or in-person only?
- How often do you typically meet with clients?

**Approach:**
- What's your therapeutic approach?
- What experience do you have with [your concerns]?
- How do you measure progress?
- What's your approach to homework/between-session work?

**Fit:**
- How would you describe your style? (Directive vs. non-directive, structured vs. flexible)
- What should I expect in our first few sessions?
- How long do clients typically work with you?

**Trust your gut:** If something feels off, it's okay to keep looking

## Before Your First Session

### Intake Preparation

**What to prepare:**

**1. Basic information:**
- Current symptoms or concerns
- When they started
- What you've tried so far
- Current medications (if any)
- Previous therapy experience

**2. Goals for therapy:**
- What do you want to change?
- How will you know therapy is working?
- What would "success" look like?

**Examples:**
- "I want to reduce panic attacks from daily to once a month"
- "I want to feel comfortable in social situations"
- "I want to process my childhood trauma"
- "I want to improve my relationships"

**3. Mental health history:**
- Previous diagnoses
- Psychiatric hospitalizations
- Suicide attempts or self-harm
- Substance use
- Family mental health history

**4. Current life context:**
- Living situation, relationships
- Work/school stress
- Major recent life changes
- Support system

---

### First Session Expectations

**What happens:**
- Therapist gathers information (intake)
- You share why you're seeking therapy
- Discuss goals and expectations
- Therapist explains their approach
- Establish logistics (frequency, fees, policies)

**What to bring:**
- Insurance card
- List of current medications
- Notes on what you want to address
- Questions for therapist

**It's okay if:**
- You cry or get emotional
- You don't know what to say
- You don't share everything first session
- It feels awkward (building rapport takes time)

**Red flags:**
- Therapist is judgmental or dismissive
- Therapist talks mostly about themselves
- Therapist oversteps boundaries
- You feel worse after session without feeling heard
- Therapist guarantees quick fixes

## Preparing for Each Session

### Pre-Session Checklist (15-30 min before)

**Review:**
- [ ] **Last session notes:** What did we discuss? What insights did I have?
- [ ] **Homework:** If assigned, what did I complete? What did I learn?
- [ ] **This week's challenges:** What was difficult this week?
- [ ] **This week's wins:** What went well?
- [ ] **What to discuss today:** Prioritize topics (what's most urgent/important?)

**Example template:**

```
SESSION DATE: 2025-12-29

SINCE LAST SESSION:
- Challenges: ________________
- Wins: ________________
- Emotions this week: ________________
- Patterns noticed: ________________

TOPICS FOR TODAY (prioritized):
1. ________________
2. ________________
3. ________________

QUESTIONS FOR THERAPIST:
- ________________
- ________________

HOMEWORK STATUS:
- Completed: ________________
- Struggled with: ________________
```

---

### What to Bring Up in Session

**High priority:**
- Crisis or safety concerns (suicidal thoughts, self-harm, severe anxiety)
- Major life events (loss, trauma, big changes)
- Patterns you're noticing
- Specific situations you need help navigating
- Progress or setbacks on goals

**Medium priority:**
- Insights from last session
- Questions about therapy process
- Skill practice results
- Relationship challenges
- Recurring thoughts or emotions

**Lower priority (if time):**
- General updates
- Venting without seeking solutions
- Topics outside therapy focus

**Don't wait to bring up hard topics:** Avoidance maintains problems

## During the Session

### Active Participation

**Be an engaged participant:**
- [ ] **Be honest:** Therapy only works if you're truthful
- [ ] **Share specific examples:** "I felt anxious" → "I had a panic attack at the grocery store Tuesday"
- [ ] **Ask questions:** If confused, ask for clarification
- [ ] **Give feedback:** "That technique didn't work for me" or "That really resonated"
- [ ] **Bring up the relationship:** If something feels off with therapist, discuss it

**It's okay to:**
- Cry, get angry, feel uncomfortable
- Not know the answer
- Disagree with therapist
- Ask to change topics
- Request a specific technique or approach

---

### Take Notes

**Benefits of notes:**
- Remember insights and homework
- Review between sessions
- Track progress over time

**What to write:**
- Key insights or "aha moments"
- Homework assignments
- Techniques to practice
- Patterns identified
- Questions that came up
- How you felt during/after

**When to take notes:**
- During session (if it helps, ask therapist if okay)
- Immediately after session (while fresh)
- Brief notes on phone/paper

---

### Common Session Structures

**First 10-15 minutes:**
- Check-in: How has the week been?
- Review homework
- Identify focus for today

**Middle 30-35 minutes:**
- Deep dive into topic
- Skill-building or processing
- Problem-solving
- Exploration of patterns

**Last 5-10 minutes:**
- Summary of session
- Assign homework
- Schedule next session
- Check if anything left unsaid

---

## After the Session

### Immediate Post-Session (0-30 min)

**Processing:**
- [ ] **Journal or voice memo:** Key insights, feelings about session
- [ ] **Note action items:** What's my homework?
- [ ] **Emotional check:** How do I feel now? (Common to feel raw/vulnerable after)
- [ ] **Self-care:** Rest, walk, comforting activity

**It's normal to feel:**
- Emotionally drained or raw
- Relieved or lighter
- Confused or overwhelmed
- Resistant or defensive
- Hopeful or empowered

**Give yourself time to process - don't rush back to demands immediately**

---

### Session Integration (Hours to Days After)

**Make therapy stick:**

**1. Review notes within 24 hours**
- Re-read insights
- Clarify homework
- Identify what resonated most

**2. Practice homework/skills**
- Daily practice (even 5-10 min)
- Track what happens when you practice
- Note challenges or successes

**3. Notice patterns in daily life**
- "Oh, that's the pattern we talked about!"
- Catch cognitive distortions in action
- Apply skills in real situations

**4. Journal prompts:**
- What did I learn about myself today?
- What do I want to explore more next session?
- What am I noticing differently since therapy started?

---

### Homework Tracking

**Therapy homework examples:**
- Thought records (CBT)
- Exposure exercises (anxiety)
- Gratitude journal
- Behavioral experiments
- Emotion tracking
- Communication practice
- Mindfulness exercises

**Tracking template:**

```
HOMEWORK: ________________
ASSIGNED: [Date]

PRACTICE LOG:
Day 1: ________________ (How it went, insights)
Day 2: ________________
Day 3: ________________

CHALLENGES:
________________

QUESTIONS FOR NEXT SESSION:
________________

EFFECTIVENESS (1-5): ___
```

**If you don't do homework:**
- It's okay, but be honest about it
- Discuss barriers (too difficult, forgot, unclear, etc.)
- Adjust homework to be realistic
- Therapy still works, but slower without practice

## Maximizing Therapy Effectiveness

### Between-Session Work

**Therapy is 1 hour/week. Your healing happens in the other 167 hours.**

**Daily practices:**
- [ ] 5-10 min mindfulness or meditation
- [ ] Practice skills learned in therapy
- [ ] Journal (emotions, patterns, insights)
- [ ] Implement behavior changes discussed

**Weekly review:**
- [ ] Review session notes
- [ ] Track progress on goals
- [ ] Prepare for next session
- [ ] Identify patterns or themes

---

### Measuring Progress

**Track:**
- **Symptom frequency/intensity:** Track panic attacks, depressive episodes, anxiety levels
- **Functioning:** Work, relationships, self-care (improving?)
- **Skill use:** How often am I using therapy skills?
- **Insight:** Am I understanding myself better?
- **Goal progress:** Am I moving toward my therapy goals?

**Timeline:**
- Weeks 1-4: Building rapport, assessment
- Months 2-3: Learning skills, small improvements
- Months 4-6: Noticeable changes, applying skills
- 6-12 months: Significant progress, maintenance

**Regression is normal:** Setbacks happen, they're part of the process

---

### When to Bring Up Concerns

**Discuss with therapist if:**
- Not feeling progress after 8-12 sessions
- Techniques aren't working
- You don't feel heard or understood
- The relationship feels off
- You want to try a different approach
- You're thinking of quitting therapy

**Example:** "I've been coming for 3 months and I don't feel like we're making progress. Can we talk about this?"

**It's okay to change therapists if not a good fit**

---

## Difficult Topics to Discuss

### Building Courage

**Topics people avoid:**
- Suicidal thoughts
- Trauma or abuse
- Shame or embarrassment
- Anger at therapist
- Sexual issues
- Addiction or substance use
- Relationship infidelity

**Strategies for bringing up hard topics:**

**1. Ease in:**
- "There's something I've been avoiding talking about..."
- "This is really hard for me to say..."

**2. Write it:**
- Write it down and hand to therapist
- Email or text before session (if therapist allows)
- Read from phone during session

**3. Start vague:**
- "Something happened in my past I haven't talked about..."
- Therapist can gently guide from there

**4. Set the intention:**
- "Today I want to talk about [topic] but it's scary for me."
- "I'm going to be brave and bring something up today."

**Remember:** Therapists have heard it all. There's nothing too shameful or shocking.

## Phases of Therapy

### Phase 1: Assessment & Alliance (Sessions 1-4)

**Focus:**
- Building trust and rapport
- Therapist understands your story
- Establishing goals
- Agreeing on approach

**Your role:**
- Be open and honest
- Share your story
- Ask questions
- Assess if therapist is good fit

---

### Phase 2: Skill-Building (Months 2-4)

**Focus:**
- Learning coping skills
- Understanding patterns
- Practicing new behaviors
- Building emotional regulation

**Your role:**
- Practice homework consistently
- Try new techniques even if uncomfortable
- Track what works
- Give feedback on techniques

---

### Phase 3: Deep Work (Months 4-12)

**Focus:**
- Processing trauma or painful experiences
- Challenging core beliefs
- Making lasting changes
- Integrating new insights

**Your role:**
- Be vulnerable
- Tolerate discomfort (growth is uncomfortable)
- Apply skills in difficult situations
- Reflect deeply

---

### Phase 4: Maintenance & Termination (Variable)

**Focus:**
- Consolidating gains
- Preparing for life without regular therapy
- Relapse prevention planning
- Celebrating progress

**Your role:**
- Practice independence
- Prepare for ending (if appropriate)
- Discuss maintenance plan
- Recognize your growth

---

## When to End Therapy

### Signs You're Ready

- [ ] Goals achieved or significantly improved
- [ ] Using skills independently
- [ ] Functioning well in daily life
- [ ] Resilient to setbacks
- [ ] Feeling confident in managing mental health

### Graduation, Not Abandonment

**Ending well:**
- Discuss ending 4-6 weeks in advance
- Review progress and growth
- Create maintenance plan
- Discuss returning if needed
- Final session to process ending

**You can return:** Therapy can be episodic (leave when well, return if needed)

---

## Red Flags in Therapy

### When to Switch Therapists

❌ **Boundary violations:**
- Romantic or sexual advances
- Asks to socialize outside therapy
- Shares excessive personal information
- Borrows money or asks for favors

❌ **Poor clinical practice:**
- Consistently late or cancels frequently
- Judgmental or shaming
- Doesn't listen or interrupts constantly
- Forces techniques you're uncomfortable with
- Guarantees cure or quick fix

❌ **Lack of progress:**
- No improvement after 6-12 months
- Refuses to try different approaches
- Blames you for lack of progress
- Keeps you dependent (therapy should lead to independence)

**Trust your gut - if it feels wrong, it probably is**

## Special Considerations

### Online/Telehealth Therapy

**Pros:**
- Convenience (no commute)
- Access to specialists anywhere
- Comfortable setting (home)

**Cons:**
- Technology issues
- Less personal connection (for some)
- Distractions at home

**Tips:**
- Private space (not public place)
- Good internet connection
- Headphones (privacy)
- Minimize distractions (turn off notifications)

---

### Therapy as Person of Color

**Considerations:**
- Racial trauma is real and valid
- Cultural context matters
- May prefer therapist of same race/ethnicity
- Use directories: TherapyDen, Inclusive Therapists, Therapy for Black Girls

---

### LGBTQ+ Therapy

**Find affirming therapist:**
- Look for "LGBTQ+ affirming" or "gender affirming"
- Ask about experience with LGBTQ+ clients
- Should use correct pronouns, understand LGBTQ+ issues
- Resources: TherapyDen, Pride Counseling

---

## Therapy Cost & Access

### Making Therapy Affordable

**Options:**
- **Insurance:** Check in-network providers
- **Sliding scale:** Many therapists offer reduced fees
- **Open Path Collective:** $30-80/session
- **Training clinics:** Universities offer low-cost therapy (supervised trainees)
- **Employee Assistance Programs (EAP):** Often 3-8 free sessions
- **Community mental health centers:** Low-cost or free
- **Online platforms:** BetterHelp, Talkspace (~$260-360/month)
