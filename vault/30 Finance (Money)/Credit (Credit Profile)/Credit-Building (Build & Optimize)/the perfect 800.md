Getting A Perfect 850 Credit Score
Intro To Credit
To get a perfect credit score in the USA you must:

1) Have 0 inquiries in the past 12 months (10% of score)

2) Have over 21+ diversified accounts (10% of score)

3) Have an average age of credit accounts of 10+ years (15% of score)

4) Utilization between 0%-9% (30% of score)

5) Have 100% on-time payments (17.5% of score)

6) Have 0 derogatory marks/bankruptcies (17.5% of score)

The banks/institutions will update your credit report every 30 days so when you fix/change something it can take up to 30 days to update your score. And remember if you are checking your vantage score through Credit Karma or something similar, it can have a 14 day delay after the bureaus update your FICO score and is not extremely accurate.

CANADA

Canada has a system that’s very similar to America’s. Their two major credit bureaus are TransUnion Canada and Equifax Canada. While they once had an Experian Credit Bureau, it closed in 2009. Credit scores in Canada range from 300-900 - a 50-point difference from the 850 maximum credit score here in the states. 

Canada has two main credit reporting agencies that collect and assess your financial information: Equifax of Canada Inc. and TransUnion Canada. Canadian credit reports contain information about every loan you’ve taken out in the past six years, including how much you owe, whether you make your repayments on time and the credit limit on each account.

Credit scores in Canada range from 300 up to 900. The formula the credit reporting bureaus use to calculate your score is secret, but 27% of the population has a score between 750 and 799. The formula of how the scores are calculated is said to be very similar to that of the United States. 

According to TransUnion, 650 is an important credit score to keep in mind. Score less than this and you may have trouble having credit applications approved, but score more and you should be able to qualify for a standard loan.

 

UK

The UK, which is comprised of four countries - England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland - follows a system that is comparable in some regards though different from the USA in others. 

Just like the USA, the UK has three main credit reporting agencies: Callcredit, Equifax and Experian. They also collect financial data in line with a comprehensive credit reporting system, ensuring that positive borrowing information, such as making repayments on time and in full, is included alongside any black marks.

However, each agency uses its own scoring system to develop your credit score:

Experian. The Experian Credit Score runs from 0-999, with anything above 881 considered good and 961 or above excellent.

Equifax. Equifax scores range from 0-710, with scores of 467 and above classed as excellent.

Callcredit generates a credit rating of between 1 and 5 for potential borrowers, with 1 being the lowest and 5 the highest.

With this in mind, you are borrowing power in the UK varies depending on the reporting agency and also on each individual credit provider’s lending criteria.

 

Here are some tips to help build/increase your credit score:

Get established with a UK address. Proof of residence is an important criteria for UK credit. Whether applying for a loan or opening a credit card or bank account, having proof of address will work in your favor.

 

Be gainfully employed. Any lender is more comfortable signing off on an individual who can prove that they have a steady, paying work situation. If you’re unemployed, self-employed, or working on a contract or part-time basis, you may not be extended the best rates or any aid at all.

 

Open a bank account in the UK. Your best bet of doing this is to open an account in the US with a bank that has UK branches. That way, when you head overseas, you’ve already established history with that bank.

 

Apply for credit cards with high-interest rates. This may sound counterintuitive but here’s our logic - we all know that no or low credit scores will only qualify you for credit cards and loans with high-interest rates. But, high-interest rates, that you pay off consistently, add points to your credit score and that’s a whole lot better than not earning points at all.

 

Put any - and all - bills in your name. Be sure to pay these bills on time and in full. Just as is true here in the states, so is it a great way to build your credit overseas.

 

Open one (or a couple) store cards. Don’t use these often and pay them off in full each month. Consider store cards the low-hanging-fruit of credit building. They’re easy to obtain and, when used responsibly, can be a fast way to establish credit.
