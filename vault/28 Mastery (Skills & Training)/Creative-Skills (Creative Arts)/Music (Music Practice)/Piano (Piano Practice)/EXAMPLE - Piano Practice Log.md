## Repertoire Practice

### Piece 1: Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2
**Current Status:** Refining
**Section Worked On:** Measures 13-24 (ornamented section)
**Practice Method:** Slow practice → Hands separate → Chunking → Full tempo

**Time Spent:** 40 minutes
**Starting Tempo:** 40 BPM (hands separate)
**Ending Tempo:** 60 BPM (hands together)
**Target Tempo:** 108-116 BPM (rubato)

**Technical Challenges:**
- Ornaments in measure 15-16 still not fluid. The grace notes into the trill need work.
- Left hand arpeggios need to stay softer - they're overpowering the melody in measures 18-20.
- Finger substitution in measure 22 (4th finger to 5th on D) keeps tripping me up.

**Musical Challenges:**
- Finding the right balance of rubato - sometimes pushing too much, sometimes too rigid.
- Dynamic shaping of the phrase in measures 13-16 needs more arch - crescendo earlier, diminuendo later.
- Voicing in measure 20-22 - melody line getting lost in the texture.

**Progress Notes:**
Real breakthrough today on the ornaments! Slow practice with the metronome at 40 BPM finally revealed the exact timing issue I was having. The trill should start ON the beat, not before. This changed everything.

Also recorded myself at 60 BPM - the phrase shape is much better than last week. Teacher will be pleased.

**Tomorrow's Focus:**
- Continue drilling measures 15-16 ornaments slowly
- Work on left hand dynamic control (pp throughout)
- Start integrating measures 13-24 with the opening section

## Challenges & Solutions

**Challenge:**
Left hand too loud in Chopin, overpowering the melody.

**Attempted Solutions:**
1. Practice left hand alone at pp
2. Practice right hand alone at mf to strengthen melody
3. Hands together with extreme dynamic contrast (LH: ppp, RH: mf)

**Result:**
Solution #3 worked best! The exaggerated contrast helped me feel the correct balance. When I brought it back to realistic dynamics, the balance was there.

**To Try Next Time:**
Continue this exaggerated contrast approach for other balance issues.
