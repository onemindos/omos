Global Trademark Search for “Legacy AI”
Trademark Status in Key Jurisdictions

United States (USPTO)

    • Existing Filings: No exact “Legacy AI” trademark is registered in the U.S., but similar marks exist.
      Notably, Digital Legacy AI (Serial No. 98843918) was filed in November 2024 by Digital Legacy AI,
      Inc. for cloud data storage services (Class 42) 1 . Another recent application is MY-LEGACY.AI (Serial
      No. 98888813), filed December 2024 by Clocr, Inc., covering an AI-based estate planning software
      service (Class 42) 2 . These indicate that “Legacy” combined with “AI” is already in use for tech
      services.
    • Registration Status: Both above marks are pending U.S. applications (status “new application –
      awaiting examination”) 3 2 . No registered U.S. trademark exactly matches “Legacy AI” as of this
      search.
    • Conflicts: The presence of “Legacy” + “AI” in pending marks could pose a conflict if your usage
      overlaps in goods/services. For example, a trademark examiner might find “Legacy AI” too similar to
      Digital Legacy AI if operating in related tech fields, potentially causing a refusal or requiring
      argument to overcome likelihood of confusion 1 . Additionally, “AI” is descriptive (i.e. it simply
     denotes artificial intelligence), so the distinctiveness of the mark rests largely on “Legacy.” This
     means protection may be narrower, and the USPTO may require a disclaimer of “AI” (as seen in the
     Digital Legacy AI application, which disclaims “DIGITAL” and “AI” 4 ).

European Union (EUIPO)

    • Existing Filings: A search did not reveal any registered EU trademark exactly for “Legacy AI.” This
      suggests the name appears available in the EU. However, numerous EU trademarks contain the
      word “Legacy” in various classes, so examiners could scrutinize an application for “Legacy AI” against
      prior “Legacy” marks in similar sectors. As of now, no EUIPO record shows a direct conflict for the
      combined term.
    • Registration Status: No EU trademark for “Legacy AI” is registered or pending in relevant Nice
      classes (e.g. classes 9 or 42 for software/AI) based on the current search. This indicates an
      opportunity to register in the EU, though a formal clearance search is recommended to ensure no
      similar marks were recently filed.
    • Conflicts: While no identical mark was found, the term’s components are common. “Legacy” by itself
      is used in various EU trademarks (in industries from finance to gaming), so if any existing mark with
      “Legacy” covers AI or software, it could be cited as a conflict. At present, we found no such EU mark
      specifically overlapping with an AI focus, so legal risk in the EU is low but not zero.

United Kingdom (UKIPO)

    • Existing Filings: No UK trademark for “Legacy AI” was found in the UKIPO database. This suggests
      the name has not been registered as a stand-alone mark in the UK. Like the EU, the word “Legacy”
      appears in other UK marks, but not in combination with “AI” for technology services.

Canada (CIPO)

    • Existing Filings: We found no Canadian trademark for “Legacy AI.” The Canadian registry shows
      marks containing “Legacy” (e.g. Legacy Memorial Websites for online memorial services 5 ), but
      none specifically for “Legacy AI.” Thus, the exact name seems unregistered in Canada.
    • Registration Status: No current registrations. The name appears to be free to apply for in Canada.
      Canadian examiners would consider inherent distinctiveness; “Legacy AI” might be seen as a
      combination of a common word and a descriptive term, which is generally registrable if not clearly
      descriptive of the services.
    • Conflicts: No direct conflicts identified. It would be prudent to ensure any application clearly defines
      the scope of services (to distinguish from marks like “Legacy” in unrelated fields). Given Canada’s use
      requirement, securing the mark will also require actual use in Canadian commerce to maintain
      rights.

Australia (IP Australia)

    • Existing Filings: Our search did not identify any Australian trademarks for “Legacy AI.” The term
      seems unregistered in Australia, similar to other jurisdictions. Australia, like other common law
      countries, will examine the mark for distinctiveness.
    • Registration Status: Available – No records of “Legacy AI” in the Australian trademarks database.
      An application can be filed; expect the IP Australia examiner to possibly query the distinctiveness of
      the term “AI” (which is generic).
    • Conflicts: No known conflicting marks. One should verify that no Australian businesses are already
      using the name without registration, as common-law usage could pose a challenge. At the registry
      level, however, no oppositions or prior marks were found for “Legacy AI” in Australia.

Other Major Markets

    • We did not find any international or Madrid System trademark registrations for “Legacy AI.” Key
      markets like China, India, and Japan show no obvious registrations of this exact name, but detailed
      local searches should be conducted before use. In WIPO’s Global Brand Database, “Legacy AI” did
      not return hits, suggesting it hasn’t been filed as an international trademark under the Madrid
      Protocol.
    • Notable Similar Marks: The term “Legacy” is common globally, so similar brands exist (without the
      “AI” part) in various industries. For example, there’s a Russian trademark for “LEGACY” 6 and other
      regional marks for “Legacy” plus another word. These generally won’t block “Legacy AI” unless they
      operate in the same industry class and jurisdiction. No current oppositions involving “Legacy AI”
      were found in public records, as no one has secured rights to oppose such a filing yet.

Potential Conflicts and Legal Risks
     • Likelihood of Confusion: The greatest risk in securing “Legacy AI” is the similarity to existing marks
       that pair “Legacy” with tech services. In the U.S., the pending Digital Legacy AI and My-Legacy.ai
       applications cover cloud storage and AI software for estate planning, respectively 1 2 . If your
       intended use overlaps (e.g. software-as-a-service, AI platforms), USPTO could find a likelihood of
       confusion with those marks, since all share the term “Legacy” and relate to AI services. This could
       lead to a refusal or a need to narrow your goods/services description to avoid overlap.
     • Descriptiveness: “AI” is a generic descriptor for artificial intelligence, and “Legacy” could be seen as
       descriptive in certain contexts (for instance, if referring to legacy systems in tech). There’s a risk that
       trademark offices may consider “Legacy AI” as not inherently distinctive. The USPTO might require a
       disclaimer of “AI” (as it did for the other applications) 4 , and in a worst case could refuse the mark
       if they feel it merely describes an AI relating to legacy data or heritage. To mitigate this, be prepared
       to demonstrate that “Legacy AI” functions as a brand name and not just a description of the product.
     • Crowded Field: “Legacy” is a commonly used term across many brands, so the trademark field for
       “Legacy” alone is crowded. While “Legacy AI” is unique in full, the prevalence of “Legacy” means any
       new trademark might have a narrower scope of protection. Owners of existing “Legacy” trademarks
       (in any market) might oppose your registration if they believe your use of “Legacy AI” could dilute
       their brand or cause confusion. For example, if a company has “Legacy” trademarked in a technology
       class, they might view “Legacy AI” as too close. No specific opposition is known currently, but this is a
       potential risk once your application is published for opposition.
     • International Considerations: Each jurisdiction will judge the mark independently. A name that
       passes in one country might face issues in another. For instance, if “Legacy AI” were to be translated
       or if “legacy” had a particular meaning in another language, there could be unforeseen obstacles. At
       present, no international registrations block the name, but monitoring is needed. It’s wise to watch
       the pending U.S. applications – if they mature to registration without opposition, they become
       stronger obstacles in the U.S. market. Conversely, if they encounter refusals or oppositions, that
       could signal trouble for “Legacy AI” as well.

Recommendations for Securing the Trademark and Handles
Trademark Strategy: - Proceed with Registration: Given that no identical mark is registered in your key
markets, you should move quickly to file for “Legacy AI” in the U.S., EU, UK, Canada, Australia, and any
other critical market where you plan to operate. Filing promptly can establish your priority. Use a clear
description of services to carve out your niche (e.g. “software platform featuring artificial intelligence for
[specific purpose]”), which can help avoid overlap with broader “Legacy” filings. In the U.S., consider an
Intent-to-Use (ITU) application if you haven’t started using the mark yet, to secure a filing date. - Class

Selection: Register in the appropriate Nice classes. For an AI product/service, Class 9 (software) and Class
42 (software as a service, AI services) are likely relevant. If your use of “Legacy AI” extends to things like
consulting (Class 35) or educational services (Class 41), include those as needed. Casting a slightly wider net
(within reason) will protect the brand from others in adjacent spaces. Just be prepared to demonstrate use
in each class in countries that require it. - Monitor and Oppose: Keep an eye on the pending applications
we identified. If Digital Legacy AI or My-Legacy.ai move toward registration and cover similar services,
and if you believe your brand could be impacted, be ready to file an opposition or reach out to their owners
to coexist. Conversely, once your trademark application is filed and published, watch for any oppositions
from those parties or others. Engage a trademark attorney to monitor official gazettes in each jurisdiction
for any new filings that include “Legacy AI” or similar phrasing, so you can act if someone else tries to
register a confusingly similar mark. - Enhance Distinctiveness: Because “Legacy AI” contains common
terms, you might strengthen your trademark protection by branding it in a unique way. This could mean
designing a distinctive logo and filing a trademark for the logo + word mark combined. A stylized mark or a
design mark can sometimes proceed even if the text alone is considered descriptive. For example, a unique
font or logo icon with “Legacy AI” could become a registered mark, giving you some protection while you
work on the word mark approval. Additionally, if “Legacy AI” remains borderline descriptive in some
jurisdictions, consider adding a tagline or secondary element to the branding to distinguish it (and even file
that as a separate mark). - Plan for International Expansion: If global reach is in your plans, consider
using the Madrid Protocol to streamline filings. You can file an international application based on one of
your national applications (e.g., the US or EU application) and designate multiple countries at once. This can
be more efficient and ensure consistent protection. However, be mindful: if the base application fails, it can
affect the Madrid filings. Alternatively, file directly in key markets like China, India, Japan where Madrid
coverage or enforcement might be weaker, to secure those rights early. Each market will have its own
examination, but establishing your filings now will create a deterrent for later copycats.


Social Media Handles: - Secure Available Variations: Immediately register variants of “LegacyAI” on all
major platforms – even if they’re not your ideal picks – to prevent squatters. This includes slight misspellings
or add-ons (e.g., @LegacyAI_Tech , @TheLegacyAI , or your company’s full name if it’s distinct).
Consistency is helpful, so try to use the same handle across platforms if possible. For instance, if you settle
on @LegacyAISolutions for Twitter, use that on Instagram, TikTok, etc. to build a unified presence. -
Contact Inactive Users: On platforms like X (Twitter), where @LegacyAi is inactive 17 , you can attempt to
request the handle. Twitter’s policy allows trademark owners to claim usernames that violate their
trademark or are inactive. Once your trademark is filed (or registered), you or legal counsel can submit a
trademark infringement report or an impersonation claim to Twitter/X, asserting your rights to the name.
Success isn’t guaranteed, but there’s a good chance to recover an inactive handle, especially if you can show
a trademark registration in hand. - Distinguish Your Pages: For platforms where another “Legacy AI”
presence exists (Facebook, Instagram), focus on differentiation. Use a distinct profile image/logo and clarify
your offering in the bio. You might add a tagline like “Legacy AI – [Your Solution]” to separate from the other
profile. If the other “Legacy AI” is in a different domain (they seem oriented to AI for business automation
with humor), make sure your content and description highlight your unique angle. This will not only avoid
confusion but also strengthen a potential case that your brand is separate (should any dispute arise). -
Obtain Domain Names: While not asked explicitly, securing the domain is critical too – check if
LegacyAI.com or regional domains (legacyai.co, legacyai.io, etc.) are available. Often, social media handle
squatters tie into domain squatters. If the .com is taken, consider a different domain (like legacy.ai if you
can acquire that country-code TLD, or a modified domain name). Owning a good domain that matches or
complements your brand can also bolster your argument for getting matching social handles. - Engage
