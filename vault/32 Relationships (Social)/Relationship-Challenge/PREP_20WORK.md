**PREP WORK**

**LESSON 1: KEEP YOUR COOL IN RELATIONSHIPS**

There's an opportunity for growth in every relationship.

Ideally, we get to grow with the person we're in a relationship with.
But sometimes, we find ourselves growing in different directions---and
this is when things get tricky.

When we sense that we're growing apart from someone, we try to control
the relationship.

There's a very simple way to see if this is happening to you.

**If you have a sense that you're forcing a relationship, it's likely
you are.**

That doesn't always mean the relationship isn't right. Most of the time,
we can let the relationship show us what we still need to heal (this
could be fear of abandonment, discomfort with vulnerability, or any kind
of attachment wound). We can then allow the tension in a relationship to
push us to rise.

**When you rise, the other person in the relationship has the
opportunity to rise with you ... or not.**

If we find that the other person doesn't embrace the opportunity to
learn, grow and evolve with us, this might reveal something.

Not every relationship is meant to last! And if it ends, that doesn't
mean it's a failure. In fact, it's quite the opposite.

Once we accept that every relationship is a learning opportunity, we can
see the spiritual value in every encounter we have---whether it's a
lifelong partnership or a brief run-in with someone who triggers or
moves us in some way.

All we have to do is stay present, relax and trust that the relationship
will unfold exactly as it's meant to. I know this isn't always easy, so
I designed a meditation to help you stop future-tripping about
relationships.

**LESSON 2: LOOK INTO THE MIRROR**

On my podcast, [**[Dear
Gabby]{.ul}**](https://gabbybernstein.com/podcast/shefali-lose-yourself-relationships/),
I take real-time questions from callers. So many of them are struggling
with relationships! They feel hurt by someone else's behavior, and
aren't sure how to move past the wound.

Here's what I tell them: As with any relationship, the ones that trigger
you the most are spiritual assignments.

When we feel hurt or confused by someone else, it's often because their
behavior mirrors something that still needs to be resolved within
ourselves. (Note that this isn\'t ALWAYS the case. In cases of violence
or abuse, visit my [**[Safety, Recovery and Mental Health
Resources)]{.ul}**](https://gabbybernstein.com/emergency-resources-2/).

I remember one Dear Gabby caller who wanted to be a spiritual coach, but
her husband seemed doubtful that she could find success in this field.

She was hurt by his reaction. But eventually, as I spoke with her on
Dear Gabby, she realized that she, too, had some doubt about her new
venture. This was really why her husband's behavior triggered her.

Other people are often a mirror reflection of what it is that we still
need to resolve. When we have the courage to turn that mirror back on
ourselves---rather than trying to change the other person---the real
healing work can begin.

Use these two steps to see into the universal mirror that relationships
present.

**Step 1**

Become willing to see your relationships as opportunities for
self-reflection. Use this affirmation:

-   ***The difficult relationships in my life are the perfect mirror for
    me to look closely at my own behavior.***

**Step 2**

Ask yourself which of your own limiting beliefs could be contributing to
an undesirable pattern in relationships.

For example, if you feel as though people don't believe in you, the
first question you want to ask yourself is, "Do I believe in me?" Or if
you think the outside world isn't giving you the money you want, ask
yourself, "Do I believe that I'm worthy of receiving?" Begin to look
inward.

When we stop pointing the finger at others and start looking inward for
guidance, we open ourselves up to transformational growth.

**LESSON 3: GET REAL TO HEAL**

Are you afraid to be vulnerable in your relationships?

Maybe you can relate to one of these limiting beliefs ...

*Vulnerability is a weakness.*

*Being vulnerable will make me look foolish.*

*If I'm vulnerable with someone else, I'll only get hurt.*

If you're nodding your head right now, you are not alone. So many of us
are taught to hide our vulnerability in an effort to protect ourselves.
The problem is, this belief can hold us back from developing deep,
authentic connections with others. It can also stop us from getting the
support we need.

So I invite you to see vulnerability in a different way.

**Our true power lies in our willingness to be vulnerable.**

The truth is, vulnerability is incredibly powerful. It creates genuine
connection. (I did a whole [**[Dear Gabby
episode]{.ul}**](https://gabbybernstein.com/podcast/shefali-lose-yourself-relationships/) on
this very topic!) When we are vulnerable, we are real---and our realness
makes us a magnet for love and connection. There is nothing sexier than
your authentic truth!

Allowing yourself to be vulnerable also serves others. When you let your
guard down, you invite others to do the same. You let others know it's
safe to be honest with you. And as a result, you strengthen your
relationships!

So how do you go about being vulnerable?

It's important to remember that vulnerability is a practice. It may even
be uncomfortable at first. That's okay! The more you allow yourself to
be vulnerable, the easier it becomes.

If you feel ready, open up to someone you feel safe with. Maybe it's
something as simple as sharing something you\'ve learned about yourself
as you prepare for the Relationship Challenge. If openly sharing this
truth seems too big for you at the moment, then write that truth in your
journal and consider whom you would feel safe to share it with.

Vulnerability feels good because you're honoring your emotions and
expressing them honestly. The more you practice it, the better you'll
feel! When we all get real, the world will heal!
