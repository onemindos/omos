# New York City — The Delacruz Family Base

> *Every family is from somewhere. For now, we are from here. And here shaped everything.*

---

## Why NYC Matters to This Family

New York City is not just where we live. It is the most Dominican city outside the Dominican Republic. Washington Heights and the Bronx are home to the largest Dominican diaspora communities in the United States. If the Delacruz / Guzman family is in New York, it is surrounded by its own heritage — its food, its music, its language, its people.

That matters. It is not accidental context. It is part of the story.

---

## The City as Heritage

| Context | What It Means |
|---------|--------------|
| **Washington Heights / Inwood** | Known as "Quisqueya Heights" — the heart of the Dominican diaspora in the US. The culture is alive here. |
| **The Bronx** | Second largest Dominican community in NYC. Family connections, food, community. |
| **Dominican-American identity** | NYC is where Caribbean heritage and American ambition meet. The Delacruz family lives at that intersection. |
| **The city itself** | Scale, density, exposure — the kids are growing up in one of the most complex cities on earth. That is its own education. |

---

## NYC and the Homestead Vision

The plan is not to stay forever. The goal is an **off-grid homestead** — land held in trust, built outside city systems.

NYC is the launch pad. The homestead is the destination. The tension between those two is intentional:

- NYC provides income infrastructure, network, and resources while the homestead is being built
- The kids experience both worlds — the city's intensity and eventually the land's rootedness
- The transition from city to land will be one of the defining chapters of the family story

When the move happens, it gets documented in `Legacy-Building/Legacy-Projects/Delacruz-Family-Timeline.md`.

## Dominican New York — Context for the Kids

When the children are old enough to understand, they should know:

- The Dominican Republic is ~1,500 miles from New York. The community made the trip and rebuilt the culture here.
- Washington Heights has been called the "second capital" of the Dominican Republic.
- Dominican-Americans have built businesses, political power, and community infrastructure in NYC for decades.
- The food, the music (merengue, bachata), the Spanish dialect — these are their inheritance, alive in the city around them.
