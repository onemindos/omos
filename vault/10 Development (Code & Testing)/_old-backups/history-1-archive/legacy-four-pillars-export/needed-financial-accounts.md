# Needed Financial Accounts

Retirement Account:

If your employer offers a 401(k) plan, it's often a good idea to take advantage of this. Alternatively or additionally, you might consider an IRA (Individual Retirement Account).

Investment Account:

If you have additional funds to invest after maximizing your retirement account contributions, you may consider having a taxable brokerage account.

Checking Account:

Typically, everyone should have at least one checking account. This is your primary account for depositing income and paying bills. Some people prefer to have a separate checking account for discretionary spending.

Savings Account:

It's a good idea to have a savings account for accumulating emergency funds and saving for short-term goals. The number of savings accounts you may need will depend on your saving goals. Some people use a method called "savings buckets" or "savings goals", where they have different savings accounts for different purposes (e.g., one for an emergency fund, one for a vacation fund, one for a new car, etc.)

High-Yield Savings or Money Market Account:

These accounts often offer higher interest rates than regular savings accounts, and can be beneficial for saving towards medium-term goals or just maximizing your return on funds that you need to keep liquid.

Business Account:

If you run a business, even as a sole proprietor, it's typically recommended to keep business and personal funds separate for clarity and tax purposes.
