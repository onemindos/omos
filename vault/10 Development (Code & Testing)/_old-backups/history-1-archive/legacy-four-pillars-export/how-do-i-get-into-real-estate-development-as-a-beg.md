# How do I get into real estate development as a beginner and make over 7 figures in 12 months

Getting into real estate development as a beginner and making over 7 figures in 12 months is a challenging goal, and it requires significant knowledge, experience, and resources. Here are some steps that you can take to get started:

- Educate yourself: Start by educating yourself about real estate development. Read books, articles, and blogs on the subject, attend seminars, and network with experienced developers. The more you learn, the better equipped you'll be to make informed decisions.

- Develop a plan: Create a plan that outlines your goals, strategies, and timelines. Identify the type of properties you want to develop, the locations you want to target, and the financing options that are available to you.

- Build a team: Real estate development involves many different professionals, including architects, engineers, contractors, and lawyers. Build a team of experienced professionals who can help you navigate the development process.

- Secure financing: Real estate development is a capital-intensive business, and you'll need to secure financing to fund your projects. Look into different financing options, such as loans, grants, and investors.

- Identify opportunities: Look for opportunities to acquire properties that have development potential. This could include distressed properties, land, or underutilized buildings.

- Develop the property: Once you have acquired a property, work with your team to develop it. This involves designing the project, obtaining permits, and overseeing construction.

- Sell or rent the property: Once the property is complete, you can sell it or rent it out to generate income.

It's important to note that making over 7 figures in 12 months is not guaranteed, and it will depend on many factors, including the type of property, location, and market conditions. Real estate development is also a highly competitive industry, and success often requires patience, persistence, and a willingness to take calculated risks.
