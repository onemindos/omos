# The 3-2-1 backup strategy (Data Backup)

The 3-2-1 backup strategy is a best practice for data protection and recovery. The numbers stand for three, two, one, and each represents a key part of a comprehensive data backup strategy. Here's how it works:

3: Keep Three Copies of Your Data
The first step is to always have three copies of your data. The first one is the original data on your device, and the other two are backups. The reason for multiple backups is simple: redundancy. If something happens to one copy of your data, you have others to fall back on.

2: Use Two Different Types of Storage Media
The "2" in the 3-2-1 rule suggests that you store your data backups on two different types of storage media. This could mean having one copy on an internal hard drive and another on an external drive, or one on a physical drive and another in the cloud. The purpose of this is to protect against different types of hazards. For example, an external hard drive might protect you against a system failure on your computer, but not against a fire or flood in your home. A cloud backup would protect against those threats.

1: Keep One Backup Offsite
The last part of the rule is to keep one backup offsite, or at least physically separate from the other backups. This could mean a cloud backup, or a physical backup stored in a different location (like a safety deposit box or a trusted friend or relative's home). This protects against local disasters like fires, floods, or theft.

This approach isn't the only way to protect your data, but it's a simple strategy that provides a lot of protection with minimal complexity. It's a good starting point for most personal or small business data backup needs.
