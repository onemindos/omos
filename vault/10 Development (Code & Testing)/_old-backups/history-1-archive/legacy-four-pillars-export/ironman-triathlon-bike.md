# Ironman triathlon Bike

In an Ironman triathlon, which consists of a 2.4-mile (3.86 km) swim, a 112-mile (180.25 km) bike ride, and a marathon 26.2-mile (42.20 km) run, competitors typically use a specific type of bicycle known as a "triathlon bike" or "time trial (TT) bike". These bikes are designed for aerodynamics and efficiency over long distances.

Triathlon/TT bikes have several distinguishing features:

- Aero Bars
: These are extended handlebars that allow the rider to lean forward in a tucked position to reduce air resistance.

- Steep Seat Tube Angle
: This positions the rider further forward on the bike, which can help to save the muscles used in running.

- Aero Frame Design
: The frame, wheels, and other components are designed to reduce drag.

- Integrated Storage
: Many modern triathlon bikes have integrated storage for nutrition, tools, and other essentials, keeping the bike's aerodynamics smooth.

While many competitors in Ironman events use triathlon/TT bikes, it's also common to see some athletes using regular road bikes, sometimes with clip-on aero bars attached. The choice often depends on the athlete's preference, budget, and the specific course conditions.
