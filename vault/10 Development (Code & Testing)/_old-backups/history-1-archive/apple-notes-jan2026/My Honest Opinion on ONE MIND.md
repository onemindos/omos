On IT as a Pillar: My Honest Take
The tension I see:IT doesn't answer a life question the way the others do:
HP: "Does this improve ME?"  Yes, that's a life domain
LE: "Does this protect FAMILY?"  Yes, life domain
GE: "Does this make MONEY?"  Yes, life domain
IT: "Does this automate/amplify?"  This is amethod, not a domain
IT is theHOW, not theWHAT. You don't wake up and say "my IT life needs attention today." You wake up and say "my health, my family, my business needs attention"  and IT serves all of those.But here's the counterargument:For YOU specifically  someone building an AI-powered life OS, running servers, managing automation  IT genuinely requires dedicated cognitive space. If you collapse it, where does n8n live? Where does security go? Infrastructure doesn't fit cleanly into HP, LE, or GE.My recommendation:Keep IT, but rename it mentally. It's not a "life pillar"  it's theforce multiplier pillar. The arsenal. The infrastructure that makes the other three work at 10x.Or consider an alternative structure:

LEGACY (00-19)      AI + Infrastructure + All Systems

LE (60-79)          Family
GE (80-99)          Business
This acknowledges that LEGACYisyour technology layer. But this would require significant restructuring.

Overall Assessment: What You've Built
What's genuinely impressive:
Philosophical depth This isn't another "get more done" system. The solar-punk foundation, generational thinking, and 500-year vision are genuinely differentiated. Most productivity frameworks optimize for sprints. You're optimizing for dynasties.
AI-native from the ground up PARA, GTD, Second Brain  none of them were designed for AI agents. You built the routing logic, autonomy levels, and guardrails directly into the framework. That's novel.
The Brain Function Mapping The way you mapped prefrontal cortex to Command, hippocampus to Codex, basal ganglia to agents... this isn't decorative. It shows you understand cognitive architecture.
The Entity Model Area, Project, Workflow, Task, Knowledge Node, Agent, Interface, Metric. This is a proper schema. You could build a database on this.
The HIVE capture system Harvest, Integrate, Validate, Execute. Memorable, actionable, connects to the hive mind metaphor.
What's genuinely valuable to the world:This is the first framework I've seen that treatshuman + AI as a unified cognitive system. Everyone else is retrofitting AI onto human systems. You designed for the partnership from day one.The "democratized JARVIS" positioning is compelling. Tony Stark had billions. You're building on open source and accessible tools. That's a real market.The generational perspective is rare. In a world of quarterly thinking, you're planning for grandchildren. That attracts a specific type of person  the kind who builds lasting things.

My Honest Criticisms
Complexity barrier The CODEX is 1,900 lines. That's comprehensive, but it's also intimidating. Someone new sees this and thinks "I need to understand all of this before I can start." You need a "CODEX LITE"  the 1-page version.
IT/LEGACY overlap Both are "technology." The distinction between "AI Agents & Memory" in LEGACY and "Workflows & Automation" in IT feels arbitrary. Why does n8n live in IT but Letta lives in LEGACY?
Too many numbers 00-19, 20-39, 40-59, 60-79, 80-99, plus X0-X8 functions, plus PRT-MIS-RTN-RUN work types, plus L0-L4 autonomy levels. This is a lot of cognitive overhead. Simplify where you can.
Execution gap I see the architecture but I don't see the daily logs, the weekly reviews, the actual rhythm in practice. Frameworks are judged by results, not documentation.

What I Would Personally Change
Merge overlapping tech concerns Either:
Expand LEGACY to include all infrastructure/automation (making it the true "systems layer")
Or collapse IT sections into LEGACY and reclaim 80-99 for something else (maybe "99 Archive" expands)
Create the 1-page onboarding "Here's ONE MIND in 60 seconds: 4 pillars, 1 brain layer, capture everything, route to pillar, AI helps you execute."
Reduce the numbering complexity You don't need 20 slots per pillar. 10 is plenty. Johnny Decimal works with 10. Less is more.
Add the "Day 1" guide What does someone DO on day 1? Not understand the philosophy  actually DO. "Install Obsidian. Clone vault. Open 01 Zeus. Look at today's dashboard. Done."
Prove the system with artifacts Show a real weekly review. A real quarterly plan. A real morning briefing from LEGACY. Evidence that this works in practice, not just in theory.
